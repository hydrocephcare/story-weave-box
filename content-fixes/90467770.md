**Q1.** 35-year-old, 30 min into pRBC transfusion: fever 38.8°C, rigors, flank pain, haemoglobinuria.
a. Febrile non-haemolytic reaction
b. Acute haemolytic transfusion reaction
c. TRALI
d. Anaphylactic reaction
e. Bacterial contamination
**Answer: b — Acute haemolytic transfusion reaction**
- Haemoglobinuria + flank pain = intravascular haemolysis, classic for AHTR (usually ABO mismatch). FNHTR lacks haemoglobinuria; TRALI causes respiratory distress, not haemolysis; anaphylaxis lacks fever/haemoglobinuria; bacterial contamination usually shows more severe sepsis picture.
**Q2.** Underlying immune mechanism of acute haemolytic transfusion reaction?

a. IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation
b. IgG/IgM-mediated complement activation
c. Cytotoxic T-cell destruction
d. Immune complex deposition in glomeruli
e. NK cell activation
**Answer: b — IgG/IgM-mediated complement activation**
- ABO antibodies (IgM mainly) activate complement → intravascular lysis. IgE (a) is Type I hypersensitivity, not relevant here; T-cells (c) and immune complexes (d) are not the primary mechanism in AHTR.
**Q3.** Which test detects IgG antibodies coating patient red cells?

a. Forward grouping
b. Reverse grouping
c. Direct antiglobulin test (DAT)
d. Indirect antiglobulin test (IAT)
e. Crossmatch by saline technique
**Answer: c — Direct antiglobulin test (DAT)**
- DAT detects antibody already bound to the patient's own cells; IAT (d) detects antibody in patient serum reacting with reagent cells; forward/reverse grouping (a,b) determine ABO type, not antibody coating.
**Q4.** Neonate with jaundice/anaemia; mother O Rh-negative, baby O Rh-positive.

a. Neonatal alloimmune thrombocytopenia
b. Haemolytic disease of the foetus and newborn
c. ABO incompatibility causing intravascular haemolysis
d. G6PD deficiency
e. Hereditary spherocytosis
**Answer: b — Haemolytic disease of the foetus and newborn**
- Rh-negative mother sensitised against Rh-positive baby's cells → anti-D crosses placenta → haemolysis. Same ABO group (both O) rules out ABO incompatibility (c); a, d, e are unrelated to this Rh mismatch scenario.
**Q5.** Group O Rh-negative patient, haemorrhagic shock, urgent transfusion before crossmatch available — safest choice?

a. Group A Rh-positive pRBC
b. Group O Rh-positive pRBC
c. Group O Rh-negative pRBC
d. Group AB Rh-positive whole blood
e. Group O Rh-negative whole blood only if crossmatched
**Answer: c — Group O Rh-negative pRBC**
- Universal donor red cells, safe without crossmatch in emergencies. Others carry ABO/Rh antigens (a,b,d) that could cause reaction; e wrongly requires crossmatch, defeating the emergency purpose.
**Q6.** 50-year-old, dyspnoea + bilateral infiltrates within 6h of transfusion, no fluid overload, SpO2 85%.

a. TACO
b. TRALI
c. Acute haemolytic reaction
d. FNHTR
e. Allergic/anaphylactic reaction
**Answer: b — TRALI**
- Acute lung injury within 6h, no volume overload, is the defining feature of TRALI. TACO (a) would show fluid overload signs; c, d, e don't fit this pulmonary/hypoxic presentation.
**Q7.** Unexpected alloantibody found in pre-transfusion testing — which technique confirms specificity?

a. Forward ABO grouping
b. Antibody panel (antibody identification)
c. DAT only
d. Platelet crossmatch
e. Clot retraction test
**Answer: b — Antibody panel (antibody identification)**
- A panel of known-antigen reagent cells identifies the specific antibody. Forward grouping (a) only determines ABO type; DAT (c) detects antibody on the patient's own cells, not identify specificity of a free antibody.
**Q8.** Sickle cell patient, long-term transfusion, anaemia despite transfusions, positive DAT.

a. Iron deficiency anaemia
b. Delayed haemolytic transfusion reaction (DHTR)
c. Transfusion-associated GVHD
d. Post-transfusion purpura
e. Hyperviscosity syndrome
**Answer: b — Delayed haemolytic transfusion reaction (DHTR)**
- Common in chronically transfused sickle cell patients due to alloimmunisation; positive DAT reflects antibody-coated transfused cells being destroyed. Others don't fit this typical chronic-transfusion complication.
**Q9.** 4-year-old with aplastic anaemia — mandatory processing step to prevent TA-GvHD?

a. Leukodepletion
b. Irradiation
c. Washing
d. Cryoprecipitate reduction
e. CMV-seronegative selection only
**Answer: b — Irradiation**
- Irradiation inactivates donor T-lymphocytes, preventing them from engrafting and causing GvHD in immunocompromised recipients. Leukodepletion (a) reduces WBC count but doesn't reliably prevent GvHD; washing (c) and CMV-negative selection (e) address different risks.
**Q10.** Which screening test uses nucleic acid amplification technology (NAT) to close the window period?

a. ELISA for HIV antibody
b. RPR for syphilis
c. NAT for HIV RNA and HCV RNA
d. HBsAg ELISA
e. Malaria RDT
**Answer: c — NAT for HIV RNA and HCV RNA**
- NAT detects viral genetic material directly, shortening the diagnostic window compared to antibody/antigen-based tests (a, d) which take longer to become positive after infection.
**Q11.** Massive transfusion (>10 units in 24h) → tetany, prolonged QT.

a. Hyperkalaemia
b. Hyponatraemia
c. Hypocalcaemia due to citrate chelation
d. Hypomagnesaemia
e. Hyperphosphataemia
**Answer: c — Hypocalcaemia due to citrate chelation**
- Citrate anticoagulant in stored blood binds calcium; massive transfusion overwhelms hepatic citrate metabolism → hypocalcaemia → tetany, prolonged QT. Hyperkalaemia (a) is also a massive transfusion risk but doesn't cause this specific tetany/QT picture.
**Q12.** 22-year-old IgA-deficient patient, history of anaphylaxis to prior transfusion — required blood modification?

a. Irradiated blood
b. CMV-negative blood
c. Washed red cells
d. Leukodepleted blood only
e. Cryopreserved blood
**Answer: c — Washed red cells**
- Washing removes residual donor plasma IgA, preventing anaphylaxis in IgA-deficient recipients with anti-IgA antibodies. Irradiation (a) and leukodepletion (d) don't remove plasma proteins; CMV-negative (b) addresses a different infectious risk.
**Q13.** Group A individual — antigens/antibodies?

a. Antigen A; anti-A and anti-B
b. Antigen A; anti-B in plasma
c. Antigen A; anti-A in plasma
d. No antigens; anti-A and anti-B
e. Antigens A and B; no antibodies
**Answer: b — Antigen A on red cells; anti-B in plasma**
- Group A people have A antigen and make antibodies only against the antigen they lack (B). (a) describes group O antibodies with wrong antigen combo; (c) would cause self-destruction (impossible); (d) is group O; (e) is group AB.
**Q14.** Chills, fever, hypotension after platelet transfusion; bag shows turbidity.

a. ABO incompatibility
b. Bacterial contamination of the platelet unit
c. TRALI
d. Post-transfusion purpura
e. FNHTR
**Answer: b — Bacterial contamination of the platelet unit**
- Turbidity of the bag strongly suggests bacterial growth; platelets are stored at room temperature, making them prone to bacterial proliferation. Other options don't explain visible turbidity.
**Q15.** Whole blood in CPDA-1, max storage at 2–6°C?

a. 21 days
b. 28 days
c. 35 days
d. 42 days
e. 56 days
**Answer: c — 35 days**
- CPDA-1 preservative extends storage to 35 days (vs 21 days for older ACD/CPD solutions).
**Q16.** Cryoprecipitate from FFP — which factor is NOT significantly enriched?

a. Factor VIII
b. von Willebrand factor
c. Fibrinogen
d. Factor XIII
e. Factor IX
**Answer: e — Factor IX**
- Cryoprecipitate concentrates Factor VIII, vWF, fibrinogen, and Factor XIII; Factor IX remains in the cryo-poor plasma supernatant, not the precipitate.
**Q17.** ITP patient, platelet count 18×10⁹/L, poor increment after platelet transfusion — why?

a. ABO incompatibility
b. Antibody-mediated rapid destruction of transfused platelets
c. Hypersplenism due to hepatomegaly
d. Dilutional thrombocytopenia
e. Storage lesion
**Answer: b — Antibody-mediated rapid destruction of transfused platelets**
- In ITP, autoantibodies destroy both native and transfused platelets rapidly, blunting the expected increment.
**Q18.** Post-partum woman, 5 units pRBC, develops oliguria, haemoglobinuria, DIC — most important initial management step?

a. Give FFP immediately
b. Stop the transfusion and send blood bag/patient sample to lab
c. Administer hydrocortisone
d. Increase drip rate to flush kidneys
e. Emergency crossmatch and restart
**Answer: b — Stop the transfusion and send blood bag and patient sample to the lab**
- First priority in any suspected haemolytic reaction is to stop the causative transfusion immediately and investigate; other management (FFP, steroids) follows only after confirming and stabilising, not before stopping the transfusion.
**Q19.** Why is the Rh system clinically important primarily?

a. Anti-Rh antibodies are naturally occurring IgM
b. Anti-D is an IgG antibody that crosses the placenta causing HDFN
c. Rh antigens are expressed only on platelet membranes
d. Rh incompatibility causes TRALI
e. Rh antigens are carbohydrate structures
**Answer: b — Anti-D is an IgG antibody that crosses the placenta causing HDFN**
- Unlike ABO antibodies (mostly IgM, naturally occurring), anti-D is IgG, immune-induced, and placenta-crossing — the basis of HDFN risk. Rh antigens are protein-based (not carbohydrate, e) and expressed on RBCs (not platelets, c).
**Q20.** HIV-positive patient on ART develops warm-antibody autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, DAT positive — most important transfusion consideration?

a. Irradiated blood must be used
b. Least incompatible blood after extended phenotyping
c. Group O Rh-negative blood must always be given
d. Washed red cells are contraindicated
e. Transfusion should be withheld regardless of clinical status
**Answer: b — Least incompatible blood should be selected after extended phenotyping**
- In warm AIHA, all units may appear incompatible due to autoantibody; extended phenotyping helps find the safest "least incompatible" unit rather than withholding transfusion (e) or defaulting to universal donor blood (c) without proper workup.
**Q21.** Female neonate, Kell-negative, born to Kell-negative mother with anti-K1 antibodies; neonate is Kell-positive.

a. Neonatal alloimmune thrombocytopenia
b. Haemolytic disease of the foetus and newborn
c. Transfusion-associated GvHD
d. Congenital aplastic anaemia
e. Post-transfusion purpura
**Answer: b — Haemolytic disease of the foetus and newborn**
- Maternal anti-K1 (from prior sensitisation) crosses the placenta and attacks Kell-positive foetal cells, causing HDFN (Kell system is a well-known non-Rh cause).
**Q22.** Most appropriate product for haemophilia A patient with significant bleed?

a. Fresh frozen plasma
b. Cryoprecipitate
c. Factor VIII concentrate
d. Platelet concentrate
e. Prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC)
**Answer: c — Factor VIII concentrate**
- Haemophilia A = Factor VIII deficiency; concentrate provides targeted, high-dose replacement. FFP (a) and cryoprecipitate (b) contain Factor VIII but in lower, less precise concentration; PCC (e) contains factors II, VII, IX, X, not VIII.
**Q23.** Obstetric patient develops TRALI — most likely donor characteristic?

a. Male donor with sickle cell trait
b. Multiparous female donor with HLA antibodies
c. CMV-positive donor
d. Donor with high-titre anti-A
e. Donor with G6PD deficiency
**Answer: b — Multiparous female donor with HLA antibodies**
- Pregnancy sensitises women to develop anti-HLA/anti-neutrophil antibodies, which are the classic cause of TRALI when transfused into a recipient.
**Q24.** Major haemorrhage protocol — recommended ratio of pRBC : FFP : platelets in damage-control resuscitation?

a. 6:1:1
b. 1:1:1
c. 3:1:2
d. 4:2:1
e. 2:1:0
**Answer: b — 1:1:1**
- Balanced 1:1:1 ratio mimics whole blood composition, reducing dilutional coagulopathy in massive transfusion protocols.
**Q25.** 10-year-old beta-thalassaemia major, chronic transfusion, >100 units pRBC — which long-term complication needs monitoring?

a. Alloimmunisation to ABO antigens
b. Iron overload (haemosiderosis)
c. Hypervolaemia
d. Transfusion-associated GvHD
e. Chronic TRALI
**Answer: b — Iron overload (haemosiderosis)**
- Each unit of pRBC adds iron with no physiological excretion mechanism; chronic transfusion leads to organ-damaging iron accumulation, requiring chelation therapy.
**Q26.** Patient with positive DAT on routine testing, no haemolysis symptoms — most appropriate next step?

a. Proceed with transfusion without further investigation
b. Perform antibody elution and further serological workup
c. Cancel transfusion permanently
d. Administer corticosteroids before proceeding
e. Request irradiated blood only
**Answer: b — Perform antibody elution and further serological workup**
- Identifying the antibody specificity is essential before deciding on transfusion safety, even if asymptomatic. (a) skips necessary safety workup; (c), (d), (e) are premature/inappropriate without knowing the cause.
**Q27.** Thawed FFP stored at 1–6°C — must be used within what time frame?

a. 6 hours
b. 12 hours
c. 24 hours
d. 48 hours
e. 5 days
**Answer: c — 24 hours**
- Once thawed, coagulation factor activity (especially labile Factor V and VIII) declines, so FFP must be used within 24 hours if kept at 1–6°C.
**Q28.** Alloantibody clinically significant but only reactive at 4°C (cold-reactive) — implicated blood group system?

a. Kell system
b. Duffy system
c. Kidd system
d. MNS system
e. I blood group system
**Answer: e — I blood group system**
- Anti-I is the classic cold-reactive antibody, active at low temperatures (as in cold agglutinin disease). Kell, Duffy, Kidd, and MNS antibodies are typically warm-reactive and clinically significant at 37°C.
**Q29.** Surgeon requests 4 units pRBC; no unexpected alloantibodies, compatible crossmatch — appropriate crossmatch method?

a. Full serological crossmatch with enzyme technique
b. Electronic (computer) crossmatch
c. Immediate-spin saline crossmatch only
d. No crossmatch required
e. Gel card crossmatch with 37°C incubation required in all cases
**Answer: b — Electronic (computer) crossmatch**
- When no clinically significant antibodies are present and ABO/Rh has been verified twice, an electronic crossmatch is safe and efficient, avoiding unnecessary serological testing.
**Q30.** Thrombocytopenia 7–10 days post-transfusion, strong immune response to HPA-1a antigen.

a. Delayed haemolytic transfusion reaction
b. Transfusion-associated GvHD
c. Post-transfusion purpura (PTP)
d. Febrile non-haemolytic reaction
e. Neonatal alloimmune thrombocytopenia
**Answer: c — Post-transfusion purpura (PTP)**
- Classic timing (7–10 days) and anti-HPA-1a response causing destruction of both donor and the patient's own platelets defines PTP.

### SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS**Question 1:** *28-year-old male with sickle cell disease, multiple transfusions over 5 years, admitted with acute chest syndrome.*
**a) Two alloantibodies + blood group systems (2 marks)**
- Anti-K (Kell blood group system)
- Anti-E or Anti-C (Rh blood group system)
- *(Sickle cell patients are frequently transfused by non-matched donors, commonly developing antibodies to Rh and Kell antigens due to phenotype mismatch between typically Caucasian donor pools and African patient populations)*
**b) Strongly positive DAT — most likely explanation + immediate management step (2 marks + 1 mark)**

- **Explanation: Delayed haemolytic transfusion reaction (DHTR) due to alloantibody-mediated destruction of transfused red cells
- **Immediate management:** Stop further transfusion of incompatible units, send samples for direct antiglobulin test/antibody identification, and provide antigen-negative (extended phenotype-matched) blood for any further transfusion
**c) After 15 years, >200 units, cardiac arrhythmias + hepatic fibrosis — underlying complication + chelating agent (1 mark + 1 mark)**

- **Underlying complication:** Transfusional iron overload (secondary haemosiderosis) — iron deposits in the heart (arrhythmias) and liver (fibrosis)
- **Chelating agent:** Deferoxamine (alternatively: deferasirox or deferiprone)

**Question 2:** *Cord blood sample + maternal sample sent for suspected HDFN — three specimen containers received (6 marks)*
**Specimen 1 — Cord blood (neonate):**
- **Investigations:** ABO and Rh(D) grouping; Direct antiglobulin test (DAT)
- **Reagents/antisera:** Anti-A, anti-B, anti-D antisera; anti-human globulin (Coombs) reagent for DAT
**Specimen 2 — Maternal blood:**

- **Investigations:** ABO and Rh(D) grouping; antibody screening and identification (Indirect antiglobulin test)
- **Reagents/antisera:** Anti-A, anti-B, anti-D antisera; reagent red cell panel; anti-human globulin (Coombs) reagent for IAT
**Specimen 3 — Neonatal serum/eluate (or repeat cord sample):**

- **Investigations:** Antibody elution from neonatal red cells followed by antibody identification (to confirm specificity of maternal antibody causing haemolysis)
- **Reagents/antisera:** Elution reagent kit; reagent red cell panel; anti-human globulin (Coombs) reagent
