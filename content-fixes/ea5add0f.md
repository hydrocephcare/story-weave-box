**Q1.** Which of the following best describes the primary mechanism underlying cellular metaplasia?
A. Irreversible genetic mutation in somatic cells
B. Reversible reprogramming of stem cells or undifferentiated cells
C. Direct transformation of fully differentiated cells
D. Failure of DNA repair mechanisms
E. Accumulation of misfolded proteins
**Answer: B**
- Metaplasia is a reversible adaptive change driven by reprogramming of stem/reserve cells to a different differentiated phenotype in response to a chronic stimulus — not a mutation (A) or direct conversion of mature cells (C).

**Q2.** In pathological hypertrophy of cardiac muscle, which molecular pathway is most commonly activated?
A. p53-mediated cell cycle arrest
B. mTOR signaling pathway
C. Caspase-dependent apoptosis
D. Autophagy-lysosomal pathway
E. NF-κB inflammatory pathway
**Answer: B**
- mTOR signalling drives the protein synthesis and cell growth underlying cardiomyocyte hypertrophy in response to increased workload. The others (A, C, D) relate to cell death/degradation, not growth.

**Q3.** Barrett's esophagus represents which type of cellular adaptation, and what is its primary clinical significance?
A. Hyperplasia; increased risk of squamous cell carcinoma
B. Atrophy; decreased digestive function
C. Metaplasia; increased risk of adenocarcinoma
D. Hypertrophy; improved acid resistance
E. Dysplasia; immediate malignant transformation
**Answer: C**

- Chronic acid reflux causes squamous epithelium to convert to columnar (intestinal-type) epithelium — classic metaplasia — which predisposes to esophageal adenocarcinoma, not squamous carcinoma (A).

**Q4.** Which cellular organelle is most critically involved in maintaining cellular energy homeostasis and is first affected during hypoxic injury?
A. Rough endoplasmic reticulum
B. Golgi apparatus
C. Mitochondria
D. Lysosomes
E. Peroxisomes
**Answer: C**
- Mitochondria generate ATP via oxidative phosphorylation; hypoxia halts this immediately, making them the first organelle affected. Others (A, B, D, E) are affected secondarily as ATP depletion progresses.

**Q5.** Oncosis differs from apoptosis primarily in which of the following characteristics?
A. Nuclear fragmentation pattern
B. Cell membrane integrity
C. Energy requirement
D. Inflammatory response
E. DNA ladder formation
**Answer: B**

- Oncosis (necrotic cell swelling) involves early loss of membrane integrity and cell lysis, while apoptosis preserves membrane integrity until late (apoptotic bodies are phagocytosed intact). D and E are also differences, but membrane integrity is the most fundamental distinguishing feature.

**Q6.** Which of the following best explains why neurons are particularly vulnerable to hypoxic injury compared to other cell types?
A. Higher metabolic rate and dependence on aerobic respiration
B. Larger cell size requiring more nutrients
C. Inability to undergo cellular adaptation
D. Lack of antioxidant mechanisms
E. Higher concentration of mitochondria
**Answer: A**
- Neurons have very high oxidative metabolic demand and rely almost entirely on aerobic respiration for ATP, making them exquisitely sensitive to even brief hypoxia.

**Q7.** Which mechanism is primarily responsible for the removal of damaged organelles during cellular stress?
A. Proteasomal degradation
B. Exocytosis
C. Macroautophagy
D. Apoptosis
E. Necroptosis
**Answer: C**
- Macroautophagy specifically engulfs and degrades damaged organelles via autophagosomes. Proteasomal degradation (A) targets individual misfolded proteins, not whole organelles.

**Q8.** Free radical injury to cells primarily affects which cellular components first?
A. Nuclear DNA
B. Mitochondrial DNA
C. Cell membrane lipids
D. Cytoplasmic proteins
E. Ribosomal RNA
**Answer: C**
- Lipid peroxidation of membrane lipids is the earliest and most characteristic effect of free radical damage, disrupting membrane integrity before DNA/protein damage becomes significant.

**Q9.** In reversible cell injury, which morphological change is typically observed first?
A. Nuclear pyknosis
B. Cell membrane blebbing
C. Cellular swelling
D. Mitochondrial fragmentation
E. Chromatin condensation
**Answer: C**

- Cellular (hydropic) swelling from ATP-dependent sodium pump failure is the earliest, classic sign of reversible injury. A and E are late/irreversible changes.

**Q10.** Necroptosis is characterized by all of the following EXCEPT:
A. RIP1 and RIP3 kinase activation
B. Caspase-independent mechanism
C. Inflammatory response
D. ATP preservation
E. Plasma membrane rupture
**Answer: D**
- Necroptosis, like necrosis, is an energy (ATP)-depleting process with membrane rupture and inflammation — not ATP-preserving. A, B, C, E are all correct hallmarks of necroptosis.

**Q11.** Which type of necrosis is most commonly seen in brain infarction?
A. Coagulative necrosis
B. Liquefactive necrosis
C. Caseous necrosis
D. Fat necrosis
E. Fibrinoid necrosis
**Answer: B**
- The brain's high lipid content and lack of structural protein framework cause it to undergo liquefactive necrosis rather than coagulative (which occurs in most other solid organs).

**Q12.** The intrinsic pathway of apoptosis is primarily triggered by:
A. Death receptor activation
B. Mitochondrial cytochrome c release
C. Caspase-8 activation
D. TNF-α binding
E. Fas ligand interaction
**Answer: B**
- The intrinsic (mitochondrial) pathway is defined by cytochrome c release triggering caspase-9 activation. A, C, D, E all describe the extrinsic (death receptor) pathway instead.

**Q13.** Which complement component is the most potent chemotactic factor for neutrophils?
A. C3a
B. C3b
C. C5a
D. C1q
E. C9
**Answer: C**
- C5a is the primary and most potent neutrophil chemoattractant/anaphylatoxin. C3a (A) has weaker chemotactic activity; C9 (E) is part of the membrane attack complex.

**Q14.** The inflammasome complex primarily produces which cytokine?
A. TNF-α
B. IL-6
C. IL-1β
D. IL-10
E. IFN-γ
**Answer: C**

- The inflammasome activates caspase-1, which cleaves pro-IL-1β into its active form — its defining downstream product.

**Q15.** Which arachidonic acid metabolite is responsible for neutrophil chemotaxis and activation?
A. PGE2
B. PGI2
C. LTB4
D. LTC4
E. TXA2
**Answer: C**
- LTB4 is the key leukotriene mediating neutrophil chemotaxis and activation. LTC4/D4/E4 (D) mediate vascular permeability/bronchoconstriction instead; prostaglandins (A, B) and thromboxane (E) have other roles.

**Q16.** Granulomatous inflammation is characterized by all of the following EXCEPT:
A. Epithelioid cells
B. Langhans giant cells
C. Central necrosis
D. Predominant neutrophil infiltrate
E. Macrophage activation
**Answer: D**
- Granulomas are defined by chronic mononuclear infiltrate (macrophages/epithelioid cells/giant cells), not neutrophils, which characterise acute inflammation instead.

**Q17.** Which mediator is primarily responsible for the pain associated with acute inflammation?
A. Histamine
B. Prostaglandin E2
C. Leukotriene C4
D. Complement C5a
E. IL-1β
**Answer: B**
- Prostaglandin E2 sensitises nociceptors, making it the principal mediator of inflammatory pain. Histamine (A) is more linked to vasodilation/permeability.

**Q18.** The resolution of acute inflammation is promoted by:
A. Increased neutrophil recruitment
B. Enhanced vascular permeability
C. Lipoxin production
D. Prostaglandin synthesis
E. Complement activation
**Answer: C**
- Lipoxins are specialised pro-resolving mediators that actively switch off inflammation and promote resolution. A, B, D, E all promote or sustain active inflammation instead.

**Q19.** Which mechanism is the most common cause of generalized edema?
A. Increased capillary hydrostatic pressure
B. Decreased plasma oncotic pressure
C. Lymphatic obstruction
D. Increased capillary permeability
E. Sodium retention
**Answer: E**

- Sodium (and consequent water) retention via renal mechanisms is the most common underlying driver of generalized/systemic edema (e.g., in heart failure, renal disease). A and B are also contributory but secondary to the sodium/volume expansion.

**Q20.** Pulmonary edema in left heart failure is primarily due to:
A. Increased pulmonary capillary pressure
B. Decreased plasma proteins
C. Lymphatic obstruction
D. Increased capillary permeability
E. Pulmonary inflammation
**Answer: A**
- Left heart failure raises pulmonary venous and capillary hydrostatic pressure, forcing fluid into alveolar spaces — a purely hydrostatic (not permeability-driven) mechanism.

**Q21.** Which type of hemorrhage is characterized by bleeding into the skin measuring 3-5mm in diameter?
A. Petechiae
B. Purpura
C. Ecchymoses
D. Hematoma
E. Hemothorax
**Answer: B**

- Purpura is defined as 3-10mm skin haemorrhages. Petechiae (A) are smaller (<3mm); ecchymoses (C) are larger (>1cm, "bruises").

**Q22.** Chronic passive congestion of the liver results in which characteristic morphological pattern?
A. Bridging necrosis
B. Nutmeg liver appearance
C. Steatosis
D. Cirrhosis
E. Cholestasis
**Answer: B**
- Chronic venous congestion (e.g., right heart failure) produces the classic mottled "nutmeg liver" pattern from centrilobular congestion and peripheral fatty change.

**Q23.** In nephrotic syndrome, edema formation is primarily due to:
A. Increased hydrostatic pressure
B. Decreased plasma albumin
C. Lymphatic obstruction
D. Increased capillary permeability
E. Sodium retention
**Answer: B**
- Massive proteinuria in nephrotic syndrome causes hypoalbuminemia, lowering plasma oncotic pressure and driving fluid into tissues — the primary edema mechanism here.

**Q24.** Which factor is most important in determining whether pulmonary thromboembolism causes infarction?
A. Size of the embolus
B. Presence of dual blood supply
C. Underlying cardiac function
D. Patient's age
E. Anticoagulation status
**Answer: B**
- The lung has dual blood supply (pulmonary + bronchial arteries), so infarction typically only occurs when this collateral circulation is compromised (e.g., in pre-existing cardiac/pulmonary disease), regardless of embolus size alone.

**Q25.** According to Virchow's triad, thrombosis results from all of the following EXCEPT:
A. Endothelial injury
B. Altered blood flow
C. Hypercoagulable state
D. Increased fibrinolytic activity
E. Stasis of blood
**Answer: D**
- Virchow's triad comprises endothelial injury, abnormal blood flow (stasis/turbulence), and hypercoagulability. Increased (not decreased) fibrinolysis would actually oppose thrombosis, not cause it.

**Q26.** Lines of Zahn in arterial thrombi represent:
A. Areas of platelet aggregation only
B. Alternating layers of platelets, fibrin, and RBCs
C. Zones of endothelial damage
D. Calcification deposits
E. Cholesterol crystals
**Answer: B**
- Lines of Zahn are the characteristic laminated appearance of antemortem thrombi, formed by alternating pale platelet/fibrin layers and darker RBC layers.

**Q27.** Fat embolism syndrome most commonly occurs after:
A. Long bone fractures
B. Myocardial infarction
C. Deep vein thrombosis
D. Atrial fibrillation
E. Aortic dissection
**Answer: A**
- Fracture of long bones (e.g., femur) releases marrow fat into the circulation, the classic cause of fat embolism syndrome.

**Q28.** In disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), the paradox of bleeding and clotting occurs because:
A. Platelet dysfunction develops
B. Consumption of clotting factors exceeds production
C. Endothelial damage is widespread
D. Fibrinolytic system is inhibited
E. Anticoagulant proteins are increased
**Answer: B**

- Widespread clotting activation consumes platelets and coagulation factors faster than the body can replace them, paradoxically causing a bleeding tendency alongside microthrombosis.

**Q29.** Septic shock is characterized by all of the following pathophysiologic changes EXCEPT:
A. Systemic vasodilation
B. Increased cardiac output (early phase)
C. Endothelial activation
D. Decreased vascular permeability
E. Disseminated intravascular coagulation
**Answer: D**
- Sepsis causes *increased* (not decreased) vascular permeability due to endothelial injury and inflammatory mediator release, contributing to fluid leakage and hypotension.

**Q30.** Which type of shock has the highest mortality rate?
A. Hypovolemic shock
B. Cardiogenic shock
C. Septic shock
D. Neurogenic shock
E. Anaphylactic shock
**Answer: C**
- Septic shock carries the highest mortality among shock types, largely due to its complex, multifactorial pathophysiology (vasodilation, DIC, multi-organ dysfunction) that is often refractory to standard resuscitation.
