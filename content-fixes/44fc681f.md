## SECTION AQ1. Mention any five functions of proteins in the body. (5 Marks)- **Structural function** — building and maintaining tissues (muscle, bone, skin, hair)
- **Enzymatic function** — acting as catalysts in biochemical reactions
- **Transport function** — carrying substances in blood (e.g., hemoglobin transports oxygen)
- **Defense function** — forming antibodies for immune protection
- **Energy function** — provides 4 kcal/g when carbohydrate intake is insufficient
-
-

Q2. Describe the vicious cycle of illness and malnutrition. (5 Marks)
- Malnutrition weakens the immune system → increases susceptibility to infection
- Illness reduces appetite, increases nutrient losses, impairs absorption
- Each infection episode further depletes nutritional status
- Without intervention, nutritional and health status progressively decline
- Severe, repeated cycles increase risk of mortality
-
-

Q3. Describe the ABCD of Nutrition assessment. (8 Marks)
- **A – Anthropometric:** height, weight, BMI, waist circumference, skinfold thickness; shows nutritional status over time
- **B – Biochemical:** blood/urine/tissue lab tests; detects subclinical deficiencies before physical signs appear
- **C – Clinical:** physical exam of hair, skin, eyes, mouth, organs; identifies advanced deficiency signs
- **D – Dietary:** 24-hour recall, food frequency questionnaires, food records; assesses adequacy of intake
-
-

Q4. Describe three complications a woman may face during pregnancy. (6 Marks)
- **Gestational diabetes mellitus** — high blood sugar in pregnancy; risk of macrosomia, delivery complications, later type 2 diabetes
- **Pre-eclampsia/PIH** — high BP with proteinuria; risk of seizures, organ damage, maternal/fetal death
- **Iron deficiency anemia** — increased iron demand; causes fatigue, weakness, higher infection risk, preterm birth, low birth weight
-
-

Q5. Explain the importance of pre-conception nutrition. (6 Marks)
- Optimizes maternal health before pregnancy begins
- Adequate folate prevents neural tube defects (e.g., spina bifida)
- Improves fertility/conception chances for both partners
- Builds nutrient stores (vitamins, minerals, energy reserves)
- Reduces risk of gestational diabetes and hypertension
- Provides optimal environment for early fetal development
-
-

Q6. Describe how proper nutrition before pregnancy prepares the mother for a smooth pregnancy. (5 Marks)
- Builds reserves of iron, calcium, vitamins needed in pregnancy
- Achieves healthy BMI, reducing complication risk
- Strengthens immune system against infection
- Supports ovulation and implantation
- Adequate B-vitamins may reduce nausea/vomiting severity
-

## SECTION BQ1. Distinguish between essential and non-essential amino acids, with two examples each. (6 Marks)- **Essential:** cannot be synthesized by the body; must come from diet — e.g., Lysine, Methionine
- **Non-essential:** can be synthesized by the body; not required in diet — e.g., Alanine, Glycine
-
-

Q2. Describe five principles for diet planning. (10 Marks)
- **Adequacy** — provides sufficient energy and all essential nutrients for individual needs
- **Balance** — proper proportions of carbs, protein, fat; no nutrient in excess at another's expense
- **Calorie control** — energy intake matches expenditure; maintains healthy weight
- **Moderation** — limits saturated fat, sugar, salt, alcohol
- **Variety** — different foods from all groups; prevents monotony, ensures diverse nutrients
-
-

Q3. Differentiate water-soluble and fat-soluble vitamins, two examples each. (6 Marks)
- **Water-soluble:** dissolve in water, not stored (excess excreted in urine) — e.g., Vitamin C, Vitamin B1
- **Fat-soluble:** dissolve in fat, need bile for absorption, stored in fat/liver (can reach toxic levels) — e.g., Vitamin A, Vitamin D
-
-

Q4. Suggest five factors influencing nutrient requirements of an individual. (5 Marks)
- Age — growth in children, changing needs in elderly
- Sex — women need more iron (menstruation); men need more calories
- Physical activity level — higher energy/nutrient needs for active individuals
- Health status — illness/chronic disease alters requirements
- Pregnancy and lactation — increased needs across most nutrients
-
-

Q5. List the nutritional deficiency disorders for: (3 Marks)
- Vitamin C → **Scurvy**
- Iron → **Iron deficiency anemia**
- Iodine → **Goiter and cretinism**

## SECTION A (30 Marks)Q1. Differentiate between essential and non-essential amino acids. (4 Marks)- **Essential** — cannot be synthesized in adequate amounts; must be obtained from diet; 9 total (histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine)
- **Non-essential** — synthesized by the body from other amino acids; not required in diet; 11 total (e.g., alanine, asparagine, aspartic acid, glutamic acid)

Q2. Explain the importance of dietary fiber. (5 Marks)
- Promotes regular bowel movement, prevents constipation
- Increases satiety, aids weight management
- Slows glucose absorption, controls blood sugar
- Binds bile acids, lowers cholesterol
- Reduces risk of colorectal cancer, heart disease, diabetes
-
-

Q3. Differentiate major minerals and trace minerals. (6 Marks)
- **Major minerals:** required >100mg/day; body content >5g — e.g., calcium, phosphorus, potassium, sodium, chloride, magnesium, sulfur
- **Trace minerals:** required <100mg/day; body content <5g — e.g., iron, zinc, copper, iodine, selenium, chromium, fluoride
-
-

Q4. Suggest five benefits of breastfeeding to the infant. (5 Marks)
- Optimal, perfectly balanced nutrition for growth
- Antibodies provide immune protection against infection
- Easy to digest; lowers GI problem risk
- Associated with better cognitive development/higher IQ
- Lowers risk of allergies, asthma, obesity, SIDS
-
-

Q5. Distinguish soluble and insoluble fiber, with sources. (5 Marks)
- **Soluble:** dissolves, forms gel; lowers cholesterol/blood sugar — sources: oats, beans, apples, citrus, carrots
- **Insoluble:** doesn't dissolve, adds bulk to stool — sources: whole grains, wheat bran, vegetables, nuts
-
-

Q6. Explain the limitations of early introduction of complementary feeds. (5 Marks)
- Increased infection risk — immature immune system
- Digestive immaturity before 6 months
- Reduces breast milk intake, displacing superior nutrition
- Increases risk of food allergies
- Complementary foods often less nutrient-dense than breast milk

## SECTION B (40 Marks)Q1a. Examine five principles of diet planning. (10 Marks)- **Adequacy** — meets energy/nutrient needs by age, sex, activity, health status; prevents deficiencies
- **Balance** — correct macronutrient ratios (45–65% carbs, 10–35% protein, 20–35% fat)
- **Calorie control** — matches intake to expenditure (BMR, activity, thermic effect of food)
- **Moderation** — limits saturated/trans fat, added sugar, sodium; portion control
- **Variety** — foods across and within groups; ensures phytochemical/nutrient diversity, adherence
-
-

Q1b. Explain the ABCD of Nutrition assessment. (10 Marks)
- **A – Anthropometric:** height, weight, BMI, waist circumference, skinfolds, growth charts — shows long-term status/growth pattern
- **B – Biochemical:** blood/urine/tissue analysis, hemoglobin, serum vitamins/minerals, enzyme markers — detects subclinical deficiency
- **C – Clinical:** physical exam (hair, skin, eyes, mouth, nails, organs), medical history — identifies clinical manifestations
- **D – Dietary:** 24-hr recall/food records/FFQ, adequacy vs recommended intake, food security — flags dietary risk areas
-
-

Q2a. Explain the vicious cycle of illness and malnutrition. (10 Marks)
- **Initial malnutrition** — poor intake → deficiencies → weakened immunity, impaired repair
- **Increased disease susceptibility** — poor immune response allows infections (diarrhea, respiratory, parasitic)
- **Disease worsens nutrition** — reduced appetite, nutrient losses (vomiting/diarrhea/fever), impaired absorption, higher metabolic demand
- **Progressive deterioration** — repeated illness deepens depletion, recovery harder, downward spiral
- **Long-term consequences** — stunting, higher mortality, reduced productivity, intergenerational effects
-
-

Q2b. Describe the feeding problems in infancy. (10 Marks)
- **Breastfeeding problems** — insufficient milk/poor let-down, nipple issues, poor latch, maternal illness/medication
- **Formula feeding issues** — preparation errors, contamination, intolerance/allergy, higher infection risk
- **Complementary food introduction** — too early (digestive problems) or too late (deficiencies), poor food choices, allergies
- **Feeding behavior problems** — refusal, poor appetite, irregular patterns, failure to thrive
- **Environmental/social factors** — poverty, poor caregiver knowledge, cultural practices, weak healthcare support
-
