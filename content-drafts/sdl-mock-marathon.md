# SDL Revision Mock Marathon — Upper Limb, Histology & Embryology

*Spot-style revision set, University of Nairobi. Scope: upper limb gross anatomy, histology and embryology. 50 questions.*

## Q1: Name the nerve labelled N and state its root value
![Q1 - dissection of the axilla with a nerve tagged N](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q01.jpg)

**Answer:** Musculocutaneous nerve. Root value: C5, C6, C7 (from the lateral cord of the brachial plexus).

## Q2: State two muscles innervated by the nerve tagged
![Q2 - same axillary dissection, nerve tagged](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q02.jpg)

**Answer:** Coracobrachialis and biceps brachii (it also supplies brachialis — any two).

## Q3: Name the vessel tagged and state its extents
![Q3 - forceps tagging a vessel in the deltopectoral/axillary region](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q03.jpg)

**Answer:** Cephalic vein. It begins in the roof of the anatomical snuffbox at the wrist, runs up the lateral forearm and arm, passes through the deltopectoral groove, then pierces the clavipectoral fascia to drain into the axillary vein.

## Q4: State the root value of the nerve tagged and two muscles it innervates
![Q4 - probe tagging a nerve root at the supraclavicular fossa](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q04.jpg)

**Answer:** Suprascapular nerve; root value C5, C6. Supplies supraspinatus and infraspinatus.

## Q5: State the root value of the nerve tagged and its distribution
![Q5 - probe tagging a nerve root near the brachial plexus roots](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q05.jpg)

**Answer:** Long thoracic nerve; root value C5, C6, C7. Runs down over the outer surface of serratus anterior, which it supplies.

## Q6: State innervation and two actions of the muscle that inserts in the part shown
![Q6 - proximal humerus with an attachment site pinned](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q06.jpg)

**Answer:** Supraspinatus, inserting on the greater tuberosity of the humerus. Innervation: suprascapular nerve (C5, C6). Actions: initiates/assists abduction of the shoulder (first 15°) and stabilises the humeral head in the glenoid (part of the rotator cuff).

## Q7: Name the muscle X and state its innervation
![Q7 - digitated muscle on the lateral chest wall marked X](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q07.jpg)

**Answer:** Serratus anterior. Innervation: long thoracic nerve (C5, C6, C7).

## Q8: Name two contents of the space probed
![Q8 - probe inserted between two converging fan-shaped muscles](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q08.jpg)

**Answer:** The apex/floor of the axilla region shown here transmits the axillary vessels and cords/branches of the brachial plexus — name any two, e.g. axillary artery and the cords of the brachial plexus.

## Q9: Name a muscle that (a) originates and (b) inserts on the part probed, and state their innervation
![Q9 - two fan-shaped muscles converging at a probed point](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q09.jpg)

**Answer:** Pectoralis major (origin: clavicle/sternum/costal cartilages — medial and lateral pectoral nerves, C5–T1) and pectoralis minor (inserts on the coracoid process — medial pectoral nerve, C8, T1).

## Q10: State the origin and innervation of the muscle probed
![Q10 - probe on a fan-shaped chest wall muscle](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q10.jpg)

**Answer:** Pectoralis major — origin: medial half of clavicle, sternum and upper costal cartilages. Innervation: medial and lateral pectoral nerves (C5–T1).

## Q11: Identify structure A and ligament B
![Q11 - labelled dissection, structures A and B](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q11.jpg)

**Answer:** A: a branch/cord of the brachial plexus in the axilla. B: coracoclavicular ligament (trapezoid/conoid parts) — verify letters against the source image before publishing.

## Q12: State 2 actions of E and origin of F
![Q12 - forearm dissection, E and F labelled](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q12.jpg)

**Answer:** E (biceps brachii): flexion of the elbow and supination of the forearm. F (brachialis): origin from the anterior surface of the distal humerus.

## Q13: State 4 sites of entrapment of A
![Q13 - probe tagging a nerve at the medial elbow, labelled A](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q13.jpg)

**Answer:** A = ulnar nerve. Sites of entrapment: (1) arcade of Struthers, (2) the medial intermuscular septum, (3) the cubital tunnel (behind the medial epicondyle, between the two heads of flexor carpi ulnaris), (4) Guyon's canal at the wrist.

## Q14: State the termination of the artery labelled V
![Q14 - artery labelled V in a forearm/cubital fossa dissection](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q14.jpg)

**Answer:** Brachial artery — terminates in the cubital fossa, at the level of the neck of the radius, by bifurcating into the radial and ulnar arteries.

## Q15: State the neurovascular structure most at risk due to a fracture at the tagged part and give the basis
![Q15 - humerus with the surgical neck pinned](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q15.jpg)

**Answer:** Axillary nerve and posterior circumflex humeral vessels. Basis: they wind directly around the surgical neck of the humerus through the quadrangular space, so a fracture here can stretch or lacerate them.

## Q16: Identify structures A and B
![Q16 - shoulder MRI with A and B labelled](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q16.jpg)

**Answer:** A: subscapularis tendon. B: supraspinatus tendon.

## Q17: State the structure tagged and its clinical utility
![Q17 - pin at the cubital fossa](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q17.jpg)

**Answer:** Median cubital vein. Clinical utility: the standard site for venepuncture and IV cannulation.

## Q18: State 2 ligaments associated with the joint probed
![Q18 - probe at the elbow joint](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q18.jpg)

**Answer:** Ulnar (medial) collateral ligament and radial (lateral) collateral ligament of the elbow.

## Q19: Identify structures 3, 4, 5 and 7
![Q19 - wrist X-ray with carpal bones numbered](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q19.jpg)

**Answer:** 3: triquetrum. 4: trapezium. 5: trapezoid. 7: hamate.

## Q20: Name the structures A, B, C and D
![Q20 - forearm dissection with A, B, C, D labelled](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q20.jpg)

**Answer:** A: median nerve. B: a branch of the musculocutaneous nerve (lateral cutaneous nerve of forearm) or brachial vessels at the elbow. C and D: cutaneous branches/vessels of the forearm — verify letters against the source image before publishing.

## Q21: Name the terminal branches of the structure below
![Q21 - palmar dissection with a probe](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q21.jpg)

**Answer:** Superficial palmar arch (formed mainly by the ulnar artery) — terminal contributions: three common palmar digital arteries and a proper palmar digital artery to the little finger.

## Q22: Identify the muscle tagged and its action
![Q22 - probe on a fan-shaped chest wall muscle](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q22.jpg)

**Answer:** Serratus anterior. Action: protracts the scapula and rotates it upward (essential for full abduction/flexion of the arm above the horizontal).

## Q23: State the communications of the space labelled X
![Q23 - diagram of the palm with a fascial space labelled X](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q23.jpg)

**Answer:** Midpalmar space. Communicates proximally with the space of Parona in the forearm (via the carpal tunnel), and distally with the web spaces/dorsum of the fingers via the lumbrical canals.

## Q24: Identify the nerve below and its nerve roots
![Q24 - forearm dissection, central nerve](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q24.jpg)

**Answer:** Median nerve; roots C6–T1 (contributions from the lateral and medial cords of the brachial plexus, ultimately C5/6–T1).

## Q25: State two muscles that form the floor of the space below
![Q25 - labelled cubital fossa prosection](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q25.jpg)

**Answer:** Cubital fossa — floor formed by brachialis and supinator.

## Q26: Name the branches of the structure labelled X
![Q26 - probe tagging a nerve/vessel in the proximal forearm](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q26.jpg)

**Answer:** Radial nerve, dividing into a superficial (sensory) branch and a deep branch (posterior interosseous nerve) near the elbow, at the level of supinator.

## Q27: Identify the tagged bone and a muscular attachment to it
![Q27 - wrist skeleton with a carpal bone tagged X](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q27.jpg)

**Answer:** Pisiform. Muscular attachment: flexor carpi ulnaris inserts on it (abductor digiti minimi also originates from it).

## Q28: State two actions of the tagged muscle
![Q28 - probe on a wrist tendon](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q28.jpg)

**Answer:** Flexor carpi radialis — flexion of the wrist and abduction (radial deviation) of the wrist.

## Q29: Identify the muscle below and its innervation
![Q29 - thenar eminence dissection](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q29.jpg)

**Answer:** Abductor pollicis brevis. Innervation: recurrent (motor) branch of the median nerve.

## Q30: State (a) the site of entrapment of the nerve below at the wrist, (b) its nerve roots
![Q30 - nerve at the wrist crease](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q30.jpg)

**Answer:** Median nerve. (a) Carpal tunnel, deep to the flexor retinaculum. (b) Roots C6–T1.

## Q31: State two characteristic features of the tissue displayed
![Q31 - skeletal muscle histology](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q31.jpg)

**Answer:** Skeletal muscle — visible cross-striations, and multiple peripherally-located (subsarcolemmal) nuclei per fibre.

## Q32: Giving reason, identify the epithelial type displayed
![Q32 - ciliated epithelium over cartilage](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q32.jpg)

**Answer:** Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium (respiratory epithelium of the trachea). Reason: all cells rest on the basement membrane, but not all reach the free surface, so nuclei appear at different levels giving a falsely stratified look; cilia are visible on the luminal surface.

## Q33: Identify A and B
![Q33 - skeletal muscle fibres with A and B labelled](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q33.jpg)

**Answer:** A: peripheral nucleus of a skeletal muscle fibre. B: myofibrils showing cross-striation.

## Q34: Identify zones labelled A and D
![Q34 - epidermis histology with zones a and d](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q34.jpg)

**Answer:** A: stratum granulosum. D: stratum basale (germinativum).

## Q35: State two sites where the tissue displayed is found
![Q35 - mucoid connective tissue around a small vessel](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q35.jpg)

**Answer:** Mucous (mucoid) connective tissue — found in Wharton's jelly of the umbilical cord and in the dental pulp of a developing tooth.

## Q36: Name 1, 2, 3 and 4
![Q36 - electron micrograph of a sarcomere](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q36.jpg)

**Answer:** 1: Z-line (Z-disc). 2: A-band. 3: I-band. 4: a sarcomere (Z-line to Z-line).

## Q37: Give the salient features of the tissue displayed
![Q37 - cartilage-type histology](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q37.jpg)

**Answer:** Hyaline cartilage — chondrocytes sit in lacunae (often as isogenous groups), the matrix is basophilic/metachromatic (rich in chondroitin sulfate), the tissue is avascular, and it is bounded by a perichondrium (absent at articular surfaces).

## Q39: State major collagen types in the tissue displayed
![Q39 - dense regular connective tissue histology](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q39.jpg)

**Answer:** Tendon (dense regular connective tissue) — predominantly type I collagen, with a minor contribution of type III collagen in the surrounding endotenon.

## Q40: Name cell types found in region a
![Q40 - testis histology with region a marked](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q40.jpg)

**Answer:** Seminiferous tubule: spermatogenic cells at various stages (spermatogonia, primary/secondary spermatocytes, spermatids) and Sertoli (sustentacular) cells.

## Q41: State the anomaly depicted and give its basis
![Q41 - grape-like translucent vesicles](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q41.jpg)

**Answer:** Hydatidiform mole (molar pregnancy). Basis: hydropic (cystic) degeneration of chorionic villi from an abnormal conceptus — a complete mole (androgenetic, diploid, empty ovum fertilised by a duplicating sperm) or partial mole (triploid, from dispermy).

## Q42: State the anomaly depicted and give its basis
![Q42 - conjoined infants](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q42.jpg)

**Answer:** Conjoined twins. Basis: incomplete splitting of the embryonic disc during monozygotic twinning after day 13, leaving the twins joined at a shared body region.

## Q43: Identify X and state a structure related to its formation
![Q43 - sagittal MRI of the lower spine showing a sac-like mass](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q43.jpg)

**Answer:** X: a spina bifida cystica sac (meningocele/myelomeningocele). Related structure: failure of closure of the neural tube (posterior neuropore) during the fourth week.

## Q44: State the anomalies labelled A and B
![Q44 - diagram comparing two spina bifida variants](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q44.jpg)

**Answer:** A: meningocele (herniation of meninges and CSF only, no neural tissue in the sac). B: myelomeningocele (meninges and neural tissue/spinal cord herniate into the sac).

## Q45: Identify A and B
![Q45 - two placental specimens](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q45.jpg)

**Answer:** A: fetal (chorionic) surface of the placenta, showing umbilical vessels branching beneath the amnion. B: the umbilical cord and its vessels dissected off the placental disc, with a marginal (battledore) insertion.

## Q46: State the procedures labeled A and B
![Q46 - diagrams of two prenatal sampling procedures](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q46.jpg)

**Answer:** A: transabdominal chorionic villus sampling (ultrasound-guided needle through the maternal abdomen into the chorionic villi). B: transcervical chorionic villus sampling (catheter passed through the cervix under ultrasound guidance).

## Q47: Identify placental attachment depicted in A & B
![Q47 - diagram of umbilical cord insertion sites on the placenta](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q47.jpg)

**Answer:** Variations in umbilical cord insertion on the placenta — A: eccentric (paracentral) insertion. B: marginal (battledore) insertion, at the edge of the placental disc.

## Q48: State 2 functions of B
![Q48 - trilaminar embryonic disc diagram, primitive node, A and B labelled](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q48.jpg)

**Answer:** B: (intraembryonic) mesoderm. Functions: gives rise to the musculoskeletal, cardiovascular and urogenital systems; forms the notochord (from the primitive node), which induces overlying ectoderm to form the neural tube.

## Q49: Identify the anomaly and state its basis
![Q49 - newborn with a large umbilical mass](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q49.jpg)

**Answer:** Omphalocele (exomphalos). Basis: failure of the herniated midgut to return to the abdominal cavity by the 10th week (or failure of the lateral body-wall folds to fuse at the umbilical ring), leaving abdominal viscera herniated into the cord base, covered by a peritoneal/amniotic sac.

## Q50: Identify the anomaly and state its basis
![Q50 - H-shaped diagram of the gut tube and a diverticulum](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/sdl-mock-marathon/q50.jpg)

**Answer:** Meckel's diverticulum. Basis: incomplete obliteration of the vitelline (omphalomesenteric) duct, which normally connects the midgut to the yolk sac and regresses by week 7.
