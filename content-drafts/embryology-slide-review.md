# Embryology Slide Review Marathon

*Numbered embryology identification set, sent 7 June 2017. Answers are the original compiler's answer key, matched to each numbered image by letter.*

## Number 1: Spermatogenesis — identify A–G
![Number 1 - diagram of spermatogenesis stages A to G](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n01.jpg)

**Answer:** A: spermatocytogenesis/mitosis. B: primary spermatocyte. C: first meiotic division. D: secondary spermatocyte. E: second meiotic division. F: spermatids. G: spermiogenesis.

## Number 2: Mature Graafian follicle — identify A–G
![Number 2 - histology of a mature Graafian follicle A to G](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n02.jpg)

**Answer:** A: corona radiata. B: zona pellucida. C: oocyte. D: follicular antrum. E: granulosa cells. F: theca. G: cumulus oophorus.

## Number 3: Identify A–C
![Number 3 - fertilised oocyte and blastocyst diagrams A to C](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n03.jpg)

**Answer:** Left image — A: zona pellucida. B: oocyte. C: polar body. Right image — A: blastocoel. B: trophoblast. C: embryoblast.

## Number 4: Implantation — identify A–H
![Number 4 - implanting blastocyst diagram A to H](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n04.jpg)

**Answer:** A: trophoblastic lacunae. B: syncytiotrophoblast. C: cytotrophoblast. D: amniotic cavity. E: epiblast. F: hypoblast. G: exocoelomic (Heuser's) membrane. H: umbilical vesicle.

## Number 5: Trilaminar disc (epiblast) — identify A–D
![Number 5 - trilaminar embryonic disc diagram A to D](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n05.jpg)

**Answer:** A: notochord. B: primitive pit. C: primitive node. D: primitive groove.

## Number 6: State the anomaly and its basis
![Number 6 - newborn with a sacrococcygeal mass](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n06.jpg)

**Answer:** Sacrococcygeal teratoma — failure of degeneration/regression of the primitive streak. (The compiler's own key leaves the follow-up "what cell types does it have" unanswered — a teratoma of this type typically contains tissue from all three germ layers.)

## Number 7: Identify A–L
![Number 7 - embryonic folding diagram A to L](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n07.jpg)

**Answer:** A: exocoelomic (Heuser's) membrane. B: intraembryonic coelom. C: intermediate mesoderm. D: neural tube. E: paraxial mesoderm. F: notochord. G: somatic (parietal) lateral mesoderm. H: splanchnic (visceral) lateral mesoderm. I: surface ectoderm. J: primordial gut. K: secondary umbilical vesicle. L: amniotic cavity.

## Number 8: Identify A–D
![Number 8 - neural tube formation diagram A to D](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n08.jpg)

**Answer:** A: surface ectoderm. B: neural folds. C: neural crest. D: notochord.

## Number 9: Identify labelled structures
![Number 9 - myotome diagram, epimere and hypomere](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n09.jpg)

**Answer:** Left diagram — A: epimere. B: hypomere. C: dorsal rami of spinal nerve. Right diagram — A: epimere. D: ventral rami of spinal nerve.

## Number 10: Name the anomalies (left to right)
![Number 10 - three anterior abdominal wall anomaly photos](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n10.jpg)

**Answer:** Prune belly syndrome — failure of migration and fusion of the anterior abdominal wall muscles. Poland syndrome — absence of pectoralis major. Congenital arthrogryposis — joint rigidity due to absent/underdeveloped muscles.

## Number 11: Chorionic villi and embryonic folding — identify labelled structures
![Number 11 - chorionic villi diagram and craniocaudal folding diagram](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n11.jpg)

**Answer:** Left diagram — A: secondary chorionic villi. B: trophoblastic lacunae. C: connecting stalk. D: amniotic cavity. E: umbilical vesicle. F: exocoelomic cavity. G: splanchnic lateral mesoderm. H: cytotrophoblast. I: syncytiotrophoblast.

![Number 11 (continued) - result of craniocaudal and lateral folding](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n11b.jpg)

**Answer (continued):** Right diagram (results of craniocaudal and lateral folding) — A: septum transversum. B: amnion. C: allantois. D: exocoelomic membrane.

## Number 13: Fetal membranes — identify labelled structures
![Number 13 - fetal membranes and decidua diagram](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n13.jpg)

**Answer:** Left diagram — A: smooth chorion. B: decidua capsularis. C: chorionic cavity. D: decidua parietalis. E: decidua basalis. F: chorion frondosum. G: amniotic cavity. H: secondary umbilical vesicle. Right diagram — I: amniochorionic membrane. J: amniotic cavity.

## Number 14: Identify the placental specimens
![Number 14 - several placenta specimens, normal and abnormal](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n14.jpg)

**Answer:** First row: bilobed (membranacea) placenta, battledore placenta. Second row: placenta previa, placenta accreta, placenta increta, placenta percreta.

## Number 16: The placenta
![Number 16 - placenta specimen with membranes doubled back](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n16.jpg)

**Answer:** Circumvallate placenta — the fetal membranes double back on themselves at the placental margin.

## Number 18: Identify the conjoined twin types
![Number 18 - four sets of conjoined twins](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n18.jpg)

**Answer:** Craniopagus, thoraco-omphalo-pygopagus, pygopagus, thoracopagus (as shown, left to right).

## Number 19: Skin development — identify labelled layers
![Number 19 - developing epidermis diagram](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n19.jpg)

**Answer:** A: surface ectoderm. B: mesenchyme. C: periderm. D: basal layer. E: mesenchyme.

## Number 20: Name the skin anomalies (left to right)
![Number 20 - seven skin condition photos](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n20.jpg)

**Answer:** Port-wine stain, cavernous haemangioma, hypertrichosis, ichthyosis vulgaris, albinism, alopecia, anonychia (absence of nails).

## Number 21: Mammary ridge and breast development
![Number 21 - milk line diagram and mammary bud development](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n21.jpg)

**Answer:** A: milk line / mammary ridge (mammary crest). Development sequence — A: surface ectoderm, B: mesenchyme, C: primary mammary bud, D: mammary pit, E: lactiferous duct.

## Number 22: Name the breast anomalies (left to right)
![Number 22 - six breast anomaly photos](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n22.jpg)

**Answer:** Polymastia, polythelia, inverted nipples, gynaecomastia, Poland syndrome (and amastia).

## Number 23: Limb bud development — identify labelled structures
![Number 23 - limb bud and pharyngeal arch diagram](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n23.jpg)

**Answer:** Left diagram — A: pharyngeal arches. B: developing heart. C: developing lower limb bud. D: somites. Right diagram — A: mesenchyme. B: zone of proliferation. C: apical ectodermal ridge (patterns limb bud growth in a proximo-distal axis). D: zone of polarizing activity (patterns limb bud growth in an antero-posterior axis).

## Number 24: Name the limb anomalies
![Number 24 - limb anomaly photos and X-rays](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n24.jpg)

**Answer:** Amelia, congenital absence of the radius, phocomelia, lobster-claw hand, limb amputation (constriction band), pre-axial polydactyly, club foot.

## Number 25: Name the skeletal anomalies (left to right)
![Number 25 - child with short stature, hip X-ray, hand anomaly photos](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n25.jpg)

**Answer:** Achondroplasia, syndactyly, congenital dislocation of the hip joint, polydactyly, macrodactyly.

## Number 26: Neural tube and brain vesicles — identify labelled structures
![Number 26 - neuropore diagram and forebrain-midbrain-hindbrain diagram](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n26.jpg)

**Answer:** Left diagram — A: cranial neuropore. B: somites. C: caudal neuropore. Right diagram — A: telencephalon. B: diencephalon. C: optic vesicles. D: mesencephalon. E: metencephalon. F: myelencephalon.

## Number 26b: Developing urogenital system — identify labelled structures
![Number 26 continued - developing kidney, ureter and bladder diagram](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n26b.jpg)

**Answer:** This diagram (developing metanephros, ureteric bud, and bladder) was not separately keyed in the original source — recommend cross-checking against a standard embryology text before publishing (labels broadly correspond to: A – developing kidney, B – renal pelvis, C – ureteric orifices, D/E – developing bladder and urogenital sinus).

## Number 27: Name the neural tube/cranial anomalies (left to right)
![Number 27 - eight photos of neural tube and cranial anomalies](/images/year1/anatomy/aponeurosis-01/embryology-slide-review/n27.jpg)

**Answer:** Raschisis, acrania coupled with anencephaly, spina bifida occulta, spina bifida cystica (meningocele), spina bifida cystica (myelomeningocele), hydrocephalus, microcrania, crania bifida.
