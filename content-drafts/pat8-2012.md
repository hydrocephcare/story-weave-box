# PAT 8 — Slideshow (2 March 2012)

*Progress Assessment Test, Dept. of Human Anatomy, University of Nairobi. Questions 12–40 (this compilation does not include 1–11), covering upper limb, spine, general histology and embryology.*

## Q12: (a) Identify A and B. (b) State the innervation of B
![Q12 - back dissection with A and B labelled](__IMG:pat8-2012/q12.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: latissimus dorsi
- B: trapezius
- B motor innervation: (spinal) accessory nerve
- B sensory innervation: cervical plexus

## Q13: (a) Give the origin of the muscle F. (b) State its innervation and actions
![Q13 - back dissection with muscle F labelled](__IMG:pat8-2012/q13.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- F: trapezius
- Origin: external occipital protuberance
- Origin: superior nuchal line
- Origin: ligamentum nuchae
- Origin: spinous processes of C7–T12
- Innervation: (spinal) accessory nerve
- Actions: elevates, retracts, and rotates the scapula (different fibres for each action)

## Q14: (a) Identify A, B and C. (b) State the pain referral pattern of B
![Q14 - axilla dissection with A, B, C labelled](__IMG:pat8-2012/q14.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: medial pectoral nerve
- B: intercostobrachial nerve
- C: intercostobrachial nerve (further branch/course)
- B pain referral: floor of the axilla
- Clinical correlate: angina pectoris — cardiac pain can also be felt in this distribution

## Q15: (a) State the clinical effect and basis of the nerve injury shown. (b) Name two other sources forming the arterial anastomosis around A
![Q15 - humerus specimen with A and B labelled](__IMG:pat8-2012/q15.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Clinical effect: claw hand
- Basis: ulnar nerve injury
- Long head of biceps brachii contributes to the anastomosis
- Latissimus dorsi is also related to this region
- Ascending (anastomotic/articular) branch of profunda brachii artery — any two of these accepted

## Q16: (a) Identify A and B. (b) Name the muscle that also flexes the elbow, apart from those shown
![Q16 - elbow region dissection with A, B, C labelled](__IMG:pat8-2012/q16.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: radial nerve
- B: brachialis muscle
- Brachioradialis also flexes the elbow
- Brachioradialis also brings the forearm to the mid-prone position

## Q17: (a) Identify the structure circled and its two components. (b) Describe its blood supply pattern and a related clinical point
![Q17 - hand dissection with the fingertip circled](__IMG:pat8-2012/q17.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Extensor expansion (extensor hood) of the finger
- Components: extensor digitorum tendon
- Components: lumbrical tendon
- Blood supply pattern: distoproximal, from the pulp rete
- Clinical point: avascular necrosis risk in pulp infections (felon)

## Q18: (a) Identify the vertebra shown and its region. (b) State the level of termination of the spinal cord and one related clinical feature
![Q18 - isolated vertebra specimen](__IMG:pat8-2012/q18.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Cervical vertebra
- Distinguishing features: foramen transversarium, bifid spinous process
- Spinal cord terminates at L1/L2 (conus medullaris)
- Related feature: presence of the lumbar cistern (subarachnoid space below the cord, used for lumbar puncture)

## Q19: (a) Identify A and B. (b) Name the two nerves supplying the region shown
![Q19 - femoral triangle dissection labelled "FEMORAL TRIANGLE"](__IMG:pat8-2012/q19.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: femoral nerve
- B: tensor fasciae latae
- C (also visible): sartorius
- Region supplied by: femoral nerve
- Region supplied by: obturator nerve

## Q20: (a) Identify the fracture, its basis and a complication. (b) State the root values of the superficial peroneal and pudendal nerves
![Q20 - knee X-ray, two views](__IMG:pat8-2012/q20.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Supracondylar femoral fracture
- Basis: pull of gastrocnemius on the distal fragment
- Complication: injury to the popliteal artery
- Superficial peroneal nerve root value: L4, L5, S1
- Pudendal nerve root value: S2, S3, S4

## Q21: (a) Identify tendons A and B. (b) Name the bony landmark at this site and two ligaments attaching there
![Q21 - ankle dissection with A, B, C labelled](__IMG:pat8-2012/q21.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: peroneus tertius
- B: extensor hallucis longus
- Landmark: lateral malleolus
- Ligaments: anterior talofibular ligament
- Ligaments: posterior talofibular ligament
- Ligaments: calcaneofibular ligament

## Q22: (a) Identify the papilla shown and give two identifying features. (b) Name two structures found in the roof of the nasal cavity
![Q22 - tongue papilla histology](__IMG:pat8-2012/q22.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Circumvallate papilla
- Features: pericircumvallate groove, taste buds, characteristic shape (any of these)
- Roof of the nasal cavity structures: upper part of the nasal septum
- Roof of the nasal cavity structures: superior concha (any two)

## Q23: (a) Identify the tissue and its features. (b) Name three cell types by functional class
![Q23 - retina histology with a layer labelled Y](__IMG:pat8-2012/q23.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Retina
- Features: many distinct layers, lies adjacent to the choroid
- Association neurons: horizontal and amacrine cells
- 1st order neuron: bipolar neurons
- Supporting cell: Müller cell (any two of these three groups)

## Q24: (a) Identify the region shown and a feature. (b) Name two other cell types found in the epidermis
![Q24 - growth plate histology](__IMG:pat8-2012/q24.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Epiphyseal (growth) plate
- Feature: palisades/stacks of cells, multiple zones, lies adjacent to bone (any one)
- Merkel cells
- Langerhans cells

## Q25: (a) Identify the structure tagged F. (b) State two cell types visible in this field
![Q25 - dorsal root ganglion histology with F labelled](__IMG:pat8-2012/q25.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Dorsal root (sensory) ganglion
- Identifying features: "open-faced" appearance, vesicular nuclei, cell bodies of sensory neurons, satellite (neurosatellite) cells, nerve fibres — any of these as reasoning
- Cell types: neurosatellite cells and sensory neurons (1st order neurons)

## Q26: (a) Identify the structure and give two features. (b) Name the two nerve ending patterns seen here
![Q26 - muscle spindle histology](__IMG:pat8-2012/q26.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Neuromuscular spindle (muscle spindle)
- Features: contains both intrafusal and extrafusal fibres
- Features: encapsulated
- Nerve ending patterns: annulospiral and flower-spray endings

## Q27: (a) Identify the cells and their location. (b) Name three supporting cell types in the organ of Corti
![Q27 - cerebral cortex neuron histology](__IMG:pat8-2012/q27.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Pyramidal neurons, in the cerebral cortex
- Organ of Corti supporting cells: pillar cells
- Organ of Corti supporting cells: sustentacular (Deiters') cells
- Organ of Corti supporting cells: phalangeal cells

## Q28: (a) Identify the tissue and two distinguishing features. (b) State three physiological properties of this tissue
![Q28 - cardiac muscle histology](__IMG:pat8-2012/q28.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Cardiac muscle
- Distinguishing features: intercalated discs, branching fibres
- Properties: contractility
- Properties: excitability
- Properties: conductivity

## Q29: (a) Identify the gland type and its two secretory classes. (b) State the two modes of bone growth
![Q29 - sweat gland histology](__IMG:pat8-2012/q29.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Sweat glands
- Secretory classes: apocrine and eccrine (merocrine)
- Bone growth modes: interstitial growth
- Bone growth modes: appositional growth

## Q30: (a) Identify the epithelium and three features. (b) Name a site with elastic laminae and a site with reticular fibres
![Q30 - olfactory epithelium histology](__IMG:pat8-2012/q30.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Olfactory epithelium (a pseudostratified epithelium)
- Features: multiple nuclear layers, Bowman's glands, cilia present, no goblet cells
- Interlaminar elastic fibres: found in large arteries (e.g. the aorta)
- Reticular fibres: found in the liver (supporting framework)

## Q31: (a) Identify the tissue and its features. (b) Name the cells lining bone surfaces
![Q31 - lactating mammary gland histology](__IMG:pat8-2012/q31.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Lactating mammary gland
- Features: numerous alveoli, ducts, lobules, high vascularity, minimal stroma
- Bone-lining cells: bone-lining cells (flattened osteoblast-lineage cells covering quiescent bone surfaces)

## Q32: (a) Identify the conditions shown in A and B. (b) Name two other cell types found in the epidermis apart from keratinocytes
![Q32 - two skin condition photos, A and B](__IMG:pat8-2012/q32.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: hypertrichosis (excessive hair growth)
- B: ichthyosis (dry, scaling skin)
- Other epidermal cells: Merkel cells
- Other epidermal cells: melanocytes
- Other epidermal cells: Langerhans cells

## Q33: (a) Name two notochord derivatives shown. (b) State the myotome subdivision and its nerve supply
![Q33 - vertebral cross-section diagram with A and B labelled](__IMG:pat8-2012/q33.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Notochord derivatives: nucleus pulposus of the intervertebral disc
- Notochord derivatives: alar (apical) ligament of the dens
- Myotome division: epimere
- Nerve supply: dorsal ramus (of the spinal nerve)

## Q34: (a) Identify the two anomalies shown. (b) State the preventive measure and its timing
![Q34 - newborn with a spinal mass](__IMG:pat8-2012/q34.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Meningocele and meningomyelocele (spina bifida cystica spectrum)
- Prevention: folic acid (folate) supplementation
- Timing: started before conception

## Q35: (a) Name the neural tube region tagged A and an adjacent region. (b) Name two anomalies of forebrain development
![Q35 - cross-section of the neural tube, region A tagged](__IMG:pat8-2012/q35.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: basal plate — gives rise to the anterior horn (motor neurons)
- Adjacent region: lateral horn
- Anencephaly
- Microcephaly
- Prosencephaly (abnormality)
- Holoprosencephaly (any two)

## Q36: (a) Identify the anomalies in A and B. (b) Name two structures that pattern limb bud growth, apart from the AER
![Q36 - clubfoot (A) and phocomelia (B) photos](__IMG:pat8-2012/q36.jpg__)

- B: phocomelia
- Limb patterning structures: apical ectodermal ridge (AER)
- Limb patterning structures: zone of polarizing activity (polarizing zone)
- Limb patterning structures: dorsal ectoderm (any two of these three)

## Q39: (a) Identify the twin type (A) and condition (B). (b) Name two features of the condition in B
![Q39 - conjoined twins (A) and a child with a parasitic twin (B)](__IMG:pat8-2012/q39.jpg__)

- Features: flat nasal ridge (any two)

## Q40: (a) Identify structure A and its adult derivative. (b) Name conditions affecting the knee that produce an abnormal angle, apart from genu valgum
![Q40 - diagram of Rathke's pouch formation](__IMG:pat8-2012/q40.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: Rathke's pouch
- Adult derivative: (anterior) pituitary gland
- Genu varum (bow-leg)
- Osteogenesis imperfecta (can predispose to deformity)
- Achondroplasia
- Genu recurvatum (hyperextended knee)
