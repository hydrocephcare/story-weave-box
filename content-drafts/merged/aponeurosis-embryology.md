![Gross photo of grape-like vesicles from a molar pregnancy](https://cdn.ompathstudy.com/uploads/2026/08/y1-anatomy-anatomy-spot-revision-q32-cdd8c07f.jpg)

## Q3: Identify the hormones plotted X, Y, Z, W (menstrual cycle correlation)

![Graph of four hormone curves across a 28-day cycle, labelled X, Y, Z, W, beside a reference diagram of the ovarian and uterine cycles](https://cdn.ompathstudy.com/uploads/2026/08/y1-anatomy-anatomy-spot-revision-q33-ec345aa7.jpg)

**Answer:**
- X (sharp, tallest peak exactly at day 14): luteinizing hormone (LH) — the LH surge that triggers ovulation
- W (smaller peak coinciding with X, at day 14): follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)
- Z (moderate peak just before day 14): oestradiol — oestrogen peaks shortly before the LH surge
- Y (broad peak around day 21, mid-luteal phase): progesterone — secreted by the corpus luteum

## Q19: State the anomaly depicted and give its basis

## Q90: Q21 (bonus essay): Outline the spinal cord — external features, support structures, arterial supply, venous drainage, extents, internal features at T2, development and congenital anomalies

**Answer:**
- **Support structures:**
- Meningeal coverings (dura, arachnoid, pia mater)
- Cornu medullaris (conus medullaris)
- Cauda equina
- Cervical and lumbar enlargements
- Cervical and lumbosacral enlargements (correspond to limb innervation)
- Filum terminale
- **Extents:**
- Continuous with the brainstem superiorly
- Continues as spinal nerves
- Anchored by denticulate ligaments
- **Arterial blood supply:**
- Anterior spinal artery (1, from the vertebral arteries) — supplies the anterior two-thirds of the spinal cord
- Posterior spinal arteries (2, from the vertebral arteries) — supply the posterior third of the spinal cord
- Radicular arteries, derived from segmental vessels:
- Cervical region — ascending cervical artery (from inferior thyroid artery); deep cervical artery (from costocervical trunk)
- Thoracic region — posterior intercostal arteries
- Lumbar region — lumbar arteries
- Sacral region — lateral sacral arteries (from internal pudendal artery)
- The largest radicular artery is the Artery of Adamkiewicz (arteria radicularis magna), found in the lumbar region
