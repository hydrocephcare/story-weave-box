# SDL Revision Mock Marathon — Upper Limb, Histology & Embryology

*Spot-style revision set, University of Nairobi. Scope: upper limb gross anatomy, histology and embryology. 50 questions.*

## Q1: Name the nerve labelled N and state its root value
![Q1 - dissection of the axilla with a nerve tagged N](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q01.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Musculocutaneous nerve
- Root value: C5, C6, C7 (lateral cord of the brachial plexus)

## Q2: State two muscles innervated by the nerve tagged
![Q2 - same axillary dissection, nerve tagged](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q02.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Coracobrachialis
- Biceps brachii
- (also innervates brachialis — any two)

## Q3: Name the vessel tagged and state its extents
![Q3 - forceps tagging a vessel in the deltopectoral/axillary region](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q03.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Cephalic vein
- Begins at the roof of the anatomical snuffbox (wrist)
- Runs up the lateral forearm and arm, through the deltopectoral groove
- Pierces the clavipectoral fascia to drain into the axillary vein

## Q4: State the root value of the nerve tagged and two muscles it innervates
![Q4 - probe tagging a nerve root at the supraclavicular fossa](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q04.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Suprascapular nerve
- Root value: C5, C6
- Supplies supraspinatus and infraspinatus

## Q5: State the root value of the nerve tagged and its distribution
![Q5 - probe tagging a nerve root near the brachial plexus roots](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q05.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Long thoracic nerve
- Root value: C5, C6, C7
- Distribution: runs over the outer surface of serratus anterior, which it supplies

## Q6: State innervation and two actions of the muscle that inserts in the part shown
![Q6 - proximal humerus with an attachment site pinned](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q06.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Muscle: supraspinatus (inserts on the greater tuberosity)
- Innervation: suprascapular nerve (C5, C6)
- Action: initiates/assists shoulder abduction (first 15°)
- Action: stabilises the humeral head in the glenoid (rotator cuff)

## Q32: Giving reason, identify the epithelial type displayed
![Q32 - ciliated epithelium over cartilage](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q32.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium (respiratory epithelium of the trachea)
- Reason: all cells rest on the basement membrane, but not all reach the free surface
- Nuclei at different levels give a falsely stratified look
- Cilia visible on the luminal surface

## Q41: State the anomaly depicted and give its basis
![Q41 - grape-like translucent vesicles](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q41.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Hydatidiform mole (molar pregnancy)
- Basis: hydropic (cystic) degeneration of chorionic villi
- Complete mole: androgenetic, diploid, empty ovum fertilised by a duplicating sperm
- Partial mole: triploid, from dispermy

## Q42: State the anomaly depicted and give its basis

**Answer:**
- Conjoined twins
- Basis: incomplete splitting of the embryonic disc during monozygotic twinning, after day 13

## Q43: Identify X and state a structure related to its formation

## Q48: State 2 functions of B
![Q48 - trilaminar embryonic disc diagram, primitive node, A and B labelled](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q48.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- B: (intraembryonic) mesoderm
- Gives rise to the musculoskeletal, cardiovascular and urogenital systems
- Forms the notochord (from the primitive node), which induces the neural tube

## Q49: Identify the anomaly and state its basis
![Q49 - newborn with a large umbilical mass](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q49.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Omphalocele (exomphalos)
- Basis: failure of the herniated midgut to return to the abdominal cavity by week 10 (or failure of the lateral body-wall folds to fuse at the umbilical ring)
- Abdominal viscera herniate into the cord base, covered by a peritoneal/amniotic sac

## Q50: Identify the anomaly and state its basis
![Q50 - H-shaped diagram of the gut tube and a diverticulum](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q50.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Meckel's diverticulum
- Basis: incomplete obliteration of the vitelline (omphalomesenteric) duct, which normally regresses by week 7
