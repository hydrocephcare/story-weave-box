# Nervous & Sensory Tissue

## Q1: Identify cell types A-C, give two reasons for each, and state where each is located

![Pyramidal cell, Purkinje cell, and astrocytes](__IMG:q01.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: pyramidal neuron - pyramidal cell body and an apical dendrite; located in the cerebral cortex
- B: Purkinje neuron - flask-shaped cell body and extensive dendritic arborisation; located in the cerebellar cortex
- C: astrocytes - star-shaped cells with numerous branching processes; located throughout the central nervous system

---

## Q10: Identify the spinal-cord level, tracts 26 and 27, one structure traversing region 13, and organisms infecting regions 11 and 28

![Thoracic spinal cord cross-section with numbered regions](__IMG:q10.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Thoracic spinal cord - lateral horn is present and the anterior horn is relatively small
- 26: dorsal spinocerebellar tract
- 27: fasciculus cuneatus
- Anterior spinal artery traverses the anterior median fissure (region 13)
- Region 11: poliovirus
- Region 28: *Treponema pallidum* (tabes dorsalis)

---

## Q16: Identify the structure, name three cell types, and name two components of the membranous labyrinth

![Organ of Corti in the cochlea](__IMG:q16.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Organ of Corti - identified by the bony labyrinth, scalae, membranes, hair cells, spiral ganglion, and stria vascularis
- Cell types include outer and inner hair cells, outer and inner pillar cells, outer and inner phalangeal (Deiters') cells, and cells of Hensen and Claudius
- Membranous labyrinth: utricle and semicircular ducts; saccule and cochlear duct are also components

---

## Q22: Identify the organ, name the layers of its outer zone, and name two fibre types entering it

![Cerebellar folium](__IMG:q22.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Cerebellum - folia, a cortical mantle, and central medulla are visible
- Cerebellar cortex layers: molecular, Purkinje-cell, and granular layers
- Afferent fibres: mossy fibres and climbing fibres

---

## Q25: Identify the structure, categorize the cells in layer X, and name two other eye components

![Retina with layer X marked](__IMG:q25.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Retina - recognized by its many neuronal layers and adjacent choroid
- Cell categories include association neurons, supporting cells, and first-order neurons
- Other eye components: iris and ciliary body

---

## Q28: Identify the structure, name two cell types, one common infection, and its embryologic origin

![Sensory ganglion](__IMG:q28.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Sensory ganglion - large first-order neuronal cell bodies, surrounding satellite cells, open-faced euchromatic nuclei, and axonal bundles
- Cell types: sensory neurons and satellite (neurosatellite) cells
- Common infection: varicella-zoster virus
- Embryologic origin: neural crest

---

## Q29: Identify the structure, two sensory modalities, and two dermal layers

![Pacinian corpuscle](__IMG:q29.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Pacinian corpuscle - onion-like lamellae, a capsule, and location in the hypodermis
- Detects vibration and deep pressure
- Dermal layers: papillary and reticular

---

## Q34: Identify the epithelium, its location, and three cell types

![Olfactory epithelium](__IMG:q34.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Olfactory epithelium
- Located in the roof of the nasal cavity, superior nasal concha, and upper nasal septum
- Basal cells, supporting (sustentacular) cells, and olfactory receptor cells

---

## Q35: Identify the structure, name its components, and state the modalities transmitted by its neurons

![Neuromuscular spindle](__IMG:q35.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Neuromuscular spindle - a capsule encloses intrafusal fibres among extrafusal muscle fibres
- Components: annulospiral and flower-spray sensory endings
- Conveys proprioception, including muscle length/stretch and vibration

---

## Q36: Identify the slide, state the functional unit and its characteristics, and name two astrocyte types

![Cerebral cortex](__IMG:q36.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Cerebral cortex - highly cellular tissue with poorly defined zones
- Functional unit: cortical module/column, characterized by vertical arrangement and lateral inhibition
- Fibrous astrocytes and protoplasmic astrocytes

# Glands & Endocrine Organs

## Q2: Identify the gland, give two reasons, name two hormones acting on it, and state two cell types and their secretory products

![Lactating mammary gland](__IMG:q02.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Lactating mammary gland - lobulated, with a well-developed duct system and numerous alveoli
- Hormones: prolactin, oxytocin, and oestrogen
- Acinar/alveolar epithelial cells produce milk; myoepithelial cells contract to expel it

---

## Q5: Identify the organ, give two reasons, and describe the cells in X and Y with their functions

![Pancreas with exocrine acini and an islet](__IMG:q05.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Pancreas - lobulated organ containing exocrine acini and endocrine islets
- X: acinar cells secrete digestive enzymes; centroacinar cells begin the duct system and contribute bicarbonate-rich fluid; pancreatic stellate cells support the stroma
- Y: alpha cells secrete glucagon, beta cells insulin, delta cells somatostatin, and PP cells pancreatic polypeptide

---

## Q8: Identify the salivary gland, name its duct types, and list the components of a salivon

![Submandibular salivary gland](__IMG:q08.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Submandibular gland - a mixed gland with serous and mucous portions, predominantly serous
- Ducts: intercalated, striated, and excretory ducts
- Salivon: terminal acinus plus intercalated, striated, and excretory duct components

---

## Q12: Identify the gland, give two reasons, name its cell types and functions, and state one distinguishing feature

![Thyroid gland](__IMG:q12.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Thyroid gland - follicles containing colloid, lined by follicular cells, with parafollicular cells between follicles
- Follicular cells produce T3 and T4; parafollicular (C) cells produce calcitonin
- Distinguishing feature: thyroid hormone is stored extracellularly in colloid

---

## Q15: Identify the organ, give two reasons, name three cell types with functions, and state its functional lobule

![Liver histology](__IMG:q15.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Liver - hepatocytes arranged in cords, central veins, portal triads, and sinusoids
- Hepatocytes are the main metabolic and synthetic cells; hepatic stellate cells store vitamin A and make extracellular matrix; Kupffer cells are phagocytes
- Functional lobule: hepatic acinus

---

## Q18: Identify the gland, name two cell types in region Z, and categorize the secretory cells in region X

![Pituitary gland with regions X, Y, and Z](__IMG:q18.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Pituitary gland - two lobes of differing cellularity separated by the pars intermedia/interglandular cleft
- Neurohypophysis (Z): pituicytes and axonal fibres/terminals
- Adenohypophysis (X): acidophils (somatotrophs and mammotrophs), basophils (thyrotrophs, corticotrophs, and gonadotrophs), and chromophobes

---

## Q26: Identify the gland, give two reasons, outline control of its zones, and name two associated metabolic diseases

![Adrenal gland](__IMG:q26.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Adrenal gland - cortical and medullary regions, three cortical zones, and a central medullary vein
- Zona glomerulosa is regulated mainly by angiotensin II and potassium; zona fasciculata by ACTH; medulla by sympathetic preganglionic fibres
- Associated disorders include Cushing syndrome and secondary diabetes mellitus

---

## Q39: Identify the gland, name a tumour marker, list two components, and name two ovarian hormones with their sources

![Prostate gland](__IMG:q39.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Prostate - compound tubuloalveolar glands in fibromuscular stroma, often with corpora amylacea
- Tumour marker: prostate-specific antigen (PSA)
- Components include secretory glands and fibromuscular stroma/trabeculae
- Oestrogen is produced mainly by follicular (granulosa) cells with androgen substrate from theca interna; progesterone is produced mainly by the corpus luteum

---

# Muscle, Connective Tissue & Bone

## Q3: Identify the tissue, give three reasons, list three ultrastructural features, and state its functional unit

![Skeletal muscle in longitudinal section](__IMG:q03.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Skeletal muscle - long striated fibres with multiple peripheral nuclei
- Ultrastructure includes myofilaments, Z lines, M lines, H zones, I bands, T tubules, sarcoplasmic reticulum, and abundant mitochondria
- Functional unit: sarcomere

---

## Q7: Identify the connective tissue, state three distribution sites, and name the components of its outer layer

![Elastic cartilage](__IMG:q07.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Elastic cartilage - chondrocytes in lacunae, elastic fibres in the matrix, and a perichondrium
- Found in the pinna, epiglottis, and small laryngeal cartilages
- Perichondrium has an outer fibrous layer and inner chondrogenic/cellular layer

---

## Q14: Identify the structure and name its zones

![Epiphyseal growth plate](__IMG:q14.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Epiphyseal growth plate - stacked chondrocytes lie parallel to the long axis of bone
- Zones: resting, proliferative, maturation/hypertrophy, calcified cartilage, and ossification

---

## Q17: Identify the fibre, give two reasons, state four locations, and give its main function

![Reticular fibres](__IMG:q17.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Reticular fibres (type III collagen) - highly branched, net-like fibres surrounding cells
- Found in liver, spleen, bone marrow, adipose tissue, lungs, basal lamina, and lymphoid tissue
- Function: delicate supporting framework/stroma

---

## Q19: Identify the tissue, give two reasons, state two ultrastructural features, and name three cells with contractile properties

![Cardiac muscle](__IMG:q19.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Cardiac muscle - branching, cross-striated fibres with one or two central nuclei and intercalated discs
- Ultrastructural features include diads and intercalated discs
- Other contractile cells: myofibroblasts, myoepithelial cells, and myoid cells

---

## Q20: Identify the tissue, give two reasons, state the connective-tissue fibre, and give its distribution and function

![Fibrocartilage](__IMG:q20.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Fibrocartilage - chondroblasts/chondrocytes in lacunae among a dense fibrous matrix
- Predominant fibre: type I collagen
- Found in articular discs, symphyses, intervertebral discs, and tendon-to-bone attachments; resists compression and tensile stress

---

## Q27: Identify the tissue, give three reasons, name its cells and their functions, name three outer-layer components, and state its mode of growth

![Compact bone](__IMG:q27.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Compact bone - Haversian canals, lamellar systems, and osteocytes in lacunae
- Osteoblasts synthesize bone; osteocytes maintain matrix; osteoclasts resorb bone
- Periosteum: outer fibrous layer and inner cellular/osteogenic layer, with Sharpey fibres anchoring it
- Bone grows appositionally; interstitial growth does not occur in mineralized bone

---

## Q32: Identify the organelle and its function, name cytoskeletal components, and state three ultrastructural features of hepatocytes

![Rough endoplasmic reticulum on electron microscopy](__IMG:q32.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Rough endoplasmic reticulum - synthesis and initial processing of secreted and membrane proteins
- Cytoskeleton: microtubules, microfilaments, and intermediate filaments
- Hepatocytes contain abundant rough ER, Golgi apparatus, mitochondria, smooth ER, glycogen, and peroxisomes

---

# Gastrointestinal & Respiratory Tissue

## Q6: Identify the organ, define metaplasia and its significance here, and name three gastric epithelial cell types with their products

![Oesophagus](__IMG:q06.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Oesophagus - non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium, submucosal glands, longitudinal folds, and proximity to the trachea
- Metaplasia is reversible replacement of one mature cell type by another; columnar metaplasia in Barrett oesophagus increases the risk of adenocarcinoma
- Parietal cells secrete hydrochloric acid and intrinsic factor; chief cells pepsinogen and gastric lipase; surface/neck mucous cells mucus and bicarbonate; enteroendocrine cells include G cells producing gastrin

---

## Q9: Identify the alimentary segment, give two reasons, state three protective properties, and mention two adaptations for absorption

![Duodenum](__IMG:q09.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Duodenum - villi, crypts of Lieberkuhn, and submucosal Brunner glands
- Protection: bicarbonate-rich alkaline mucus, mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue, and rapid epithelial regeneration
- Absorptive adaptations: great length/surface area, villi, microvilli, and muscularis mucosae movement

---

## Q11: Identify the structure, name three associated receptor-cell types, state its innervation, and name its associated glands

![Circumvallate papilla](__IMG:q11.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Circumvallate papilla - mushroom-shaped papilla with grooves and parakeratinized stratified squamous epithelium
- Taste buds contain gustatory receptor cells, sustentacular/supporting cells, and basal cells
- Taste innervation: glossopharyngeal nerve (CN IX)
- Associated glands: serous glands of von Ebner

---

## Q13: Identify the alimentary segment, give two reasons, name three regional cell types, and state three unique gross features

![Colon](__IMG:q13.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Colon - long straight glands, abundant goblet cells, absence of villi, and lymphoid aggregates
- Cell types include colonocytes/columnar absorptive cells, goblet cells, and enteroendocrine cells
- Gross features: appendices epiploicae, taeniae coli, and haustra

---

## Q23: Identify the organ, give three reasons, and state ultrastructural features of most lining epithelial cells

![Kidney histology](__IMG:q23.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Kidney - renal corpuscles, medullary rays, numerous tubules, and a capsule
- Proximal tubular cells specialized for active ion transport have basal membrane infoldings, microvilli, tight junctions, and abundant basal mitochondria

---

## Q24: Identify the organ, give two reasons, list two resident cells in zone D with functions, and state three functional-unit components

![Lung histology with zone D](__IMG:q24.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Lung - alveoli and airways with cartilage
- Type I pneumocytes form the thin gas-exchange lining; type II pneumocytes secrete surfactant and regenerate alveolar epithelium
- Functional unit (acinus): respiratory bronchioles, alveolar ducts, and alveoli

---

## Q33: Identify the epithelium, give two reasons, state three sites, and name two parathyroid-gland cell types

![Transitional epithelium](__IMG:q33.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Transitional epithelium (urothelium) - umbrella-shaped surface cells and a variable number of layers
- Found in renal calyces/pelvis, ureters, urinary bladder, and proximal urethra
- Parathyroid gland cell types: principal (chief) cells and oxyphil cells

---

## Q38: Identify the slide, name two cells responsible for myelination, and name two parts of the neurohypophysis

![Ureter](__IMG:q38.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Ureter - urothelium, star-shaped lumen, and a muscular wall
- Myelinating cells: Schwann cells in the PNS and oligodendrocytes in the CNS
- Neurohypophysis: pars nervosa, infundibular stalk, and median eminence

---

## Q40: Identify the artery, name two tunica-intima components, and list two capillary types with locations

![Elastic conducting artery](__IMG:q40.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Elastic (conducting) artery - numerous elastic lamellae in the tunica media
- Tunica intima: vascular endothelium and subendothelial connective tissue; the internal elastic lamina is less distinct in elastic arteries
- Sinusoidal capillaries: liver and hematopoietic tissues
- Fenestrated capillaries: kidneys and endocrine organs

# Lymphoid, Reproductive & Other Organs

## Q4: Identify the slide, give two reasons, state two functions, and name the blood-air barrier components

![Spleen](__IMG:q04.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Spleen - capsule with incomplete trabeculae, red and white pulp, and central arterioles
- Functions include immune surveillance and removal/sequestration of aged red blood cells
- Blood-air barrier: type I alveolar epithelium, fused basal laminae, and capillary endothelium

---

## Q21: Identify the structure, give three reasons, name two medullary components, and state two adaptive immune responses

![Lymph node](__IMG:q21.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Lymph node - capsule, lymphoid follicles, and germinal centres
- Medulla: medullary cords and medullary sinuses
- Adaptive immune responses: humoral and cell-mediated immunity

---

## Q30: Identify the slide, give three reasons, name its four main cell types, and name four hormones it produces

![Thymus](__IMG:q30.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Thymus - lobules with cortex and medulla, trabeculae, and Hassall corpuscles
- Main cells include thymocytes/T-cell precursors, epithelial reticular cells, macrophages, and hormone-producing epithelial cells
- Thymic hormones include thymosin, thymopoietin, thymulin, and thymic humoral factor

---

## Q31: Identify the structures, give two reasons, name blood-thymus barrier components, and name two neural-crest cells in skin

![Sweat glands in skin](__IMG:q31.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Sweat glands - tubular profiles in cross-section within dermal/hypodermal connective tissue
- Blood-thymus barrier includes continuous capillary endothelium, thick basal lamina, perivascular connective tissue/macrophages, and epithelial reticular cells
- Neural-crest-derived skin cells: melanocytes and Merkel cells

---

## Q37: Identify the slide, give three reasons, and name the cell types with their functions

![Testis histology](__IMG:q37.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Testis - seminiferous tubules, interstitial Leydig cells, and developing spermatozoa
- Sertoli cells support spermatogenesis; Leydig cells secrete testosterone; peritubular myoid cells contract; germ cells form spermatozoa
