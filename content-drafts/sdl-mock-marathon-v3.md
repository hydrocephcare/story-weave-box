# SDL Revision Mock Marathon — Upper Limb, Histology & Embryology

*Spot-style revision set, University of Nairobi. Scope: upper limb gross anatomy, histology and embryology. 50 questions.*

## Q1: Name the nerve labelled N and state its root value
![Q1 - dissection of the axilla with a nerve tagged N](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q01.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Musculocutaneous nerve
- Root value: C5
- Root value: C6
- Root value: C7
- Arises from the lateral cord of the brachial plexus
- Pierces coracobrachialis, then runs between biceps and brachialis
- Continues as the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm

## Q3: Name the vessel tagged and state its extents
![Q3 - forceps tagging a vessel in the deltopectoral/axillary region](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q03.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Cephalic vein
- Begins at the roof of the anatomical snuffbox (wrist)
- Ascends the lateral forearm and arm
- Passes through the deltopectoral groove
- Pierces the clavipectoral fascia
- Drains into the axillary vein
- Related structure in this region: the deltopectoral (infraclavicular) lymph nodes lie alongside it

## Q6: State innervation and two actions of the muscle that inserts in the part shown

## Q15: State the neurovascular structure most at risk due to a fracture at the tagged part and give the basis

## Q31: State two characteristic features of the tissue displayed
![Q31 - skeletal muscle histology](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q31.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Skeletal muscle
- Visible cross-striations
- Multiple peripherally-located (subsarcolemmal) nuclei per fibre
- Fibres are long, unbranched, and cylindrical
- Compare: cardiac muscle has central nuclei and intercalated discs; smooth muscle is non-striated with central nuclei

## Q32: Giving reason, identify the epithelial type displayed
![Q32 - ciliated epithelium over cartilage](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q32.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium (respiratory epithelium of the trachea)
- Reason: all cells rest on the basement membrane
- Reason: not all cells reach the free surface
- Nuclei sit at different levels, giving a falsely stratified look
- Cilia are visible on the luminal surface
- Also seen in this field: goblet cells within the epithelium, and hyaline cartilage of the tracheal ring beneath it

## Q36: Name 1, 2, 3 and 4

## Q37: Give the salient features of the tissue displayed
![Q37 - cartilage-type histology](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q37.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Hyaline cartilage
- Chondrocytes sit in lacunae, often as isogenous groups
- Matrix is basophilic/metachromatic (rich in chondroitin sulfate)
- Avascular
- Bounded by a perichondrium (absent at articular surfaces)
- Found at: articular surfaces, costal cartilages, trachea/bronchi, epiphyseal growth plate

## Q41: State the anomaly depicted and give its basis
![Q41 - grape-like translucent vesicles](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q41.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Hydatidiform mole (molar pregnancy)
- Basis: hydropic (cystic) degeneration of chorionic villi
- Complete mole: androgenetic, diploid, empty ovum fertilised by a duplicating sperm
- Partial mole: triploid, from dispermy
- Clinical relevance: risk of gestational trophoblastic disease

## Q42: State the anomaly depicted and give its basis
![Q42 - conjoined infants](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q42.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Conjoined twins
- Basis: incomplete splitting of the embryonic disc during monozygotic twinning, after day 13
- Twinning before day 8: separate twins, separate amnion/chorion
- Twinning day 8–13: shared chorion, separate amnions

## Q43: Identify X and state a structure related to its formation
![Q43 - sagittal MRI of the lower spine showing a sac-like mass](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q43.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- X: spina bifida cystica sac (meningocele/myelomeningocele)
- Related structure: failure of closure of the neural tube (posterior neuropore)
- Occurs during the 4th week of development
- Associated with low folic acid intake in early pregnancy

## Q44: State the anomalies labelled A and B
![Q44 - diagram comparing two spina bifida variants](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q44.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: meningocele (meninges and CSF only, no neural tissue)
- B: myelomeningocele (meninges and neural tissue/spinal cord herniate)
- Mildest form (not shown here): spina bifida occulta — no herniation, just a vertebral arch defect

## Q48: State 2 functions of B
![Q48 - trilaminar embryonic disc diagram, primitive node, A and B labelled](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q48.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- B: (intraembryonic) mesoderm
- Gives rise to the musculoskeletal system
- Gives rise to the cardiovascular system
- Gives rise to the urogenital system
- Forms the notochord (from the primitive node)
- The notochord induces the overlying ectoderm to form the neural tube

## Q49: Identify the anomaly and state its basis
![Q49 - newborn with a large umbilical mass](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q49.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Omphalocele (exomphalos)
- Basis: failure of the herniated midgut to return to the abdominal cavity by week 10
- (or) failure of the lateral body-wall folds to fuse at the umbilical ring
- Abdominal viscera herniate into the cord base
- Covered by a peritoneal/amniotic sac (unlike gastroschisis, which has no covering sac)

## Q50: Identify the anomaly and state its basis
![Q50 - H-shaped diagram of the gut tube and a diverticulum](__IMG:sdl-mock-marathon/q50.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Meckel's diverticulum
- Basis: incomplete obliteration of the vitelline (omphalomesenteric) duct
- Normally regresses by week 7
- "Rule of 2s": ~2% of population, within 2 feet of the ileocaecal valve, ~2 inches long
