## Q4: Milk line — identify structure A and its fate; effect of abnormal persistence (3+1 marks)

![Fetus and adult torso showing the mammary/milk line (A)](__IMG:week-12-review/q04.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A — the milk line (mammary ridge)
- Fate in humans: it obliterates (regresses) except in the pectoral/chest region, where the mammary gland develops
- Abnormal persistence causes: polythelia (accessory/supernumerary nipples along the line)

## Q7: Cervical rib — symptoms of B; varieties of D (2+2 marks)

![Cervical rib (B) on a skeleton and a soft-tissue swelling in a neonate (D)](__IMG:week-12-review/q07.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Symptoms of a cervical rib (B) — thoracic outlet compression:
- Ischaemia of the upper limb
- Loss of sensation in the upper limb
- Loss of motor innervation to the upper limb
- Varieties of D (encephalocele-type swelling):
- Meningocele
- Myelomeningocele
- Related, also worth knowing: syringomyelia (a related spinal cord anomaly, not a variety of D itself)

## Q13: Breast anatomy — identify A and C; functional variation of B; surface landmark of D (2+1+1 marks)

![Sagittal section of the breast with A, B, C, D labelled](__IMG:week-12-review/q13.jpg__)

- C — loose areolar tissue surrounding the nipple (retromammary space)

## Q21 (bonus essay): Outline the spinal cord — external features, support structures, arterial supply, venous drainage, extents, internal features at T2, development and congenital anomalies

**Answer:**
- **Support structures:**
- Meningeal coverings (dura, arachnoid, pia mater)
- Cornu medullaris (conus medullaris)
- Cauda equina
- Cervical and lumbar enlargements
- Cervical and lumbosacral enlargements (correspond to limb innervation)
- Filum terminale
- **Extents:**
- Continuous with the brainstem superiorly
- Continues as spinal nerves
- Anchored by denticulate ligaments
- **Arterial blood supply:**
- Anterior spinal artery (1, from the vertebral arteries) — supplies the anterior two-thirds of the spinal cord
- Posterior spinal arteries (2, from the vertebral arteries) — supply the posterior third of the spinal cord
- Radicular arteries, derived from segmental vessels:
- Cervical region — ascending cervical artery (from inferior thyroid artery); deep cervical artery (from costocervical trunk)
- Thoracic region — posterior intercostal arteries
- Lumbar region — lumbar arteries
- Sacral region — lateral sacral arteries (from internal pudendal artery)
- The largest radicular artery is the Artery of Adamkiewicz (arteria radicularis magna), found in the lumbar region
