## Practice Questions

These are the "example of question" prompts given throughout the lecture, with answers.

**Q. Name the muscles attached to a labelled area of a bone, and give two structures passing through an adjacent foramen (generic hip-bone template).**
- Muscles typically asked about at the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS): sartorius and part of the inguinal ligament
- Muscles typically asked about at the ischial tuberosity: the hamstrings (semitendinosus, semimembranosus, long head of biceps femoris) and part of adductor magnus
- Structures through the greater sciatic foramen: sciatic nerve; superior and inferior gluteal vessels and nerves; pudendal nerve; internal pudendal vessels

**Q. Name the muscle attaching to a labelled hamstring origin; state two actions of biceps femoris.**
- Hamstring origin: ischial tuberosity
- Two actions of biceps femoris: flexes the knee; laterally rotates the flexed knee (also extends the hip)

**Q. (a) Identify the parts labelled A and B on a knee radiograph. (b) Name two structures attached to the anterior intercondylar area of the tibia.**
- A and B on a knee radiograph typically mark the medial and lateral tibial condyles/intercondylar eminences
- Two structures attached to the anterior intercondylar area of the tibia: anterior cruciate ligament; anterior horn of the medial meniscus

**Q. State the nerve supply and actions of the muscle whose tendon is tagged (posterior ankle/calcaneal tendon).**
- Muscle: gastrocnemius (forming part of the calcaneal/Achilles tendon)
- Nerve supply: tibial nerve
- Actions: plantarflexes the ankle; flexes the knee

**Q. Describe the course of the popliteal artery (5 marks). Name two structures found in the popliteal fossa.**
- Course: continuation of the femoral artery at the adductor hiatus; runs through the popliteal fossa deep to the popliteal vein and tibial nerve; gives off genicular branches around the knee; ends at the lower border of popliteus by dividing into the anterior tibial artery and the tibiofibular trunk (which becomes the posterior tibial and fibular arteries)
- Two structures in the popliteal fossa: tibial nerve; common fibular (peroneal) nerve
- Also accepted: popliteal vein; popliteal artery; small saphenous vein

**Q. Name two muscles that cause eversion of the foot, and two that cause inversion.**
- Eversion: fibularis (peroneus) longus; fibularis (peroneus) brevis
- Inversion: tibialis anterior; tibialis posterior

**Q. Name the muscles attached to two labelled areas of the humerus.**
- Greater tubercle: supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor (upper to lower facets)
- Lesser tubercle: subscapularis
- Deltoid tuberosity: deltoid
- Medial epicondyle: common flexor origin of the forearm flexors
- Lateral epicondyle: common extensor origin of the forearm extensors

**Q. Name two structures that attach to the area labelled A on a shoulder radiograph (greater tubercle). State the components of the rotator cuff.**
- Two structures attaching at the greater tubercle: supraspinatus tendon; infraspinatus tendon
- Components of the rotator cuff ("SITS"): supraspinatus; infraspinatus; teres minor; subscapularis

**Q. List the contents of the axilla (5 marks).**
- Axillary artery and its branches
- Axillary vein and its tributaries
- Cords and branches of the brachial plexus
- Axillary lymph nodes
- Fat and loose connective tissue

**Q. Describe the formation and branches of the brachial plexus (10 marks).**
- Formation: ventral rami of C5–T1 → form the upper (C5, C6), middle (C7), and lower (C8, T1) trunks → each trunk splits into anterior and posterior divisions → divisions recombine into the lateral, posterior, and medial cords (named for their relation to the axillary artery)
- Branches of the lateral cord: lateral pectoral nerve; musculocutaneous nerve; lateral root of the median nerve
- Branches of the posterior cord: upper and lower subscapular nerves; thoracodorsal nerve; axillary nerve; radial nerve
- Branches of the medial cord: medial pectoral nerve; medial cutaneous nerves of the arm and forearm; ulnar nerve; medial root of the median nerve
- Branches from the roots/trunks: dorsal scapular nerve; long thoracic nerve; nerve to subclavius; suprascapular nerve

**Q. State the motor distribution of the median, ulnar and radial nerves.**
- Median nerve: most forearm flexors and pronators (except flexor carpi ulnaris and the ulnar half of flexor digitorum profundus); thenar muscles; the two lateral lumbricals
- Ulnar nerve: flexor carpi ulnaris; ulnar half of flexor digitorum profundus; most small hand muscles (interossei, hypothenar muscles, adductor pollicis, the two medial lumbricals)
- Radial nerve: triceps brachii; all the extensor muscles of the forearm (via its posterior interosseous branch)

**Q. State the boundaries and contents of the cubital fossa (6 marks).**
- Boundaries: laterally, brachioradialis; medially, pronator teres; superiorly, an imaginary line between the humeral epicondyles; floor formed by brachialis and supinator; roof formed by deep fascia and the bicipital aponeurosis
- Contents (lateral to medial): biceps brachii tendon; brachial artery; median nerve

**Q. (a) State the attachments (origin and insertion) of a labelled forearm muscle. (b) Name two muscles that cause medial (ulnar) deviation at the wrist.**
- A commonly tagged forearm muscle here is flexor carpi ulnaris — origin: common flexor origin (medial epicondyle) and the olecranon/posterior ulna; insertion: pisiform, hamate, and base of the 5th metacarpal
- Two muscles causing medial (ulnar) deviation at the wrist: flexor carpi ulnaris; extensor carpi ulnaris

**Q. Identify the bones labelled X and Y on a hand radiograph (distal carpal row).**
- X — trapezium
- Y — trapezoid

**Q. State the boundaries and contents of the carpal tunnel (4 marks).**
- Boundaries: floor formed by the carpal bones; roof formed by the flexor retinaculum
- Contents: tendons of flexor digitorum superficialis and flexor digitorum profundus; tendon of flexor pollicis longus; median nerve

**Q. (a) State two movements impaired by injury to the median nerve at the wrist. (b) State the cutaneous distribution of the median, radial and ulnar nerves in the hand.**
- Two movements impaired by median nerve injury at the wrist: thumb opposition; abduction of the thumb (thenar muscle weakness)
- Median nerve cutaneous distribution: palmar surface of the lateral 3½ digits and the corresponding palm
- Radial nerve cutaneous distribution: dorsal surface of the lateral 3½ digits (over the proximal phalanges) and the dorsum of the lateral hand
- Ulnar nerve cutaneous distribution: palmar and dorsal surface of the medial 1½ digits and the corresponding medial hand
