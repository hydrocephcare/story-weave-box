# Anatomy Marathon 1 — Lower Limb

*Revision set by Beda Otieno, Level V MBChB, sent 11 June 2017. 22 questions, lower limb focus.*

## Q1: Name two structures attached at the part of the pelvis pointed
![Q1 - probe on the pelvis at a bony landmark](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q01.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Iliofemoral ligament
- Straight head of rectus femoris
- Both attach at the anterior inferior iliac spine (AIIS)

## Q2: (a) State the blood supply to the femoral head (D) and its clinical relevance. (b) State the attachment and innervation of the iliopsoas tendon
![Q2 - femoral head with a landmark tagged D](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q02.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Retinacular vessels supply the femoral head
- Arise from ascending cervical branches of the extracapsular arterial ring
- Ring formed anteriorly by the lateral circumflex femoral artery
- Ring formed posteriorly by the medial circumflex femoral artery
- Clinical relevance: risk of avascular necrosis after a fracture of the neck of femur
- Iliopsoas tendon attaches at the lesser trochanter
- Innervation: femoral nerve (L2–L4) for iliacus
- Innervation: L1–L3 (ventral rami) for psoas major

## Q3: (a) State two actions of the muscle Y. (b) Name the muscles inserting at X and their innervation
![Q3 - thigh dissection with Y and X labelled](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q03.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Y: rectus femoris
- Flexes the hip
- Extends the knee
- (most two-joint muscles have opposite actions at each joint — sartorius is an exception)
- X: iliotibial tract
- Tensor fasciae latae inserts here — superior gluteal nerve (L4, L5, S1)
- Gluteus maximus also inserts here — inferior gluteal nerve (L5, S1, S2)

## Q4: (a) Identify the structure tagged Q and its distribution. (b) Name two factors that prevent the natural tendency of patellar dislocation, apart from Q
![Q4 - thigh dissection with a structure tagged Q](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q04.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Note: the source question calls Q a "nerve," but the tagged region visually corresponds to a muscle belly (most consistent with vastus medialis / VMO) — flagging this mismatch for you to verify against the original image
- If Q = saphenous nerve: purely sensory; supplies the knee joint via the subsartorial plexus, skin over the front of the patella, and the anteromedial leg
- If Q = vastus medialis (oblique fibres): pulls the patella medially, resisting lateral dislocation
- Other stabilising factors: the medial patellofemoral ligament
- Other stabilising factors: the prominent lateral femoral condyle / deep trochlear groove

## Q5: (a) Identify N and its distribution. (b) Name one other muscle that forms the floor of Hunter's canal, apart from M
![Q5 - femoral triangle dissection with M and N labelled](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q05.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- N: profunda femoris artery
- Gives perforating branches (supply the posterior thigh muscles)
- Gives the medial and lateral circumflex femoral arteries (supply the femoral head)
- M: adductor longus (forms part of the floor of the adductor/Hunter's canal)
- Other floor muscle: adductor magnus
- Roof of the canal: sartorius

## Q6: (a) Name the landmark tagged and its clinical significance. (b) Identify tendon "PINT." (c) Identify G
![Q6 - ischial region with landmarks tagged](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q06.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Ischial spine
- Landmark for pudendal nerve block (pudendal anesthesia)
- Pudendal nerve root value: S2, S3, S4
- "PINT" mnemonic tendon: obturator internus
- G: reflected head of rectus femoris

## Q7: (a) Identify nerve L. (b) Name two modifications of the fascia lata apart from the iliotibial tract. (c) Give a cause of a positive Trendelenburg test
![Q7 - posterior thigh dissection with nerve L tagged](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q07.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- L: posterior cutaneous nerve of the thigh (lies superficial to biceps femoris)
- Fascia lata modification: intermuscular septa
- Fascia lata modification: saphenous opening (fossa ovalis)
- Positive Trendelenburg sign: hip abductor (gluteus medius/minimus) dysfunction
- Causes: superior gluteal nerve injury, superior gluteal vessel injury, hip joint arthritis, hip dislocation

## Q8: (a) State two sources of blood supply to R. (b) State the attachment of muscle S
![Q8 - gluteal region with R and S labelled](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q08.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Blood supply is segmental
- Perforating branches of profunda femoris
- Inferior gluteal artery
- Internal pudendal artery
- Medial circumflex femoral artery
- These form a longitudinal anastomosis
- S: piriformis
- Originates from the anterior aspect of the middle three sacral pieces
- Inserts on the tip of the greater trochanter

## Q9: (a) Name two branches of X, proximal to the tagged point. (b) State the direction of dislocation of the distal fragment after a supracondylar femoral fracture
![Q9 - popliteal fossa dissection with X tagged](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q09.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- X: sural nerve (in the popliteal fossa)
- In the popliteal fossa, the parent nerve gives branches to gastrocnemius, popliteus, soleus, plantaris
- Also gives an articular branch to the knee
- Gives a cutaneous branch that becomes the sural nerve (joined by a contribution from the common fibular nerve)
- Sural nerve supplies the lateral side of the foot
- Fracture displacement: posterior displacement of the distal fragment
- Cause: pull of gastrocnemius, which originates from the supracondylar ridge

## Q10: (a) Identify the tendon tagged. (b) Describe the stability factors of the ankle joint
![Q10 - ankle dissection with a tendon tagged](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q10.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Peroneus tertius
- Static stability: the mortise-and-tenon (talocrural) joint structure itself
- Static stability: deltoid ligament
- Static stability: anterior and posterior talofibular ligaments
- Static stability: distal tibiofibular ligament
- Static stability: calcaneonavicular ligament
- Dynamic stability: all tendons of the anterior and posterior leg compartments crossing the joint

## Q11: (a) Identify the muscle tagged and its actions. (b) Describe the pattern of blood supply to N and its clinical relevance
![Q11 - sole of the foot with N labelled](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q11.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Tibialis posterior
- Actions: inversion and plantarflexion of the foot
- N: the plantar "watershed" area
- Proximal part receives blood supply from the muscles forming it
- Distal part receives blood supply from the plantar aponeurosis
- Middle segment has poor blood supply
- Clinical relevance: slow healing after injury in this zone

## Q12: (a) Identify and side the bone shown. (b) Name a muscle inserting on its body and the clinical relevance. (c) Name two joints where inversion/eversion occur
![Q12 - isolated tarsal bone specimen](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q12.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Left talus
- Sided by the "table-top test": with the rounded head pointing forward, the trochlear surface (convex front-to-back, concave side-to-side) faces upward
- No muscle inserts directly on the talus — this was left unanswered in the original source, but it's worth knowing that no tendon attaches to it at all
- Clinical relevance: with only ligamentous attachments and a retrograde blood supply, the talus is prone to avascular necrosis after a talar neck fracture
- Inversion/eversion occur at the subtalar joint
- Inversion/eversion also occur at the talocalcaneonavicular joint
- (the calcaneocuboid joint contributes too, as part of the transverse tarsal joint)

## Q13: (a) State the origin of the muscle tagged. (b) State its innervation and actions
![Q13 - back dissection with a muscle tagged F](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q13.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Note: the source answer key mixes two muscles here — the origin given (external occipital protuberance, superior nuchal line, ligamentum nuchae, spinous processes of C7–T12) matches trapezius, while the innervation/actions given (thoracodorsal nerve; adduction, medial rotation, extension of the arm) match latissimus dorsi. Verify which muscle is actually tagged in the source image before publishing.
- If trapezius: innervation is the spinal accessory nerve (CN XI); actions include scapular rotation/elevation/retraction
- If latissimus dorsi: origin is the thoracolumbar fascia, iliac crest, lower ribs, and inferior angle of the scapula; innervation is the thoracodorsal nerve; actions are adduction, medial rotation, and extension of the arm

## Q14: (a) State the root value of M. (b) Describe the anatomical basis of gallbladder pain referral to the right shoulder
![Q14 - shoulder/neck dissection with M tagged](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q14.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- M: phrenic nerve
- Root value: C3, C4, C5 (source key states "C1–C5"; the classical root value is C3–C5, with C4 as the major contributor)
- The phrenic nerve innervates the diaphragm and, via visceral afferents, the biliary apparatus
- Referred pain to the right shoulder occurs because the phrenic nerve (C3–C5) shares spinal cord segments with the supraclavicular nerves, which supply the shoulder skin

## Q15: (a) Identify the nerve associated with the clinical features shown in A and B. (b) Describe the clinical grouping of the axillary nodes and its importance
![Q15 - winged scapula (A) and clawed hand (B)](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q15.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: winged scapula — long thoracic nerve injury (serratus anterior paralysis)
- B: claw hand — ulnar nerve injury
- Axillary nodes have five groups: lateral, anterior (pectoral), posterior (subscapular), central, apical
- Lateral, anterior and posterior groups drain into the central group, which drains into the apical group
- Importance: this pattern lets the spread of infection or cancer (e.g. breast cancer) be traced and staged

## Q16: (a) Identify K and its terminal distribution. (b) Give two causes of injury to L
![Q16 - right axilla and arm dissection with K and L labelled](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q16.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- K: musculocutaneous nerve

- L: median nerve
- Cause of injury: supracondylar humeral fractures
- Cause of injury: inflammation of tendons at the carpal tunnel

## Q17: (a) State the insertion and actions of the muscle tagged. (b) What is "tennis elbow"?
![Q17 - forearm dissection with a muscle tagged](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q17.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Brachioradialis
- Origin: lateral supracondylar ridge of the humerus
- Insertion: distal radius (near the styloid process)
- Action: flexes the elbow joint
- Tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis): pain from excessive/repetitive use of the forearm extensor muscles at their common origin

## Q18: (a) Identify the nerve injuries shown in A and B. (b) Explain why the scapular anastomosis pulsates in coarctation of the aorta
![Q18 - shoulder drop (A) and wrist drop (B)](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q18.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A: axillary nerve injury — shoulder drop, paraesthesia over the "regimental badge" area of the arm
- B: radial nerve injury — wrist drop (unopposed flexion of the hand)
- In coarctation of the aorta distal to the subclavian artery origin, blood is diverted through the subclavian artery
- This markedly enlarges its branches contributing to the scapular anastomosis
- The dilated collateral vessels pulsate in time with the heartbeat

## Q19: (a) Identify the muscle tagged. (b) Name one non-neurovascular content of the cubital fossa. (c) What is Tinel's sign?
![Q19 - forearm dissection at the elbow](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q19.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Pronator quadratus
- Non-neurovascular content of the cubital fossa: tendon of biceps brachii
- Tinel's sign: a tingling sensation produced when an irritated/regenerating nerve is tapped
- Used to test the functional recovery of a nerve

## Q20: (a) Name the terminal branches of the radial artery. (b) Describe the surface landmark of the deep palmar arch
![Q20 - palmar dissection](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q20.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Radialis indicis artery
- Princeps pollicis artery
- Together these form the deep palmar arch
- Surface landmark: fully extend the thumb and draw a line across the palm from the distal border of the thumb — this marks the level of the superficial palmar arch (Boeckel's line)
- The deep palmar arch lies about a finger's width proximal to this line

## Q21: Name the structures involved in the "unhappy triad" of O'Donoghue
![Q21 - knee injury context photo](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q21.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Anterior cruciate ligament
- Medial collateral ligament
- Medial meniscus

## Q22: (a) Classify the radio-ulnar joints. (b) Name two features contributing to the high mobility of the 1st carpometacarpal joint
![Q22 - dissection specimen](__IMG:anatomy-marathon-1/q22.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Radio-ulnar joints are pivot (uniaxial) synovial joints
- Proximal and distal radio-ulnar joints act together
- High mobility of the 1st carpometacarpal joint comes from its saddle-shaped joint surfaces
- Also from the surrounding thenar muscles, which actively increase its range of motion
- It is the joint between the first metacarpal and the trapezium (angle formed with the first phalanx is relevant to thumb movements)
