## Q1: Muscle A — insertion; root value and distribution of nerve B (2+2 marks)

![Gluteal/thigh dissection with muscle A and nerve B labelled](__IMG:pat3-2015/q01.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Insertion of muscle A (tensor fasciae latae):
- 3/4 into Gerdy's tubercle via the iliotibial tract
- 1/4 into the gluteal tuberosity of the femur
- Nerve B (sciatic nerve) root value and distribution: sacral plexus (S1, S2, S3)

- Two actions of muscle Y (adductor group):
- Strong adductor of the hip

## Q6: Hip joint — name A–C; main stability factor of the hip joint (3+1 marks)

![Coronal illustration of the hip joint with A, B, C labelled](__IMG:pat3-2015/q06.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A — lunate (articular) surface of the acetabulum
- B — ligamentum teres (round ligament of the femoral head)
- C — acetabular labrum
- Main stability factors of the hip joint:
- Bony anatomy — the most important factor
- The labrum — contributes via a "suction seal" effect, creating negative pressure between the femoral head and acetabulum
- Ligaments of the hip joint capsule

## Q13: Outline four actions of the intervertebral disc, A (4 marks)

## Q22: Identify the type of chromatin labelled A and B; two features of a cell actively synthesizing protein (2+2 marks)

- A large, prominent nucleolus
- Abundant euchromatin (dispersed chromatin) and well-developed rough endoplasmic reticulum

## Q27: Two unique features of the cartilage type displayed; two cartilage types lacking a perichondrium (2+2 marks)

![Photomicrograph of hyaline cartilage with chondrocytes in lacunae](__IMG:pat3-2015/q27.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Two unique features of hyaline cartilage: chondrocytes sit in lacunae, often arranged in isogenous groups; the matrix appears homogeneous/glassy since its type II collagen fibres are not visible on routine staining
- Two cartilage types that lack a perichondrium: fibrocartilage; articular (hyaline) cartilage

## Q28: Name four cell types in the tissue displayed and state the function of each (4 marks)

![Ground section of compact bone showing Haversian systems](__IMG:pat3-2015/q28.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- Osteoblasts — bone-forming cells; secrete osteoid (unmineralized bone matrix)
- Osteocytes — mature bone cells within lacunae; maintain bone matrix and sense mechanical strain
- Osteoclasts — large multinucleated cells; resorb bone
- Osteoprogenitor cells — stem cells that differentiate into osteoblasts

## Q31: Hormonal basis of events A and B; two phases of spermatogenesis (2+2 marks)

- Event A (endometrial shedding/menstruation) hormonal basis: withdrawal of oestrogen and progesterone following corpus luteum regression

## Q32: One derivative and one anomaly of B; one function and one anomaly associated with D (2+2 marks)

**Answer:**
- B — notochord. Derivative: nucleus pulposus of the intervertebral disc. Anomaly: chordoma (tumour of notochordal remnants)
- D — primitive gut tube. Function: gives rise to the epithelial lining and glands of the gastrointestinal tract and its associated organs (liver, pancreas). Anomaly: duodenal atresia

## Q33: Syndromes depicted by the karyotypes displayed; most vulnerable period for congenital anomalies (2+2 marks)

![Two karyotypes, A showing trisomy 21 and B showing an extra sex chromosome](__IMG:pat3-2015/q33.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A — Down syndrome (trisomy 21)
- B — Klinefelter syndrome (47, XXY)
- Most vulnerable period for congenital anomalies: the embryonic period (weeks 3–8) — reason: this is the period of organogenesis, when all major organ systems are being laid down, making the embryo most susceptible to teratogens

## Q35: Identify the types of conjoined twins displayed; two meiotic arrests during oogenesis (2+2 marks)

![Two clinical photos of conjoined twin anomalies, A and B](__IMG:pat3-2015/q35.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A — craniopagus (twins conjoined at the head)
- B — parapagus (twins conjoined side-by-side, sharing a pelvis)
- Two meiotic arrests during oogenesis: arrest in prophase I (diplotene stage — persists from fetal life until puberty); arrest in metaphase II (persists until fertilization)

## Q36: Foetal membrane associated with these malformations; two causes of polyhydramnios (2+2 marks)

![Clinical photo of talipes (clubfoot), A, beside an intraoperative photo of exteriorized bowel, B](__IMG:pat3-2015/q36.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A (talipes/clubfoot) — associated foetal membrane: amnion (oligohydramnios from reduced amniotic fluid can compress and deform the developing limb)
- B (exteriorized bowel/omphalocele) — associated foetal membrane: amnion (forms the covering membrane of the herniation sac)
- Two causes of polyhydramnios: fetal anomalies that prevent normal swallowing of amniotic fluid (e.g. oesophageal atresia, anencephaly); maternal diabetes mellitus

## Q40: Identify the parts labeled A and B; embryological basis of syndactyly and amelia (2+2 marks)

![Cross-section through pharyngeal arches with arch muscle mass (A) and arch cartilage (B) labelled](__IMG:pat3-2015/q40.jpg__)

**Answer:**
- A — pharyngeal (branchial) arch muscle mass (mesodermal core of the arch)
- B — pharyngeal arch cartilage (arch skeletal element)
- Embryological basis of syndactyly: failure of apoptosis in the interdigital mesenchyme, so the webbing between digits fails to be resorbed
- Embryological basis of amelia: early failure of limb bud formation/disruption of the apical ectodermal ridge, so the limb bud never develops
