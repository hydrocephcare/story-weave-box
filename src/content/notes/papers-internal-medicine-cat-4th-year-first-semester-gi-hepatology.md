## Section A: Multiple choice questions

### 1. The following are typical ulcerative HIV-associated lesions. Which one is NOT?
- A. Pseudomembranous candidiasis
- B. Herpes simplex viral infection
- C. Oral hairy leukoplakia
- D. Kaposi's sarcoma
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Herpes simplex is the truly ulcerative lesion. Pseudomembranous candidiasis, hairy leukoplakia and Kaposi's sarcoma are all non-ulcerative, so the question has three possible answers. Kaposi's sarcoma (a purple plaque or nodule) is the clearest non-ulcerative lesion.

### 2. All the following are associated with HIV-related oesophageal candidiasis. Which one is NOT?
- A. Echinocandins
- B. Mostly associated with solitary lesions
- C. Voriconazole
- D. No role for topical agents
- E. All of the above

**Answer: B.** Oesophageal candidiasis causes multiple white plaques over a diffuse area, not a single lesion. Oesophageal disease needs systemic treatment (fluconazole, or an echinocandin if resistant), so topical agents alone do not work.

### 3. Cryptosporidiosis is associated with all of the following EXCEPT one:
- A. Acalculous cholecystitis
- B. Incubation period of 10 days
- C. Nitazoxanide
- D. Modified Ziehl-Neelsen stain
- E. Treatment with fumagillin

**Answer: E.** Fumagillin is used for microsporidiosis. Cryptosporidium is treated with nitazoxanide and, above all, by restoring immunity with ART.

### 4. The following statements are true of Isospora belli. Which one is NOT?
- A. Multicellular
- B. Protozoan
- C. Associated with eosinophilia
- D. Transmission is faeco-oral
- E. None of the above

**Answer: A.** It is a single-celled protozoan parasite.

### 5. Which one of the following statements is true of HIV-hepatitis B co-infection?
- A. TDF monotherapy can be used if there is liver decompensation
- B. There is no role for adefovir
- C. Entecavir is the first-line treatment
- D. Entecavir induces M164V mutation
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Entecavir has some activity against HIV, so used alone it can select the HIV resistance mutation (printed here as M164V, which is a typing error for M184V). Co-infected patients should receive a full antiretroviral regimen that includes tenofovir with lamivudine or emtricitabine, not monotherapy.

### 6. The following statements are associated with non-alcoholic steatohepatitis. Which one is NOT?
- A. Pre-diabetes
- B. Inflammation
- C. Ballooning
- D. Occurs in men who drink more than 30 g of ethanol
- E. Occurs in women who drink less than 20 g of alcohol

**Answer: D.** NASH is defined by little or no alcohol: under 30 g a day in men and under 20 g in women. Heavier drinking is alcoholic liver disease.

### 7. Which one of the following conditions may NOT be a typical association with NASH?
- A. Heredity
- B. Polycystic ovarian syndrome
- C. Sleep apnoea
- D. Commoner in the black race
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** NASH is commoner in Hispanic and white people than in black people.

### 8. All the following mechanisms may be associated with NASH. Which one is NOT?
- A. Mitochondrial dysfunction
- B. Insulin hypersensitivity
- C. Cirrhosis
- D. Fat droplets in the cytoskeleton
- E. Inflammation

**Answer: B.** NASH comes with insulin resistance, not hypersensitivity.

### 9. The following statements are true of portal hypertension. Which one is NOT?
- A. Portal hypertension is defined as HVPG greater than 5 mmHg
- B. Portal hypertension
- C. Portal vein thrombosis is a post-hepatic cause of portal hypertension
- D. Budd-Chiari syndrome is associated with post-hepatic portal hypertension
- E. Banti's syndrome is associated with pre-hepatic portal hypertension

**Answer: C.** Portal vein thrombosis blocks the vein before the liver (pre-hepatic).

### 10. The following conditions fall under sinusoidal causes of portal hypertension. Which one is NOT?
- A. Primary biliary cirrhosis
- B. NASH
- C. Amyloidosis
- D. Alcoholic hepatitis
- E. Secondary syphilis

**Answer: A.** Primary biliary cirrhosis begins by damaging small bile ducts, giving a pre-sinusoidal block early on. NASH, amyloid and alcoholic hepatitis block the sinusoids. Secondary syphilis gives a granulomatous hepatitis, so the question is not clear-cut.

### 11. The following statements on varices caused by portal hypertension are true. Which one is NOT?
- A. Sengstaken-Blakemore tube may be used
- B. HVPG of 5 mmHg is typically associated with variceal rupture
- C. 30% of patients with varices will bleed
- D. About 10% of cirrhotics develop varices every year
- E. Tense ascites may predict bleeding

**Answer: B.** Varices form at an HVPG of about 10 and bleed at about 12 mmHg or above, not 5.

### 12. Which of the following is true of treatment of oesophageal varices?
- A. Initial 50 µg IV bolus of octreotide
- B. Initial 500 µg IV bolus of octreotide
- C. 500 µg IV/hour infusion for 1 week
- D. Vasopressin 20 to 40 units in normal saline every 2 hours until bleeding stops
- E. None of the above

**Answer: A.** Octreotide is given as a 50 µg bolus followed by 50 µg an hour for 2 to 5 days.

### 13. The following statements are true of liver function tests. Which one is NOT?
- A. GGT is sensitive but non-specific
- B. 30% of the population has abnormal liver function tests
- C. AST may be altered in brain pathology
- D. Isolated increase in GGT does not require further evaluation
- E. GGT is necessary to evaluate the source of GGT

**Answer: E.** This option is circular and is not a true statement. GGT is used to show that a raised ALP comes from the liver. Options B and D are also weak (the usual figure for abnormal tests is under 10%), so the question is poor.

### 14. Which of the following statements is FALSE concerning primary sclerosing cholangitis?
- A. Can be cured on proper medical therapy
- B. Liver transplantation is the only effective therapy
- C. May recur after liver transplantation
- D. Is associated with autoimmune conditions
- E. Associated with non-suppurative inflammation

**Answer: A.** No medicine cures PSC. Transplant is the only effective treatment, and it can recur.

### 15. One of the following statements is true of liver disease:
- A. Haemolytic jaundice is not associated with urobilinogen in urine
- B. Trace bilirubin is usually found in urine
- C. In obstructive jaundice no urobilinogen is found in urine
- D. Van den Bergh reaction is indirect positive in obstructive jaundice
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** If the bile duct is blocked, bilirubin cannot reach the gut, so no urobilinogen forms. Haemolysis gives extra urobilinogen.

### 16. The following statements are true of liver disease except one:
- A. In hepatocellular disease the faeces pigment is light brown
- B. Dubin-Johnson syndrome is associated with decreased urobilinogen in urine
- C. Gilbert's syndrome is associated with normal conjugated bilirubin
- D. Post-hepatic obstructive jaundice is associated with increased urine urobilinogen
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** In obstruction the urine urobilinogen is absent or reduced.

### 17. A 30-year-old pregnant woman is HBsAg positive. Which factor is MOST useful in determining the risk of transmission to her baby?
- A. Hepatitis B e antigen (HBeAg) status
- B. Hepatitis B core IgG status
- C. ALT of 23 IU/L
- D. Her alcohol history
- E. AST/ALT ratio over 2

**Answer: A.** A positive HBeAg means a high viral load and a high chance of transmission (above 70% without prevention).

### 18. A 20-year-old man has 3 months of bloody mucoid diarrhoea. Colonoscopy shows diffuse erythema and ulcerations in the entire colon and patchy ulceration in the terminal ileum. The MOST likely diagnosis is:
- A. Ulcerative colitis
- B. Crohn's disease
- C. Amoebic colitis
- D. Shigellosis
- E. Tuberculous enteritis

**Answer: B.** Patchy ileal ulceration points to Crohn's. Ulcerative colitis is continuous from the rectum, though "backwash ileitis" can confuse the picture.

### 19. A 25-year-old man with chronic hepatitis B has HBV DNA of 100 million IU/mL. All of the following would be required before initiating treatment EXCEPT:
- A. Liver function tests
- B. Hepatitis B e antigen test
- C. Hepatitis B core antigen test
- D. Hepatitis C virus screen
- E. Thyroid function tests

**Answer: E.**

### 20. A 70-year-old man has generalised pruritus and weight loss, green jaundice and a palpable, non-tender gall bladder. The MOST likely diagnosis is:
- A. Acute cholelithiasis
- B. Acute cholecystitis
- C. Hepatocellular carcinoma
- D. Pancreatic malignancy

**Answer: D.** A painless palpable gall bladder with jaundice is Courvoisier's sign, typical of a pancreatic head cancer.

### 21. Which of the following seromarkers is correctly matched with its role?
- A. HBsAg: immunity to hepatitis B infection
- B. HBsAb: immunity to hepatitis B infection
- C. HBeAg: active replication of virus
- D. HBcIgM: chronic infection
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** B is the clearest, but C is also correct, so the question has two true options. HBsAg shows infection and anti-HBc IgM shows recent infection.

### 22. Eligibility for treatment of hepatitis may require all of the following except one:
- A. ALT
- B. Biopsy
- C. HBeAg
- D. AST
- E. HBsAg

**Answer: E.** HBsAg diagnoses infection. Treatment decisions use ALT, AST, HBeAg, viral load and fibrosis assessment (biopsy).

### 23. The following statements are true of acute pancreatitis EXCEPT one:
- A. It is generally self-limiting
- B. A minority of cases have systemic complications
- C. Viral hepatitis is not a possible cause
- D. Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency is a possible susceptibility factor
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** Viruses (mumps, Coxsackie, hepatitis, CMV) can cause pancreatitis.

### 24. Which of the following statements is NOT true concerning acute pancreatitis?
- A. Pain worsens by lying flat
- B. Pulmonary decompensation may arise
- C. Absolute lipase level correlates with severity
- D. Serial measurements of lipase during treatment are not of much value
- E. Raised carboxypeptidase levels may indicate pancreatitis

**Answer: C.** The height of the lipase or amylase does not predict severity.

### 25. The statements below are true of acute pancreatitis. Which one is NOT?
- A. Haematocrit above 48% at 48 hours after admission predicts necrosis
- B. Obesity is always linked to mortality
- C. Base deficit is assessed as part of Ranson's criteria
- D. Pethidine is a first-line narcotic in treatment
- E. Early enteral feeding is encouraged

**Answer: B.** Obesity raises risk, but "always" is not true.

### 26. Which of the following statements concerning cholera is FALSE?
- A. Patients with blood group AB are less likely to be affected
- B. El Tor is involved in mild disease
- C. Is Gram negative
- D. Colonises the small intestine
- E. Is always aerobic

**Answer: E.** Vibrio cholerae can grow with or without oxygen (facultative anaerobe).

### 27. Which of the following is the most common cause of duodenal ulcers?
- A. NSAIDs
- B. Helicobacter pylori
- C. Alcohol abuse
- D. Chronic corticosteroid therapy
- E. Zollinger-Ellison syndrome

**Answer: B.**

### 28. Which organism is NOT responsible for causing blood-stained diarrhoea?
- A. Campylobacter spp.
- B. Salmonella spp.
- C. Escherichia coli
- D. Shigella spp.
- E. Staphylococcus spp.

**Answer: E.** Staphylococcal food poisoning causes watery diarrhoea and vomiting from a preformed toxin, without blood.

### 29. A 50-year-old woman has a week of jaundice and right upper quadrant pain, with dark urine and pale stools, no weight loss and no alcohol. Bilirubin 40 µmol/L, ALT 40, AST 50, ALP 350 IU/L. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Gallstones
- B. Viral hepatitis
- C. Alcoholic hepatitis
- D. Carcinoma of the head of the pancreas
- E. Autoimmune hepatitis

**Answer: A.** Normal transaminases with a high ALP means obstruction, and painful jaundice without weight loss favours a stone in the bile duct.

### 30. The following are roles of TH17 in HIV enteropathy. Which one is NOT?
- A. Intestinal homeostasis
- B. Cessation of mucin production
- C. Epithelial regeneration
- D. Induces claudins expression
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Th17 cells promote mucin production, repair and tight-junction proteins. Losing them in HIV weakens the gut barrier.

## Section B: Short answer questions

### 1. A 53-year-old man presents to A & E with recurrent haematochezia. List 6 possible aetiological factors.

1. **Haemorrhoids** (internal).
2. **Anal fissure.**
3. **Colorectal polyps or carcinoma.**
4. **Diverticular disease.**
5. **Inflammatory bowel disease** (ulcerative colitis, Crohn's).
6. **Angiodysplasia** (vascular malformations).
7. Infective colitis (Shigella, amoeba, Campylobacter, CMV), ischaemic colitis, radiation proctitis, and a brisk upper GI bleed.

### 2. List the causes of portal hypertension under the following sub-headings.

- **Presinusoidal (3 marks):** schistosomiasis; congenital hepatic fibrosis; sarcoidosis; myeloproliferative disease; portal and splenic vein thrombosis.
- **Sinusoidal (3 marks):** cirrhosis (alcohol, hepatitis B and C, NASH); fatty liver; metastatic malignant disease; partial nodular transformation of the liver; amyloidosis.
- **Post-sinusoidal:** veno-occlusive disease; Budd-Chiari syndrome (hepatic vein thrombosis or obstruction of outflow); constrictive pericarditis and right heart failure; inferior vena cava webs and primary vascular malignancies.
