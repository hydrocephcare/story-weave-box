# Upper gastrointestinal bleeding — Year 4 Internal Medicine (Gastroenterology) Notes

*Clinical reasoning notes. Resuscitate first, diagnose second.*

**The reasoning chain:** is the patient shocked → resuscitate (airway, IV access, fluids, blood) → stratify risk → find the cause (ulcer or varices?) → endoscopy within 24 hours → treat the cause and prevent rebleeding.

## 1. Recognise it

**Haematemesis** (vomiting blood, red or "coffee-ground") and **melaena** (black, tarry, foul-smelling stool). Rapid bleeding can give **fresh red blood per rectum** with shock. The bleed is **upper** if it arises above the ligament of Treitz (oesophagus, stomach, duodenum).

## 2. Assess severity and resuscitate (ABC first)

- **Shock signs:** pulse above 100, systolic BP below 100, cold peripheries, low urine output, confusion.
- **Two large-bore IV cannulae**, **group and crossmatch**, give **crystalloid**, and **transfuse blood** for shock or ongoing bleeding. A **restrictive threshold (about 7 g/dL)** is usually used, higher with heart disease.
- **Correct clotting problems** (stop warfarin and give vitamin K or specific reversal; consider platelets if very low).
- **Catheterise** to monitor urine output; give oxygen.
- Be alert for **airway protection** in a patient who is vomiting blood and drowsy.

**Risk score (Glasgow-Blatchford):** uses urea, haemoglobin, systolic BP, pulse, melaena, syncope, liver disease and heart failure to decide who needs admission and urgent endoscopy. **A score of 0 means very low risk and outpatient care is possible.**

## 3. Find the cause

| Cause | Clues |
|---|---|
| **Peptic ulcer (duodenal or gastric)** | Epigastric pain, **NSAIDs, aspirin, H. pylori**, previous ulcer |
| **Oesophageal or gastric varices** | **Known or suspected cirrhosis, jaundice, ascites, spider naevi, splenomegaly, alcohol, hepatitis B**, **schistosomiasis** with portal hypertension; **massive** bleeding |
| **Gastritis or erosions** | Alcohol, NSAIDs, stress in a sick patient |
| **Mallory-Weiss tear** | Retching, then fresh blood after repeated vomiting |
| **Oesophagitis, cancer, angiodysplasia, Dieulafoy lesion** | Less common |

**Think local:** in many African settings, **bleeding varices from cirrhosis (hepatitis B or schistosomiasis)** are a leading cause, and the management differs from that of an ulcer.

## 4. Investigate with purpose

| Test | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Haemoglobin** | How much has been lost? (It may be normal in the first hours.) |
| **Urea and creatinine** | A **raised urea with a normal creatinine** suggests an upper GI bleed (blood is digested as protein) |
| **Group and crossmatch, clotting screen, platelets** | Blood ready? Coagulopathy? |
| **Liver tests, albumin, INR** | Is there cirrhosis and how severe? |
| **ECG and troponin** | Anaemia can cause ischaemia in older patients |
| **Upper GI endoscopy within 24 hours** | The cause, the risk of rebleeding, and treatment in one test |
| **H. pylori test** | If an ulcer is found |

## 5. Management by cause

**Peptic ulcer**
1. **High-dose IV proton pump inhibitor** (before endoscopy in many protocols).
2. **Endoscopic therapy** (injection, clips, thermal) for high-risk ulcers.
3. **Test for and treat H. pylori**, **stop NSAIDs**, continue PPI for ulcer healing.
4. If endoscopy fails: **interventional radiology embolisation** or **surgery**.

**Variceal bleeding (an emergency with high mortality)**
1. **Resuscitate cautiously** (do not over-transfuse: it raises portal pressure and rebleeding).
2. **Vasoactive drug** (terlipressin or octreotide) started at once.
3. **Antibiotic prophylaxis** (such as ceftriaxone): reduces infection and death.
4. **Endoscopic band ligation** within 12 hours.
5. If bleeding is uncontrolled: **balloon tamponade** (Sengstaken-Blakemore tube) or **TIPS** as a bridge.
6. After the bleed: **non-selective beta-blocker (propranolol or carvedilol)** plus repeated band ligation to prevent rebleeding; treat the cirrhosis.

## 6. Where management can go wrong

- **Over-transfusion** in varices raises the portal pressure and bleeding.
- Giving a PPI does not replace endoscopy.
- **Not taking an NSAID or aspirin history** (including over-the-counter and herbal remedies).
- Missing **hepatic encephalopathy**: blood in the gut can trigger it, so treat with **lactulose**.

## Practice questions

**1.** A 55-year-old with cirrhosis has a large haematemesis and BP 80/50. Which drug should be started at once?
A. Oral omeprazole · **B. Terlipressin or octreotide, with antibiotic prophylaxis** · C. Ibuprofen · D. Warfarin · E. Propranolol only
*Answer: B.*

**2.** The best timing of endoscopy in a stable upper GI bleed is:
A. After a week · B. Only if bleeding recurs · **C. Within 24 hours** · D. Never · E. After a barium swallow
*Answer: C.*

**3.** A raised urea with a normal creatinine in a patient with melaena suggests:
A. Kidney failure · **B. An upper gastrointestinal bleed** · C. Dehydration only · D. Liver failure · E. A lower GI bleed
*Answer: B.*

**4.** After a variceal bleed, the best long-term prevention of rebleeding is:
A. Aspirin · B. NSAIDs · **C. A non-selective beta-blocker plus endoscopic band ligation** · D. Warfarin · E. Antacids
*Answer: C.*
