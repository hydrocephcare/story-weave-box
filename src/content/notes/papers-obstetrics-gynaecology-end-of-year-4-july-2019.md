### 1. All are correct EXCEPT. Pregnancy is associated with:
- A. Increased cardiac output
- B. Increased venous return
- C. Increased peripheral resistance
- D. Increased pulse rate
- E. Increased stroke volume

**Answer: C.** Vascular resistance falls under progesterone's vasodilating effect.

### 2. Lowered haemoglobin during normal pregnancy is a physiological finding. It is mainly due to:
- A. Low iron stores in all women
- B. Blood lost to the placenta
- C. Increased plasma volume
- D. Increased cardiac output causing greater red cell destruction
- E. Decreased reticulocytosis

**Answer: C.** Plasma expands more than red cell mass (haemodilution).

### 3. After birth, all of these vessels constrict EXCEPT:
- A. Ductus arteriosus
- B. Umbilical arteries
- C. Ductus venosus
- D. Hepatic portal vein
- E. Umbilical vein

**Answer: D.** The portal vein remains and carries blood to the liver. The others close and become ligaments.

### 4. During clinical pelvimetry, which is routinely measured?
- A. Bi-ischial (intertuberous) diameter
- B. Transverse diameter of the inlet
- C. Shape of the pubic arch
- D. Flare of the iliac crest
- E. Elasticity of the levator muscles

**Answer: A.** The diagnostic conjugate and the bituberous diameter are measured. The pubic arch is assessed rather than measured, and the transverse diameter of the inlet cannot be measured clinically.

### 5. The fetal head follows the pelvic axis. The axis is best described as:
- A. A straight line
- B. A curved line, first directed anteriorly then caudal
- C. A curved line, first directed posteriorly then anteriorly
- D. A curved line, first directed posteriorly then cephalic
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** The curve of Carus runs downward and backward, then downward and forward.

### 6. In a vertex presentation, the position is determined by the relationship of which fetal part to the mother's pelvis?
- A. Mentum
- B. Sacrum
- C. Acromion
- D. Occiput
- E. Sinciput

**Answer: D.** The denominator of a vertex presentation is the occiput (mentum for face, sacrum for breech).

### 7. Signs of placental separation after delivery include:
- A. Bleeding
- B. Change in uterine shape from discoid to globular
- C. Lengthening of the umbilical cord
- D. Presentation of the placenta at the cervical os
- E. All of the above

**Answer: E.**

### 8. The relationship of the long axis of the fetus to the long axis of the mother is called:
- A. Lie
- B. Presentation
- C. Position
- D. Attitude
- E. None of the above

**Answer: A.**

### 9. Which statement about episiotomy is FALSE?
- A. A median episiotomy is generally less painful than a mediolateral one
- B. A mediolateral episiotomy may be associated with more blood loss than a median one
- C. Indications include avoiding an imminent tear, forceps, breech delivery and premature infants
- D. The earlier the episiotomy is done, the more beneficial it is in speeding delivery
- E. Episiotomy incisions are repaired anatomically in layers

**Answer: D.** It is done at crowning, not early. Early incision only increases blood loss.

### 10. The heart rate of a normal term fetus:
- A. 80 to 100 bpm
- B. 100 to 120 bpm
- C. 120 to 160 bpm
- D. 160 to 180 bpm
- E. There is no baseline heart rate

**Answer: C.** The normal baseline is 110 to 160 bpm (120 to 160 in this paper's terms).

### 11. Repetitive late decelerations most commonly indicate:
- A. Fetal alkalosis
- B. Fetal hypoxia
- C. Fetal sleep state
- D. Fetal effects of maternal sedation
- E. Rapid cervical dilatation

**Answer: B.** They signal uteroplacental insufficiency.

### 12. The Bishop score includes all of the following EXCEPT:
- A. Dilatation of the cervix
- B. Position of the cervix
- C. The presenting part of the fetus
- D. Length of the cervix
- E. Consistency of the cervix

**Answer: C.** Bishop scores dilatation, effacement (length), consistency, position and **station** of the head. The question is awkwardly worded, and the item that is not a cervical finding is the presenting part itself.

### 13. The following are typical of the female bony pelvis EXCEPT:
- A. Transverse diameter of the inlet greater than the anteroposterior diameter
- B. Obstetric conjugate of 11 to 12 cm
- C. Funnel shaped
- D. Obtuse greater sciatic notch
- E. Pubic angle greater than 90 degrees

**Answer: C.** A funnel-shaped pelvis is a male (android) feature. The female pelvis is wide and shallow.

### 14. The main blood supply of the vulva is:
- A. Inferior haemorrhoidal artery
- B. Pudendal artery
- C. Ilioinguinal artery
- D. Femoral artery
- E. Inferior hypogastric artery

**Answer: B.** The internal pudendal artery (a branch of the internal iliac).

### 15. Regarding the fetal head, choose the CORRECT answer:
- A. Can be delivered vaginally in persistent mentoposterior position
- B. Shows Spalding's sign within 12 hours of intrauterine death
- C. Can be delivered vaginally in persistent brow presentation
- D. Is likely to be a vertex presentation when the head is deflexed
- E. Is considered engaged when the biparietal diameter passes the level of the pelvic inlet

**Answer: E.** Spalding's sign takes several days. Persistent mentoposterior and brow presentations cannot deliver vaginally.

### 16. The cilia of the fallopian tube function to:
- A. Remove the zona pellucida
- B. Transport the ovum towards the uterus
- C. Enhance rapid division of the zygote
- D. Transport the ovum towards the peritoneal cavity
- E. Have a bactericidal function

**Answer: B.**

### 17. Glycogen is seen in the lumina of endometrial glands:
- A. During the luteal phase
- B. During pregnancy only
- C. During pre- and post-ovulatory phases
- D. During the proliferative phase only

**Answer: A.** Secretory (luteal) glands are glycogen-rich, and glycogen persists in early pregnancy.

### 18. Monilial vaginitis occurs frequently in pregnancy because:
- A. Glycosuria is commoner
- B. The vagina contains more glycogen
- C. Higher vaginal acidity suppresses other organisms
- D. All of the above
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Glycosuria, glycogen-rich epithelium and acidity all favour Candida.

### 19. Clinical findings of PCOS (PCOD) include all EXCEPT:
- A. Obesity
- B. Oligomenorrhoea
- C. Infertility
- D. Tall stature

**Answer: D.**

### 20. The commonest cause of death in cancer of the cervix is:
- A. Infection
- B. Uraemia
- C. Haemorrhage
- D. Cachexia
- E. Distant metastasis

**Answer: B.** Local spread obstructs the ureters, causing hydronephrosis and renal failure.

### 21. HPV types ____ account for about 70% of cervical cancers:

- A. The show

### 25. Poor prognostic factors for malignant change in trophoblastic disease include:
- A. Disease following a normal delivery
- B. Beta-hCG above 80,000 mIU/ml
- C. Disease following an abortion
- D. A and C
- E. A and B

**Answer: E.** Antecedent term pregnancy and high hCG are adverse features. Following an abortion is more favourable.

### 26. True of oral contraceptive pills:
- A. They decrease the risk of ovarian cancer
- B. They are contraindicated in parous women with endometriosis
- C. They are contraindicated in young nulliparous girls
- D. All of the above
- E. None of the above

**Answer: A.** Endometriosis is an indication, and young nulliparous women can use them.

### 27. True about vesicovaginal fistula (VVF):
- A. Should be repaired at least 2 months after delivery
- B. Surgical repair is the only mode of treatment
- C. Amenorrhoea is a very common finding
- D. The commonest cause in Kenya is surgery
- E. The diagnosis is by direct inspection of the anterior vaginal wall with a Sims speculum

**Answer: A.** Repair waits until oedema and slough settle (about 2 to 3 months). A small fresh fistula can be treated with continuous catheter drainage, so B is wrong. Obstructed labour is the leading cause.

### 28. The most common cause of male factor infertility is:
- A. Cryptorchidism
- B. Testicular failure
- C. Obstruction
- D. Varicocele
- E. Impotence

**Answer: D.**

### 29. The most common site of endometriosis is:
- A. The pouch of Douglas
- B. The ovary
- C. The posterior surface of the uterus
- D. The broad ligament
- E. The pelvic peritoneum

**Answer: B.**

### 30. A classical caesarean section is:
- A. A vertical incision in the upper uterine segment
- B. A vertical incision in the lower uterine segment
- C. A vertical incision from the upper to the lower segment
- D. A transverse incision in the lower segment
- E. None of the above

**Answer: A.** A vertical incision into the contractile upper segment. It is used for placenta praevia, a poorly formed lower segment or a transverse back-down lie, and it needs repeat caesarean in future pregnancies.

### 31. Regimens recommended by the Ministry of Health for simple malaria in pregnancy:
- A. Oral quinine
- B. Fansidar
- C. Artemisinin
- D. Artemether-lumefantrine
- E. Parenteral chloroquine

**Answer: A and D.** Oral quinine (with clindamycin) in the first trimester, and artemether-lumefantrine in the second and third trimesters.

### 32. The following favours mother-to-child transmission of HIV:
- A. HIV type 2
- B. HIV type 1
- C. High CD4 count
- D. Seroconversion in pregnancy
- E. HAART

**Answer: D.** Seroconversion gives a very high viral load. HAART lowers transmission.

### 33. Nausea during the first cycle of pills. Select the most appropriate response:
- A. Stop pills and resume after 7 days
- B. Continue pills as usual
- C. Continue pills and use an additional contraceptive
- D. Take an additional pill
- E. Stop pills and seek a medical examination

**Answer: B.** Nausea is common and settles within 1 to 3 months. Taking the pill with food or at night helps.

### 34. A gravida 6 para 4+1 admitted with severe pre-eclampsia. True:
- A. After control of blood pressure she should have a caesarean section as the quickest mode of delivery
- B. Her blood vessels show an abnormal reaction to vasopressor agents
- C. A bleeding profile is part of the work-up to diagnose intravascular coagulopathy
- D. B and C
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Vessels are hypersensitive to pressors, and clotting is checked for DIC and HELLP. Vaginal delivery is preferred if conditions allow.

### 35. About renal changes in pregnancy, the following are true EXCEPT:
- A. GFR increases by 50%
- B. Renal plasma flow increases by 50%
- C. Oestrogens are responsible for general ureteric relaxation
- D. There is decreased predisposition to urinary tract infections
- E. There is increased creatinine clearance

**Answer: D.** Predisposition to UTI increases because of ureteric dilatation and urinary stasis (the relaxation is mainly progesterone's effect).

### 36. Indications for medical (methotrexate) treatment of ectopic pregnancy include the following EXCEPT:
- A. Absence of cardiac activity
- B. Beta-hCG below 5000 mIU/ml
- C. Unruptured ectopic
- D. An ectopic greater than 3.5 cm
- E. An ectopic less than 3.5 cm

**Answer: D.**

### 37. Active management of the third stage of labour (AMTSL) involves:
- A. A balloon tamponade to enhance involution
- B. Delivery of the placenta by controlled cord traction with counter-traction over the suprapubic area
- C. Monitoring BP, pulse, GCS and vaginal bleeding every 20 minutes for one hour
- D. Pelvic floor exercises
- E. Administration of 10 IU oxytocin IM on the anterior thigh within 10 minutes of delivery of the baby

**Answer: E.** Oxytocin 10 IU IM within one minute of birth is the key step (the paper says 10 minutes). Controlled cord traction (B) is also a component of AMTSL, so both B and E are standard components. The best single answer is E.

### 38. A 23-year-old at 33 weeks has a headache, BP 166/112 mmHg and proteinuria ++. The most adequate management:
- A. Hydralazine 5 mg IV every 15 min plus MgSO4 4 g IM
- B. Hydralazine 5 mg IV every 30 min until BP is under 160/100 plus MgSO4, dexamethasone 24 mg within 24 hours and induction of labour after this time
- C. Hydralazine, MgSO4, dexamethasone, then conservative management after BP control
- D. BP control and emergency caesarean section
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Severe pre-eclampsia with a symptom: control BP, give magnesium sulphate, give antenatal steroids for fetal lungs, then deliver within 24 to 48 hours.

### 39. A 25-year-old at 32 weeks is pale, dehydrated, pulse 120, BP 90/60, fundal height 36 cm, hard tender uterus, no fetal heart, scanty blood PV, pink cervix. Most likely diagnosis:
- A. Placental abruption
- B. Placenta praevia type IV
- C. Cervical carcinoma
- D. Severe placental abruption with IUFD and DIC
- E. Vasa praevia with IUFD

**Answer: D.** Shock out of proportion to visible bleeding, a woody hard uterus and a dead fetus point to concealed severe abruption.

### 40. Regarding her management:
- A. Two peripheral lines, FBC, clotting profile, transfusion and emergency caesarean
- B. Immediate induction of labour using a Foley catheter
- C. General measures for all APH, AROM, correction of DIC and emergency caesarean
- D. General measures for all APH, AROM, correction of DIC and induction of labour
- E. General measures for all APH, AROM, correction of shock and DIC and induction of labour

**Answer: E.** Resuscitate and correct shock and coagulopathy first, rupture the membranes and deliver vaginally since the fetus is dead. Caesarean is reserved for maternal indications.

### 41. A 22-year-old has severe right inguinal and right shoulder pain, tenderness, minimal vaginal bleeding, free intra-abdominal fluid, hCG 1600, BP 90/42, pulse 105. Next best step:
- A. Pelvic MRI
- B. Abdominal X-ray
- C. IV fluids and monitor
- D. Exploratory laparotomy
- E. Administer methotrexate

**Answer: D.** A ruptured ectopic with haemodynamic instability needs resuscitation and emergency surgery.

### 42. Risk factors for puerperal infection EXCEPT:

- B. Prolonged labour or ruptured membranes

### 43. Among the commonest causative organisms of puerperal infection, the following are anaerobes EXCEPT:
- A. Klebsiella
- B. Peptococcus species
- C. Peptostreptococcus
- D. Bacteroides fragilis
- E. Proteus mirabilis

**Answer: A.** Klebsiella is an aerobic Gram-negative rod. The question is flawed because Proteus is also an aerobic coliform.

### 44. The following affect wound healing EXCEPT:
- A. Steroid therapy
- B. Proper apposition of layers
- C. Immune status
- D. Infection
- E. Cancer

**Answer: B.** Good apposition promotes healing, while steroids, immunosuppression, infection and cancer impair it. The question is loosely worded.

### 45. A physiological change in the puerperium:
- A. Maternal heart rate reduced by 20 to 30 beats per minute
- B. Endometrium is physiologically restored within 15 days
- C. Increased water retention
- D. Oedema reabsorption
- E. Foul-smelling vaginal discharge

**Answer: D.** Diuresis removes the excess pregnancy fluid. Foul lochia indicates infection.

### 48. True about the partograph:
- A. Started at 3 cm cervical dilatation
- B. Baseline fetal heart rate of 110 to 160 beats per minute
- C. Always deliver by caesarean section when the patient reaches the action line
- D. The alert line means do a caesarean section
- E. Ruptured membranes cannot be done

**Answer: B.** The modified WHO partograph starts in the active phase at 4 cm. The action line prompts reassessment, not automatic surgery.

### 49. Softening of the cervix in the second month of pregnancy is known as:
- A. Hegar's sign
- B. Braxton Hicks sign
- C. Goodell's sign
- D. Chadwick's sign
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** Hegar's is softening of the isthmus, and Chadwick's is bluish discolouration of the vagina and cervix.

### 50. In cervical incompetence, which is true?
- A. The diagnosis is made only after an abortion occurs
- B. It is a habitual mid-trimester abortion
- C. Rupture of membranes is not a feature
- D. The only treatment is a cerclage

**Answer: B.** It causes recurrent painless mid-trimester loss. It can be diagnosed on history or by ultrasound cervical length, membranes may rupture, and treatment also includes progesterone and pessaries.

### 51. Using Naegele's rule, the EDD for an LMP of 10 March:
- A. December 3
- B. December 7
- C. December 10
- D. December 17
- E. November 17

**Answer: D.** Add 7 days and 9 months: 10 March becomes 17 December.

### 52. Correct about menopause:
- A. Progesterone levels rise
- B. The pituitary stops secreting LH
- C. LH levels rise
- D. The number of oocytes in the ovary remains constant until menopause
- E. Androgen levels remain unchanged

**Answer: C.** Loss of oestradiol and inhibin feedback raises FSH and LH.

### 62. The most appropriate statement on oestrogen replacement therapy:
- A. It causes ulceration of the vaginal wall
- B. It can cause carcinoma of the vaginal wall
- C. It can help improve mild vaginal prolapse
- D. It can cause dyspareunia

**Answer: C.** It improves tissue quality and symptoms. It treats, not causes, atrophic dyspareunia.

### 63. Regarding bladder innervation:
- A. The sympathetic system is active during bladder emptying
- B. The parasympathetic system is active during bladder filling
- C. The pelvic nerve is involved in bladder emptying
- D. The hypogastric nerve is involved in bladder emptying

**Answer: C.** The pelvic splanchnic (parasympathetic, S2 to S4) nerves cause detrusor contraction. The hypogastric (sympathetic) nerve aids storage.

### 64. In the Baden-Walker classification, a third-degree uterine prolapse is characterised by:
- A. Descent up to the hymen
- B. Descent below the ischial spines
- C. Descent beyond the introitus
- D. Minimal descent

**Answer: C.** Grade 1 is halfway to the hymen, grade 2 reaches the hymen, grade 3 goes beyond the hymen and grade 4 is complete eversion.

### 66. In the medical treatment of menorrhagia, which is true?
- A. Danazol is not associated with serious side effects
- B. GnRH analogues are useful in long-term treatment of menorrhagia
- C. Combined oral contraceptives can be used
- D. GnRH analogues are the treatment of choice

**Answer: C.** Danazol has androgenic side effects, and GnRH analogues are limited to 6 months by bone loss. First-line options are the levonorgestrel IUS, tranexamic acid, NSAIDs and combined pills.
