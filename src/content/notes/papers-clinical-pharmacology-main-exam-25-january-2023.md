## Section A: Multiple choice questions

### 1. A non-sympathomimetic drug given by aerosol that most likely promotes bronchodilation with few systemic effects:
- A. Salmeterol
- B. Atropine
- C. Isoproterenol
- D. Ipratropium bromide

**Answer: D.** Ipratropium is an inhaled antimuscarinic with poor systemic absorption.

### 2. Effective in asthma but most likely to cause tachycardia and arrhythmias:
- A. Salmeterol
- B. Atropine
- C. Isoproterenol
- D. Ipratropium bromide

**Answer: C.** Isoproterenol is a non-selective beta agonist that stimulates cardiac beta-1 receptors.

### 3. Which class is not primary therapy of asthma and is used as an alternative when first-line regimens fail to control it?
- A. SABAs
- B. ICSs
- C. Leukotriene modifiers
- D. LABAs

**Answer: C.**

### 4. What drug therapy should be available to ALL asthma patients (intermittent and persistent)?
- A. Low-dose ICS
- B. PRN SABA
- C. LABA
- D. Leukotriene antagonist

**Answer: B.** A reliever (short-acting beta agonist as needed) is for every patient.

### 5. Dextromethorphan is an:
- A. Analgesic
- B. Antitussive
- C. Expectorant
- D. Antihistaminic

**Answer: B.** A non-opioid cough suppressant.

### 6. Codeine is used clinically as:
- A. Analgesic
- B. Antitussive
- C. Antidiarrhoeal
- D. All of the above

**Answer: D.**

### 7. Which agent suppresses both B and T lymphocytes by inhibiting de novo purine synthesis?
- A. Methotrexate
- B. Mycophenolate mofetil
- C. Prednisone
- D. Tacrolimus

**Answer: B.** Mycophenolate inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase. Lymphocytes depend on de novo purine synthesis.

### 8. Regarding local anaesthetic agents:
- A. Lignocaine is also a Vaughan Williams group 1A antiarrhythmic
- B. At normal tissue pH the larger fraction of local anaesthetic is the uncharged form
- C. The duration of action of procaine is increased in liver disease
- D. Local anaesthetics block conduction in small myelinated axons before other axons

**Answer: D.** Lignocaine is class 1B. Local anaesthetics are weak bases, so most is ionised at tissue pH. Procaine is cleared by plasma esterases.

### 9. The skeletal muscle relaxant with the longest duration of action:
- A. Suxamethonium
- B. Mivacurium
- C. Vecuronium
- D. Pancuronium

**Answer: D.**

### 10. Regarding nondepolarising muscle relaxants:
- A. Jaw and eye muscles are paralysed before limb and trunk muscles
- B. Rocuronium is the most potent
- C. Atracurium is a steroid derivative
- D. Vecuronium blocks cardiac muscarinic receptors and increases heart rate

**Answer: A.** Small fast muscles go first and the diaphragm last. Rocuronium has low potency, atracurium is a benzylisoquinolinium, and pancuronium (not vecuronium) is the vagolytic.

## Section B: Short questions (5 marks each)

### 1. Three main non-lipid (pleiotropic) effects of statins
1. **Improved endothelial function** (more nitric oxide).
2. **Anti-inflammatory action** (lower CRP) and **plaque stabilisation.**
3. **Antithrombotic and antioxidant effects** (less platelet aggregation and tissue factor, less LDL oxidation). Others: immunomodulation and reduced smooth muscle proliferation.

### 2. Mechanism of action of fibrinolytics
They are **plasminogen activators** (alteplase, tenecteplase, streptokinase). They convert plasminogen to **plasmin,** which digests fibrin and dissolves the clot. Alteplase and tenecteplase are fibrin-selective, while streptokinase forms a complex that activates plasminogen. Used in STEMI, ischaemic stroke and massive PE. The main risk is bleeding.

### 3. One drug class least likely to cause orthostatic hypotension, and why
**ACE inhibitors (or ARBs).** They lower pressure by reducing angiotensin II and aldosterone and by arteriolar vasodilatation, but they do not block sympathetic baroreflex vasoconstriction or cause pooling in the veins, so standing pressure is maintained. (Dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers act mainly on arteries and are similar.) Alpha-blockers, centrally acting agents and ganglion blockers cause it.

### 4. Justify each drug
- **(a) Furosemide in CHF:** a loop diuretic that blocks the Na-K-2Cl cotransporter in the thick ascending limb, causing natriuresis. It reduces preload and congestion and relieves breathlessness and oedema. It treats symptoms but does not improve survival.
- **(b) Amiodarone in AF:** a class III antiarrhythmic that blocks potassium channels (plus sodium, calcium and beta effects), prolonging the action potential and refractoriness. It maintains sinus rhythm and controls rate, and it is safe in LV dysfunction. Monitor thyroid, liver, lung and eyes.
- **(c) Clopidogrel in coronary heart disease:** an irreversible P2Y12 (ADP receptor) antagonist, a prodrug activated by CYP2C19. It reduces platelet aggregation and prevents thrombosis after ACS or stenting, or when aspirin is not tolerated.
- **(d) Tacrolimus in transplant:** a calcineurin inhibitor that binds FKBP12, blocks NFAT activation and so reduces IL-2 production and T-cell activation. It prevents rejection. It has a narrow therapeutic index, so trough levels are monitored. Toxicity: nephrotoxicity, diabetes and tremor.

### 5. Omeprazole versus ranitidine in acid reflux
- **Omeprazole** is a proton pump inhibitor that irreversibly blocks the H+/K+ ATPase, the final step of acid secretion. It gives strong, prolonged suppression (over 90%), once daily, and heals oesophagitis best.
- **Ranitidine** is an H2 receptor antagonist that competitively blocks only the histamine pathway. It is less effective, works mainly on nocturnal and basal acid, and tolerance develops.

### 6. Warfarin versus enoxaparin
- **Warfarin:** oral vitamin K antagonist (blocks VKORC1) that reduces factors II, VII, IX and X. Onset takes 3 to 5 days, it needs INR monitoring, interacts with many drugs and is teratogenic.
- **Enoxaparin:** low molecular weight heparin, given **subcutaneously.** It acts through antithrombin to inhibit factor Xa. Onset is immediate, the dose is predictable without routine monitoring, and it is safe in pregnancy.

### 7. Beta blockers in angina versus arrhythmia
- **Angina:** they reduce myocardial oxygen demand (lower heart rate, contractility and afterload) and lengthen diastolic filling, so they prevent ischaemia and relieve symptoms.
- **Arrhythmia:** as class II agents they block sympathetic effects on the SA and AV nodes, lowering automaticity and slowing AV conduction. They control the ventricular rate in AF and SVT and suppress catecholamine-driven ectopy.

### 8. The four main classes affecting the RAAS
1. **ACE inhibitors** (captopril, lisinopril)
2. **Angiotensin receptor blockers** (losartan, valsartan)
3. **Direct renin inhibitors** (aliskiren)
4. **Aldosterone (mineralocorticoid receptor) antagonists** (spironolactone, eplerenone)

### 9. Sodium and calcium channel blockers as antiepileptics
- **Sodium channel blockers** (phenytoin, carbamazepine, lamotrigine) bind the inactivated state of voltage-gated sodium channels in a use-dependent way. This limits high-frequency repetitive firing and stops seizure spread.
- **Calcium channel blockers:** ethosuximide blocks **T-type calcium channels** in thalamic neurons, which controls absence seizures. Gabapentin and pregabalin act on the alpha-2-delta subunit and reduce glutamate release.

### 10. Lithium: main indication, adjunct and why
- **Main indication:** long-term prophylaxis of bipolar disorder and treatment of acute mania (it also reduces suicide risk).
- **Adjunct:** a **benzodiazepine** (lorazepam, clonazepam) or an **antipsychotic** (olanzapine, haloperidol, risperidone, quetiapine).
- **Why:** lithium takes one to two weeks to work, so the adjunct controls agitation, insomnia and psychosis in the meantime.

### 11. Main limitation of antipsychotics in schizophrenia
They treat **positive symptoms** (hallucinations, delusions) well but do little for **negative and cognitive symptoms.** About a third of patients are treatment-resistant (they need clozapine), and side effects (extrapyramidal, metabolic, prolactin, QT) limit adherence.

### 12. Three ways of achieving immunosuppression for a transplant
1. **Calcineurin inhibitors:** tacrolimus or ciclosporin (block T-cell activation).
2. **Antiproliferatives:** mycophenolate or azathioprine (block lymphocyte proliferation).
3. **Corticosteroids** (prednisolone), plus induction with antithymocyte globulin or basiliximab, and mTOR inhibitors (sirolimus, everolimus).

### 13. Therapeutic role of oral contraceptives
- **Contraception:** suppress ovulation (by negative feedback on FSH and LH), thicken cervical mucus and thin the endometrium.
- **Non-contraceptive uses:** treat dysmenorrhoea, menorrhagia, endometriosis, acne and hirsutism (PCOS), and premenstrual syndrome. They reduce ovarian and endometrial cancer risk.

### 14. Metformin, alendronate and levothyroxine
- **Metformin:** type 2 diabetes. It lowers hepatic glucose output and raises insulin sensitivity (AMPK).
- **Alendronate:** osteoporosis (often postmenopausal). A bisphosphonate that inhibits osteoclasts and reduces fracture risk.
- **Levothyroxine:** hypothyroidism. Replaces thyroxine.

## Section C: Case questions (answer any one)

### Question 1. A 32-year-old woman with schizophrenia and type 1 diabetes has gained 6 kg and her glucose is high. On olanzapine 15 mg, Insuman Comb 25 insulin, naproxen and omeprazole. Fasting glucose 8 mmol/L, HbA1c 75 mmol/mol, BMI 26.

**(a) Which medicine is contributing to the weight gain and poor diabetes control? (4 marks)**
**Olanzapine.** It blocks histamine H1 and serotonin 5-HT2C receptors, which increases appetite, and it causes insulin resistance, hyperglycaemia and dyslipidaemia. Naproxen and omeprazole do not explain the weight gain or glycaemia.

**(b) Antipsychotics least likely to cause extrapyramidal side effects (2 marks)**
**Second-generation (atypical) drugs:** clozapine, quetiapine, olanzapine and aripiprazole, and low-potency first-generation drugs such as chlorpromazine. High-potency drugs (haloperidol, trifluoperazine) cause the most.

**(c) Aripiprazole or olanzapine, and how to switch (2 marks)**
**Aripiprazole.** It is a dopamine D2 partial agonist with little effect on weight and glucose. Switch by **cross-titration:** start aripiprazole (10 to 15 mg) and reduce olanzapine gradually over 2 to 4 weeks, watching for relapse and akathisia.

**(d) Assessment of her diabetes (2 marks)**
Control is poor: HbA1c 75 mmol/mol (target 48 to 59) and fasting glucose 8 (target 4 to 7). The twice-daily premixed insulin is not matching her needs and olanzapine adds to insulin resistance. Review the antipsychotic, optimise insulin (consider a basal-bolus regimen), reinforce diet and activity, and monitor lipids and weight.

### Question 2. Mrs A, 52, adriamycin-induced cardiomyopathy and chronic heart failure on captopril, bisoprolol, furosemide and spironolactone

**(a) Statin? (3 marks)**
**No.** Her heart failure is not ischaemic, and statins have not shown a survival benefit in non-ischaemic heart failure. With poor liver function they carry a hepatotoxicity risk, so the harm outweighs the benefit.

**(b) BP 90/55, pulse 80, asymptomatic, no postural drop. Adjust the antihypertensives? (1 mark)**
**No.** Asymptomatic low blood pressure in heart failure does not need a dose reduction. The drugs improve survival, so continue them and review if symptoms appear.

**(c) Ankle oedema and breathlessness at Christmas: management (2 marks)**
Reduce salt, and **increase the loop diuretic** (furosemide, IV if severe). If needed add a thiazide (metolazone) for sequential nephron blockade. Check urea, electrolytes and weight daily.

**(d) Main limitation of dihydropyridine CCBs here (1 mark)**
They give **no survival benefit** in heart failure and cause **reflex sympathetic activation and ankle oedema.**

**(e) Digoxin: why, and its limitation compared with ACE inhibitors (1 mark)**
It inhibits the Na+/K+ ATPase, raising intracellular sodium and calcium (via the Na/Ca exchanger), which gives a **positive inotropic effect,** and it slows AV conduction. Its limitation is that it **does not improve survival** (it only reduces hospital admissions) and has a **narrow therapeutic index** (toxicity, worse with hypokalaemia).

**(f) Concerns with an opioid or a skeletal muscle relaxant for severe back pain (2 marks)**
- **Opioids:** hypotension (histamine release), sedation, respiratory depression and constipation (straining). She is already hypotensive.
- **Muscle relaxants:** hypotension, sedation and, with tricyclic-like agents, arrhythmia.
- Use paracetamol first, avoid NSAIDs (fluid retention and kidney injury with ACE inhibitor and diuretic), and use the lowest dose if an opioid is unavoidable.

### Question 3. A 74-year-old woman, fatigue, irregular pulse 120, atrial fibrillation, EF 20% with global hypokinesis (earlier 40%), on lisinopril, metoprolol XL and aspirin

**(a) Medications to control the rate acutely (2 marks)**
**Beta-blocker** (metoprolol, with care because of low EF and low BP), **digoxin,** or **IV amiodarone.** Avoid verapamil and diltiazem in reduced ejection fraction.

**(b) What "restoring sinus rhythm" and "rate control" mean (2 marks)**
- **Restoring sinus rhythm (rhythm control):** converting AF back to normal rhythm by electrical (DC) or drug cardioversion and then keeping it with antiarrhythmic drugs.
- **Rate control:** accepting AF and keeping the resting ventricular rate below about 110 per minute with AV-nodal drugs.

**(c) Which strategy and why (2 marks)**
**Rhythm control.** Her EF has fallen from 40% to 20% with a fast rate and no other cause, which suggests **tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy** that can recover if sinus rhythm is restored. Rate control was also limited by hypotension.

**(d) Anticoagulation (2 marks)**
CHA2DS2-VASc is at least 4 (age 65 to 74, female, hypertension, LV dysfunction), so anticoagulation is needed to prevent stroke from left atrial thrombus, and before and for 4 weeks after cardioversion.
- **Warfarin:** vitamin K antagonist, INR monitoring (target 2 to 3), many interactions, slow onset, reversed by vitamin K.
- **Dabigatran:** direct thrombin inhibitor, fixed dose, no INR monitoring, fewer interactions, mostly renal clearance, reversed by idarucizumab.

**(e) Four antiarrhythmic choices in LV dysfunction without heart failure (2 marks)**
1. **Amiodarone** (the usual first choice)
2. **Dofetilide**
3. **Sotalol** (with caution because of QT prolongation and negative inotropy)
4. **Dronedarone** (only if heart failure is not decompensated)

Class IC drugs (flecainide, propafenone) are contraindicated in structural heart disease.
