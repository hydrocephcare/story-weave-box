# Physiological changes in pregnancy

*From the Department's lecture slides, rewritten as a system-by-system table with the clinical meaning of each change. Know what is normal first, so you can spot what is not.*

**The reasoning chain:** a hormone-driven, hyperdynamic state starts at conception and touches every organ → each change helps the mother carry the baby and prepare for blood loss at delivery → but each can also **mimic disease** (a murmur, a low haemoglobin, a glycosuria) or **change how drugs behave**. Resolve it by asking: is this the expected change, and is it in the expected range?

## 1. Why it matters on the ward

- Changes **begin after conception** and affect every system; most **resolve after delivery** with little residual effect in an uncomplicated pregnancy.
- They can **confuse the examination** (a systolic murmur, a displaced apex, pigmentation) and **laboratory results** (a lower haemoglobin, a higher ESR, a raised ALP), so you need **pregnancy-specific reference ranges**.
- They alter **absorption, distribution, metabolism and elimination** of drugs: more volume of distribution, faster kidney clearance, different liver handling.

## 2. Skin

- **Striae gravidarum (stretch marks)**: Stretching in genetically predisposed women: abdomen, breasts, thighs, buttocks
- **Linea nigra**: Pigmented line from the pubis to the umbilicus (MSH and oestrogen)
- **Chloasma (melasma)**: Blotchy facial pigmentation
- **Spider naevi and palmar erythema**: Raised oestrogen and skin blood flow; normal
- **Chadwick's sign**: Bluish discolouration of vagina and cervix

## 3. Cardiovascular system: a hyperkinetic state

- **Blood pressure**: Falls early; the **diastolic falls more (up to 15 mmHg)**; lowest at **24–28 weeks**; rises towards term but not above pre-pregnancy values — **A raised BP is never normal**: think pre-eclampsia
- **Plasma volume**: **Rises up to 50%** (most by 30 weeks; more in twins) — A cushion against blood loss at delivery
- **Cardiac output**: **Rises up to 50%** (most by 20 weeks) — Heart rate rises about **20 beats/min** by the third trimester and **stroke volume by about 30%**
- **Systemic vascular resistance**: Falls about **30%** (lowest at 20 weeks) — Improves blood flow to the uterus and placenta
- **Central venous pressure**: Unchanged
- **Femoral venous pressure**: **Rises two- to threefold** by 30 weeks — **Leg oedema, varicose veins and haemorrhoids**
- **Position**: Cardiac output is **lowest supine** (the uterus compresses the inferior vena cava) and **highest in left lateral** — **Supine hypotensive syndrome**: examine and rest the woman **tilted to the left**

**The heart** is enlarged and pushed up and to the left, so **ECG and chest X-ray changes** (left axis deviation, flattened T waves, a larger heart shadow) can look pathological.

**Murmurs:** a **systolic ejection murmur** along the left sternal border (increased output through the aortic and pulmonary valves), a **third heart sound** and a mammary soufflé are **normal**. **Diastolic murmurs are never normal in pregnancy**: investigate them (**rheumatic mitral stenosis** becomes dangerous in pregnancy because the heart rate and volume load rise).

## 4. Blood

- **Red cell mass**: Rises about **30%** — More oxygen carrying capacity
- **Haemoglobin and haematocrit**: **Fall about 15%**, lowest at **28–30 weeks** — **Physiological (dilutional) anaemia**: plasma rises more than red cells. The WHO cut-off for **anaemia in pregnancy is 11 g/dL** (10.5 g/dL in the second trimester)
- **White cells**: Rise progressively; up to **16,000/mm³** in the third trimester (and far higher in labour) — Do not over-read a mild leucocytosis
- **ESR**: **Rises** (more globulins and fibrinogen) — Useless as an infection marker in pregnancy
- **Platelets**: The range is **unchanged** (can fall slightly: gestational thrombocytopenia)
- **Clotting**: **Factors V, VII, VIII, IX, X, XII, von Willebrand factor and fibrinogen (doubles) rise**; **antithrombin III and protein S fall**; fibrinolysis is reduced — A **hypercoagulable state**: protects against postpartum haemorrhage but raises the risk of **venous thromboembolism**
- **Immune system**: T and B cell counts unchanged, **function suppressed** — More susceptible to **malaria, viral infections, listeria and TB reactivation**

**Iron:** needs rise (about 1 g over pregnancy) and gastric acidity falls, so **iron deficiency is common**; supplement **iron and folic acid** routinely.

## 5. Respiratory system

The **diaphragm rises about 4 cm** and the **chest widens**; the **subcostal angle** broadens. **Tidal volume rises about 40%** and **minute ventilation about 40%**; **residual volume falls**; **vital capacity** and **FEV1 are unchanged**. The woman **breathes faster in volume (not rate) and has a mild respiratory alkalosis** (**PaCO₂ about 30 mmHg**, bicarbonate lower at about 20 mmol/L). Many women feel **breathless** (**physiological dyspnoea**) with no disease. **Oxygen consumption rises about 20%.** A PaCO₂ of **40 mmHg is abnormal in pregnancy** and means respiratory failure is near.

## 6. Gastrointestinal system and liver

- **Ptyalism, altered taste, increased appetite and thirst**: Hormonal — Small frequent snacks help
- **Nausea and vomiting (about 50%)**: hCG and oestrogen — **Hyperemesis gravidarum** is the severe form
- **Heartburn (reflux)**: **Relaxed lower oesophageal sphincter** (progesterone, relaxin) and a pressing uterus — Antacids, small meals, head-up sleeping
- **Constipation**: Reduced motility (**progesterone**) and more water absorption — Fibre and fluids
- **Reduced gastric acidity**: Interferes with iron absorption
- **Gallbladder**: Larger, empties slowly; **cholestasis is almost physiological** — **Gallstones and intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy**
- **Liver tests**: **Albumin falls**, **ALP rises (placental)**, globulins, fibrinogen and binding proteins rise; **ALT and AST stay normal** — Raised ALT/AST is abnormal: **HELLP, fatty liver, hepatitis**

## 7. Urinary system

The **kidneys enlarge slightly**; the **renal pelvis and ureters dilate** (more on the right) from progesterone relaxing smooth muscle and from pressure at the pelvic brim. **Renal plasma flow rises about 50–80% and GFR about 50%**, so **serum creatinine falls** (a **creatinine above 75 µmol/L (0.8 mg/dL) should raise concern**) and **urea falls**. **Glycosuria** (reduced tubular reabsorption) and mild **proteinuria** (up to 300 mg/day) can be normal. **Frequency and nocturia** are common; **urinary stasis explains the high risk of pyelonephritis**, so **screen for asymptomatic bacteriuria**.

## 8. Endocrine system

- **Pituitary**: Anterior pituitary **enlarges** (prolactin rises); oxytocin released in labour
- **Thyroid**: Enlarges slightly (a "physiological goitre"); **thyroid-binding globulin doubles**, so **total T3 and T4 rise but free T3 and T4 do not**; most women are euthyroid; hCG can mildly stimulate the thyroid in early pregnancy
- **Parathyroid**: More active to meet calcium needs
- **Adrenal**: **Total cortisol rises; free cortisol is unchanged**; aldosterone rises (salt and water retention)
- **Pancreas**: **Insulin resistance** rises in the second and third trimesters (**human placental lactogen**), the basis of **gestational diabetes**

### Placental hormones

![The rise of hCG, progesterone and other pregnancy hormones through pregnancy. Osmosis, CC BY-SA 4.0](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/Pregnancy_hormone_graph.png/960px-Pregnancy_hormone_graph.png)

- **hCG:** made by the trophoblast; **peaks at 8–12 weeks**; keeps the **corpus luteum** producing progesterone until the placenta takes over (about 8–10 weeks).
- **Progesterone:** first from the corpus luteum, then the **placenta** (output about 250 mg/day at term). It **relaxes smooth muscle** (less gut, bladder and ureteric tone: constipation, reflux, urinary stasis), **dilates veins**, **lowers diastolic pressure**, **raises body temperature** and drives **nausea**; it **maintains the pregnancy** and prevents uterine contractions.
- **Oestrogens:** from the ovary early, then the **placenta** (oestriol about 85%, rising up to term; **oestriol needs the fetal adrenal and liver**). They **grow the uterus**, **develop the breasts** (with progesterone), **soften connective tissue**, cause **water and sodium retention**.
- **Human placental lactogen (hPL):** **anti-insulin**, shifts energy to the fetus.
- **Relaxin:** loosens pelvic ligaments.

## 9. Reproductive system

- **Uterus**: **Hypertrophy and hyperplasia.** **Length rises from 7.5 cm to about 35 cm** and **weight from about 50 g to 1,000 g** at term; capacity from about 10 mL to 5 L. **Dextrorotation** (tilted and twisted to the right in about 80%). **Lower uterine segment** forms from the **isthmus** from about the **fourth month** and reaches about **10 cm at term**
- **Cervix**: **Softens (Goodell's sign), oedematous and congested**; glands multiply and secrete a thick **mucus plug (operculum)** that closes the canal
- **Vagina and vulva**: **Increased vascularity**, soft, moist, bluish (**Chadwick's sign**); **varicosities**; increased discharge with lower pH
- **Ovary**: One contains the **corpus luteum of pregnancy**; **no ovulation** during pregnancy
- **Pelvic ligaments and joints**: **Relax** (relaxin), so the pelvis becomes more mobile and the capacity increases; **backache and pubic pain**
- **Breasts**: **Increased size, vascularity, warmth and tenderness**; **darker nipples and areola**, **secondary areola**, **Montgomery's tubercles**; **colostrum** by the end of the third month

## 10. Metabolism, weight and the musculoskeletal system

- **Weight gain** averages **10–12 kg** (about **0.4 kg a week** in the second and third trimester); **more than 1 kg a week is abnormal** (fluid retention).
- **Basal metabolic rate** rises about **15–20%**; **calorie needs rise about 300 kcal a day** in the second and third trimester.
- **Fasting glucose falls** (the fetus uses it), **post-meal glucose rises**; **cholesterol and triglycerides rise**.
- The **centre of gravity shifts**: **lordosis**, **backache**, a waddling gait.

## 11. Clinical pearls

1. **Hb of 10.5–11 g/dL at 30 weeks may be dilutional**; do iron studies and treat deficiency, but do not transfuse for physiological anaemia.
2. **A diastolic murmur is always abnormal.** A soft systolic murmur is normal.
3. **BP of 130/80 or more in the second trimester** is not "normal for pregnancy": check urine protein and think pre-eclampsia.
4. **A normal pregnancy PaCO₂ is about 30 mmHg.** "Normal" 40 mmHg may be a warning.
5. **Glycosuria alone does not mean diabetes** in pregnancy; do a glucose tolerance test.
6. Women are **hypercoagulable**: think **DVT and PE** in a woman with a swollen calf or chest pain.

## Practice questions

**1.** By term, plasma volume has risen by about:
A. 10% · B. 20% · **C. 50%** · D. 30% · E. 100%
*Answer: C. The red cell mass rises only about 30%, so the haemoglobin concentration falls.*

**2.** Which murmur is NOT normal in pregnancy?
A. A soft systolic ejection murmur · **B. An early diastolic murmur** · C. A mammary soufflé · D. A third heart sound · E. A venous hum
*Answer: B.*

**3.** Which change in pregnancy explains the tendency to heartburn?
**A. Relaxation of the lower oesophageal sphincter by progesterone** · B. Increased gastric acid · C. Reduced saliva · D. Increased gastric emptying · E. Reduced intra-abdominal pressure
*Answer: A.*

**4.** Which statement about thyroid hormones in pregnancy is TRUE?
A. Free T4 doubles · B. TSH is always high · C. Most women are hypothyroid · D. Total T4 falls · **E. Total T4 rises because thyroid-binding globulin doubles, while free T4 is unchanged**
*Answer: E.*

**5.** At term the uterus weighs about:
A. 100 g · B. 300 g · C. 500 g · **D. 1,000 g** · E. 2,500 g
*Answer: D.*
