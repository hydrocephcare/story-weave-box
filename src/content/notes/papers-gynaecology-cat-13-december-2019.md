## Section A: Short answer questions

### Question 1

**(a) What is a congenital anomaly? (3 marks)**
A structural or functional defect of the body that is present at birth, arising during intrauterine development. It may be genetic, environmental or multifactorial in origin, and may be recognised at birth or later.

**(b) What is a teratogen? (2 marks)**
An agent (drug, infection, chemical or radiation) that interferes with normal fetal development and causes a congenital anomaly. Examples: warfarin, alcohol, rubella virus, thalidomide.

**(c) List broad causes of congenital anomalies (3 marks)**
1. **Genetic:** chromosomal (Down, Turner) and single-gene disorders
2. **Environmental:** infections (TORCH), drugs, radiation, chemicals, maternal disease such as diabetes
3. **Multifactorial:** genes plus environment (neural tube defects, cleft lip)
4. Unknown, which is the largest group.

**(d) Prevention strategies (4 marks)**
- **Primary (before conception and in pregnancy):** folic acid supplementation, rubella and other immunisation, avoiding teratogens, alcohol and smoking, optimising diabetes and epilepsy treatment, avoiding pregnancy at extremes of age, genetic counselling.
- **Secondary (early detection):** antenatal ultrasound, maternal serum screening, amniocentesis, chorionic villus sampling, with options for counselling and termination where legal.
- **Tertiary:** treatment, surgery and rehabilitation for affected children.

### Question 2

**(a) What is abnormal menstruation? (2 marks)**
Menstrual bleeding that departs from the normal pattern in **frequency** (cycle shorter than 24 or longer than 38 days), **regularity,** **duration** (over 8 days) or **volume** (over 80 ml, or too light).

**(b) Two examples, explained (4 marks)**
1. **Menorrhagia:** regular cycles with excessive bleeding of more than 80 ml per cycle or lasting over 7 days.
2. **Oligomenorrhoea:** infrequent periods, with cycles longer than 35 days.

Others: polymenorrhoea (cycles under 21 days), metrorrhagia (irregular bleeding between periods), amenorrhoea.

### Question 3

**(a) Define puberty (3 marks)**
The transition from childhood to reproductive maturity, marked by development of secondary sexual characteristics, a growth spurt and attainment of fertility. In girls it usually starts between **8 and 13 years** with breast budding, and in boys between **9 and 14** with testicular enlargement.

**(b) Define precocious puberty (3 marks)**
Onset of secondary sexual characteristics before **8 years in girls** or **9 years in boys,** from early activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis (central) or from sex steroid excess independent of it (peripheral).

**(c) Changes of the uterus after puberty (4 marks)**
1. The uterus increases in size and weight, and the **body grows faster than the cervix** (ratio changes from 1:2 to 2:1 body to cervix).
2. The shape changes from tubular to the **adult pear shape.**
3. The **myometrium thickens** and blood supply increases.
4. The **endometrium** becomes oestrogen-responsive and undergoes cyclic proliferation, secretion and shedding.

### Question 4

**(a) Define leiomyoma (2 marks)**
A benign smooth-muscle tumour of the myometrium, also called a uterine fibroid.

**(b) Four risk factors (2 marks)**
1. Age 30 to 50 years
2. Black African race
3. Nulliparity, early menarche and obesity
4. Family history, hypertension and unopposed oestrogen exposure

**(c) Medical treatment options (6 marks)**
1. **Observation** for small asymptomatic fibroids.
2. **Symptom control:** NSAIDs and **tranexamic acid** for heavy bleeding, and iron for anaemia.
3. **Hormonal control of bleeding:** combined oral contraceptive pill, progestogens, and the **levonorgestrel intrauterine system.**
4. **GnRH agonists** (for example leuprolide) shrink fibroids and correct anaemia before surgery, limited to 3 to 6 months because of bone loss.
5. **Selective progesterone receptor modulators** (ulipristal) where available.
6. Surgery (myomectomy, hysterectomy) and uterine artery embolisation if medical treatment fails.

## Section B: Multiple choice questions

### 1. While treating an 18-year-old's gonorrhoea, treatment must also be given for:
- A. Bacterial vaginosis
- B. Chlamydia
- C. Herpes
- D. Syphilis

**Answer: B.** Co-infection is common, so chlamydia is treated at the same time.

### 2. Which will not present with acute pelvic pain?
- A. Candida albicans infection
- B. Tubo-ovarian abscess
- C. Mittelschmerz
- D. Septic abortion

**Answer: A.** Candidiasis causes itching and discharge, not acute pelvic pain.

### 3. Which is unlikely to be a feature of a malignant lesion on ultrasound?
- A. Unilateral cyst
- B. Positive colour Doppler
- C. Heterogeneous structure
- D. Ascites

**Answer: A.** Malignancy is suggested by solid areas, thick septa, vascularity, bilaterality and ascites. A simple unilateral cyst is typically benign.

### 4. Which statement is false?
- A. Tanner stage 2 defines the breast bud in females
- B. Tanner stage 3 marks the development of the areolar mound
- C. The prepubertal ovarian volume is under 0.9 cm³
- D. Pubarche occurs before peak growth velocity

**Answer: B.** The areola and nipple form a secondary mound at Tanner stage 4. At stage 3 the breast and areola enlarge with no separation of contours.

### 5. During the menstrual cycle, which is true?
- A. The proliferative phase is relatively constant
- B. Ovulation coincides with menstruation
- C. Anovulatory cycles are characteristically painless
- D. Menstrual blood loss is usually 150 ml

**Answer: C.** Ovulatory cycles are often accompanied by prostaglandin-mediated pain. The luteal phase (not the proliferative) is constant at about 14 days, and normal loss is 30 to 40 ml.

### 6. A woman has milky white discharge with a fishy odour and no itching. Most likely diagnosis:
- A. Bacterial vaginosis
- B. Candida albicans
- C. Trichomonas
- D. Malignancy

**Answer: A.** Thin grey-white discharge with a fishy smell, with no inflammation or itch.

### 7. A 28-year-old has amenorrhoea after treatment for an incomplete miscarriage. Most likely diagnosis:
- A. Post-abortion endometritis
- B. Pelvic inflammatory disease
- C. Asherman syndrome
- D. Kallmann syndrome

**Answer: C.** Curettage can scar the endometrium (intrauterine adhesions), giving secondary amenorrhoea.

### 8. The main cause of abnormal uterine bleeding in a 13-year-old girl:
- A. Trauma
- B. Ectopic pregnancy
- C. Anovulation
- D. Polycystic ovarian disease

**Answer: C.** The immature hypothalamic-pituitary axis causes anovulatory cycles.

### 9. Which is used as emergency contraception?
- A. Low-dose combined contraceptive
- B. Levonorgestrel
- C. Ergometrine
- D. Depo-Provera

**Answer: B.** Levonorgestrel 1.5 mg as soon as possible, ideally within 72 hours (effective up to 120 hours).

### 10. Can be used to treat leiomyoma in a woman desiring fertility EXCEPT:
- A. Myomectomy
- B. Uterine artery embolisation
- C. Tranexamic acid
- D. Magnetic resonance-guided focused ultrasound

**Answer: B.** Uterine artery embolisation can compromise ovarian and uterine blood flow, so it is avoided when fertility is desired. Focused ultrasound is also generally reserved for women who have completed childbearing, but embolisation is the one most strongly avoided.

### 11. External components of the female genital tract include all EXCEPT:
- A. Vagina
- B. Pudendal cleft
- C. Labia minora
- D. Bartholin glands

**Answer: A.** The vagina is an internal genital organ. The vulva includes the labia, pudendal cleft, clitoris and vestibular glands.

### 12. Which is not a segment of the fallopian tube?
- A. Infundibulum
- B. Isthmus
- C. Vestibule
- D. Ampulla

**Answer: C.** The tube has interstitial (intramural), isthmic, ampullary and infundibular parts. The vestibule belongs to the vulva.
