# Antenatal care — Year 4 Obstetrics and Gynaecology Notes

*From the Department's antenatal care lecture (photographed slides), organised as a visit-by-visit guide. Visit schedules differ between the WHO 2016 model and the focused (FANC) model used in many Kenyan facilities: know both.*

**The reasoning chain:** confirm the pregnancy and the due date → decide whether she needs basic or specialised care → screen and treat what can harm her or the baby → prevent (tetanus, iron and folate, malaria, HIV transmission) → prepare a birth and emergency plan → repeat, and watch for danger signs at every visit.

## 1. What antenatal care (ANC) is, and why it matters

**ANC** is the general health care given to pregnant women to **promote and maintain the health of mother and baby through pregnancy, labour and the puerperium**, and to help her **have and rear a healthy baby**. There are two models: **routine** and **focused**.

**What it achieves:** through timely, evidence-based health promotion, disease prevention, screening and treatment, ANC **reduces complications of pregnancy and childbirth**, **reduces stillbirths and perinatal deaths**, and delivers **integrated care** throughout pregnancy.

**Purpose of focused ANC (FANC):**

1. Promote and maintain the **physical, mental and social health** of mother and baby, through education on nutrition, rest, sleep and personal hygiene.
2. **Detect and treat** medical, surgical or obstetric conditions that complicate pregnancy.
3. Ensure the delivery of a **healthy full-term baby** with minimal stress or injury to mother and baby.
4. Prepare the mother to **breastfeed**, have a **normal puerperium** and care for the child physically, psychologically and socially.

## 2. Routine versus focused ANC

| | **Routine (risk-approach) model** | **Focused (FANC) model** |
|---|---|---|
| **Idea** | Sort women into **high risk and low risk**; more visits for the high-risk group | **Goal-oriented**: do what matters at each visit for every woman |
| **Visits** | Every 4 weeks to 28 weeks (5–6 visits), every 2 weeks to 36 weeks (4 visits), then weekly to delivery | **At least four** scheduled visits |
| **Problem** | The risk approach **failed**: most women who have an obstetric emergency were **assessed as not at risk**; it cannot tell **who will** develop complications; it gives a **false sense of security** to low-risk women; identifying a need does not guarantee action | Screens **to detect** a problem rather than **to predict** one, and **treats any problem** that can complicate the pregnancy |

The lesson of FANC: **every pregnant woman is at risk of a complication**, so every woman gets a birth plan and knows the danger signs.

## 3. Timing of visits

| Model | Schedule |
|---|---|
| **WHO 2016 (eight contacts)** | **1st within 12 weeks**; then at **20, 26, 30, 34, 36, 38 and 40 weeks** |
| **WHO focused ANC (four visits)** | **1st within 16 weeks**; **2nd at 24–28 weeks**; **3rd at 32 weeks**; **4th at 36 weeks** |
| **Visits as taught locally** | **1st at 8–12 weeks, 2nd at 24–26 weeks, 3rd at 32 weeks, 4th at 36–38 weeks** |

## 4. What happens at each visit

| Visit | Gestation | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | **8–12 weeks** | **Confirm the pregnancy and the EDD**; **classify the woman** for basic ANC (four visits) or specialised care; **screen, treat and give preventive measures** (anaemia, syphilis, malaria, HIV); start the **birth plan and immunisation schedule**; counsel on **ITN use**; give **iron and folic acid** whether anaemic or not |
| **Second** | **24–26 weeks** | Assess **maternal and fetal well-being**; **exclude pregnancy-induced hypertension (PIH) and anaemia**; give preventive measures; **review the birth and emergency plan**; advise and counsel |
| **Third** | **32 weeks** | Assess well-being; exclude PIH, anaemia and **multiple pregnancy**; preventive measures; review the plan |
| **Fourth** | **36–38 weeks** | Assess well-being; **repeat the HIV test**; exclude PIH, anaemia, multiple pregnancy and **malpresentation**; review the plan; counsel on **labour, delivery and newborn care** |
| **Subsequent** | Any | Confirm **fetal life**, detect abnormalities, finalise the birth plan. The **ANC card** is the basic tool of FANC |

## 5. The first visit in detail

### History

Welcome the woman and ensure a **quiet, private place**. Take: **personal and social history**, **menstrual history** (LNMP, EDD), **current problems with the pregnancy**, **obstetric history** (see the note on assessment of the obstetric patient), **medical and surgical history** and **family history**.

### Examination (clinical)

General examination, **weight**, **blood pressure**, **oedema**, **pallor**, **breast examination**, and the **obstetric examination**: **symphysis-fundal height**, **fetal heart**, **number of fetuses**, **lie** and **presentation**. The aim: detect **undiagnosed physical problems** and set **baseline values** to guide treatment through the pregnancy.

### Investigations

| Group | Tests | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Haematological** | **Blood group, rhesus, sickle cell screen, haemoglobin and red cell indices** | Rhesus incompatibility? Anaemia (iron, sickle cell)? |
| **Common conditions** | **Blood pressure and urine protein** (pre-eclampsia), **malaria slide or rapid test**, **stool microscopy** (worms), **OGTT** (gestational diabetes) | Is there a treatable condition now? |
| **Infections** | **HIV, syphilis, asymptomatic bacteriuria, hepatitis B**, asymptomatic bacterial vaginosis, **group B streptococcus** | Prevent transmission to the baby (**PMTCT**), prevent preterm labour and pyelonephritis |
| **Fetal anomalies** | **Ultrasound, amniocentesis, chorionic villus sampling** | Neural tube defects, **Down syndrome** |
| **Fetal growth and well-being** | **Ultrasound, symphysis-fundal height, biophysical profile, non-stress test, electronic fetal monitoring, fetal movement counts, fetal blood sampling**, L/S ratio and phosphatidylglycerol (lung maturity) | Is the baby growing and healthy? |

## 6. Prevention: what every woman should be given

- **Tetanus toxoid** (to prevent neonatal and maternal tetanus).
- **Iron and folic acid** supplements.
- **PMTCT** of HIV (testing, antiretrovirals, safe infant feeding).
- **Region-specific measures:** **iodine** supplements, **presumptive treatment for hookworm**, and for **malaria**, **intermittent preventive treatment (IPTp)** and **insecticide-treated nets (ITNs)**.

## 7. Early detection and treatment: at every visit

| Condition | How it is found |
|---|---|
| **Severe anaemia** | Pallor, haemoglobin |
| **Pre-eclampsia / eclampsia** | **Blood pressure** and **urine protein** |
| **HIV** | Voluntary counselling and testing |
| **STIs, including syphilis** | Testing |
| **Malaria** | History and examination (fever and signs); test and treat |
| **Complicated versus uncomplicated** | Decide who needs referral |

## 8. Health education: the topics

Nutrition, care for **common discomforts**, avoiding **harmful substances** (alcohol, smoking, drugs, unprescribed medicines), hygiene, **rest and activity**, **sexual relations and safer sex**, **early and exclusive breastfeeding**, **prevention of tetanus and anaemia**, **HIV counselling and testing**, prevention of other endemic diseases and deficiencies, and **PMTCT**.

**Objectives of health education:** inform the woman with messages **suited to her needs and her gestational age**, based on the **commonest health issues**; **support her in making decisions** and solving real or expected problems; **involve her partner and family**.

## 9. Birth preparedness and complication readiness

**Objective: a birth plan**, an exact plan for a **normal birth and for possible complications**. It is **made in advance by the woman and her family with a skilled provider**, usually **not a written document**, **reviewed at every visit**, and it **reduces disorganisation at the time of birth or in an emergency** so that **timely, appropriate care** is given.

**Essential elements of a birth plan**

1. **Place of birth** (home or facility) and **the facility for emergencies**.
2. **A skilled provider** to attend the birth.
3. **Contact information** for the provider and the facility.
4. **Transport:** reliable and available at any hour.
5. **Funds:** savings and emergency funds, **NHIF, Linda Mama** and other insurance.
6. **Decision-making:** **who decides in an emergency** (a delay in deciding is the first of the "three delays").
7. **Blood donor and a companion**, and **clean delivery items**.

**Complication readiness: know the danger signs and what to do**

- **Vaginal bleeding**
- **Difficulty breathing**
- **Fever**
- **Severe abdominal pain**
- **Severe headache or blurred vision** (pre-eclampsia)
- **Convulsions or loss of consciousness** (eclampsia)
- **Rupture of membranes or labour pains before 37 weeks**
- **Reduced or absent fetal movements**

## 10. Record keeping and communication

Record on the **ANC chart and clinic card**: dates of visits, **interim history**, **targeted examination and tests**, care given, **counselling (including the birth plan and ITN use)**, and the **date of the next visit**.

**Inter-personal skills:** speak in a **quiet, gentle voice**; **listen** and respond; **encourage questions**; let her **show she understood**; **explain every procedure and obtain permission**; **respect cultural beliefs**; be **empathetic and non-judgemental**; avoid distractions.

## 11. Barriers to effective ANC

Inadequate infrastructure and supplies, **poor quality of care and poor treatment of clients**, ignorance of the value of ANC, no tradition of ANC in some communities, **cultural, traditional and religious practices**, **women's lack of control over decisions about their own health**, **poverty** (transport and treatment costs), **household responsibilities** and **illiteracy**.

## Practice questions

**1.** The WHO focused antenatal care model recommends at least:
A. Two visits · **B. Four visits** · C. Three visits · D. Six visits · E. Weekly visits
*Answer: B.*

**2.** The "risk approach" to antenatal care failed mainly because:
**A. Most women who had obstetric emergencies were assessed as low risk** · B. It was too cheap · C. It needed no staff · D. It detected too many problems · E. Women disliked it
*Answer: A.*

**3.** Which action belongs to the FIRST ANC visit?
A. Repeat HIV test at 38 weeks · B. Plan induction · C. Assess for malpresentation only · D. Give anti-D · **E. Confirm pregnancy and EDD, screen for anaemia, syphilis, malaria and HIV, and start iron and folic acid**
*Answer: E.*

**4.** Which is a danger sign of pregnancy that demands immediate attention?
A. Mild nausea · B. Backache · **C. Severe headache with blurred vision** · D. Frequency of micturition · E. Mild ankle swelling in the evening
*Answer: C.*

**5.** Which intervention is used for malaria prevention in pregnancy?
A. Chloroquine only · B. Quinine daily · C. Doxycycline · **D. Intermittent preventive treatment and insecticide-treated nets** · E. Primaquine
*Answer: D.*
