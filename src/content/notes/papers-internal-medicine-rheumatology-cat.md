## Section A: Multiple choice questions

### 1. Which of the following is the most common area affected by rheumatoid arthritis?
- A. Temporomandibular joint
- B. Metatarsophalangeal joint
- C. Metacarpophalangeal and proximal interphalangeal joints
- D. Wrist joint

**Answer: C.** RA starts in the small joints of the hands (MCP and PIP) and usually spares the distal interphalangeal joints.

### 2. Which of the following is NOT associated with a poor prognosis in rheumatoid arthritis?
- A. Large number of involved joints
- B. HLA-DRB1*02 / HLA-DRB1*03 alleles
- C. Extra-articular manifestations
- D. High serum titre of autoantibodies (rheumatoid factor, anti-CCP)

**Answer: B.** The "shared epitope" alleles linked to severe RA are HLA-DRB1*01 and *04, not *02 or *03. The other three all predict worse disease.

### 3. Regarding extra-articular disease, which organ system is more likely to be affected by rheumatoid arthritis?
- A. Urinary system
- B. Digestive system
- C. Cardiovascular system
- D. Lymphatic system

**Answer: C.** Of these, cardiovascular disease (accelerated atherosclerosis, pericarditis) is the commonest and the main cause of early death. The lung is also often involved.

### 4. Which is the first choice for imaging in rheumatoid arthritis?
- A. CT scan
- B. MRI
- C. Ultrasonography
- D. Radiography

**Answer: D.** Plain X-rays of hands and feet are the first test and the baseline for erosions. Ultrasound and MRI are used for early disease when films are normal.

### 5. Which drug is the recommended first-line therapy for rheumatoid arthritis?
- A. Abatacept
- B. Etanercept
- C. Sarilumab
- D. Methotrexate

**Answer: D.** Methotrexate is the anchor DMARD. Biologics (A to C) are added when it fails.

### 6. What are the early signs and symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis?
- A. Joint pain, tenderness, redness and swelling
- B. Loss of joint range of movement
- C. Limping
- D. All of the above

**Answer: D.** All are early features, with prolonged morning stiffness.

### 7. Rheumatoid arthritis differs from some other forms of arthritis because it:
- A. Is more painful than other forms
- B. Occurs below the waist
- C. Is symmetrical, affecting the right and left side of the body
- D. Generally occurs above the waist

**Answer: C.** A symmetrical small-joint polyarthritis is the hallmark.

### 8. Rheumatology is the branch of medicine that involves the study of:
- A. The immune system
- B. The musculoskeletal system (muscle and bone)
- C. Rheumatic disease
- D. All of the above

**Answer: D.**

### 9. The sudden appearance or worsening of RA symptoms is called a:
- A. Outbreak
- B. Flare
- C. Burst
- D. Eruption

**Answer: B.**

### 10. The term arthritis refers to:
- A. Stiffness in a joint
- B. Inflammation of the joints
- C. Both (a) and (b)
- D. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Arthritis means joint inflammation. Stiffness is a symptom, not the definition. Option C is a trap and the wording is weak.

### 11. Rheumatoid arthritis is more severe in:
- A. Men
- B. Women
- C. Both (a) and (b)
- D. None of the above

**Answer: B.** It is about 3 times commoner in women and is usually worse in them.

### 12. People with rheumatoid arthritis experience the most stiffness at night.
- A. True
- B. False

**Answer: B (False).** The stiffness is worst on waking in the morning and lasts more than 30 minutes (often over an hour). Night pain happens, but morning stiffness is the classic feature.

### 13. Rheumatoid arthritis can be treated by:
- A. Medication
- B. Exercise
- C. Surgery
- D. All of the above

**Answer: D.** Drugs are the mainstay, with physiotherapy, and surgery for damaged joints.

### 14. Which test cannot be used to aid diagnosis or monitor progression of rheumatoid arthritis?
- A. ESR
- B. RF test
- C. Anti-CCP
- D. Heterophile antibody

**Answer: D.** The heterophile antibody (Monospot) test is for infectious mononucleosis. ESR and CRP monitor activity. RF and anti-CCP help diagnosis.

### 15. The cause of rheumatoid arthritis is:
- A. Heterophile antibodies
- B. Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)
- C. IgM
- D. IgG

**Answer: B.** The true cause is not known: genes plus environment (smoking, infection). Of these options, EBV is the one with a proposed link, and the others are antibodies, not causes. The question is badly worded.

### 16. Which is NOT a criterion for remission in RA by the ACR/EULAR 2011 criteria?
- A. C-reactive protein of 1 mg/dL or less
- B. Swollen joint count of 1 or less
- C. Tender joint count of 1 or less
- D. Physician global assessment of 1 or less

**Answer: D.** The remission criteria use the patient's global assessment (10 or less on a 0 to 100 scale), not the physician's.

### 17. A 45-year-old woman has widespread joint pains worse after a stressful day, puffy hands and feet, a painful neck and poor concentration. Rheumatoid factor is mildly positive. BMI is raised and movement is restricted by pain, with no synovitis. Which investigation is most useful?
- A. Anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibody
- B. Ultrasound of hands and feet
- C. Anti-Jo-1 antibody
- D. ESR and C-reactive protein
- E. Anti-mitochondrial antibody

**Answer: D.** This is fibromyalgia (widespread pain, fatigue, poor concentration, no synovitis). Normal ESR and CRP help exclude an inflammatory arthritis. A weakly positive RF does not change that.

### 18. Which drug is most likely to cause a systemic lupus-like syndrome?
- A. Baclofen
- B. Isoniazid
- C. Methotrexate
- D. Procainamide
- E. Sulfasalazine

**Answer: D.** Procainamide, hydralazine and (less often) isoniazid cause drug-induced lupus. Procainamide has the strongest link.

### 19. A 25-year-old woman has a baby with complete heart block that needs a pacemaker. Which maternal antibody is most likely?
- A. Anti-double-stranded DNA
- B. Anti-endomysial
- C. Anti-Ro/SSA
- D. Anti-Scl-70
- E. Rheumatoid factor

**Answer: C.** Maternal anti-Ro (SSA) crosses the placenta and damages the fetal conduction system (neonatal lupus).

### 20. Which of the following is a pro-inflammatory cytokine?
- A. CRP
- B. Interleukin 4
- C. IL-10
- D. Serum amyloid precursor protein
- E. Tumour necrosis factor-alpha

**Answer: E.** CRP and amyloid protein are acute phase proteins. IL-4 and IL-10 are anti-inflammatory.

### 21. A 72-year-old man has an acutely painful hot right knee, temperature 37°C, WBC 12.6 × 10⁹/L, an X-ray showing reduced joint space and calcification of the articular cartilage, and aspirate culture with no growth. Most likely diagnosis?
- A. Gout
- B. Psoriatic monoarthropathy
- C. Pseudogout
- D. Rheumatoid arthritis
- E. Septic arthritis

**Answer: C.** Chondrocalcinosis on X-ray in an older person with an acute hot joint is calcium pyrophosphate (pseudogout). The sterile culture argues against sepsis.

### 22. Which statement about infliximab is most true?
- A. It is a monoclonal antibody to the glycoprotein IIb-IIIa receptor
- B. It is authorised for severe ulcerative colitis
- C. It is licensed for the treatment of RA
- D. It prevents relapse of Crohn's disease in patients in remission
- E. It must not be used with methotrexate because of toxicity

**Answer: C.** Infliximab is an anti-TNF antibody licensed for RA, given with methotrexate. Option A describes abciximab. Option B is also true in practice, so the question is weak, but C is the intended answer.

### 23. A 25-year-old woman with SLE has nephrotic syndrome. Biopsy shows diffuse proliferative glomerulonephritis (WHO class IV). Which regimen would you advise?
- A. Azathioprine alone
- B. Prednisolone alone
- C. Azathioprine and prednisolone
- D. Prednisolone and intravenous cyclophosphamide
- E. Prednisolone and methotrexate

**Answer: D.** Class IV lupus nephritis needs induction with high-dose steroids plus cyclophosphamide (or mycophenolate), then maintenance.

### 24. Which autoantibody may have a role in monitoring disease activity?
- A. Rheumatoid factor in RA
- B. Antinuclear antibody in SLE
- C. Anti-Sm in SLE
- D. Anti-dsDNA in SLE
- E. Anti-Ro in Sjögren's syndrome

**Answer: D.** Anti-dsDNA titres rise with flares and nephritis (with falling complement). ANA and anti-Sm are for diagnosis.

### 25. A 69-year-old woman on hydralazine for hypertension has joint pain and chest pain with a pericardial rub. Diagnosis?
- A. Dermatomyositis
- B. Systemic lupus erythematosus (drug-induced)
- C. Polymyalgia rheumatica
- D. Felty syndrome

**Answer: B.** Arthralgia plus serositis on hydralazine is drug-induced lupus. Stop the drug.

### 26. Which drug class has been shown to halt or reverse the progression of RA in most patients?
- A. Aspirin
- B. Azathioprine
- C. Everolimus
- D. Methotrexate
- E. Prednisone

**Answer: D.** A disease-modifying antirheumatic drug. Aspirin and prednisone relieve symptoms only.

### 27. Which statement about allopurinol is correct?
- A. It increases glomerular filtration of uric acid
- B. It reduces tubular reabsorption of uric acid
- C. It inhibits the conversion of xanthine to uric acid
- D. It is indicated in a 37-year-old with uric acid 0.42 mmol/L and one attack of gout
- E. It is associated with aplastic anaemia in 1% of cases

**Answer: C.** Allopurinol blocks xanthine oxidase. Options A and B describe uricosurics (probenecid). It is not started after a first attack with a modest level.

### 28. Alendronate has been proven effective in randomised trials in which condition?
- A. Osteomalacia
- B. Bony metastases
- C. Algodystrophy
- D. Postmenopausal osteoporosis
- E. Hypercalciuria

**Answer: D.** Bisphosphonates reduce vertebral and hip fractures in postmenopausal osteoporosis.

### 29. A 33-year-old woman has a malar rash worse with sun, myalgia, pleural effusion, pericarditis and arthralgia without deformity over 7 years, with haematuria and no drug history. Best screening test?
- A. Antinuclear antibody
- B. Anti-dsDNA
- C. Anti-RNP antibody
- D. Anti-histone antibody

**Answer: A.** ANA is the sensitive screening test for SLE. If it is positive, anti-dsDNA and anti-Sm confirm.

### 30. A 40-year-old woman with asthma for 4 years has a 2-month history of asymmetric neuropathy in the right arm and both legs, palpable purpura on the legs and eosinophilia. Likely diagnosis?
- A. Systemic lupus erythematosus
- B. Polyarteritis nodosa
- C. Giant cell arteritis
- D. Churg-Strauss syndrome

**Answer: D.** Asthma, eosinophilia, mononeuritis multiplex and purpura describe eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (Churg-Strauss).

## Section B: Short answer questions (5 marks each)

### 1. What is CREST syndrome?

A limited form of systemic sclerosis (limited cutaneous scleroderma). The letters stand for:

1. **C**alcinosis: calcium deposits in the skin.
2. **R**aynaud's phenomenon.
3. **E**sophageal dysmotility.
4. **S**clerodactyly: tight, thick skin of the fingers.
5. **T**elangiectasia.

It is linked to **anti-centromere antibody**. The skin disease is limited to hands, forearms and face. Lung hypertension is its serious late complication.

### 2. Typical changes expected in a patient with rheumatoid arthritis (5 marks)

- **Joints:** symmetrical swelling and tenderness of MCP, PIP, wrists and MTP joints; morning stiffness over 30 minutes.
- **Hand deformities:** ulnar deviation, swan-neck, boutonniere and Z-thumb.
- **Extra-articular:** rheumatoid nodules, anaemia of chronic disease, pleural effusion and lung fibrosis, pericarditis, scleritis, vasculitis, Sjögren's syndrome, Felty syndrome.
- **Blood tests:** raised ESR and CRP, positive RF and anti-CCP, normochromic anaemia.
- **X-ray:** periarticular osteopenia, loss of joint space and marginal erosions.

### 3. List the features of systemic lupus erythematosus (5 marks)

- **General:** fever, fatigue, weight loss.
- **Skin and mucosa:** malar (butterfly) rash, photosensitivity, discoid rash, oral ulcers, alopecia.
- **Joints:** non-erosive arthritis.
- **Serositis:** pleurisy and pericarditis.
- **Kidney:** lupus nephritis (proteinuria, haematuria).
- **Nervous system:** seizures and psychosis.
- **Blood:** haemolytic anaemia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia.
- **Antibodies:** ANA, anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, low complement; antiphospholipid antibodies.

### 4. Difference between polymyositis and dermatomyositis (5 marks)

| Feature | Polymyositis | Dermatomyositis |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | No rash | Heliotrope rash, Gottron's papules, shawl sign |
| Muscle | Symmetrical proximal weakness | Same proximal weakness |
| Age | Adults (over 20) | Children and adults |
| Cancer link | Weaker | Stronger (screen for malignancy) |
| Biopsy | Endomysial inflammation (CD8 T cells) | Perifascicular atrophy, perivascular inflammation |
| Both | Raised CK, EMG changes, anti-Jo-1 in some | Raised CK, EMG changes |

### 5. Features of fibromyalgia (5 marks)

- Chronic widespread pain for more than 3 months, with tender points.
- Fatigue and unrefreshing sleep.
- Cognitive problems ("fibro fog"): poor concentration and memory.
- Mood: depression and anxiety.
- Associated: irritable bowel, headaches, restless legs, paraesthesia.
- **Normal** examination of the joints, and normal ESR, CRP and X-rays.
- Management: education, graded exercise, sleep hygiene, amitriptyline, duloxetine or pregabalin.
