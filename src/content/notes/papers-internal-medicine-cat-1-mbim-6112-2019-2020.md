## Section A: Multiple choice questions

### 1. HIV enteropathy will involve all of the following EXCEPT:
- a) Cobalamin malabsorption
- b) Viral destruction
- c) Viral transmission
- d) Dysmaturation of intestinal epithelial cells
- e) None of the above

**Answer: c.** HIV enteropathy comes from direct viral effects on gut cells, immune activation, villous damage and malabsorption. "Viral transmission" is not a mechanism of the enteropathy.

### 2. All the following are NOT associated with Th17 cells in HIV infection EXCEPT:
- a) No role in microbial translocation
- b) Th17 causes epithelial degeneration
- c) Impaired production of mucins
- d) Depressed production of claudins
- e) Recruitment of neutrophils and macrophages to infected enterocytes

**Answer: e.** Th17 cells protect the gut by recruiting neutrophils and macrophages. In HIV they are lost, which weakens the barrier and allows bacterial translocation. The wording is awkward.

### 3. Which statement least describes angular cheilitis?
- a) Association with erythema
- b) Can occur with or without erythematous candidiasis
- c) Fistula of the corner of the mouth
- d) Pseudomembranous candidiasis can be an association
- e) None of the above

**Answer: c.** Angular cheilitis is fissuring and inflammation at the mouth corners, not a fistula.

### 4. The following may cause diarrhoea in HIV/AIDS:
- a) Blastocystis hominis
- b) Isospora belli
- c) Entamoeba histolytica
- d) Shigella
- e) All of the above

**Answer: e.** All can cause diarrhoea in HIV. (The stem says "bacteria", but most listed organisms are protozoa.)

### 5. The following is useful in the diagnosis of cryptosporidiosis:
- a) DNA extraction from stool
- b) Acid-fast staining
- c) EIAs
- d) Direct fluorescent antibody
- e) All of the above

**Answer: e.**

### 6. The following best explains the role of pentoxifylline in alcoholic liver disease:
- a) Increases blood viscosity
- b) No role in altering portal hypertension
- c) Associated with a significant decrease in the risk of developing hepatorenal syndrome
- d) Associated with angiogenesis
- e) Blocks oxygenation of pathogenic hepatocytes

**Answer: c.** Pentoxifylline (a TNF inhibitor) lowers the risk of hepatorenal syndrome in severe alcoholic hepatitis.

### 7. Examination findings suggestive of chronic liver disease may include the following EXCEPT:
- a) Caput medusae
- b) Spider naevi
- c) Gynaecomastia
- d) Anorexia
- e) Pruritic skin lesions

**Answer: d.** Anorexia is a symptom, not an examination finding.

### 8. Concerning alcohol metabolism, which statement is FALSE?
- a) Alcohol is well absorbed in the jejunum
- b) There is significant elimination in urine
- c) A small amount of alcohol is eliminated through sweating
- d) Cytochrome P450 metabolises alcohol in the liver
- e) Catalase is important in elimination

**Answer: b.** More than 90% is metabolised in the liver. Only a small amount leaves in urine, breath and sweat.

### 9. Concerning alcoholic hepatitis, which statement is FALSE?
- a) Bilirubin level is an independent prognostic marker
- b) Level of TGF is an independent prognostic marker
- c) Maddrey factor above 32 indicates steroid consideration
- d) ALT is usually below 400 IU/ml
- e) Kupffer cell activation is pathogenic

**Answer: b.** The cytokine linked to outcome is TNF-alpha, not TGF. Bilirubin and the Maddrey score are standard prognostic markers.

### 10. Alcoholic liver disease is associated with the following EXCEPT:
- a) Elevated carbohydrate-deficient transferrin
- b) Increased mean corpuscular volume
- c) Increased IgA to IgG ratio
- d) ALT to AST ratio of greater than 2
- e) None of the above

**Answer: d.** The ratio is reversed: AST is higher than ALT, with AST:ALT above 2.

### 11. The following are important in Child-Turcotte-Pugh scoring EXCEPT:
- a) Encephalopathy
- b) Serum bilirubin
- c) Ascites
- d) Prothrombin time
- e) Urine albumin

**Answer: e.** The score uses serum albumin, not urine albumin.

### 12. The following are associated with inflammatory diarrhoea EXCEPT:
- a) Cytomegalovirus
- b) Salmonella
- c) Shigella
- d) Entamoeba histolytica
- e) Clostridium difficile

**Answer: none is a clear exception.** All five can produce an inflammatory (bloody, pus-filled) diarrhoea. The question appears flawed.

### 13. Concerning osmotic diarrhoea, which statement is NOT true?
- a) Fasting worsens the condition
- b) Lactose intolerance is the most common cause
- c) Osmotically active agents in the intestinal lumen prevent water absorption
- d) Antacids can be a cause
- e) Cholestyramine can be a cause

**Answer: a.** Osmotic diarrhoea stops with fasting, because the offending solute is no longer taken in. Secretory diarrhoea continues during fasting.

### 14. Which is FALSE concerning Escherichia coli O157:H7?
- a) Produces Shiga toxin
- b) Undercooked raw milk is a cause
- c) May lead to haemorrhagic diarrhoea
- d) Responds well to antibiotics if treated early
- e) May be associated with thrombocytopenia

**Answer: d.** Antibiotics are avoided because they increase toxin release and the risk of haemolytic uraemic syndrome.

### 15. Which microbe is well matched with its incubation period?
- a) Salmonella: 10 days
- b) Campylobacter: 1 to 3 hours
- c) Vibrio cholerae: up to 10 days
- d) Bacillus cereus: 12 to 72 hours
- e) Haemorrhagic E. coli: 5 to 7 days

**Answer: e.** EHEC incubates for about 3 to 8 days, so 5 to 7 days fits best. Salmonella takes 6 to 72 hours, Campylobacter 2 to 5 days, cholera a few hours to 5 days, and Bacillus cereus 1 to 16 hours. The options are loosely worded.

### 16. Which is the odd one out?
- a) Campylobacter
- b) Entamoeba histolytica
- c) Shigella
- d) Enterotoxigenic E. coli
- e) Salmonella

**Answer: d.** ETEC causes watery (secretory) diarrhoea through toxins without invading the gut wall. The others invade and cause inflammatory diarrhoea.

### 17. The following endocrine conditions may cause non-infectious diarrhoea:
- a) Carcinoid
- b) Gastrinoma
- c) Addison's disease
- d) Hyperthyroidism
- e) All of the above

**Answer: e.**

### 18. Which form of viral hepatitis can exist for years without symptoms?
- a) D
- b) B and C
- c) A
- d) E
- e) All of the above

**Answer: b.** Chronic hepatitis B and C are often silent for decades. Hepatitis A and E do not become chronic (E can in the immunosuppressed).

### 19. In most patients, what are the symptoms of hepatitis C when first infected?
- a) Itching
- b) Jaundice
- c) Myalgia
- d) Nausea and vomiting
- e) Most patients do not experience symptoms

**Answer: e.** About 80% of acute infections are asymptomatic.

### 20. Which marker is the only one present after vaccination for hepatitis B?
- a) Anti-HBs
- b) Anti-HBc
- c) HBV DNA
- d) Anti-HBe
- e) None of the above

**Answer: a.** The vaccine contains only surface antigen, so only anti-HBs appears.

### 21. Which marker is best matched with its description?
- a) Anti-HBc IgG: a general marker of infection
- b) HBeAg: active replication of the virus
- c) HBsAg: past or chronic infection
- d) Anti-HBc IgM: active viral replication
- e) None of the above

**Answer: b.** HBeAg marks active viral replication and high infectivity. HBsAg is the general marker of infection, anti-HBc IgG marks past or chronic infection, and anti-HBc IgM marks recent (acute) infection.

### 22. Which statement is true of hepatitis C virus infection?
- a) Replicates exclusively in the cytoplasm
- b) Genotype 1 and 4 have a good response to interferon
- c) The morphological structure is well characterised
- d) All infected people present with jaundice
- e) In ascites, pegylated interferon is indicated

**Answer: a.** HCV is an RNA virus that replicates in the cytoplasm. Genotypes 1 and 4 respond poorly to interferon.

### 23. The risk of contracting cholera is increased in the following circumstances EXCEPT:
- a) Use of H2 receptor antagonists
- b) Use of protease inhibitors
- c) Gastrectomy
- d) Use of esomeprazole
- e) None of the above

**Answer: b.** Anything that reduces stomach acid (H2 blockers, proton pump inhibitors, gastrectomy) lowers the dose needed to cause cholera. Protease inhibitors do not.

### 24. Clinical findings in cholera with acidosis may include all of the following EXCEPT:
- a) Prerenal azotaemia
- b) Leukocytosis
- c) Raised packed cell volume
- d) Severe hypokalaemia
- e) Sodium retention

**Answer: e.** Cholera causes sodium loss, not retention. The other findings are expected.

### 25. Concerning the cholera vaccine, which statement is true?
- a) Dukoral is not given with a buffer solution
- b) Euvichol requires a buffer for administration
- c) There must be a minimum of 7 days and no more than 6 weeks between doses
- d) Protection is lifelong
- e) None of the above

**Answer: c.** Dukoral needs a buffer and Euvichol (a non-buffered oral vaccine) does not. Protection lasts months to a few years.

### 26. Which statement is FALSE concerning shigella?
- a) No natural food products harbour endogenous shigella species
- b) The infectious dose is quite low
- c) The incubation period is typically 2 to 4 days
- d) Shigella flexneri is associated with moderate infection
- e) Shigella invades mucosal cells beyond the lamina propria

**Answer: e.** Shigella invades the colonic epithelium and lamina propria but not deeper, which is why it rarely causes bacteraemia.

### 27. Haemolytic uraemic syndrome is associated with all of the following EXCEPT:
- a) Coombs positive haemolytic anaemia
- b) Coombs negative haemolytic anaemia
- c) Plasma exchange
- d) Thrombocytopenia
- e) None of the above

**Answer: a.** HUS gives a Coombs-negative microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia with thrombocytopenia and kidney injury. Plasma exchange is used in atypical HUS.

### 28. Which is true of liver cirrhosis?
- a) There is peripheral arterial vasoconstriction
- b) SAAG is below 1.1 g/dL
- c) There is colloid shift to the spleen and bone marrow on imaging
- d) Anaerobes are the causative organisms in spontaneous bacterial peritonitis
- e) All of the above

**Answer: c.** On a liver-spleen colloid scan, uptake shifts from the liver to the spleen and marrow. Cirrhosis causes peripheral vasodilatation, SAAG is above 1.1, and SBP is caused mainly by gut aerobes (E. coli).

### 29. Which is true of hepatocellular carcinoma?
- a) Aflatoxin B1 leads to p53 tumour suppressor gene mutation
- b) Incidence is higher with HBeAg-positive hepatitis
- c) On CT there is early enhancement in the venous phase
- d) Fine-needle biopsy is more useful than core-directed biopsy
- e) None of the above

**Answer: a.** Aflatoxin B1 causes a characteristic codon 249 p53 mutation. Enhancement on CT is in the arterial phase, with washout later. The statement about HBeAg is also plausible, but a is the cleanest.

### 30. Which statement is true regarding hepatic haemangiomas?
- a) The majority are greater than 5 cm
- b) Hormonal influence has a role
- c) If less than 10 cm there is no need to follow up
- d) It is the rarest of the liver tumours
- e) All of the above

**Answer: b.** Haemangiomas are the commonest benign liver tumours, are mostly small, and can enlarge with oestrogen (pregnancy, the pill).

## Section B: Short answer questions

### 1(i). List the radiological findings in liver cirrhosis (5 marks)

1. **Ultrasound:** small, shrunken liver (late) with a nodular surface and coarse, heterogeneous echo texture.
2. Caudate and left lateral lobe enlargement with right lobe atrophy.
3. **Portal hypertension:** dilated portal vein (over 13 mm), splenomegaly, collaterals and varices, reversed or slow portal flow on Doppler.
4. Ascites.
5. Hepatic nodules (regenerative or dysplastic), and screen for hepatocellular carcinoma.
6. CT or MRI show the same features plus a widened fissure and gallbladder fossa.

### 1(ii). Describe the investigations to evaluate alcoholic liver disease (10 marks)

- **History and examination:** quantity and duration of alcohol use (CAGE or AUDIT), stigmata of chronic liver disease, jaundice, ascites, encephalopathy.
- **Liver tests:** AST and ALT (AST:ALT above 2, AST rarely over 300), GGT, ALP, bilirubin, albumin.
- **Clotting:** PT or INR (Maddrey score and Child-Pugh).
- **Blood count:** raised MCV, thrombocytopenia, neutrophil leucocytosis.
- **Other markers:** carbohydrate-deficient transferrin, urea, electrolytes and creatinine (hepatorenal syndrome), glucose, lipids, ferritin.
- **Exclude other causes:** hepatitis B and C serology, HIV, autoimmune markers, iron studies and ceruloplasmin if young.
- **Imaging:** ultrasound for fatty liver, cirrhosis, portal hypertension and tumour (alpha-fetoprotein if cirrhotic).
- **Endoscopy:** for varices.
- **Liver biopsy:** if the diagnosis is uncertain (steatosis, Mallory bodies, neutrophil infiltrate, fibrosis); transjugular route if coagulopathy.
- **Ascitic tap:** cell count, culture and SAAG.
- **Fibrosis scores:** FibroScan or ELF.

### 2(i). Describe the bacterial causes and treatment of HIV-associated diarrhoea (10 marks)

- **Causes:** Salmonella (including non-typhoidal, often bacteraemic), Shigella, Campylobacter, enteropathogenic and enteroaggregative E. coli, Clostridium difficile, Mycobacterium avium complex, M. tuberculosis. (Other groups: protozoa such as Cryptosporidium, Isospora, microsporidia and Giardia; viruses such as CMV; and HIV enteropathy.)
- **Treatment:**
  1. **Rehydration** first: oral rehydration, or IV fluids if severe, and correct electrolytes.
  2. Antibiotics guided by stool culture: ciprofloxacin or azithromycin for Salmonella, Shigella and Campylobacter (ceftriaxone if bacteraemic or resistant); oral metronidazole or vancomycin for C. difficile; clarithromycin, ethambutol and rifabutin for MAC; standard antituberculous drugs for TB.
  3. **Start or optimise antiretroviral therapy**, which cures many cases.
  4. Nutrition support, zinc, and loperamide only for non-bloody diarrhoea.
  5. Prevent with safe water and food, hygiene and co-trimoxazole prophylaxis.

### 2(ii). Describe the risk factors and treatment of CMV colitis (5 marks)

- **Risk factors:** advanced HIV (CD4 below 50), organ transplant and immunosuppressive drugs, chemotherapy, steroids, inflammatory bowel disease, critical illness and diabetes.
- **Features:** fever, bloody diarrhoea, abdominal pain, weight loss; endoscopy shows ulcers, and biopsy shows owl's-eye inclusion bodies.
- **Treatment:** IV **ganciclovir** for 2 to 3 weeks, then oral valganciclovir; foscarnet if resistant or ganciclovir is not tolerated; start or optimise ART in HIV; reduce immunosuppression; supportive care and surgery for perforation.

### 3(i). Describe Vibrio cholerae (5 marks)

A curved, Gram-negative, comma-shaped, motile rod (serogroups O1 classical and El Tor, and O139). It is spread by the faecal-oral route in contaminated water and food. After an incubation of hours to 5 days, cholera toxin activates adenylate cyclase in gut cells, raising cAMP and causing massive secretion of chloride and water. The patient passes profuse watery "rice water" stools with vomiting, rapid dehydration and shock, hypokalaemia and metabolic acidosis. Treat with rapid rehydration (ORS or IV Ringer's lactate), doxycycline or azithromycin, and zinc in children. Control rests on clean water, sanitation, hygiene, and oral cholera vaccine in outbreaks.

### 3(ii). Identify and describe the different types of Shigella (6 marks)

| Species (group) | Features |
|---|---|
| Shigella dysenteriae (A) | The most severe; serotype 1 produces Shiga toxin; epidemics; can cause HUS |
| Shigella flexneri (B) | Moderate illness; common in developing countries; linked to reactive arthritis (Reiter's syndrome) in HLA-B27 people |
| Shigella boydii (C) | Rarer; mostly the Indian subcontinent; moderate illness |
| Shigella sonnei (D) | The mildest; commonest in developed countries; often watery diarrhoea |

All are non-motile, Gram-negative rods with a very low infectious dose (10 to 100 organisms). They invade the colonic mucosa and cause bloody mucoid diarrhoea (dysentery) with tenesmus.

### 3(iii). Describe Reiter's syndrome (4 marks)

A **reactive arthritis** after an infection, classically in an HLA-B27-positive young man. It follows an enteric infection (Shigella flexneri, Salmonella, Campylobacter, Yersinia) or a genital infection (Chlamydia). The classic triad is:

1. **Urethritis** (or cervicitis).
2. **Conjunctivitis** (or uveitis).
3. **Asymmetric oligoarthritis** of the lower limbs, often with enthesitis and sacroiliitis.

Also: circinate balanitis and keratoderma blennorrhagica. The joints are sterile (seronegative). Treat with NSAIDs, with intra-articular steroids and sulfasalazine if persistent, and treat any triggering infection.

### 4(i). Complications associated with cholelithiasis (5 marks)

1. Biliary colic.
2. Acute and chronic cholecystitis; empyema and mucocele of the gallbladder.
3. Obstructive jaundice from choledocholithiasis.
4. Ascending cholangitis.
5. Acute gallstone pancreatitis.
6. Gallstone ileus (fistula into the bowel) and Mirizzi syndrome.
7. Gallbladder perforation and peritonitis.
8. Gallbladder carcinoma (long-standing stones, porcelain gallbladder).

### 4(ii). Define acute pancreatitis (2 marks)

An acute inflammatory process of the pancreas caused by premature activation of pancreatic enzymes within the gland, with variable involvement of nearby tissues and distant organs. It is diagnosed when two of three are present: typical upper abdominal pain, serum lipase or amylase above 3 times normal, and characteristic imaging findings.

### 4(iii). Discuss the early management of acute pancreatitis (8 marks)

1. **Assess severity** (Glasgow or Ranson score, CRP, APACHE II) and admit to high-dependency care if severe.
2. **Aggressive fluid resuscitation** with Ringer's lactate, aiming for good urine output; monitor vitals, urea and electrolytes.
3. **Analgesia:** opioids (such as pethidine or morphine) and antiemetics.
4. **Oxygen**, with blood gases if there is respiratory compromise.
5. **Early feeding:** oral intake as tolerated within 24 to 48 hours; nasojejunal or enteral feeding if severe. Avoid prolonged fasting.
6. **Treat the cause:** ERCP within 24 to 72 hours for gallstones with cholangitis or persistent obstruction; stop alcohol, stop offending drugs, and correct hypercalcaemia or hypertriglyceridaemia.
7. **Antibiotics** only for proven infected necrosis or cholangitis.
8. **Look for and treat complications:** necrosis, pseudocyst, abscess, ARDS, renal failure, hypocalcaemia and hyperglycaemia. Cholecystectomy in the same admission for mild gallstone pancreatitis.
