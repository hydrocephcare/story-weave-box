## Question 1. Outline 10 complications associated with chronic renal failure in children (10 marks)

1. **Growth failure** and delayed puberty (from acidosis, poor nutrition, bone disease and growth hormone resistance)
2. **Anaemia** (reduced erythropoietin)
3. **Fatigue** and poor school performance
4. **Hypertension** (salt and water retention, renin)
5. **Renal osteodystrophy:** rickets, bone pain and deformity (low vitamin D, high phosphate and PTH)
6. **Metabolic acidosis**
7. **Hyperkalaemia**
8. **Fluid overload:** left ventricular failure and **pulmonary oedema**
9. **Bleeding tendency** from platelet dysfunction
10. **Infections** and **pericarditis** (uraemic), and neurodevelopmental problems
11. Others: hypocalcaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hyperlipidaemia, and psychosocial effects.

## Question 2. Pathophysiology behind the clinical presentation of nephrotic syndrome (15 marks)

Nephrotic syndrome is **heavy proteinuria** (over 40 mg/m² per hour or urine protein:creatinine ratio over 200 mg/mmol), **hypoalbuminaemia, oedema** and **hyperlipidaemia.**

**1. Proteinuria**
- The glomerular filtration barrier has three layers: fenestrated endothelium, glomerular basement membrane and the foot processes of podocytes with their filtration slits.
- In minimal change disease (the commonest childhood cause), a T-cell cytokine alters podocyte foot processes and removes the negative charge, so albumin passes into the urine.
- Other causes (focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, membranous nephropathy) damage the basement membrane or podocytes directly.

**2. Hypoalbuminaemia**
- Loss of albumin in urine, with increased renal catabolism of albumin by the tubules, and synthesis in the liver that cannot keep pace.

**3. Oedema**
- Low albumin lowers plasma oncotic pressure, so fluid moves into the interstitium (underfill hypothesis).
- The fall in intravascular volume activates the **renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system** and **ADH,** so the kidney retains salt and water, and some primary sodium retention at the distal nephron (overfill) adds to the swelling.
- Oedema is periorbital in the morning, then generalised with ascites, pleural effusion and scrotal swelling.

**4. Hyperlipidaemia**
- The liver increases lipoprotein synthesis in response to low oncotic pressure, and lipoprotein lipase activity falls.

**5. Other features**
- **Infections** (loss of immunoglobulin and complement), especially peritonitis and cellulitis by encapsulated organisms.
- **Thrombosis** (loss of antithrombin III and increased fibrinogen).
- Hypovolaemia and acute kidney injury, and loss of binding proteins causing low calcium and thyroxine-binding changes.

## Question 3. Short notes on 3 childhood immunisable diseases in Kenya (15 marks)

**Measles** (measles-rubella vaccine at 9 and 18 months)
- **Cause:** RNA paramyxovirus, airborne spread.
- **Features:** fever, cough, coryza, conjunctivitis, Koplik spots, then a descending maculopapular rash.
- **Complications:** pneumonia, otitis media, diarrhoea, encephalitis, blindness and malnutrition.
- **Treatment:** supportive, vitamin A on two days, and treat infections. Prevention is by vaccination.

**Tetanus / diphtheria / pertussis** (pentavalent vaccine at 6, 10 and 14 weeks)
- **Pertussis:** paroxysmal cough with whoop and post-tussive vomiting. Treat with macrolides.
- **Diphtheria:** sore throat with adherent grey membrane and bull neck. Treat with antitoxin and penicillin.
- **Tetanus:** trismus, spasms and neonatal tetanus from unclean cord care. Treat with tetanus immunoglobulin, metronidazole and diazepam.

**Poliomyelitis** (oral and inactivated polio vaccine)
- **Features:** acute flaccid paralysis, asymmetric, with no sensory loss.
- **Prevention:** vaccination. Any child with acute flaccid paralysis needs stool tests and notification.

Others that could be chosen: tuberculosis (BCG), hepatitis B, Haemophilus influenzae type b, pneumococcal disease, rotavirus gastroenteritis.

## Question 4. Components of the cold chain in vaccinology (10 marks)

The **cold chain** is the system of transporting and storing vaccines at the recommended temperature (+2 to +8 °C, except some at -20 °C) from the manufacturer to the point of use.
1. **Vaccine manufacturer and national store** (central vaccine store)
2. **Distribution:** regional (county) and sub-county **vaccine depots** and health facilities.
3. **Storage equipment:** walk-in cold rooms and deep freezers (-15 to -25 °C for oral polio, measles and varicella vaccines), and **ice-lined refrigerators** or solar fridges at facilities. Cold rooms hold stock for up to about 3 months.
4. **Transport equipment:** **cold boxes** and **vaccine carriers** with conditioned ice packs, and **day carriers** for outreach.
5. **Temperature monitoring:** thermometers, fridge-tag data loggers and **vaccine vial monitors (VVM)** that show heat exposure.
6. **Freeze protection:** shake test, and keeping freeze-sensitive vaccines (pentavalent, hepatitis B, tetanus toxoid) away from ice.
7. **Diluents:** kept in the fridge and used with the same vaccine (diluents must be cooled before reconstitution).
8. **Trained staff** and record keeping: stock registers, **first-expiry-first-out** and **multi-dose vial policy.**
