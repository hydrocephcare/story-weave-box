## Section A: Multiple choice questions

### 1. Bronchial asthma is characterised by all the following, except:
- A. Hyperactive airway
- B. Clubbing
- C. Spasmodic cough
- D. Bilateral chest wheezes

**Answer: B.** Clubbing is not a feature of asthma. It points to bronchiectasis, lung cancer or fibrosis.

### 2. All of the following are common causes of empyema, except:
- A. Staphylococcal pneumonia
- B. Lung abscess
- C. Measles
- D. Chest injury

**Answer: C.** Empyema follows pneumonia, abscess, chest trauma or surgery. Measles is not a usual cause.

### 3. Wheezing is a hallmark symptom of:
- A. Asthma
- B. COPD
- C. Cystic fibrosis
- D. Lung cancer

**Answer: A.**

### 4. "Tree in bud" findings and cysts on a high-resolution CT scan of the chest are indicative of:
- A. COPD
- B. Bronchiectasis
- C. Asthma
- D. Lung cancer

**Answer: B.**

### 5. Which of the following is most likely to cause haemoptysis?
- A. Asthma
- B. COPD
- C. Lung cancer
- D. Pneumothorax

**Answer: C.** Of these, lung cancer is the most likely. TB and bronchiectasis are also common causes.

### 6. Haemoglobin S:
- A. Is the result of a mutation on chromosome 16
- B. Is due to the substitution of valine for leucine
- C. Is less soluble at low PO2 than HbA
- D. Has an equivalent half-life to HbA
- E. Levels greater than 5% are detected by the sickle cell test

**Answer: C.** HbS polymerises when oxygen is low. The mutation is on chromosome 11 and replaces glutamic acid with valine.

### 7. Complications of sickle cell disease include all of the following, except:
- A. Sequestration crisis
- B. Aplastic crisis
- C. Haemolytic crisis
- D. Hypovolaemic crisis

**Answer: D.** The recognised crises are vaso-occlusive, sequestration, aplastic and haemolytic.

### 8. Precipitants of a sickle cell crisis include all of the following, except:
- A. Blood transfusion
- B. Dehydration
- C. Infection
- D. Stress
- E. Hypoxia

**Answer: A.** Transfusion treats a crisis. The others trigger one.

### 9. Anaemia of chronic disease is mainly characterised by all of the following, except:
- A. Inadequate erythrocyte production
- B. Low serum iron
- C. Increased iron-binding capacity
- D. Normochromic normocytic anaemia

**Answer: C.** The iron-binding capacity (TIBC) is low or normal in chronic disease and high in iron deficiency.

### 10. Causes of anaemia of chronic disease include the following, except:
- A. Viral infection
- B. Cancer
- C. Fungal infection
- D. Pregnancy
- E. Bacterial infection

**Answer: D.** Pregnancy gives dilutional or iron-deficiency anaemia.

### 11. Aplastic anaemia is characterised by:
- A. Iron deficiency anaemia
- B. Pancytopenia
- C. Lymphadenopathy
- D. Splenomegaly
- E. Jaundice

**Answer: B.** Low red cells, white cells and platelets with an empty bone marrow, and no organ enlargement.

### 12. Tests useful in the diagnosis of aplastic anaemia include the following, except:
- A. Full blood count
- B. ANA
- C. Urine catecholamines
- D. Bone marrow aspirate
- E. Liver function tests

**Answer: C.**

### 13. Aplastic anaemia is characterised by which of the following?
- A. ANC below 0.5 G/L
- B. Platelets below 100 × 10⁹/L
- C. Reticulocyte count below 10%
- D. Auer rods

**Answer: A.** Severe aplastic anaemia has a neutrophil count below 0.5 × 10⁹/L. Auer rods belong to acute myeloid leukaemia.

### 99. Haemorrhagic strokes constitute what percentage of all strokes?

## Section B: Short answer questions (10 marks each, answer all)

### 1. A 48-year-old alcoholic has acute abdominal pain after a night of binge drinking. List the probable diagnosis and differential diagnosis, and the investigations to make a diagnosis.

- **Probable diagnosis:** acute pancreatitis (alcohol-induced).
- **Differential diagnosis:** acute alcoholic hepatitis, alcoholic gastritis, perforated or bleeding peptic ulcer, acute cholecystitis, acute appendicitis, mesenteric ischaemia, bowel obstruction, and inferior myocardial infarction.
- **Investigations:** serum lipase (or amylase), full blood count, liver tests (AST, ALT, ALP, bilirubin, GGT), urea, electrolytes and creatinine, calcium, glucose, triglycerides, blood gases and CRP; abdominal ultrasound to look for gallstones; CT with contrast after 48 to 72 hours if unclear or severe; erect chest X-ray (free air); ECG.

### 2. A 76-year-old patient has easy fatigability, weight gain, cold intolerance and slowed mentation. What is the probable diagnosis? What are the necessary tests to make a diagnosis and the management?

- **Probable diagnosis:** primary hypothyroidism (commonly Hashimoto's thyroiditis).
- **Tests:** TSH (raised) and free T4 (low); anti-thyroid peroxidase antibodies; full blood count (anaemia), lipids (high cholesterol), creatine kinase, sodium and glucose; ECG.
- **Management:** lifelong **levothyroxine**. In an older patient, start low (25 micrograms daily) and increase every 4 to 6 weeks, aiming for a TSH in the normal range. Check TSH 6 to 8 weeks after each dose change. Look for and treat associated heart disease, and educate the patient.

### 3. A 62-year-old man, hypertensive for 10 years and non-compliant with medication, has easy fatigability, bipedal oedema and difficulty in breathing. What is the likely diagnosis? What investigations make a diagnosis and what is the appropriate management?

- **Likely diagnosis:** congestive cardiac failure (hypertensive heart disease with left ventricular failure).
- **Investigations:** ECG (left ventricular hypertrophy, ischaemia, arrhythmia), chest X-ray (cardiomegaly, pulmonary congestion, effusions), **echocardiogram** (ejection fraction, wall thickness, valves), BNP or NT-proBNP, full blood count, urea, electrolytes, creatinine, liver and thyroid tests, glucose and lipids.
- **Management:** educate, restrict salt, and treat with diuretics (furosemide) for congestion, plus an ACE inhibitor (or ARB) and a beta blocker (bisoprolol, carvedilol), with a mineralocorticoid antagonist (spironolactone). Control blood pressure and stress the need for continuing treatment. Look for and treat precipitants.

### 4. A 45-year-old woman has dysphagia, thickening of the skin and Raynaud's phenomenon. What is the likely diagnosis and what are the appropriate investigations and treatment?

- **Likely diagnosis:** systemic sclerosis (scleroderma), probably the limited form (CREST).
- **Investigations:** ANA, anti-centromere antibody (limited) or anti-Scl-70 (diffuse); full blood count, urea and creatinine, urinalysis; barium swallow or endoscopy and manometry for the oesophagus; chest X-ray and high-resolution CT with lung function and DLCO; echocardiogram (pulmonary hypertension); nailfold capillaroscopy.
- **Treatment:** keep warm and stop smoking; calcium channel blockers (nifedipine) for Raynaud's; PPIs and prokinetics for oesophageal reflux; ACE inhibitors (captopril) for renal crisis; immunosuppression (cyclophosphamide, mycophenolate) for lung disease; bosentan or sildenafil for pulmonary hypertension; skin care and physiotherapy.

### 5. A 22-year-old has iron deficiency anaemia. What are the likely causes, investigations and management?

- **Causes:** blood loss (menstruation, hookworm, peptic ulcer, haemorrhoids), increased need (pregnancy, growth), poor intake (diet), poor absorption (coeliac disease, atrophic gastritis).
- **Investigations:** full blood count (low Hb, low MCV and MCH), blood film (hypochromic, microcytic, pencil cells), serum ferritin (low), serum iron (low) and TIBC (high), reticulocytes; stool for ova and occult blood; endoscopy or colonoscopy if there is no clear cause; coeliac serology if suspected.
- **Management:** treat the cause (deworm, treat ulcer, stop the bleeding). Give oral ferrous sulphate 200 mg three times a day (or equivalent) with vitamin C for 3 to 6 months after the Hb is normal, and review the Hb after 2 to 4 weeks. Use IV iron if oral iron is not tolerated or absorbed. Give dietary advice.

## Section C: Essay questions (15 marks each, answer 2 only)

### 1. A 35-year-old presented with a history of confusion, fruity breath, polyuria and polydipsia. Random blood sugar is 25 mmol/L and serum ketones are 5.6 mmol/L. What is the probable diagnosis? What history, examination and investigations will be useful to arrive at this diagnosis? How would you manage this patient? (15 marks)

**Probable diagnosis:** diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).

**History:** known diabetes and insulin use, missed doses, recent infection (urine, chest), vomiting, abdominal pain, alcohol, drugs, new symptoms of weight loss and thirst.

**Examination:** dehydration (dry mucosa, poor skin turgor, low blood pressure, fast pulse), Kussmaul breathing and acetone breath, abdominal tenderness, reduced consciousness, and signs of infection, with a Glasgow Coma Scale and a search for the trigger.

**Investigations:** capillary and plasma glucose, **urine or blood ketones**, **arterial or venous blood gas** (pH below 7.3 and bicarbonate below 15), urea, electrolytes (potassium, sodium) and creatinine, full blood count, blood and urine cultures, ECG (potassium changes, ischaemia), chest X-ray, amylase, and pregnancy test.

**Management (ABC, then):**
1. **Fluids:** 0.9% saline 1 litre in the first hour, then about 1 litre every 2 hours as needed (total 4 to 6 litres over 24 hours), adding dextrose once glucose is below 14 mmol/L.
2. **Insulin:** fixed-rate IV soluble insulin at 0.1 units/kg/hour, started after the first fluid bolus and after checking that potassium is above 3.3.
3. **Potassium:** add to the fluids if potassium is below 5.5 and urine is passing; hold insulin if potassium is below 3.3.
4. Monitor glucose hourly, and ketones, bicarbonate and electrolytes every 2 hours. Avoid a fall in glucose faster than 5 mmol/L an hour (risk of cerebral oedema).
5. **Treat the precipitant** (antibiotics for infection). Use bicarbonate only if pH is below 6.9. Give thromboprophylaxis.
6. When eating, change to subcutaneous insulin with an overlap, and educate about sick-day rules.

### 2. A 42-year-old patient presents with headache, generalised joint pains, a malar rash, reduced urine output and bizarre behaviour. A preliminary test revealed a positive ANA. What is the diagnosis? What other history, examination and investigations are needed to make a diagnosis, and how would you manage this patient? (15 marks)

**Diagnosis:** systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) with renal (lupus nephritis) and neuropsychiatric involvement.

**History and examination:** photosensitivity, oral ulcers, hair loss, Raynaud's, pleuritic chest pain, miscarriages, drugs (hydralazine, procainamide), seizures and psychosis; malar and discoid rash, ulcers, arthritis, pericardial or pleural rub, oedema, hypertension and a mental state and neurological examination.

**Investigations:** full blood count (anaemia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia), ESR (raised) and CRP (usually normal), urea, creatinine, urinalysis and urine protein (haematuria, casts, proteinuria), **anti-dsDNA and anti-Sm**, complement C3 and C4 (low), antiphospholipid antibodies, Coombs test, **renal biopsy** (class), chest X-ray, ECG and echocardiogram, brain imaging and lumbar puncture if neuropsychiatric lupus is suspected. The diagnosis uses the ACR or SLICC criteria (4 of 11).

**Management:**
- **General:** sun protection (sunscreen), stop smoking, treat blood pressure (ACE inhibitor), vaccinate, and bone protection.
- **Hydroxychloroquine** for all patients.
- **Severe disease** (nephritis or CNS): high-dose IV methylprednisolone then oral prednisolone with **cyclophosphamide** or mycophenolate for induction, then azathioprine or mycophenolate for maintenance.
- **CNS features:** antipsychotics or anticonvulsants as needed.
- **Monitor** disease activity and complications, and manage pregnancy planning. Counsel about adherence and infection risk.

### 3. A 56-year-old male known hypertensive is brought in by his wife reporting that since he woke up 2 hours ago he has right-sided weakness and is not speaking. What is his probable diagnosis and differential diagnosis? What investigations are needed to make a diagnosis and how would you go about treating this patient? (15 marks)

**Probable diagnosis:** acute stroke (left middle cerebral artery territory), either ischaemic or haemorrhagic. Right hemiparesis with aphasia points to the dominant (left) hemisphere.

**Differential diagnosis:** hypoglycaemia, seizure with Todd's paralysis, migraine with aura, intracranial tumour or abscess, subdural haematoma, encephalitis, hypertensive encephalopathy, conversion disorder.

**Investigations (urgent):**
- **Blood glucose** immediately.
- **Non-contrast CT head** (urgent, within an hour) to tell haemorrhage from infarct. MRI if CT is unclear.
- ECG (atrial fibrillation), full blood count, urea, electrolytes, creatinine, lipids, clotting profile, ESR, HIV and syphilis serology where relevant, and troponin.
- Carotid Doppler, echocardiogram, and CT or MR angiography to look for the cause.

**Treatment:**
1. ABC, oxygen if hypoxic, a nil-by-mouth swallow test, and treat hypoglycaemia. Admit to a stroke unit.
2. **Ischaemic stroke within 4.5 hours:** IV **alteplase** if there is no contraindication (confirm onset time and blood pressure under 185/110); mechanical thrombectomy for large vessel occlusion. Otherwise give **aspirin 300 mg** after the CT excludes bleeding.
3. **Haemorrhagic stroke:** control blood pressure, reverse anticoagulation, treat raised intracranial pressure, and refer for neurosurgery if indicated.
4. Do not lower blood pressure aggressively in ischaemic stroke unless it is extreme (over 220/120) or thrombolysis is planned.
5. Prevent complications: DVT prophylaxis, pressure care, nutrition, and avoid fever and hyperglycaemia.
6. **Secondary prevention:** antiplatelet (aspirin plus clopidogrel briefly), statin, blood pressure control, treat atrial fibrillation with anticoagulation, stop smoking, and diabetes control.
7. **Rehabilitation:** physiotherapy, occupational therapy and speech therapy, with counselling for the family.
