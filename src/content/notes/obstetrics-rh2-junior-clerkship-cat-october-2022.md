## Question 1 — 5 marks

> **Differentiate normal and abnormal labour (5 marks)**

### Answer

**Normal labour**
1. Spontaneous onset in a low-risk pregnancy at **37–42 weeks**.
2. **Singleton fetus in vertex presentation**.
3. Regular, coordinated uterine contractions produce progressive cervical effacement and dilatation.
4. Progressive **descent of the presenting part** occurs.
5. Ends in spontaneous vaginal birth with **no significant maternal or fetal complication**.

**Abnormal labour**
- Labour deviates from the normal pattern because of abnormal uterine activity, abnormal cervical dilatation/descent, or maternal/fetal mechanical problems.
- Examples include **prolonged/arrested labour, obstructed labour, malpresentation/malposition and fetal compromise**.

## Question 2 — 5 marks

> **List 5 complications of cesarean section (5 marks)**

### Answer

Any five:
1. **Haemorrhage** — including intraoperative or postpartum haemorrhage.
2. **Infection** — wound infection, endometritis or pelvic infection.
3. **Venous thromboembolism** — DVT or pulmonary embolism.
4. **Injury to adjacent organs** — especially bladder, ureter or bowel.
5. **Anaesthetic complications** — e.g. aspiration, hypotension or drug-related complications.

Other acceptable complications include wound dehiscence, ileus, adhesions, transfusion-related complications and complications in subsequent pregnancies such as placenta praevia/accreta or uterine scar complications.

## Question 3 — 5 marks

> **List 5 prerequisites for vacuum delivery (5 marks)**

### Answer

Before vacuum-assisted vaginal delivery, ensure:

1. **Cervix is fully dilated**.
2. **Membranes are ruptured**.
3. **Fetal head is engaged and sufficiently low** in the pelvis.
4. **Fetal head position is known** and is compatible with vacuum delivery.
5. **Pelvis is clinically adequate and there is no significant cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD)**.

Also required in practice: informed consent, adequate analgesia/anaesthesia, an empty bladder, appropriate equipment and personnel, and readiness to perform caesarean section if the procedure fails. Vacuum extraction is generally avoided before **34 weeks** because of increased neonatal intracranial injury risk.

## Question 4 — 6 marks

> **Outline the normal mechanisms of labour (6 marks)**

### Answer

1. **Engagement and descent** — the biparietal diameter passes through the pelvic inlet and the head descends.
2. **Flexion** — the fetal chin moves towards the chest, presenting the smaller suboccipitobregmatic diameter.
3. **Internal rotation** — the occiput rotates anteriorly to align with the maternal pelvic outlet.
4. **Extension** — the head extends beneath the pubic symphysis and is delivered.
5. **Restitution and external rotation** — the head returns to its relationship with the shoulders, then rotates as the shoulders rotate internally.
6. **Expulsion** — the anterior and posterior shoulders are delivered, followed by the rest of the body.

## Question 5 — 5 marks

> **List 5 risk factors for breech presentation (5 marks)**

### Answer

Any five:
1. **Prematurity**.
2. **Multiple pregnancy**.
3. **Uterine abnormalities** — e.g. fibroids or congenital uterine anomalies.
4. **Placenta praevia/low-lying placenta**.
5. **Abnormal amniotic fluid volume** — oligohydramnios or polyhydramnios.

Other risk factors include fetal congenital abnormalities, previous breech presentation and some maternal pelvic factors.

## Question 6 — 5 marks

> **Outline 5 contraindications for external cephalic version (5 marks)**

### Answer

Any five important contraindications/conditions in which ECV should not be attempted include:

1. **Placenta praevia** or another condition in which vaginal delivery is contraindicated.
2. **Significant antepartum haemorrhage**.
3. **Current fetal compromise/abnormal fetal heart-rate pattern**.
4. **Severe oligohydramnios**.
5. **Significant fetal growth restriction**, particularly when associated with fetal compromise/abnormal Doppler findings.

Other situations requiring avoidance or specialist assessment include major uterine abnormalities/scars and multiple pregnancy, depending on the clinical circumstances.

## Question 7 — 10 marks

> **Define obstructed labour and briefly outline its management (10 marks)**

### Answer

### Definition

**Obstructed labour** is failure of the fetus to descend through the birth canal **despite adequate/strong uterine contractions because of a mechanical obstruction**.

### Management

1. **Recognise and assess the obstruction** — assess maternal condition, uterine contractions, cervical dilatation, fetal station/position, moulding and caput.
2. **Assess fetal wellbeing** — monitor the fetal heart rate and identify fetal compromise.
3. **Call for senior obstetric help** and arrange theatre/neonatal support where required.
4. **Stop oxytocin** if it is being used.
5. Establish **large-bore IV access** and correct dehydration/electrolyte abnormalities with appropriate IV fluids.
6. Insert a **urinary catheter** and monitor urine output.
7. Take appropriate **blood investigations and cross-match blood**, anticipating haemorrhage.
8. Give **broad-spectrum antibiotics** when indicated, particularly with prolonged labour, infection or operative delivery.
9. **Expedite delivery according to the cause and fetal/maternal condition**:
   - Caesarean section for a live fetus when vaginal delivery is not safely possible.
   - Operative vaginal delivery only when the prerequisites are fulfilled and the obstruction has been excluded.
   - Destructive operation may be considered for a dead fetus when appropriate and by a skilled clinician.
10. After delivery, actively manage and look for complications such as **postpartum haemorrhage, uterine rupture, genital tract trauma and sepsis**, and assess the newborn for hypoxia/trauma.

**High-yield causes:** CPD, fetal macrosomia, malpresentation/malposition, fetal anomalies such as hydrocephalus, pelvic masses and an abnormal/contracted pelvis.

## Question 8 — 5 marks

> **Outline 5 reasons why a partogram is important in obstetrics (5 marks)**

### Answer

1. **Monitors progress of labour** — particularly cervical dilatation and fetal descent.
2. **Detects abnormal or prolonged labour early**.
3. **Monitors maternal and fetal wellbeing** through recorded observations.
4. **Guides timely clinical decisions**, including referral, augmentation or operative delivery when indicated.
5. Provides a **written record for communication, continuity of care, audit and medico-legal documentation**.

## Question 9 — 4 marks

> **Define precipitate labour and list 3 complications of the same (4 mark)**

### Answer

### Definition

**Precipitate labour** is labour in which the fetus is delivered **within less than 3 hours from the onset of regular painful uterine contractions**.

### Complications

1. **Maternal genital tract trauma** — cervical, vaginal or perineal lacerations.
2. **Postpartum haemorrhage** — commonly related to genital tract trauma and/or uterine atony.
3. **Fetal/neonatal complications** — including birth trauma, intracranial haemorrhage or fetal hypoxia/asphyxia.

## Rapid revision — answers at a glance

1. **Normal vs abnormal labour:** normal = spontaneous, term, vertex, progressive, uncomplicated; abnormal = deviation from normal progress/condition.
2. **CS complications:** haemorrhage, infection, VTE, organ injury, anaesthetic complications.
3. **Vacuum prerequisites:** fully dilated cervix, ruptured membranes, engaged/low head, known position, adequate pelvis/no CPD.
4. **Mechanisms:** engagement/descent → flexion → internal rotation → extension → restitution/external rotation → expulsion.
5. **Breech risks:** prematurity, multiple pregnancy, uterine abnormality, placenta praevia, abnormal liquor.
6. **ECV contraindications:** placenta praevia, significant APH, fetal compromise, severe oligohydramnios, significant FGR/fetal compromise.
7. **Obstructed labour:** mechanical failure of descent despite adequate contractions; stabilize, assess, correct dehydration/infection, and expedite appropriate delivery.
8. **Partogram:** monitors progress/wellbeing, detects delay, guides intervention, improves communication/documentation.
9. **Precipitate labour:** delivery <3 hours; trauma, PPH, fetal trauma/hypoxia.
