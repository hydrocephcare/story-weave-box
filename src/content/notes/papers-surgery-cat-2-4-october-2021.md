## Question 1. A young man, drowsy, after blunt abdominal injury: pulse 100, BP 80/60, tenderness and guarding in the left hypochondrium

### (a) Likely diagnosis (2 marks)
**Ruptured spleen** (splenic injury) with haemorrhagic shock.

### (b) Initial investigations, with reasons (10 marks)
Resuscitation starts at the same time as the tests.
1. **Focused abdominal sonography for trauma (FAST):** a bedside test that detects free fluid in the abdomen within minutes, with no radiation. It is the key test in an unstable patient.
2. **Full blood count:** baseline haemoglobin and platelets (haemoglobin may be normal early).
3. **Group and crossmatch:** blood must be ready for transfusion.
4. **Urea, electrolytes and creatinine:** baseline renal function before contrast and during resuscitation.
5. **Arterial blood gas and lactate:** show the depth of shock and acidosis.
6. **Clotting profile:** to detect coagulopathy before surgery.
7. **Urinalysis** for haematuria (renal or bladder injury) and a pregnancy test in a woman.
8. **CT scan of the abdomen with IV contrast,** only if the patient responds to resuscitation: it grades the splenic injury and finds other organ injuries.
9. **Diagnostic peritoneal lavage** if FAST is not available or is equivocal in an unstable patient. More than 10 ml of gross blood is positive.
10. **Chest and pelvic X-ray** for associated rib, diaphragm and pelvic injury.

### (c) Initial management (6 marks)
Follow ATLS (ABCDE):
1. **Airway** with cervical spine protection.
2. **Breathing:** high-flow oxygen by non-rebreather mask, and check for associated chest injury.
3. **Circulation and haemorrhage control:** two large-bore cannulae, send bloods, give a warmed crystalloid bolus (1 to 2 L, Ringer's lactate or saline), then **blood transfusion** early if there is no response, and tranexamic acid within 3 hours.
4. **Disability:** GCS, pupils and glucose.
5. **Exposure:** undress fully, keep warm, and log-roll.
6. **Analgesia,** a urinary catheter, nil by mouth, and reassess continuously.
7. **Definitive care:** a **responder** who is stable is observed (non-operative management, with CT) in a high-dependency bed. A **non-responder** or unstable patient goes for **emergency laparotomy** and splenic repair or splenectomy. After splenectomy give pneumococcal, Hib and meningococcal vaccines and penicillin prophylaxis.

## Question 2. Three electrolyte abnormalities of refeeding syndrome (3 marks)
1. **Hypophosphataemia** (the hallmark)
2. **Hypokalaemia**
3. **Hypomagnesaemia**

Also thiamine deficiency and salt and water retention. It occurs when feeding is restarted after prolonged starvation, because insulin drives these ions into cells.

## Question 6. Which is thicker, 1/0 or 3/0 suture? (1 mark)
**1/0 is thicker.** The more zeros, the finer the suture (3/0 is thinner than 1/0).

## Question 7. Five steps in inserting a peripheral IV catheter (5 marks)
1. **Prepare:** wash hands, explain to the patient, gather the equipment and put on gloves.
2. **Apply a tourniquet** above the chosen vein and select a suitable vein.
3. **Clean the skin** with an alcohol or chlorhexidine swab and allow it to dry.
4. **Insert the cannula** bevel up at 15 to 30 degrees until **flashback** of blood appears, then advance the catheter slightly.
5. **Withdraw the stylet,** release the tourniquet, connect, **secure** with a dressing, flush with saline, and put the needle straight into the sharps box.

## Question 8. Placing a Foley catheter in a male patient (7 marks)
1. **Explain and obtain consent,** and check there is no contraindication (urethral injury).
2. **Position** the patient supine with legs apart, and prepare a **sterile trolley.**
3. **Wash hands,** wear sterile gloves, and drape the penis.
4. **Retract the foreskin** and clean the glans with antiseptic.
5. **Instil sterile lignocaine gel** into the urethra and wait 3 to 5 minutes.
6. **Hold the penis upright** (at 90 degrees to the body) to straighten the urethra, and **insert** the lubricated catheter to the hilt until urine flows.
7. **Inflate the balloon** with 10 ml of sterile water, and pull back gently until it rests at the bladder neck.
8. **Connect the drainage bag,** **replace the foreskin** (to avoid paraphimosis) and record the volume and any problems.

## Question 15. Routes of tumour metastasis, with examples (4 marks)
1. **Haematogenous:** through the blood, for example sarcomas and renal carcinoma to lung, liver and bone.
2. **Lymphatic:** to regional lymph nodes first, for example breast and cervical carcinoma.
3. **Transcoelomic (peritoneal) spread:** across body cavities, for example ovarian and gastric cancer (Krukenberg tumour).
4. **Direct local invasion,** and **implantation** along surgical or biopsy tracts (iatrogenic).

## Question 16. Types of intestinal motility (10 marks)
1. **Peristalsis:** a wave of contraction preceded by relaxation that propels contents onward, controlled by the enteric nervous system (Auerbach's plexus) and hormones (motilin, gastrin, CCK). A **peristaltic rush** is a rapid powerful wave that sweeps contents along the whole small bowel (for example in diarrhoea).
2. **Segmentation:** ring-like contractions that chop and mix chyme with digestive juices and expose it to the absorptive surface. It slows transit and increases absorption, and it is the main movement of the small intestine.
3. **Pendular movements:** longitudinal muscle contraction that moves the segment back and forth to mix the contents.
4. **Haustral contractions:** in the colon, circular muscle contracts at intervals and forms sac-like haustra that mix the contents and aid water absorption.
5. **Mass movements:** powerful peristaltic contractions in the colon, 1 to 3 times a day, that push faecal matter into the rectum and trigger defecation (gastrocolic reflex).
6. **Migrating motor complex:** a cleaning wave that sweeps the stomach and small bowel every 90 to 120 minutes in the fasting state.

## Question 19. Six pre-operative care points for acute appendicitis (3 marks)

2. **Analgesia** and antiemetics
3. **Nil by mouth**
4. **Pre-operative antibiotics** (for example ceftriaxone with metronidazole)
5. **Blood tests:** full blood count, urea and electrolytes, group and save
6. **Informed consent,** anaesthetic review and theatre booking

## Question 20. Medical treatment of hypokalaemic hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis, with an example (3 marks)
The paper says "hyperchloraemic", but the picture that goes with hypokalaemic alkalosis is **hypochloraemic** (for example, vomiting from pyloric obstruction).
1. **Replace volume and chloride** with IV **0.9% sodium chloride.**
2. **Replace potassium** with potassium chloride in the fluids, for example 20 to 40 mmol per litre.
3. Treat the cause (relieve the obstruction, give antiemetics, and a PPI), monitor electrolytes and give nothing by mouth until stable.

## Question 24. Benign breast disease and breast cancer risk (11 marks)
Risk is stratified by the **histology of the lesion** (aberrations of normal development and involution).
1. **No increased risk (non-proliferative):** cysts, simple fibroadenoma, duct ectasia, mild hyperplasia.
2. **Slightly increased risk (about 1.5 to 2 times; proliferative without atypia):** moderate or florid hyperplasia, sclerosing adenosis, **intraductal papilloma** and complex fibroadenoma, radial scar.
3. **Moderately increased risk (about 4 to 5 times; atypical hyperplasia):** **atypical ductal hyperplasia** and **atypical lobular hyperplasia.**
4. **Highest risk:** lobular carcinoma in situ (about 8 to 10 times).

A phyllodes tumour is a separate fibroepithelial neoplasm that needs wide excision.

## Question 25. Formula for cerebral perfusion pressure, with reference ranges (1 mark)
**CPP = MAP − ICP**
- Mean arterial pressure: about 70 to 110 mmHg
- Intracranial pressure: 5 to 15 mmHg
- CPP: normally about 60 to 80 mmHg. It must stay above 60 mmHg in head injury.
