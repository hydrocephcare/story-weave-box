## Multiple choice questions

### 1. The concept of symptoms assessed by descriptive psychopathology has both form and content as its components. Which of the following is true?
- A. Form provides sufficient information for management
- B. Form provides sufficient information for severity
- C. Form is more important than content
- D. Form provides sufficient information for diagnosis
- E. Content is more important than form

**Answer: D.** The form of a symptom (for example a hallucination heard in the third person) is what points to the diagnosis. The content (what the voice says) is shaped by the person's culture and life, and says little about the diagnosis.

### 2. Which of the following is NOT a type of paranoid delusion?
- A. 'Someone is following me.'
- B. 'People in the street are talking about me.'
- C. 'Aliens are making my body rot.'
- D. 'The Messiah is watching my every move.'
- E. 'Martians have landed on earth.'

**Answer: E.** Paranoid delusions are about being persecuted or watched by others (reference and persecution). A belief about Martians landing is not about the self.

### 3. A patient is experiencing increased brightness and acuity of visual objects. Intense perceptions occur in all of the following EXCEPT:
- A. Migraine
- B. Hallucinogens
- C. Mania
- D. Delirium
- E. Depression

**Answer: E.** Perceptions are intensified in mania, migraine, hallucinogen use and delirium. In depression the world looks dull and colourless.

### 4. A 12-year-old boy, at a school anniversary celebration, vividly describes what Martians may look like. The imagery is:
- A. A perception
- B. A fantasy
- C. A pseudo-hallucination
- D. An illusion
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Vivid imagery that the child knows is made up is fantasy (imagination). It is not a perception.

### 5. A 35-year-old lady reports hearing voices in her head. Which of the following differences between hallucination and pseudo-hallucination is true?
- A. In true hallucinations insight is often retained
- B. True hallucinations are often identified to be originating from self
- C. True hallucinations occur in subjective space
- D. True hallucinations are sought in other modalities by the patient
- E. True hallucinations cannot occur in two modalities simultaneously

**Answer: D.** A person with a true hallucination looks for the source (turns to see who is speaking) and checks it with other senses. In a pseudo-hallucination the person knows it comes from inside the mind, so insight is retained and the image is in subjective space.

### 6. Which of the following is NOT a common feature of schizophrenic auditory hallucinations?
- A. Being multiple
- B. Male voice
- C. Speaks in one's mother tongue
- D. Often continuously present
- E. Has a different accent

**Answer: D.** In schizophrenia the voices are usually several, commenting or talking about the patient, in the patient's own language. They come and go, and they are often not continuous, so this is the least typical option. The question is loosely worded.

### 7. Which of the following can cause visual hallucinations?
- A. Occipital lobe tumours
- B. Post-concussion states
- C. Hepatic failure
- D. Dementia
- E. All of the above

**Answer: E.** Visual hallucinations are organic until proved otherwise. All four are causes.

### 8. Which of the following drugs on withdrawal produce disturbed proprioceptive perceptions?
- A. Cannabis
- B. Amphetamines
- C. LSD
- D. Benzodiazepines
- E. Nicotine

**Answer: D.** Benzodiazepine (and alcohol) withdrawal causes perceptual distortions, such as a feeling that the floor is moving, with tremor, anxiety and seizures.

### 9. Which one of the following is NOT matched correctly?
- A. Form of thought: loosening of association
- B. Content of thought: persecutory belief
- C. Stream of thought: poverty of speech
- D. Form of thought: obsessions
- E. Form of thought: circumstantiality

**Answer: D.** Obsessions are intrusive, unwanted thoughts, a disorder of the possession and content of thought, and not a disorder of its form. The question is loosely worded.

### 10. Which one of the following is more common in manic rather than schizophrenic speech disturbance?
- A. Clanging
- B. Derailment
- C. Thought blocking
- D. Tangentiality
- E. Poverty of content of speech

**Answer: A.** Clanging (words linked by sound) is seen in mania. Derailment, thought blocking and poverty of content are more typical of schizophrenia.

### 11. A patient looks dishevelled, with unkempt hair and dirty, unwashed clothes. Self-neglect is commonly noted in all of the following EXCEPT:
- A. Alcoholism
- B. Chronic schizophrenia
- C. Depression
- D. Dementia
- E. Social phobia

**Answer: E.** Social phobia causes avoidance, not self-neglect.

### 12. A 57-year-old lady with many previous hospitalisations is brought by police to casualty. She is wearing full make-up, green lipstick, shiny green nail polish and green jewellery. She asks everyone at the admission unit to call her Ms Green. Which diagnosis is most consistent with this presentation?
- A. Mania
- B. Depression
- C. Panic attacks
- D. Obsessive compulsive disorder
- E. Learning disability

**Answer: A.** Flamboyant dress, a theme (green), over-familiarity and relapsing admissions point to mania.

### 13. A 33-year-old man attending an out-patient clinic turns towards his left and spits out, muttering unintelligibly. He does this at least three times in half an hour and appears very guarded. This gesture is suggestive of which of the following?
- A. Depression
- B. Suicidal thinking
- C. Low self-esteem
- D. Responding to hallucination
- E. Acute confusion

**Answer: D.** Repeated gestures, turning to one side and muttering while guarded suggest that he is hearing and answering voices.

### 14. Which of the following is the major difference between mood and affect in the mental state examination?
- A. Mood is short-lived while affect is longer lasting
- B. Mood is objective while affect is subjective
- C. Affect is transient but difficult to interpret
- D. Affect is transient and self-reported
- E. None of the above

**Answer: E.** Mood is the sustained, subjective emotional state reported by the patient. Affect is the observed, moment-to-moment emotional expression. Options A, B and D reverse this, so none is correct.

### 15. On physical examination, a person with a history of substance misuse has piloerection, dilated pupils, rhinorrhoea and is yawning frequently. Withdrawal from which of the following substances can cause this presentation?
- A. Cocaine
- B. Opiate
- C. Cannabis
- D. Alcohol
- E. Amphetamines

**Answer: B.** Opioid withdrawal gives yawning, goose flesh (piloerection), a runny nose, dilated pupils, cramps and diarrhoea.

### 16. A man admitted to the psychiatric intensive care unit with a manic episode received 10 mg of haloperidol intramuscularly as he had turned violent. He became unresponsive shortly afterwards. On examination there is confusion, labile BP, hyperthermia, rigidity and dysphagia. What is the most important differential diagnosis?
- A. Acute dystonia
- B. Neuroleptic malignant syndrome
- C. Tardive dyskinesia
- D. Akathisia
- E. Parkinsonism

**Answer: B.** Fever, rigidity, confusion and autonomic instability after an antipsychotic is neuroleptic malignant syndrome, a medical emergency.

### 17. Epilepsy associated with learning disability, shagreen patches and ash leaf macules are seen in which of the following?
- A. Epidermal naevus syndrome
- B. Tuberous sclerosis
- C. Neurofibromatosis
- D. Sturge-Weber syndrome
- E. Fabry's disease

**Answer: B.** Tuberous sclerosis causes epilepsy, learning disability, ash-leaf macules, shagreen patches and facial angiofibromas.

### 18. A 32-year-old school teacher is admitted for constipation and acute abdominal pain. She experiences visual and tactile hallucinations with intense anxiety. She develops motor weakness of her legs on administration of hypnotics and diclofenac. Which laboratory test is indicated?
- A. Serum lipid levels
- B. Serum folate
- C. Urine glucose
- D. Urine porphyrins
- E. Serum ceruloplasmin

**Answer: D.** Abdominal pain, constipation, neuropsychiatric symptoms and weakness precipitated by drugs is acute intermittent porphyria, and urine porphobilinogen and porphyrins make the diagnosis.

### 19. Which of the following is an advantage of using CT scan over MRI for diagnostic purposes?
- A. Finer details are seen easily with CT scan
- B. Absence of radiation exposure in CT scan
- C. CT scan is more suitable in pregnant women
- D. CT scan is more immediately available in emergencies
- E. Anterior fossa is better visualised with CT scan

**Answer: D.** CT is quicker and more widely available in emergencies. MRI shows finer detail and uses no radiation.

### 20. Which of the following nutrients, if deficient, can make treatment of depression difficult?
- A. B12
- B. Riboflavin
- C. Nicotinamide
- D. Folate
- E. Magnesium

**Answer: D.** Low folate is linked to a poorer response to antidepressants, and folate is needed for making monoamines. B12 deficiency also causes depression, so the question is loosely worded.

### 21. Which of the following is the most clinically useful method of diagnosing Alzheimer's disease?
- A. Clinical interview
- B. CT scans
- C. Functional MRI
- D. SPECT
- E. Lumbar puncture

**Answer: A.** The diagnosis is clinical, from a history of a progressive decline in memory and function, with an examination and cognitive testing. Scans are used to rule out other causes.

### 22. Which one of the following can increase plasma levels of lamotrigine?
- A. Lorazepam
- B. Chlorpromazine
- C. Valproate
- D. Carbamazepine
- E. Zopiclone

**Answer: C.** Valproate blocks the metabolism of lamotrigine and roughly doubles its level, so the dose must be halved. Carbamazepine lowers it.

### 23. A patient presents with recurrent episodes of feeling detached and unreal. A pharmacological agent that can worsen these symptoms is:
- A. Clozapine
- B. Caffeine
- C. Lamotrigine
- D. Clonazepam
- E. Valproate

**Answer: B.** Depersonalisation and derealisation are worsened by anxiety, and caffeine is a stimulant that increases anxiety. Clonazepam would reduce it, and the others are not typically implicated. The question is loosely worded.

### 24. The ECG changes produced by long-term lithium therapy mimic which one of the following electrolyte disturbances?
- A. Hyperkalaemia
- B. Hypokalaemia
- C. Hypocalcaemia
- D. Hypercalcaemia
- E. Hyperuricaemia

**Answer: B.** Lithium causes T wave flattening and inversion, which looks like hypokalaemia.

### 25. Which of the following postpartum disorders is correctly matched with its time of onset?
- A. Postpartum blues: within a few months of delivery
- B. Postpartum depression: first week of delivery
- C. Postpartum psychosis: within 2 weeks of delivery
- D. Postpartum pituitary apoplexy: 12 months after delivery
- E. All of the above are correct

**Answer: C.** Postpartum psychosis starts abruptly within the first 2 weeks. The blues come in the first week, and depression in the first weeks to months.

### 26. The most powerful predictors of recurrence of depressive episodes among the following are:
- A. Previous episodes of depression and presence of residual symptoms
- B. Non-bipolar diagnosis and later age of onset
- C. Female gender and earlier age of onset
- D. Higher degree of life events and family history of affective disorders
- E. Male gender and past history of psychiatric admission

**Answer: A.** The number of previous episodes and residual symptoms after treatment are the strongest predictors of relapse.

### 27. All of the following factors are significantly associated with increased risk of violence among those with schizophrenia EXCEPT:
- A. Comorbid substance use
- B. Comorbid personality disorder
- C. Acute psychotic symptoms
- D. Non-compliance with treatment
- E. Comorbid depression

**Answer: E.** Substance misuse, personality disorder, active psychosis and not taking treatment raise the risk of violence. Depression does not.

### 28. The most common group of mental disorders diagnosed among homicide offenders is:
- A. Personality disorders
- B. Substance use disorders
- C. Schizophreniform disorders
- D. Affective disorders
- E. Learning disabilities

**Answer: A.** In studies of homicide offenders with a mental disorder, personality disorder is the commonest diagnosis, and substance misuse is also very frequent. The answer depends on the study, so the question is loosely worded.

### 29. The most powerful predictor of overall functional outcome in children with autism is given by:
- A. Family history of autism
- B. Autistic symptom count
- C. Presence of soft neurological signs
- D. IQ level
- E. Non-verbal skill

**Answer: D.** A higher IQ (together with early language) predicts a better outcome in autism.

### 30. The estimated heritability of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is around:
- A. 10%
- B. 25%
- C. 33%
- D. 50%
- E. 70%

**Answer: E.** Twin studies give a heritability of about 70 to 75%.
