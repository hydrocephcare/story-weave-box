## Multiple choice questions

### 1. A 7-year-old girl has persisting fevers and a migratory murmur, and the doctor suspects infective endocarditis. Which is the truest answer that can be used to make the diagnosis according to the modified Duke criteria?
- A. 2 major
- B. 1 major and 3 minor
- C. 5 minor
- D. All the above
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Definite endocarditis needs 2 major criteria, or 1 major and 3 minor, or 5 minor criteria.

### 2. The most common organism that causes infective endocarditis on a native valve is:
- A. Streptococcus viridans
- B. Staphylococcus aureus
- C. E. coli
- D. Group B streptococcus
- E. HACEK group

**Answer: B.** Staphylococcus aureus is now the commonest cause on native valves. Viridans streptococci are the classic cause in children with pre-existing heart disease, so some texts give option A.

### 3. The following form part of the major categories in the Jones criteria for acute rheumatic fever EXCEPT:
- A. Polyarthritis
- B. Carditis
- C. Sydenham chorea
- D. Erythema multiforme
- E. Subcutaneous nodules

**Answer: D.** The skin sign is erythema marginatum, not erythema multiforme.

### 4. A newborn has worsening cyanosis that does not respond to high-flow oxygen. Which congenital heart condition is LEAST likely?
- A. Tricuspid atresia
- B. Transposition of the great vessels
- C. Truncus arteriosus
- D. Ventricular septal defect
- E. Hypoplastic left ventricle

**Answer: D.** An isolated VSD is acyanotic (left-to-right shunt).

### 5. A 1-year-old boy with frequent cyanotic spells is diagnosed with tetralogy of Fallot. He is expected to have the following findings EXCEPT:
- A. Overriding aorta
- B. Right ventricular hypertrophy
- C. Ventricular septal defect
- D. Pulmonary stenosis
- E. Atrial septal defect

**Answer: E.** An ASD is not part of the tetralogy (it becomes a pentalogy with an ASD).

### 6. In the management of a child with tetralogy of Fallot having an acute cyanotic spell, which medication is NOT used?
- A. Propranolol
- B. Sodium bicarbonate
- C. Morphine
- D. Phenobarbitone
- E. Oxygen

**Answer: D.** Treatment is knee-chest position, oxygen, morphine, fluids, bicarbonate and propranolol. Phenobarbitone has no role.

### 7. A 2-year-old born preterm has a machinery murmur at the left upper sternal border, bounding pulses and a wide pulse pressure. The most likely cardiac lesion is:
- A. Ventricular septal defect
- B. Aortic regurgitation
- C. Pulmonary regurgitation
- D. Patent ductus arteriosus
- E. Aortic regurgitation

**Answer: D.** A continuous machinery murmur with bounding pulses is a patent ductus, and prematurity is a risk factor. (Option E repeats B in the paper.)

### 8. A 10-year-old girl has difficulty breathing, swollen legs, periorbital oedema, a gallop rhythm, a loud murmur of mitral regurgitation and a tender enlarged liver. The most appropriate next step is:
- A. Do intense exercise to help with breathing and quicken recovery
- B. Advise a diet high in salt
- C. Start oxygen, bed rest and diuretics
- D. Start antibiotics
- E. Do a lumbar puncture

**Answer: C.** This is heart failure from rheumatic carditis. Rest, oxygen and diuretics come first, with anti-inflammatory therapy and salt restriction.

### 9. In a child with coarctation of the aorta, the following signs are expected EXCEPT:
- A. Radio-femoral delay
- B. Significant blood pressure difference between upper and lower limbs
- C. Severe central cyanosis
- D. Weak pulses in the lower limbs
- E. Signs of circulatory failure in the lower limbs

**Answer: C.** Coarctation is an acyanotic lesion.

### 10. Which sign is found in a patient with aortic regurgitation?
- A. Loud P2
- B. Pistol shots at the brachial pulse
- C. Absent S1
- D. Continuous machinery murmur at the apex
- E. Corrigan sign

**Answer: E.** Corrigan sign is the visible forceful (water-hammer) carotid pulsation. The pistol-shot sound is over the femoral artery, so option B is worded loosely.

### 11. Medication that should be given to keep the patent ductus open in children with a duct-dependent lesion includes:
- A. Prostaglandin E1
- B. Prostaglandin D1
- C. Prostaglandin I2
- D. Prostaglandin E2
- E. Indomethacin

**Answer: A.** Alprostadil (PGE1) keeps the duct open. Indomethacin and ibuprofen close it.

### 12. A 1-week-old preterm baby weighing 1.2 kg has a haemodynamically significant PDA. Which is the correct step in management?
- A. The patient can be observed until 1 year of age, then the PDA can be closed
- B. The ductus should be closed immediately since it is haemodynamically significant
- C. A trial of ibuprofen or indomethacin should be given to see if the ductus shall close
- D. There is no role for fluid restriction in this case
- E. Diuretics are dangerous at this age and should not be tried

**Answer: C.** In preterm babies, ibuprofen or indomethacin often closes the duct. Fluid restriction and diuretics also help. Surgery or device closure is kept for drug failure.

### 13. The most common cardiac lesion in children with Down syndrome is:
- A. Pulmonary stenosis
- B. Patent ductus arteriosus
- C. Atrioventricular canal defect (AVSD)
- D. Aortic regurgitation
- E. Tricuspid atresia

**Answer: C.** AV septal defect is the commonest lesion in Down syndrome.

### 14. Wairimu, 4 years, has a 2-day history of lower abdominal pain, painful micturition and a low-grade fever. The most likely urinalysis findings are:
- A. Red cell casts, 2+ protein, pyuria and red cells too numerous to count
- B. Numerous maltese crosses
- C. 4+ protein, 1+ ketones, 0 to 5 RBC/HPF, 0 to 5 WBC/HPF
- D. RBC and WBC casts, more than 50 RBC/HPF, 20 WBC/HPF and many bacteria
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Painful micturition with fever suggests a urinary tract infection, with white cells and bacteria in the urine. Option A is nephritis and option C is nephrotic syndrome. Option D is the nearest, though casts point higher up the tract.

### 15. An 8-year-old girl has a seizure, a blood pressure of 180/110 mmHg and several episodes of coca-cola coloured urine. She had a sore throat 2 weeks before. Choose the most correct statement.
- A. An urgent renal biopsy is needed to confirm a diagnosis of nephrotic syndrome
- B. Alport syndrome is a likely diagnosis in this child
- C. Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis is likely to be the diagnosis here
- D. An urgent haemogram and plan to transfuse should be the immediate next step as the child is most likely in cardiac failure due to anaemia
- E. None of the above statements are true

**Answer: C.** Cola-coloured urine, hypertension and a recent throat infection fit post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, and the seizure is hypertensive encephalopathy.

### 16. Concerning this child's condition, choose the true fact:
- A. Serotype 49 causes post-streptococcal nephritis after an upper respiratory infection, mainly in the cold season
- B. Serotype 12 causes post-streptococcal nephritis after a skin infection, usually in a warm climate
- C. PSGN usually develops 1 to 3 weeks after an acute infection with specific nephritogenic strains of group A beta-haemolytic streptococcus
- D. The incidence of GN is approximately 50% in persons with pharyngitis and 35% in those with skin infections

**Answer: C.** The rash or sore throat comes first, with 1 to 2 weeks after pharyngitis and 3 to 6 weeks after skin infection. Serotype 12 is the pharyngitis strain and serotype 49 is the skin strain, so A and B are swapped. Only a small proportion (under 15%) of infected people develop nephritis.

### 17. In the management of acute post-infectious glomerulonephritis, choose the most correct statement.
- A. Antibiotic therapy with penicillin in the early phase improves outcomes
- B. Loop diuretics, fluid restriction and antihypertensives have a role
- C. The prognosis in most cases is guarded and the disease takes a prolonged and protracted course
- D. Haematuria resolves in about a week after initiation of treatment and the prognosis is excellent in most cases
- E. None of the above is true

**Answer: B.** Treatment is supportive: salt and fluid restriction, a loop diuretic and antihypertensives. Penicillin eradicates the organism but does not change the kidney outcome, and the prognosis is excellent, but microscopic haematuria can persist for months.

### 18. A 3-year-old boy has oedema and shortness of breath, 3+ proteinuria, a serum albumin of 2 g/dL, and 3 g of protein in a 24-hour urine. A kidney biopsy would most likely reveal:
- A. Diffuse crescent formation
- B. Diffuse thickening of the glomerular basement membrane
- C. Mesangial cell proliferation
- D. Minimal change disease with fusion of podocyte foot processes
- E. All the above

**Answer: D.** Nephrotic syndrome in a young child is usually minimal change disease, seen only on electron microscopy as foot process fusion.

### 19. After consultation with a paediatric nephrologist, what would be the most appropriate combination of medications for the above patient?
- A. ACE inhibitor, furosemide and prednisone
- B. Prednisone, ACE inhibitor, albumin
- C. Prednisone, furosemide, albumin
- D. Furosemide, spironolactone, digoxin
- E. Furosemide, ACE inhibitor, IV immunoglobulin

**Answer: C.** Prednisolone is the main treatment, with albumin and a loop diuretic for severe oedema and effusions. ACE inhibitors are for steroid-resistant disease.

### 20. In regard to the pathophysiology of nephrotic syndrome in children, which statement is true?
- A. The predominant presentation is the gross oedema, which is attributed to progressive nephron loss and loss of kidney filtration capacity
- B. Hyperlipidaemia is a common presentation and is associated with coagulation defects
- C. Acute bacterial peritonitis is a common complication in patients with ascites
- D. Hypoalbuminaemia and reduced oncotic pressure are attributable to progressive loss of nephrons
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis is a common complication. Oedema comes from protein loss through leaky glomeruli, not loss of nephrons. Option B is also partly true (hyperlipidaemia and a clotting tendency occur together), so the question is loosely worded.

### 21. Radiological signs of rickets include all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Splaying
- B. Cupping
- C. Fraying
- D. Increased bone density
- E. Pathological fractures

**Answer: D.** Rickets thins the bones.

### 22. Risk factors for developing rickets include all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Lack of exposure to sunshine
- B. Chronic diarrhoea
- C. Taking a diet rich in dairy
- D. Chronic kidney disease

### 23. A 14-year-old who is a pure vegetarian is most likely to suffer from which vitamin deficiency?

- B. Vitamin C
- C. Vitamin B12
- D. Vitamin B1

- B. Hypopigmented hair
- C. Dermatitis
- D. Mental challenge

### 25. A neural tube defect is prevented by:
- A. Folic acid

### 26. Which nutrient reduces morbidity and mortality in children with severe measles?

### 27. Julia is a 24-month-old girl. She is expected to do all the following at her age EXCEPT:

- C. Make a tower of 7 cubes

### 28. In terms of language development in a child, which statement is true?

### 29. A 12-year-old girl has sparse, lightly pigmented straight pubic hair and a small elevated mound of the breast and papilla. In what stage of sexual maturity is she?

### 30. A severely malnourished child has diarrhoea, fever and is unable to feed. The following should be addressed in the first day of treatment EXCEPT:

### 31. Which is an expected laboratory finding in the malnourished child above?

### 35. A normal 3-year-old should be able to:

### 36. The sense that is not fully developed at birth is:
- A. Hearing
- B. Smell
- C. Taste
- D. Touch
- E. Vision

**Answer: E.** Vision matures over the first few years.

### 37. Which of the following regarding childhood tuberculosis is true?
- A. Pulmonary cavitation is common
- B. The Mantoux test is useful even with a previous history of BCG vaccination
- C. The diagnostic yield of Mycobacterium from gastric aspirates is more than 40%
- D. Streptomycin, rifampicin and isoniazid are the recommended initial therapy
- E. Meningitis may be the first presentation

**Answer: B.** A positive Mantoux still counts after BCG, with the size and the clinical picture guiding interpretation. Cavities are uncommon in young children, gastric aspirate yield is about 30 to 40%, and the initial regimen is RHZE. Option E is also true, so the question is loosely worded.

### 38. A 3-year-old boy has cough, difficulty breathing and wheezing, with a strong family history of allergy. The following can be treatment EXCEPT:
- A. Steroids
- B. Sedatives
- C. Theophyllines
- D. Terbutaline
- E. Oxygen

**Answer: B.** Sedatives can suppress breathing.

### 39. A 2-year-old has inspiratory stridor, a hoarse voice and a barking cough, preceded by a runny nose and low-grade fever for 3 days. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Croup
- B. Laryngomalacia
- C. Foreign body obstruction
- D. Acute bronchiolitis
- E. Choanal atresia

**Answer: A.** Barking cough, hoarseness and stridor after a cold is laryngotracheobronchitis.

### 40. The most common site of aspiration in a child lying supine is:
- A. The lower lobe of the left lung
- B. The lower lobe of the right lung
- C. The upper lobe of the left lung
- D. None of the above
- E. All of the above

**Answer: B.** The right main bronchus is wider and more vertical.

### 41. Baby John was born at term. His mother had a difficult labour with thick meconium-stained liquor. John has respiratory distress soon after resuscitation. Which statement is true?
- A. The baby is most likely to have respiratory distress syndrome
- B. Most likely the baby has pneumonia
- C. Its prevalence is affected by the gender of the baby
- D. Specific procedures for this condition before delivery would have prevented this occurrence
- E. Prior antibiotic treatment of the mother is beneficial in this condition

**Answer: A.** The picture is meconium aspiration syndrome, a cause of respiratory distress in a term baby. Of the options offered, respiratory distress is the nearest.

### 42. In a 3-week-old baby with a serum bilirubin of 275 micromol/L, which finding supports a possible diagnosis of biliary atresia?
- A. Absence of dark urine and pale stools
- B. Fevers
- C. Presence of dark urine and pale stools
- D. Tender hepatomegaly
- E. Ultrasound is able to visualise the gall bladder

**Answer: C.** Conjugated bilirubin spills into the urine, and none reaches the gut, so the stool is pale.

### 43. Mary, a 10-month-old girl, has cough, wheezing and fever, with sneezing and watery rhinorrhoea, and no history of atopy. The most probable diagnosis is:
- A. Pneumonia
- B. Tuberculosis
- C. Acute bronchiolitis
- D. Asthma
- E. Foreign body aspiration

**Answer: C.** A viral cold followed by wheeze in an infant under 1 year is bronchiolitis.

### 44. About kangaroo mother care, which statement is true?

### 45. Concerning epiglottitis, which statement is true?

- E. It is preceded by a flu-like illness for 2 to 3 days before symptoms occur

### 46. Risk factors for neonatal sepsis include all EXCEPT:

### 47. The correct regimen for management of miliary tuberculosis in children is:

### 60. Concerning the chest X-ray, which statement is true?
- A. The cardiothoracic ratio should be more than 50%
- B. The AP view is better for interpreting the cardiac size than the PA view

**Answer: C.** The cardiothoracic ratio is normally under 50% (up to 60% in newborns), and the PA view gives the truest heart size. X-rays should always be labelled, and poor films mislead anyone.

### 61. Aisha, 3 years, has had an upper respiratory tract infection followed by acute diarrhoea, and now has anaemia, a low platelet count, azotaemia and reduced urine output. She most likely has:
- A. Severe dehydration with renal failure
- B. Acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis
- C. Severe septicaemia
- D. Haemolytic uraemic syndrome
- E. Toxic nephropathy

**Answer: D.** Anaemia (haemolysis), low platelets and kidney failure after diarrhoea is the triad of haemolytic uraemic syndrome.

### 62. In chronic kidney disease, which finding is characteristic?
- A. Metabolic alkalosis
- B. Hypokalaemia
- C. Hypophosphataemia
- D. Hypocalcaemia
- E. Low uric acid

**Answer: D.** Failing kidneys cannot activate vitamin D, so calcium falls. They also cause metabolic acidosis, high potassium, high phosphate and high uric acid.

### 63. Proven interventions that prevent diarrhoea and pneumonia in children include the following EXCEPT:
- A. Vaccination against measles, pertussis, pneumococcus, Hib and rotavirus
- B. Prevention of HIV in children
- C. Co-trimoxazole prophylaxis for HIV-infected children
- D. Hand washing with soap
- E. Oral zinc

**Answer: E.** Zinc is given as part of the treatment of a diarrhoea episode. The other four are preventive measures. (Zinc supplementation also has a preventive role, so the question is loosely worded.)

### 64. A newborn should be given hepatitis B immunisation and specific immunoglobulin if:
- A. The mother is Chinese
- B. The mother is HBsAg negative
- C. The mother is HBsAg and anti-HBe positive
- D. The mother is anti-HBc positive
- E. The mother is HIV positive

**Answer: C.** A mother who carries the surface antigen can infect her baby at birth, so the baby gets vaccine and immune globulin within 12 hours.

### 65. Which statement about childhood vaccination schedules in Kenya is true?
- A. BCG is not important since the efficacy is very low
- B. Rotavirus vaccine should be given up to 6 months of age if missed
- C. Oral polio vaccine can be given at least once even if injectable polio vaccine has been given, to elicit mucosal immunity
- D. A child with severe immunosuppression should be given live vaccines
- E. There is no need to give any DPT booster after the 14-week vaccinations

**Answer: C.** OPV gives gut immunity and is still used with IPV. BCG protects against severe childhood TB, live vaccines are avoided in severe immunosuppression, and the rotavirus first dose cannot be given after 15 weeks (the last after 32 weeks). Option E is true of the routine schedule, so the question is loosely worded.

### 66. In a young child with a 4-day history of bloody diarrhoea:
- A. Shigellosis is a likely diagnosis
- B. The finding of cysts of E. histolytica in the stools confirms a diagnosis of acute amoebic dysentery
- C. Cholera is a likely diagnosis

- E. Giardiasis is the most common likely diagnosis

**Answer: A.** Bloody diarrhoea is dysentery, and Shigella is the commonest cause. Cysts alone do not prove invasive disease, and cholera and giardia do not cause bloody stools.

### 69. A 10-day-old newborn has intermittent stridor. The most common cause would be:

## Section B and Section C: essay questions

### 1. Section B, Question 1(a). Outline the benefits of breastfeeding (5 marks).

**Benefits to the baby**

1. **Best nutrition:** the right balance of protein, fat, lactose and vitamins, easy to digest, and it changes as the baby grows (colostrum, then mature milk).
2. **Protection against infection:** IgA, lactoferrin, lysozyme and white cells lower diarrhoea, pneumonia, ear infections and neonatal sepsis.
3. **Better growth and brain development:** long-chain fatty acids, and breastfed children score higher on cognitive tests.
4. **Lower risk of later disease:** less obesity, type 2 diabetes, allergy and sudden infant death.
5. **Bonding and temperature:** skin contact promotes bonding and keeps the baby warm.

**Benefits to the mother**

1. **Less bleeding after delivery:** oxytocin contracts the uterus.
2. **Child spacing:** exclusive breastfeeding in the first 6 months gives natural protection against pregnancy (lactational amenorrhoea).
3. **Lower risk of breast and ovarian cancer.**
4. **Faster return to pre-pregnancy weight.**

**Benefits to the family and society**

1. It is **free, clean and always ready** at the right temperature.
2. **Saves money** on formula and on treating illness, and lowers child deaths.

### 2. Section B, Question 1(b). Describe the physiology of milk let-down (5 marks).

The milk let-down (ejection) reflex is a **neuro-endocrine reflex** that lets milk already made in the breast reach the baby.

1. **Stimulus:** the baby suckles, which stimulates sensory nerve endings in the nipple and areola. Seeing, hearing or thinking about the baby can also trigger it.
2. **Nerve pathway:** impulses travel up the spinal cord to the **hypothalamus**.
3. **Hormone release:** the hypothalamus signals the **posterior pituitary** to release **oxytocin** into the blood.
4. **Action on the breast:** oxytocin reaches the breast and makes the **myoepithelial cells around the alveoli contract**, squeezing milk into the ducts and towards the nipple.
5. **Result:** the milk flows (the let-down). A mother may feel a tingling in the breast, milk dripping from the other breast, and uterine cramps.
6. **Effects of the mother's state:** stress, pain and anxiety block the reflex, and confidence and relaxation help it.

**Compare with milk production:** suckling also releases **prolactin** from the anterior pituitary, which makes the milk (the milk-making reflex).

### 3. Section B, Question 2. Outline the components of the cold chain system (10 marks).

The cold chain is the system that keeps vaccines at the right temperature (2 to 8 degrees Celsius for most vaccines) from the manufacturer to the child.

**1. Storage equipment**
- Cold rooms and freezer rooms at national and regional stores, with back-up generators.
- Refrigerators and freezers at district and facility level (ice-lined or solar-powered refrigerators).

**2. Transport equipment**
- Refrigerated vehicles, cold boxes and vaccine carriers with frozen ice packs, used for outreach and moving vaccines between stores.

**3. Temperature monitoring devices**
- Thermometers in every refrigerator, with twice-daily readings on a chart.
- Vaccine vial monitors (VVM), freeze indicators (such as the freeze-tag) and data loggers.

**4. People and management**
- Trained staff in charge of the cold chain, vaccine stock management, and a plan for breakdowns and power cuts.
- Vaccine handling rules: first-expiry-first-out, opened-vial policy, and keeping the diluent cold.

**5. Maintenance and support**
- Preventive maintenance and repair of the equipment, and a reliable power supply (with solar or gas backup).

**Storage temperatures:** BCG, measles-rubella and oral polio vaccines (freeze-tolerant: OPV) are stored at 2 to 8 degrees (OPV can be frozen), and the freeze-sensitive vaccines (tetanus toxoid, DPT, hepatitis B, pentavalent, pneumococcal, and rotavirus) must **never be frozen**.

### 4. Section C, Question 1. A 2-year-old girl has cough and difficulty in breathing for 1 day, with no fever. She has had daily night coughs, and coughs on exercise and on laughing. Her father and mother have a history of severe atopy. She has food allergy and eczema. On examination she is in respiratory distress with bilateral wheezes. (a) What is the most likely diagnosis (1 mark)? (b) What are the risk factors for developing this condition (4 marks)? (c) What are 3 other differentials for wheezing in this child (3 marks)? (d) Describe the treatment for this child in an acute setting (12 marks).

**(a) Most likely diagnosis:** **Bronchial asthma** (an acute exacerbation). Wheeze with night cough, cough on exercise and laughing, and atopy (eczema, food allergy and family history) points to it.

**(b) Risk factors for asthma**

1. **Atopy:** personal (eczema, allergic rhinitis, food allergy) and a family history of atopy or asthma.
2. **Genetic factors** (a strong family tendency).
3. **Environmental triggers and exposure:** house dust mite, pollen, pets, cockroach and mould; tobacco smoke (including in pregnancy); air pollution and smoke from indoor cooking fires.
4. **Respiratory infections:** viral infections in early life (especially RSV).
5. **Others:** male sex in childhood, prematurity and low birth weight, obesity, and not being breastfed.

**(c) Other differentials for wheeze in a 2-year-old**

1. Bronchiolitis (viral, usually under 2 years, with fever).
2. Foreign body aspiration (sudden onset, often one-sided).
3. Gastro-oesophageal reflux with aspiration.
4. Others: pneumonia, tuberculosis, cystic fibrosis, congenital heart disease with heart failure, and tracheobronchomalacia.

**(d) Treatment in an acute setting**

*Assess first:* the airway, breathing and circulation, the oxygen saturation, the respiratory rate and the severity (mild, moderate or severe: unable to speak or feed, cyanosis, silent chest and exhaustion).

1. **Oxygen** to keep saturation at 94% or above (by nasal prongs or mask), and a calm, comfortable position.
2. **Inhaled short-acting beta-2 agonist (salbutamol):** 2 to 4 puffs by metered-dose inhaler and spacer (or a nebuliser at 2.5 mg), repeated every 20 minutes for the first hour as needed, then 4-hourly or as the child needs. Add **ipratropium** in severe attacks.
3. **Systemic corticosteroids early:** oral prednisolone 1 to 2 mg/kg/day (maximum 40 mg) for 3 to 5 days, or IV hydrocortisone if the child cannot take oral medicine.
4. **For severe or life-threatening asthma:** IV magnesium sulphate, IV salbutamol or aminophylline, adrenaline, and admission to the high-dependency unit, with ventilation if exhausted.
5. **Fluids** and treat dehydration, and keep feeding. Avoid sedatives.
6. **Antibiotics only if** there is a bacterial infection.
7. **Monitor** the response (saturation, respiratory rate, work of breathing), and **admit** if poor response, saturation under 92%, or the child cannot feed.
8. **At discharge:** give a written asthma plan, teach inhaler and spacer technique, avoid triggers, and start a **controller** (inhaled corticosteroid) if attacks are frequent. Follow up the child in clinic.

### 5. Section C, Question 2. Write short notes on acute rheumatic fever. (a) What is the most common infection and the organism implicated in the development of the condition (5 marks)? (b) What are the major criteria for diagnosis (5 marks)? (c) Describe the management of acute rheumatic fever (10 marks).

**(a) The infection and organism**

Acute rheumatic fever follows a throat infection (**pharyngitis or tonsillitis**) with **Lancefield group A beta-haemolytic streptococcus (Streptococcus pyogenes)**, 2 to 4 weeks earlier. It is an immune reaction: antibodies to the streptococcal M protein cross-react with heart, joint, brain and skin tissue (molecular mimicry). It mainly affects children aged 5 to 15 years, and is linked to poverty and overcrowding.

**(b) Major criteria (the Jones criteria)**

1. **Carditis** (pancarditis: endocarditis with a murmur such as mitral regurgitation, myocarditis and pericarditis).
2. **Polyarthritis** (migratory, affecting large joints).
3. **Sydenham chorea** (purposeless jerky movements).
4. **Erythema marginatum** (a pink rash with a clear centre on the trunk).
5. **Subcutaneous nodules** (painless, over bony prominences).

**Minor criteria:** fever, arthralgia, raised ESR or CRP, and a prolonged PR interval.

**Diagnosis:** evidence of preceding group A streptococcal infection (raised ASO titre, positive throat culture or rapid antigen test) **plus 2 major criteria, or 1 major and 2 minor criteria.**

**(c) Management**

1. **Admit and rest:** bed rest until the inflammation settles (longer with carditis).
2. **Eradicate the streptococcus:** benzathine benzylpenicillin 1.2 million units IM once (or 10 days of oral penicillin V). Erythromycin if allergic to penicillin.
3. **Anti-inflammatory treatment:** aspirin (or another NSAID) for arthritis (the arthritis responds within 24 to 48 hours), and **prednisolone** (1 to 2 mg/kg/day) for moderate to severe carditis, tapered over weeks.
4. **Heart failure:** oxygen, a loop diuretic, ACE inhibitors, salt and fluid restriction. Digoxin is used with caution (the inflamed heart is sensitive to it).
5. **Chorea:** a calm environment, and sodium valproate, carbamazepine or haloperidol for severe cases.
6. **Secondary prophylaxis (key):** benzathine penicillin 1.2 million units IM every 3 to 4 weeks (or oral penicillin V daily, or erythromycin). Duration: at least 5 years or until age 18 (whichever is longer) with no carditis, 10 years or until age 21 with carditis and no residual valve disease, and lifelong (or to age 40) with persisting valve disease.
7. **Treat valve disease:** follow up with echocardiography, and surgery (valve repair or replacement) for severe lesions.
8. **Prevention and education:** treat sore throats early, improve living conditions, and give dental prophylaxis against endocarditis when there is valve disease.
