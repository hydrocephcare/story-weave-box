## Multiple choice questions

### 1. An 18 year old lady complains of vaginal discharge which started 2 days ago. She also complains of dysuria. She is sexually active with one partner and uses condoms intermittently. While treating this patient's gonorrhoea infection, treatment must also be given for which of the following?
- A. Bacterial vaginosis
- B. Chlamydia
- C. Herpes
- D. Syphilis

**Answer: B.** Gonorrhoea and chlamydia often occur together, so treatment for gonorrhoea always covers chlamydia too (for example ceftriaxone plus azithromycin or doxycycline).

### 2. Which of the following will NOT present with acute pelvic pain?
- A. Candida albicans infection
- B. Tubo-ovarian abscess
- C. Mittelschmerz
- D. Septic abortion

**Answer: A.** Candida causes itching and a curdy discharge. The others all cause acute lower abdominal or pelvic pain.

### 3. Which of the following is unlikely to be a feature of ovarian torsion?
- A. Presents usually after sustained immobility
- B. Usually ipsilateral
- C. Can be reduced by oral contraceptives
- D. Can be caused by previous abdominal surgery

**Answer: A.** Torsion presents with sudden severe one-sided pain, often after sudden movement or exercise, with nausea and vomiting, and not after immobility. Risk factors are an ovarian mass, pregnancy and ovarian stimulation. The question is loosely worded.

### 4. You will suspect pelvic inflammatory disease in all EXCEPT:
- A. Early coitarche
- B. Previous abortion
- C. Previous contraceptive use
- D. Previous ectopic pregnancy

**Answer: C.** Early first intercourse (more partners), a previous abortion (ascending infection) and a previous ectopic pregnancy (tubal damage from earlier infection) all point towards PID. Contraceptive use alone does not, and barrier methods and the combined pill are even slightly protective. The question is loosely worded.

### 5. During the menstrual cycle:
- A. The proliferative phase is relatively constant
- B. Ovulation coincides with menstruation
- C. Anovulatory cycles are characteristically painless
- D. Menstrual blood loss is usually 150 mls

**Answer: C.** Pain (dysmenorrhoea) is driven by prostaglandins made after ovulation, so anovulatory cycles are usually painless. The luteal phase is the constant one (about 14 days), ovulation is mid-cycle, and normal loss is under 80 ml.

### 6. A woman complains of a milky whitish discharge with a fishy odour. There is no history of itching. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Bacterial vaginosis
- B. Candida albicans
- C. Trichomonas
- D. Malignancy

**Answer: A.** A thin grey-white fishy-smelling discharge without itching is bacterial vaginosis (clue cells, pH above 4.5, positive whiff test).

### 7. A 28 year old complains of amenorrhoea after being treated in hospital following an incomplete miscarriage. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Post-abortion endometritis
- B. Pelvic inflammatory disease
- C. Asherman's syndrome
- D. Kallmann syndrome

**Answer: C.** Curettage damages the basal endometrium and causes intrauterine adhesions (Asherman syndrome), with secondary amenorrhoea. Kallmann syndrome is a cause of primary amenorrhoea.

### 8. The main cause of abnormal uterine bleeding in a 13 year old girl is:
- A. Trauma
- B. Ectopic pregnancy
- C. Anovulation
- D. Polycystic ovarian disease

**Answer: C.** In the first years after menarche the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis is immature, so cycles are anovulatory.

### 9. Which of the following is used as an emergency contraceptive?
- A. Low dose combined contraceptive
- B. Levonorgestrel
- C. Ergometrine
- D. Depo-Provera

**Answer: B.** Levonorgestrel 1.5 mg, within 72 hours (and up to 120 hours with decreasing effect).

### 10. The following apply to a pelvic abscess EXCEPT:
- A. Can be caused by Neisseria gonorrhoeae
- B. Can cause multiple organ failure
- C. Can cause chronic pelvic pain
- D. Can cause amenorrhoea

**Answer: D.** A pelvic abscess is caused by ascending infection, and can lead to sepsis and organ failure, and to chronic pain. It does not itself cause amenorrhoea.

## Section A: short answer questions

### 1. Question 1. (a) What are ovarian cysts (2 marks)? (b) What are the 3 main broad classifications (3 marks)? (c) List one type of each (3 marks). (d) On ultrasound list 2 benign and 3 malignant features (5 marks).

**(a) Ovarian cysts**

Fluid-filled sacs in or on the ovary. Most are benign and many are found by chance. They can be physiological (part of the normal cycle) or pathological.

**(b) Three broad classifications**

1. **Functional (physiological) cysts**
2. **Benign neoplastic cysts**
3. **Malignant (neoplastic) cysts**

**(c) One type of each**

- Functional: follicular cyst (also corpus luteum cyst).
- Benign neoplastic: serous or mucinous cystadenoma, or mature cystic teratoma (dermoid cyst).
- Malignant: serous cystadenocarcinoma (epithelial ovarian cancer).

**(d) Ultrasound features**

*Benign (any 2):* unilocular, thin walls, anechoic (clear) fluid, size under 5 cm, no solid parts, no internal blood flow, no ascites, unilateral.

*Malignant (any 3):* multilocular with thick septa, solid or papillary areas, irregular thick walls, large size, bilateral, ascites, increased internal blood flow on Doppler, and peritoneal deposits.

### 2. Question 2. (a) What is abnormal menstruation (2 marks)? (b) List and briefly explain two examples of abnormal menstruation (4 marks).

**(a) Abnormal menstruation**

Any menstruation outside the normal pattern of a cycle of 21 to 35 days, bleeding for 2 to 7 days, and losing about 30 to 80 ml of blood. The problem can lie in the cycle length, the regularity, the duration or the amount.

**(b) Examples**

1. **Menorrhagia (heavy menstrual bleeding):** regular cycles but excessive loss (over 80 ml, or bleeding that interferes with daily life), for example from fibroids, adenomyosis or bleeding disorders.
2. **Oligomenorrhoea:** infrequent periods, with a cycle longer than 35 days, usually from anovulation as in polycystic ovary syndrome.

Others you could use: polymenorrhoea (cycle under 21 days), amenorrhoea (no periods), metrorrhagia (irregular bleeding between periods) and dysmenorrhoea (painful periods).

### 3. Question 3. (a) Define a congenital abnormality (2 marks). (b) What is a teratogen (2 marks)? (c) What is a mutagen (2 marks)? (d) Describe Klinefelter's syndrome (3 marks).

**(a) Congenital abnormality**

A structural or functional defect that is present at birth. It may come from genetic or chromosomal causes, from environmental factors in pregnancy, or from both.

**(b) Teratogen**

An agent that causes a structural or functional abnormality in the developing embryo or fetus. Examples are thalidomide, alcohol, isotretinoin, warfarin, phenytoin, rubella and ionising radiation.

**(c) Mutagen**

An agent that causes a permanent change in the DNA (a mutation). Examples are ionising radiation, ultraviolet light and some chemicals. A mutagen may or may not be a teratogen.

**(d) Klinefelter syndrome**

- **Genetics:** a male with an extra X chromosome (47,XXY), from non-disjunction.
- **Features:** tall with long legs, small firm testes, gynaecomastia, little body hair, a high-pitched voice, and sometimes mild learning difficulty.
- **Effects:** hypogonadism (low testosterone, raised FSH and LH) and infertility from azoospermia.
- **Diagnosis and care:** karyotype. Testosterone replacement. Assisted reproduction may help.

### 4. Question 4. (a) What is PID (2 marks)? (b) List known causes of PID (4 marks). (c) Briefly describe treatment options for a pelvic abscess (6 marks).

**(a) Pelvic inflammatory disease**

An infection of the upper female genital tract (the uterus, fallopian tubes, ovaries and the pelvic peritoneum), usually ascending from the cervix and vagina.

**(b) Causes**

- ***Neisseria gonorrhoeae*** and ***Chlamydia trachomatis***: the commonest.
- Anaerobes, *Gardnerella vaginalis*, *Mycoplasma genitalium* and enteric organisms (*E. coli*, *Bacteroides*).
- **Predisposing events:** instrumentation (IUCD insertion, hysterosalpingography, curettage), abortion and the puerperium, and multiple sexual partners or early sexual debut.
- Spread from nearby sites (for example appendicitis), and *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*.

**(c) Treatment of a pelvic abscess**

1. **Admit and resuscitate:** IV fluids, analgesia and oxygen if needed.
2. **Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics**, covering gonococcus, chlamydia and anaerobes. For example ceftriaxone with doxycycline and metronidazole, or clindamycin with gentamicin. Then switch to oral antibiotics, for 14 days in all.
3. **Drainage** if there is no response in 48 to 72 hours or the abscess is large (over 8 cm): ultrasound-guided or CT-guided drainage, or a **laparoscopic** or open drainage.
4. **Surgery** in a ruptured abscess or peritonitis: laparotomy, with washout, and in severe cases a hysterectomy with salpingo-oophorectomy.
5. **Remove the IUCD** if one is in place.
6. **Treat the partner** and counsel on safe sex, and test for HIV and syphilis.
7. **Follow-up:** the risks are infertility, ectopic pregnancy and chronic pelvic pain.
