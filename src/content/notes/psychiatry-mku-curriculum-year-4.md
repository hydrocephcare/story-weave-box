# Psychiatry and Mental Health Curriculum: Year 4 (MKU)

## Key points

- Year 4 psychiatry is the **Junior Clerkship in Psychiatry and Mental Health**, with two lecture units: **MBPS 4537** (I) and **MBPS 4539** (II), each 26 hours and 2 credits.
- Unit I builds the **foundations**: psychopathology, psychopharmacology, psychiatric disorders, psychological therapies and psychiatric emergencies.
- Unit II is about **clinical skills**: history, mental state examination, formulation, investigations and managing acute mental illness.
- Assessment: **continuous assessment 30%**, **logbook 10%**, **end-of-clerkship examination 60%** (MCQ and SAQ, plus OSCE and viva).
- Prerequisite: successful completion of Year 1, 2 and 3 MBChB.

*From the Psychiatry and Mental Health MBChB Curriculum, Mount Kenya University School of Medicine. The weekly teaching plan is in the Year 4 Psychiatry course outline.*

## MBPS 4537: Junior Clerkship in Psychiatry and Mental Health I

**Purpose:** to equip learners with foundational knowledge and skills to understand the psychopathology of common psychiatric disorders.

### Expected learning outcomes

1. Understand the psychopathology of common mental disorders.
2. Understand psychopharmacology in psychiatry.
3. Formulate a diagnosis by exploring differential diagnoses of common mental illnesses.
4. Become familiar with the medical treatment commonly prescribed to psychiatric patients.
5. Apply research principles in mental health.
6. Explain the principles of psychopharmacology and non-pharmacological interventions.
7. Show awareness of the cultural, social and legal aspects of mental health in Kenya.
8. Appreciate the importance of mental health promotion, stigma reduction and community psychiatry.

### Course outline

| Topic | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Introduction to psychopathology** | Phenomenology and classification; theoretical models; normal and abnormal thoughts; signs versus symptoms versus syndrome; causes, prognosis and comorbidity; disorders of thought, perception, mood and affect, psychomotor behaviour, speech, memory and cognition; DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 |
| **Introduction to psychopharmacology** | Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics; neurotransmitters and dopamine pathways; antidepressants, anxiolytics and hypnotics, antipsychotics, mood stabilisers, ADHD medication; special populations; adherence and polypharmacy |
| **Psychiatric disorders** | Schizophrenia spectrum and other psychotic disorders; catatonia; bipolar and related disorders |
| **Psychological therapies and non-pharmacological interventions** | Supportive therapy, psychoeducation, CBT, behaviour therapy, IPT, psychodynamic, family and group therapy, motivational interviewing, mindfulness, relaxation, occupational therapy, community and cultural approaches |
| **Psychiatric emergencies** | Suicidal behaviour, violence, acute psychosis and mania, severe depression, delirium, substance-related emergencies, agitation, catatonia, special populations and other crises |

## MBPS 4539: Junior Clerkship in Psychiatry and Mental Health II

**Purpose:** to equip students with knowledge, skills and attitudes in diagnosing common psychiatric disorders.

### Expected learning outcomes

1. Recognise psychiatric emergencies and start first-line management.
2. Take a patient history, especially the chief complaints and history of presenting illness.
3. Diagnose common mental illnesses such as major depressive disorder and schizophrenia.
4. Perform the mental state examination.
5. Order basic laboratory and imaging investigations.
6. Formulate a diagnosis by exploring differential diagnoses.
7. Apply the principles and practice of mental health in managing patients.
8. Conduct an interview and a comprehensive physical examination in a way that builds a therapeutic alliance.
9. Take part in managing common mental health disorders.
10. Take part in common procedures in mental health.
11. Use preventive and rehabilitative measures and carry out health promotion in mental health.

### Clinical processes

- Take a history from patients, relatives and other relevant sources, with corroborating information.
- Do a physical and mental state examination, and distinguish psychosis from delirium.
- Formulate a diagnosis using the biopsychosocial model and make a management plan.
- Record findings and present cases.

### Routine investigations

- Routine tests to confirm or rule out medical conditions, prescribed medicines or illicit drug use as causes of psychiatric conditions: complete blood count, urine toxicology, kidney and liver function, thyroid function and autoimmune tests.
- Imaging and procedures: CT scan, MRI, EEG and ECG.

### Managing acute mental illness

Apply the best evidence, stabilise the mental state, then make a comprehensive management plan. Consult and refer where necessary, use drug and psychotherapy options, and give psychoeducation to the patient and the family support system.

### Clinical rotation components

Psychiatric history taking, mental status examination, formulation and management planning; screening tools such as **PHQ-9, GAD-7 and MINI**; crisis intervention and emergency protocols; communication with patients and families; documentation and case presentation; multidisciplinary team meetings; inpatient, outpatient, emergency and community settings; mental health promotion; ethical and legal considerations; reflective practice with a logbook; and cultural competence.

## Teaching and assessment

| | Details |
|---|---|
| **Teaching methods** | Lectures, seminars, clinicopathological conferences, bedside teaching, simulated patients, grand rounds, emergency calls, tutorials, group discussions, e-learning, ward rounds and self-directed learning |
| **Practicals** | Clinical rotations, skills lab (percussion, palpation, auscultation) and the mental state examination |
| **What to bring** | Stethoscope, torch, patella hammer and other examination tools |
| **Continuous assessment (30%)** | Written tests, assignments, clinical assessment, long cases and vivas |
| **Logbook (10%)** | A log of procedures performed, assisted in and observed, showing the competencies achieved |
| **End-of-clerkship examination (60%)** | MCQ and short answer questions, plus clinical skills assessed with OSCEs and vivas |

MBPS 4537 (Junior Clerkship in Psychiatry and Mental Health I) and MBPS 4539 (Junior Clerkship in Psychiatry and Mental Health II).

Continuous assessment tests 30%, logbook 10% and the end-of-clerkship examination 60%.

PHQ-9, GAD-7 and MINI.

**5. Which routine tests may be ordered to look for medical causes of a psychiatric presentation?**
Complete blood count, urine toxicology, kidney and liver function tests, thyroid function tests and autoimmune tests, with imaging such as CT scan, MRI, EEG and ECG where needed.
