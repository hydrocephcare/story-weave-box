### Question 1

**(a) What is a teratogen? (2 marks)**
An agent that causes abnormal development of structures and organs of a fetus, such as a drug, infection, chemical or radiation.

**(b) Give 3 examples of teratogens (3 marks)**
1. **Warfarin:** nasal hypoplasia and chondrodysplasia punctata.
2. **Alcohol:** fetal alcohol syndrome.
3. **Isotretinoin, thalidomide, valproate, phenytoin, ACE inhibitors, tetracyclines, rubella virus** and ionising radiation are other examples.

**(c) Strategies to reduce congenital anomalies (5 marks)**
- **Primary (prevent the anomaly):** avoid pregnancy at extremes of age, folic acid before and in early pregnancy, immunisation (rubella), avoid teratogens, alcohol and smoking, good control of diabetes and epilepsy, and genetic counselling.
- **Secondary (early detection):** antenatal screening with ultrasound and maternal serum markers, and diagnostic tests (amniocentesis, chorionic villus sampling), with counselling and termination options where permitted.
- **Tertiary:** early treatment, surgical correction and rehabilitation of the affected child.

### Question 2

**(a) A 21-year-old has vaginal discharge for 4 days with odour and dysuria. List 4 differential diagnoses (4 marks)**
1. Chlamydial cervicitis or urethritis
2. Gonorrhoea
3. Trichomoniasis
4. Bacterial vaginosis (also vulvovaginal candidiasis, UTI, pelvic inflammatory disease or a retained foreign body)

**(b) Briefly explain two examples of the syndromic approach to STIs (4 marks)**
The syndromic approach treats a **group of symptoms and signs** with antibiotics covering the main causes, without waiting for laboratory results.
1. **Vaginal discharge syndrome:** a woman with discharge is assessed for cervicitis risk. If risk is present, treat gonorrhoea and chlamydia (for example ceftriaxone plus azithromycin or doxycycline), plus metronidazole for trichomonas and bacterial vaginosis, and add fluconazole if there is a curdy discharge.
2. **Lower abdominal pain (PID) syndrome:** a woman with lower abdominal pain and cervical motion tenderness is given a combination covering gonorrhoea, chlamydia and anaerobes (ceftriaxone, doxycycline, metronidazole).

Other syndromes: urethral discharge, genital ulcer disease, inguinal bubo, scrotal swelling, ophthalmia neonatorum.

### Question 3

**(a) Define puberty (3 marks)**
The period of transition from childhood to reproductive maturity, with development of secondary sexual characteristics, rapid growth and attainment of fertility, under activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis. In girls it starts at about 8 to 13 years.

**(b) Describe the changes of the uterus after puberty (4 marks)**
1. Increase in length and overall size, with the body outgrowing the cervix (body to cervix ratio from 1:2 to 2:1)
2. Change from a tubular to the adult **pear shape**
3. Thickening of the **myometrium** and increased vascularity
4. Endometrial **sensitivity to oestrogen and progesterone** with cyclic proliferation, secretion and shedding

### Question 4

**(a) What are ovarian cysts? (2 marks)**
Fluid-filled sacs in or on the ovary that may be functional or pathological.

**(b) The 3 main categories (3 marks)**
1. Functional cysts (follicular, corpus luteum)
2. Benign neoplastic cysts (serous and mucinous cystadenoma, dermoid, endometrioma)
3. Malignant tumours (cystadenocarcinoma, dysgerminoma)

**(c) Medical treatment options (5 marks)**
1. **Observation** with repeat ultrasound in 6 to 12 weeks, because most functional cysts resolve.
2. **Analgesia:** paracetamol or NSAIDs.
3. **Combined oral contraceptive pills** to suppress ovulation and prevent new functional cysts (they do not hasten the resolution of an existing one).
4. **Antibiotics** for a tubo-ovarian abscess or infected cyst.
5. **Tumour markers** (CA-125, AFP, hCG, LDH) to select who needs surgery. Surgery (cystectomy or oophorectomy) is for large, persistent, complex, symptomatic or suspicious cysts, and torsion.

### Question 5

**(a) Define menopause (3 marks)**
Permanent cessation of menstruation after loss of ovarian follicular activity, confirmed retrospectively after **12 consecutive months of amenorrhoea** with no other cause. The average age is about 50 years.

**(b) Changes at menopause (6 marks)**
1. **Vasomotor:** hot flushes and night sweats.
2. **Genitourinary:** vaginal dryness, dyspareunia, thin atrophic urethra and bladder epithelium with urinary frequency, urgency and recurrent UTI.
3. **Skeletal:** accelerated bone loss, leading to osteoporosis.
4. **Cardiovascular:** adverse lipid profile (raised LDL cholesterol) and increased cardiovascular risk.
5. **Psychological:** mood changes, irritability, poor sleep and reduced libido.
6. **Hormonal:** low oestrogen with high FSH and LH, and skin thinning.

**(c) Treatment options (6 marks)**
1. **Menopausal hormone therapy:** oestrogen alone after hysterectomy, and oestrogen with progestogen (combined) in women with a uterus, to protect the endometrium. Tibolone is a synthetic alternative.
2. **Local vaginal oestrogen** for urogenital symptoms.
3. **Non-hormonal drugs:** SSRIs, SNRIs, gabapentin or clonidine for hot flushes.
4. **Bone protection:** calcium, vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise, and bisphosphonates for osteoporosis.
5. **Lifestyle:** stop smoking, reduce alcohol, light clothing, healthy diet, and water-based lubricants.
6. Counsel on risks of hormone therapy (breast cancer, venous thrombosis, stroke) and review regularly.
