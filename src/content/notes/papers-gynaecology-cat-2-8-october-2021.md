### Question 1

**(a) What is adenomyosis? (2 marks)**
The presence of **endometrial glands and stroma within the myometrium,** with surrounding smooth muscle hyperplasia. It causes a diffusely enlarged, tender uterus, heavy painful periods and is common in multiparous women over 35.

**(b) What is endometriosis? (3 marks)**
The presence of **functioning endometrial glands and stroma outside the uterine cavity,** most often in the ovaries, pouch of Douglas, uterosacral ligaments and pelvic peritoneum. It responds to cyclical oestrogen and causes dysmenorrhoea, dyspareunia, chronic pelvic pain and infertility.

**(c) Strategies to reduce cervical cancer (5 marks)**
1. **HPV vaccination** of girls aged 9 to 14 years (before sexual debut), covering types 16 and 18.
2. **Screening** of women (VIA/VILI, Pap smear or HPV DNA testing) from age 25, or from 30 years where resources are limited, and from diagnosis in HIV-positive women.
3. **Treatment of precancerous lesions** (cryotherapy, thermal ablation, LEEP) at the same visit where possible.
4. **Behavioural measures:** delay first intercourse, reduce the number of partners, use condoms, stop smoking.
5. **Control of HIV** with antiretroviral therapy, and public education and awareness.

### Question 2. A 52-year-old woman has vaginal discharge for 4 days with odour and blood stains

**(a) Four differential diagnoses (4 marks)**
1. **Cervical cancer**
2. **Endometrial cancer** (postmenopausal bleeding)
3. **Atrophic vaginitis,** or cervical or endometrial polyps
4. **Infection:** cervicitis, endometritis, trichomoniasis or chlamydia (also a retained foreign body such as a pessary)

**(b) Investigations to make the diagnosis (4 marks)**
1. **Speculum and bimanual examination** with cervical smear or VIA, and **colposcopy with biopsy** of any suspicious lesion.
2. **Transvaginal ultrasound** to measure endometrial thickness (over 4 mm after menopause needs further assessment).
3. **Endometrial sampling:** pipelle biopsy, hysteroscopy with biopsy, or dilatation and curettage.
4. **High vaginal swab** and culture for infection, and **full blood count** for anaemia.
5. HIV test, and imaging (CT or MRI) to stage a confirmed cancer.

### Question 3

**(a) Define stress urinary incontinence (3 marks)**
Involuntary leakage of urine on **effort or exertion, coughing, sneezing or laughing,** that is, when intra-abdominal pressure rises, in the absence of a detrusor contraction. It is caused by weak urethral sphincter or pelvic floor support.

**(b) Causes of urinary incontinence (7 marks)**
1. **Stress:** urethral hypermobility and intrinsic sphincter deficiency from childbirth, ageing, menopause or previous surgery.
2. **Urge:** detrusor overactivity, UTI, bladder stones or tumour, neurological disease.
3. **Overflow:** bladder outlet obstruction (prolapse, fibroid), diabetic neuropathy, drugs.
4. **Fistula:** vesicovaginal or ureterovaginal fistula after obstructed labour or surgery.
5. **Functional:** immobility, dementia, poor access to a toilet.
6. **Drugs:** diuretics, alpha-blockers, sedatives.
7. Congenital causes (ectopic ureter), pregnancy, obesity, chronic cough and constipation.

### Question 4

**(a) Examples of long-acting reversible contraception (2 marks)**
1. **Subdermal implants** (Jadelle, Implanon)
2. **Intrauterine devices:** copper IUCD and the levonorgestrel intrauterine system (Mirena)
(DMPA injectables are sometimes listed as well.)

**(b) Non-contraceptive advantages of combined oral contraceptives (5 marks)**
1. Reduced menstrual loss and dysmenorrhoea, with **regular cycles**
2. Reduced iron-deficiency anaemia
3. **Reduced risk of ovarian and endometrial cancer** (and colorectal cancer)
4. Fewer functional ovarian cysts and benign breast disease
5. Improvement in acne and hirsutism, and treatment of endometriosis symptoms and premenstrual syndrome
6. Reduced risk of pelvic inflammatory disease and ectopic pregnancy

**(c) Advantages of the intrauterine device (5 marks)**
1. **Highly effective** (over 99%) and not dependent on daily use
2. **Long-acting,** up to 10 years for copper and 5 years for the levonorgestrel system
3. **Immediately reversible,** with prompt return of fertility
4. Suitable when **oestrogen is contraindicated,** and safe in breastfeeding
5. The **copper IUCD is an emergency contraceptive** when inserted within 5 days of unprotected sex. The levonorgestrel system reduces heavy periods and protects the endometrium.
6. Cost-effective and discreet.
