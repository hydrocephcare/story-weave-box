## Multiple choice questions

### 1. A previously well 1-year-old infant has had a running nose, sneezing and coughing for 2 days. Four hours ago his cough became much worse. He is in moderate respiratory distress with nasal flaring, hyperexpansion of the chest and easily audible wheezing without rales. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Bronchiolitis
- B. Viral croup
- C. Asthma
- D. Epiglottitis
- E. Diphtheria

**Answer: A.** A cold, then wheeze and hyperinflation in an infant under 2 years during an outbreak is bronchiolitis.

### 2. Which of the following is true of tetanus?
- A. Failure to culture Clostridium tetani from the wound would make the diagnosis doubtful
- B. Infection confers lifelong immunity
- C. There is a characteristic EEG
- D. Clostridium-specific intravenous immunoglobulin is of no benefit once spasm has started
- E. Cephalic tetanus causes severe dysphagia

**Answer: E.** Cephalic tetanus affects the cranial nerves and causes trismus and difficulty swallowing. Tetanus is a clinical diagnosis (the wound culture is often negative), the disease does not confer immunity, and immune globulin is still given.

### 3. Stiff neck in a young child is a feature of the following conditions EXCEPT:
- A. Acute poliomyelitis
- B. Retropharyngeal abscess
- C. Rubeola (measles)
- D. Subarachnoid haemorrhage
- E. Tuberculosis

**Answer: C.** Measles does not cause neck stiffness unless complicated by encephalitis.

### 4. Two weeks after a viral illness, Mukiri, 9 years, has a drooping left mouth and cannot completely shut her left eye. Her smile is asymmetric, and the examination is otherwise normal. She likely has:
- A. Guillain-Barre syndrome
- B. Botulism
- C. Cerebrovascular accident
- D. Brainstem tumour
- E. Bell palsy

**Answer: E.** An isolated lower motor neurone facial palsy after a viral illness is Bell palsy.

### 5. In the diagnosis of acute rheumatic fever, which one of the following is most helpful?
- A. A generalised papular rash
- B. Elevated ASOT titres
- C. Polyarthritis
- D. Splinter haemorrhages
- E. Positive blood culture of viridans streptococci

**Answer: C.** Migratory polyarthritis is the commonest major criterion. A raised ASOT shows a past streptococcal infection but is only supporting evidence, so B is also needed. Splinter haemorrhages and a positive culture point to endocarditis.

### 6. An infant can move his head from side to side while following a moving object, lift his head from a prone position 45 degrees off the examining table, smile when encouraged and make cooing sounds. He cannot maintain a seated position. The most likely age is:
- A. 1 month
- B. 3 months
- C. 6 months
- D. 9 months
- E. 12 months

**Answer: B.** Head lift to 45 degrees, tracking, smiling and cooing are 2 to 3 month skills. Sitting comes at 6 months.

### 7. Orio, 11 years, has bilateral cervical lymphadenopathy, and a lymph node biopsy shows nodular sclerosing Hodgkin disease. Which feature indicates a poorer prognosis?
- A. Fatigue
- B. Mediastinal mass of 3 cm
- C. Night sweats
- D. Pruritus
- E. Recent Epstein-Barr virus infection

**Answer: C.** The B symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss) mark a poorer outlook. A mediastinal mass counts only when it is bulky (more than a third of the chest width).

### 8. An 8-month-old infant has had a 2-day history of diarrhoea and poor fluid intake. You diagnose 10 to 15% dehydration. Which fluid is appropriate to begin immediate resuscitation?
- A. Oral rehydration solution
- B. 5% dextrose with half normal saline
- C. Normal saline
- D. Ringer's lactate solution
- E. Half-strength Darrow's solution

**Answer: D.** Severe dehydration needs rapid IV fluid, and Ringer's lactate is the WHO choice (normal saline is an acceptable alternative).

### 9. Juma, 4 years, has the classical murmur of patent ductus arteriosus. Which is characteristic of this murmur?
- A. Continuous
- B. Mid-diastolic
- C. Pansystolic
- D. Ejection
- E. Early diastolic

**Answer: A.** It is a continuous machinery murmur, heard at the left upper sternal border.

### 10. Hepatitis C virus:
- A. The incubation period is 7 to 9 weeks
- B. Food is the usual mode of transmission
- C. Has a DNA genome
- D. Is commonly transmitted through breast milk
- E. Is a predominantly non-cytopathic virus that causes injury by immune-mediated processes

**Answer: E.** HCV is an RNA virus spread by blood, and the liver injury comes from the immune response and not from direct cell killing. Option A is close to the true average (6 to 7 weeks, range 2 to 26), so the question has two near-correct options.

### 11. The following is consistent with brainstem death:
- A. Absence of consensual pupillary light reflexes
- B. Absence of the cold caloric reflex
- C. Periodic breathing
- D. Absent tendon reflexes
- E. Cheyne-Stokes breathing

**Answer: B.** Absent vestibulo-ocular (caloric) responses are part of the brainstem death tests. Absent pupil reflexes are also required, so A is also consistent. Breathing and tendon reflexes may persist (spinal reflexes).

### 12. Metabolic alkalosis is characteristically found in one of the following:
- A. Pyloric stenosis
- B. Mineralocorticoid deficiency
- C. Cholera
- D. Salicylate poisoning
- E. An infusion of Ringer's lactate

**Answer: A.** Vomiting of gastric acid causes hypochloraemic hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis.

### 13. The following is true of chromosomal disorders:
- A. Down syndrome is most commonly due to an extra copy of chromosome 21 inherited from the father
- B. A haploid cell has 46 chromosomes
- C. A foetus with triploidy will have 47 chromosomes
- D. The normal human karyotype consists of 44 pairs of autosomes
- E. Robertsonian translocation involving chromosome 21 accounts for 3 to 4% of Down syndrome

**Answer: E.** About 95% of cases are trisomy 21 (mostly from the mother's egg), 3 to 4% are translocations, and the rest are mosaic. A haploid cell has 23 chromosomes, triploidy has 69, and there are 22 pairs of autosomes.

### 14. A 1-year-old infant is known to have heart disease and is noted to be cyanosed. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
- A. Atrial septal defect
- B. Coarctation of the aorta
- C. Patent ductus arteriosus
- D. Tricuspid atresia
- E. Ventricular septal defect

**Answer: D.** Of these, only tricuspid atresia is a cyanotic lesion.

### 15. Features of a third nerve (oculomotor) lesion include:
- A. Proptosis
- B. Convergent squint
- C. Loss of corneal reflex
- D. Dilatation of the pupil
- E. Loss of lacrimation

**Answer: D.** A third nerve palsy gives a dilated pupil, ptosis and an eye turned down and out (a divergent squint).

### 16. Which statement regarding acute severe asthma is correct?
- A. Agitation should be managed with a benzodiazepine
- B. Inhaled salmeterol is indicated as first-line therapy
- C. A high inspired oxygen concentration should be used routinely
- D. Absence of a wheeze is reassuring
- E. Tachycardia is not a reliable sign of severity

**Answer: C.** High-flow oxygen is given to every child with severe asthma. Sedatives are dangerous, a long-acting beta agonist is not for acute attacks, and a silent chest is an ominous sign.

### 17. A previously healthy 18-month-old child has unilateral nasal obstruction and foul-smelling discharge. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
- A. Foreign body
- B. Nasal polyps
- C. Frontal sinusitis
- D. Deviated septum
- E. Choanal atresia

**Answer: A.** Unilateral foul discharge in a toddler is a nasal foreign body until proved otherwise.

### 18. A child with trisomy 21 is at risk of all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Duodenal atresia
- B. Hirschsprung disease
- C. Hypothyroidism
- D. Hydrocephalus
- E. Atlanto-axial instability

**Answer: D.** Children with Down syndrome have a small, short head (brachycephaly), not hydrocephalus.

### 19. Developmental delay should be considered in all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Not able to roll over by 5 months
- B. No social smile by 3 months
- C. Not able to walk by 16 months
- D. Not able to stand without support by 9 months
- E. Not able to sit with support by 5 months

**Answer: D.** A 9-month-old is only expected to stand with support, and he stands alone at about 12 months.

### 20. Failure of tooth eruption by 1 year of age is likely to be due to one of the following:
- A. Hypothyroidism
- B. Rickets
- C. Scurvy
- D. Iron deficiency
- E. Ectodermal dysplasia

**Answer: B.** Rickets (and hypothyroidism) delay teething. Rickets is the commonest cause.

### 21. The recommended parenteral antibiotic in treating acute rheumatic fever is:
- A. Benzyl penicillin
- B. Crystalline penicillin
- C. Benzathine penicillin
- D. Ampicillin
- E. Ceftriaxone

**Answer: C.** A single IM dose of benzathine penicillin eradicates the streptococcus, and then monthly doses give prophylaxis. (Crystalline and benzyl penicillin are the same drug.)

### 22. In an infant, which of the following makes the diagnosis of cardiac failure unlikely?
- A. A respiratory rate of more than 50 per minute
- B. A heart rate of more than 180 per minute
- C. An enlarged liver
- D. Ascites
- E. Excessive perspiration

**Answer: D.** Infants with heart failure have a fast breathing rate and heart rate, a large liver and sweating, but ascites is rare.

### 23. Doughy skin in a child with acute diarrhoea is associated with:
- A. Hypernatraemic dehydration
- B. Isotonic dehydration
- C. Hypotonic dehydration
- D. Oedema of heart failure
- E. Cholera

**Answer: A.** Skin feels thick and doughy when serum sodium is high.

### 24. The mother of a 6-month-old infant asks about teething. The first teeth to erupt in most children are:
- A. Mandibular central incisors
- B. Maxillary lateral incisors
- C. Maxillary first molars
- D. Mandibular canines
- E. First premolars (bicuspids)

**Answer: A.** The lower central incisors come first, at about 6 months.

### 25. An awake, alert infant with a 2-day history of diarrhoea has a depressed fontanelle, tachycardia, sunken eyes and loss of skin elasticity. Which is the correct percentage of dehydration?
- A. Less than 1%
- B. 1% to 5%
- C. 5% to 9%
- D. 10% to 15%
- E. More than 20%

**Answer: C.** A child who is still alert but has sunken eyes and a slow skin pinch has some (moderate) dehydration of 5 to 9%. Severe dehydration (10% or more) comes with lethargy and a very slow pinch.

### 26. All the following enter the human body by piercing the skin EXCEPT:
- A. Necator americanus
- B. Strongyloides stercoralis
- C. Schistosoma mansoni
- D. Toxocara
- E. Entamoeba histolytica

**Answer: E.** Entamoeba histolytica cysts are swallowed. Toxocara eggs are also swallowed, so option D is another exception and the question is loosely worded.

### 27. Otundo, a 4-week-old boy weighing 4.2 kg, has had a week of forceful vomiting after feeds. He is alert and eager to feed and has loss of skin elasticity and an apparent distension in the left hypochondrium. Which statement is correct?
- A. The most likely diagnosis is hiatus hernia
- B. He has hyperosmolar dehydration
- C. Features are consistent with Hirschsprung disease
- D. Metabolic acidosis would be anticipated
- E. He is likely to have hypokalaemia

**Answer: E.** This is infantile pyloric stenosis, with a hypochloraemic, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis (not acidosis).

### 28. Which of the following is a live attenuated vaccine?
- A. Pertussis
- B. Yellow fever
- C. Hepatitis B
- D. Tetanus
- E. Diphtheria

**Answer: B.** The others are inactivated or toxoid vaccines.

### 29. Juliet, a 10-month-old girl, has abdominal pain and diarrhoea with recent pink staining, episodes of crying and holding her abdomen, and a curved firm mass 10 cm long at the right iliac fossa. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Inborn errors of metabolism
- B. Constipation or encopresis
- C. Intussusception
- D. Polycystic kidney disease
- E. Malignancy (neuroblastoma, Wilms tumour, lymphoma)

**Answer: C.** Colic, redcurrant jelly stools and a sausage-shaped mass.

### 30. Haemorrhagic cystitis, conjunctivitis, diarrhoea and bronchiolitis have all been linked to one of the following:
- A. RSV
- B. Rotavirus
- C. HSV
- D. Adenovirus
- E. Influenza type B

**Answer: D.** Adenoviruses cause all four.

### 31. Achieng, 5 years, has a three-week history of cough and dyspnoea, with stony dullness on the right side of the chest. Which is true?
- A. The tactile fremitus would be reduced on the right
- B. The mediastinum may be shifted to the right
- C. The protein level of the fluid from thoracentesis will be less than 3 g/dL if tuberculosis is the cause
- D. The LDH of the fluid will be elevated if heart failure was the cause
- E. A chest tube insertion is not feasible as part of management since Achieng is still a toddler

**Answer: A.** A pleural effusion damps the vibrations. The mediastinum is pushed away (to the left), a TB effusion is an exudate (protein above 3 g/dL), a heart failure effusion is a transudate, and chest tubes are used at any age.

### 32. Aaron, 6 years, has had diarrhoea and vomiting for two days, is alert, with a respiratory rate of 56, deep breathing, cold hands and a weak radial pulse. In managing this child:
- A. A fever of more than 38.3 degrees should be a priority during treatment
- B. The immediate action is to open the airway and administer oxygen
- C. An intraosseous line at the proximal fibula should be attempted after 2 failed attempts to get an intravenous line
- D. A bolus of 20 ml/kg of Hartmann's solution should be given over the next two hours
- E. IV 20 ml/kg of 50% dextrose should be infused within 15 minutes

**Answer: B.** This is shock, so airway and oxygen come first. Then a 20 ml/kg bolus over 15 minutes. The intraosseous site is the tibia.

### 33. Compensatory changes in heart failure include all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Activation of the parasympathetic nervous system
- B. Release of catecholamines
- C. Increased heart rate
- D. Myocardial hypertrophy
- E. Activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system

**Answer: A.** The sympathetic (not parasympathetic) system is activated.

### 34. A one-year-old admitted with pneumonia has just collapsed. There is no respiratory movement and the peripheral pulse is not palpable. The immediate measure is:
- A. Initiate external cardiac massage
- B. Give oxygen by nasal catheter
- C. Clear the airway and establish respiration by bag and mask
- D. Establish the cause of the sudden change in condition
- E. Give IV 10% dextrose

**Answer: C.** In children the arrest is usually respiratory, so airway and breathing are started first, followed at once by compressions if there is no pulse.

### 35. A 2-month-old baby has a cough and difficulty in breastfeeding. The mother says she sweats a lot during feeding. The baby is afebrile, neither pale nor cyanosed. The respiratory rate is 70/minute with no chest indrawing, and there are fine crepitations on auscultation. The heart rate is 160 beats per minute and the liver is 5 cm below the costal margin. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Pulmonary tuberculosis
- B. Pertussis
- C. Congestive cardiac failure
- D. Bronchiolitis
- E. Acidosis

**Answer: C.** Tachypnoea, tachycardia, sweating on feeding and a big liver in an afebrile infant are heart failure.

### 36. The following are helpful in the establishment of successful breastfeeding:
- A. Allowing the mother to rest after delivery before initiation of breastfeeding
- B. Provision of extra water to the mother during the first few days of breastfeeding
- C. Establishing a support system within the community
- D. Daily weighing in the immediate post-partum period
- E. Introduction of pre-lacteal feeds

**Answer: C.** Community support helps mothers continue. Breastfeeding should start within the first hour, and pre-lacteal feeds undermine it.

### 37. A baby born at term with a weight of 3.2 kg and a head circumference of 35.6 cm. Which of the following will apply when he is 6 months old?
- A. A weight of 9 kg
- B. Head circumference of 44.6 cm
- C. No weight bearing on the legs when supported in a standing position
- D. Laughs when stimulated
- E. Transfers objects from one hand to another

**Answer: B.** The head grows about 9 cm in the first 6 months, so 35.6 + 9 = 44.6 cm. Weight at 6 months is about 6.5 to 7 kg, and a 6-month-old bears weight on supported legs. Options D and E are also true of this age, so the question has more than one defensible option.

### 38. At age 12 months a normal infant:
- A. Weighs triple the birth weight
- B. Becomes attentive to their name
- C. Waves bye
- D. Measures 67.5 cm
- E. Is able to draw a circle

**Answer: A.** Birth weight triples by 1 year. Length is about 75 cm, and drawing a circle comes at 3 years.

### 39. A 2-year-old child is admitted with refusal to feed, gross oedema of the legs and crying a lot. He has mild diarrhoea. The anticipated problems during management include all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Gram-negative septicaemia
- B. Hyperglycaemia during the first two days of admission
- C. Worsening of the diarrhoea after feeding with milk
- D. Congestive cardiac failure
- E. A slow weight gain

**Answer: B.** This is kwashiorkor. Hypoglycaemia (not hyperglycaemia) is the early danger.

### 40. Which of the following are priority signs during triage of children in a health facility?
- A. A child with cold hands and a capillary refill of 4 seconds
- B. Child with a body temperature of 37 degrees
- C. All babies more than 6 months
- D. A child who is convulsing
- E. A child with a history of poisoning

**Answer: E.** Poisoning is a priority sign. Cold hands with slow capillary refill and convulsions are emergency signs, and it is babies under 2 months (tiny babies) who are priority, not those over 6 months.

### 41. The following is a differential diagnosis of a 3-year-old wheezy child:
- A. Loeffler syndrome
- B. Bronchiolitis
- C. Tonsillitis
- D. Meconium aspiration
- E. Tinea capitis

**Answer: A.** Ascaris larvae passing through the lungs cause wheeze and eosinophilic pneumonia (Loeffler syndrome). Bronchiolitis is for infants, and meconium aspiration is a newborn problem.

### 42. In the treatment of shock secondary to severe dehydration:
- A. In the absence of intravenous access, drinking water is given through an NG tube
- B. IV 100 ml/kg of normal saline is given as the initial bolus
- C. Calcium replacement is mandatory
- D. Intraosseous is an alternate route for rehydration
- E. Intravenous insulin with glucose is indicated to lower the high potassium

**Answer: D.** If a vein cannot be found, the intraosseous route is used. The first bolus is 20 ml/kg.

### 43. The following statements are true regarding childhood tuberculosis EXCEPT:
- A. Mostly acquired from adults
- B. Tuberculous meningitis is often the first presentation
- C. Treatment in the initial phase requires a four-drug combination
- D. Diagnosis rests on the identification of Mycobacterium in body fluids
- E. Can reliably be diagnosed using several scoring systems

**Answer: D.** In children the organism is rarely found, so the diagnosis rests on the history of contact, symptoms, the tuberculin test and X-ray. Option B is also doubtful (meningitis is a complication, not the usual first sign), so the question is loosely worded.

### 44. A 4-year-old has had a running nose for 2 days. His mother now notices skin lesions that appear at different stages and feel itchy. A child in the same school had similar lesions the previous week. The following is true:
- A. She should be isolated from her sister who is two years younger
- B. This condition is caused by the pox virus
- C. She should be started on prophylactic antibiotics to prevent pneumonia
- D. A poor outcome is expected
- E. This is not a vaccine-preventable illness

**Answer: A.** This is chickenpox (varicella-zoster), which spreads by droplets and contact. The child should be kept apart from non-immune siblings until the lesions crust. It is vaccine-preventable, and the outcome is usually good.

### 45. Regarding HIV infection in children:
- A. Absolute CD4 T-cell count is an important indicator of disease progression in children above 5 years
- B. CD4 T-cell percentage is not an important indicator of disease progression
- C. Cotrimoxazole is beneficial to children with a low CD4 percentage
- D. Encephalopathy occurs in about one fifth of the children
- E. It is an absolutely preventable condition

**Answer: A.** Under 5 years the CD4 percentage is used, and from 5 years the absolute count is used. Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis is now given to all HIV-infected children, so C is also true and the question is loosely worded.

### 46. Achieng is born to a 36-year-old mother. She has a prominent occiput, low-set ears, a small mouth, clenched hands and micrognathia. Which is true regarding this condition?
- A. This is most likely a congenitally acquired infection
- B. This is most likely a chromosomal anomaly
- C. The prognosis is often poor
- D. Septal defects may be an association
- E. The mother's age is important in causation

**Answer: B.** This is Edwards syndrome (trisomy 18). It is a chromosomal anomaly, and C, D and E are also true of it, so the question has more than one defensible option.

### 47. Which of the following antiretroviral drugs is correctly matched with its common side effect?
- A. Zidovudine: elevated liver enzymes
- B. Nevirapine: raised serum amylase
- C. Stavudine: peripheral neuropathy
- D. Efavirenz: neutropenia
- E. Nelfinavir: neuropsychiatric disorders

**Answer: C.** Stavudine causes peripheral neuropathy (and lipodystrophy). Zidovudine causes neutropenia and anaemia, nevirapine causes liver toxicity and rash, efavirenz causes neuropsychiatric effects, and nelfinavir causes diarrhoea.

### 48. The following is true regarding acute kidney injury in children:
- A. Red blood cell casts in the urine indicate pre-renal failure
- B. Urine output less than 0.5 ml/kg per hour
- C. Low protein diet is part of the standard management
- D. All children should be dialysed
- E. Gastrointestinal bleeding is common

**Answer: B.** Oliguria (under 0.5 ml/kg/hour) is the defining feature. Red cell casts mean glomerular disease, and only some children need dialysis.

### 49. Musonge, 5 years, has recurrent yellowing of the eyes, three blood transfusions and skull bossing. The following is true about this condition:
- A. The condition follows sex-linked inheritance
- B. Hydroxyurea treatment leads to complete cure
- C. Distribution in Kenya follows ethnic lines
- D. Diagnosis requires bone marrow examination
- E. Infection by encapsulated bacteria can be fatal

**Answer: E.** This is sickle cell disease (or thalassaemia). Loss of splenic function leaves the child open to pneumococcus and other encapsulated bacteria. Inheritance is autosomal recessive, hydroxyurea does not cure, and the diagnosis is by electrophoresis.

### 50. A pertinent investigation in a male child with joint swelling, easy bruising and bleeding of the gums is:
- A. Liver function test
- B. ELISA for HIV antibodies
- C. Urea and electrolytes
- D. Haemoglobin electrophoresis
- E. Factor VIII assay

**Answer: E.** A boy with joint bleeds and bruising has haemophilia A until proved otherwise.

### 51. Causes of a small pupil include:
- A. Carbon monoxide poisoning
- B. Sub-arachnoid haemorrhage
- C. Methanol poisoning
- D. Narcotic use
- E. Third nerve palsy

**Answer: D.** Opiates cause pinpoint pupils. Third nerve palsy and methanol cause dilated pupils.

### 52. In the management of a child with diabetic ketoacidosis, which are the two most important interventions in the first two hours?
- A. Insulin and intravenous normal saline
- B. Insulin and intravenous dextrose
- C. Insulin and potassium supplementation
- D. Insulin and bicarbonate
- E. Insulin and parenteral antibiotics

**Answer: A.** Rehydration with saline comes first, and insulin is started after the first hour of fluids.

### 53. The following are part of the standard management of congestive cardiac failure EXCEPT:
- A. Oral digoxin
- B. Oral angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors
- C. Beta blockers
- D. Fluid restriction
- E. No added salt diet in older children

**Answer: C.** Beta blockers are used only in selected stable cases.

### 54. Factors associated with a favourable outcome of acute lymphoblastic leukaemia include one of the following:
- A. Age below two years at diagnosis
- B. White cell count below 20,000/mm3 at diagnosis
- C. Testicular involvement in boys
- D. Hyperdiploidy
- E. Occurrence in a child with trisomy

**Answer: D.** Hyperdiploidy (more than 50 chromosomes) responds well to treatment. A white cell count below 20,000 (or 50,000) is also favourable, so the question has two defensible options. Age under 1 year and testicular disease carry a poor outlook.

### 55. While bathing her 4-year-old son, Wambui notices a firm mass in the child's abdomen. He is well nourished and has a bimanually palpable mass in the left lumbar region. The following are associated with this condition EXCEPT:
- A. Asymmetry in the various body parts
- B. Absent iris
- C. Hypertension
- D. Simian palmar crease
- E. Haematuria

**Answer: D.** This is Wilms tumour, linked to hemihypertrophy, aniridia, hypertension and haematuria. A simian crease is a feature of Down syndrome.

### 56. Atieno, 6 years, has progressive unilateral jaw swelling and displacement of some teeth. The rest of the examination is normal. True about this condition:
- A. Parasitic infection has a role in the girl's illness
- B. Late treatment complications may include infertility
- C. Diagnosis must be made with open biopsy for histology
- D. The finding of Reed-Sternberg cells is diagnostic
- E. A poor outcome can be anticipated with appropriate treatment

**Answer: A.** This is endemic Burkitt lymphoma, linked to Epstein-Barr virus and chronic malaria. B is also true (alkylating chemotherapy can cause infertility). A needle aspirate is enough for diagnosis, Reed-Sternberg cells are for Hodgkin disease, and the outcome with treatment is good.

### 57. Dave, 5 months, has recurrent vomiting after feeds and has been treated twice for pneumonia, with the chest film always showing upper lobe involvement. The following are true EXCEPT:
- A. Surgical management may be indicated
- B. Pulmonary tuberculosis should be considered
- C. Positioning in the sitting position and diet modification suffice in most cases
- D. Severity of the symptoms rises to a peak at 1 year before declining
- E. He has a higher than average chance of having a congenital anomaly of the diaphragm

**Answer: D.** Reflux symptoms peak at about 4 months and settle by 12 to 18 months.

### 58. The following is true regarding atopic dermatitis in children:
- A. It presents initially with flexural lesions
- B. Pruritus is a constant feature
- C. Systemic corticosteroids are indicated in many cases
- D. Expected to resolve spontaneously
- E. More common in boys than girls

**Answer: B.** Itching is the hallmark. In infants the face and extensor surfaces are first affected, flexures come later, systemic steroids are rarely needed, and it is slightly commoner in girls.

### 59. The following are suggestive of child abuse EXCEPT:
- A. A child promptly rushed to hospital even for minor injuries
- B. History not tallying with severity of the injury
- C. Multiple fractures in different parts of the body
- D. Injuries in a child with severe mental handicap
- E. Isolated fractures in a 2-month-old baby

**Answer: A.** Prompt care-seeking is normal. Delay is the warning sign.

### 60. The following statement is NOT true concerning meningococcal meningitis:
- A. If it is suspected, benzyl penicillin should be administered prior to hospital admission
- B. Vaccination is available against all strains of meningococcus
- C. Sensorineural deafness has been shown to be the most common permanent sequela
- D. It has been shown to be more common in those children in contact with cigarette smoke
- E. Antibiotic prophylaxis has been recommended for close contacts

**Answer: B.** There is no vaccine against serogroup B in the standard schedule, and vaccines cover only some serogroups.

### 61. The KEPI yellow fever vaccine is administered at:
- A. 6 months
- B. 7 months

### 1. Section B, Question 1. Describe the biosynthesis of vitamin D in the human body (10 marks).

Vitamin D comes from **skin synthesis** (the main source) and from **food**. It then goes through two activation steps.

1. **In the skin:** the precursor **7-dehydrocholesterol** (provitamin D3) in the deep layers of the epidermis absorbs **ultraviolet B light (290 to 315 nm)**, which opens its ring to form **previtamin D3**. This changes by heat (thermal isomerisation) to **vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol)**. Skin pigment, sunscreen, clothing, age and season reduce the amount made.
2. **From the diet:** vitamin D3 from oily fish, egg yolk, liver and fortified foods, and vitamin D2 (ergocalciferol) from plants and yeast, are absorbed with fat in the small intestine into chylomicrons.
3. **Transport:** vitamin D in the blood is carried by the **vitamin D-binding protein**.
4. **First hydroxylation in the liver:** the enzyme **25-hydroxylase** (CYP2R1 and CYP27A1) forms **25-hydroxyvitamin D (calcidiol)**. This is the main circulating and storage form, and it is the one measured to judge vitamin D status.
5. **Second hydroxylation in the kidney:** in the proximal tubule the enzyme **1-alpha-hydroxylase** (CYP27B1) forms **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol)**, the active hormone. This step is **stimulated by parathyroid hormone, low calcium and low phosphate**, and inhibited by FGF-23, high calcium and calcitriol itself (negative feedback). The enzyme **24-hydroxylase** makes inactive 24,25-dihydroxyvitamin D.
6. **Actions of calcitriol:** it binds the vitamin D receptor in the gut (raising absorption of calcium and phosphate), bone (mineralisation and remodelling) and kidney (reabsorption of calcium), and it suppresses parathyroid hormone.

**Clinical link:** failure at any step causes rickets: poor sun and diet (lack of the substrate), liver or kidney disease (failure of hydroxylation), or a defective receptor.

### 2. Section B, Question 2. Arimi, a four-year-old boy from Thika, has a history of prolonged diarrhoea and fever, and is said to have lost both his parents two years ago. Further evaluation reveals moderate malnutrition and oral thrush. (a) What is the likely underlying diagnosis (1 mark)? (b) Which WHO stage can he be classified in and why (1 mark)? (c) Describe the life cycle of the aetiological agent (8 marks).

**(a) Likely underlying diagnosis:** **HIV infection (symptomatic, progressing to AIDS).** Both parents have died, and the boy has persistent diarrhoea, fever, malnutrition and thrush. The diagnosis is confirmed with an HIV antibody test (a child over 18 months) or an HIV DNA PCR.

**(b) WHO clinical stage: stage 3.** The WHO paediatric staging gives stage 3 for unexplained persistent diarrhoea (14 days or more), unexplained moderate malnutrition not responding to standard treatment, and oral candidiasis after the first 6 to 8 weeks of life. Severe wasting or malnutrition would make it stage 4.

**(c) Life cycle of HIV** (a retrovirus that infects CD4 cells)

1. **Attachment (binding):** the viral envelope protein **gp120** binds the **CD4 receptor** on the T helper cell, and then a co-receptor (**CCR5 or CXCR4**).
2. **Fusion and entry:** **gp41** changes shape and fuses the viral envelope with the cell membrane, and the viral core enters the cell.
3. **Uncoating:** the capsid breaks open and releases the viral RNA and enzymes.
4. **Reverse transcription:** the enzyme **reverse transcriptase** copies the single-stranded RNA into double-stranded DNA.
5. **Integration:** the viral DNA enters the nucleus, and the enzyme **integrase** inserts it into the host DNA as a **provirus**.
6. **Transcription and translation:** the host cell machinery makes viral RNA and long viral polyproteins.
7. **Assembly:** viral RNA and proteins gather at the cell membrane to form immature virus particles.
8. **Budding and maturation:** the particles bud off from the cell, and the enzyme **protease** cuts the polyproteins into working proteins, giving a mature infectious virus. The CD4 cell is eventually destroyed.

**Link to treatment:** drugs act at each step: entry inhibitors, nucleoside and non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (zidovudine, lamivudine, abacavir, efavirenz, nevirapine), integrase inhibitors (dolutegravir) and protease inhibitors (lopinavir-ritonavir).

### 3. Section C, Question 1. Discuss paediatric acute kidney injury (AKI) under the following headings: (a) Definition (2 marks). (b) Causes of AKI, giving examples in each category (8 marks). (c) Explain the pathophysiological mechanisms of hypertension, hyperkalaemia and anaemia in a patient with AKI (10 marks).

**(a) Definition**

Acute kidney injury is a **sudden fall in kidney function over hours to days**, with a rise in serum creatinine and a build-up of urea, water, potassium and acids. By the KDIGO definition it is a rise in creatinine of **at least 26.5 micromol/L (0.3 mg/dL) within 48 hours**, or **1.5 times the baseline within 7 days**, or **urine output below 0.5 ml/kg/hour for 6 hours** (pRIFLE and KDIGO grade the severity). It is often reversible.

**(b) Causes**

*1. Pre-renal (reduced blood flow to the kidneys)*
- Dehydration from diarrhoea and vomiting, burns and haemorrhage.
- Shock from sepsis or heart failure, and hypoalbuminaemia in nephrotic syndrome.
- Drugs that lower kidney blood flow (NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors).

*2. Intrinsic renal (the kidney itself)*
- **Acute tubular necrosis:** prolonged ischaemia, nephrotoxins (aminoglycosides, amphotericin, contrast), haemoglobinuria (malaria, G6PD) and myoglobinuria.
- **Glomerular:** post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis and rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis.
- **Vascular and microangiopathy:** haemolytic uraemic syndrome, and renal vein thrombosis.
- **Interstitial:** acute interstitial nephritis (drugs, infection), and pyelonephritis.

*3. Post-renal (obstruction of urine flow)*
- Posterior urethral valves, bilateral ureteric stones, tumours pressing on the ureters, neurogenic bladder and a blocked catheter.

**(c) Pathophysiology**

*1. Hypertension*
- The kidney cannot excrete salt and water when the GFR falls, so the **blood volume expands** (fluid overload).
- Injured kidneys release **renin**, which activates angiotensin II and aldosterone, causing **vasoconstriction** and more sodium retention.
- Increased sympathetic activity, and in glomerulonephritis the inflammation, add to it.

*2. Hyperkalaemia*
- Less potassium is filtered and secreted when the GFR falls and urine output drops (oliguria).
- **Metabolic acidosis** pushes potassium out of the cells.
- **Cell breakdown** (rhabdomyolysis, haemolysis, tumour lysis, burns) releases intracellular potassium, and potassium-rich feeds, blood transfusion and drugs (ACE inhibitors, spironolactone) add to it.
- The danger is cardiac: peaked T waves, a wide QRS and arrhythmias or arrest.

*3. Anaemia*
- **Reduced erythropoietin** production by the damaged kidney (more important in chronic disease).
- **Uraemic toxins** suppress the bone marrow and shorten red cell survival (haemolysis).
- **Blood loss** from poor platelet function (uraemic bleeding, gastrointestinal bleeding) and blood sampling.
- **Haemodilution** from fluid overload, and in haemolytic uraemic syndrome, direct red cell destruction.
- Iron or folate deficiency and the underlying illness may add.

### 4. Section C, Question 2. (a) State the child, infant and neonatal mortality rates in Kenya according to the 2014 Kenya Demographic and Health Survey report (3 marks).
