## Multiple choice questions

### 1. The following are features of infective endocarditis EXCEPT:
- A. Fever
- B. Migratory polyarthritis
- C. Positive blood culture
- D. Elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate
- E. Migratory murmur

**Answer: B.** Migratory polyarthritis is a major criterion for rheumatic fever. A changing murmur (new or changing regurgitation) is the endocarditis feature, and "migratory murmur" in option E is loosely worded.

### 2. To make a diagnosis of infective endocarditis using the modified Duke criteria, choose the correct combination that can be used to make a definite diagnosis.
- A. 2 major
- B. 1 major and 3 minor
- C. 5 minor
- D. All the above
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Definite endocarditis needs 2 major, or 1 major and 3 minor, or 5 minor criteria.

### 3. The most common organism that causes infective endocarditis on native valves is:
- A. Streptococcus pyogenes
- B. Staphylococcus aureus
- C. E. coli
- D. Group B streptococcus
- E. HACEK group

**Answer: B.** Staphylococcus aureus is now the commonest cause on native valves.

### 4. The following form part of the major categories in the Jones criteria for acute rheumatic fever EXCEPT:
- A. Polyarthritis
- B. Carditis
- C. Sydenham chorea
- D. Erythema multiforme
- E. Subcutaneous nodules

**Answer: D.** The skin sign is erythema marginatum, not erythema multiforme.

### 5. A newborn baby presents with worsening cyanosis that does not respond to high-flow oxygen. Which congenital heart condition is LEAST likely to be present?
- A. Tricuspid atresia
- B. Transposition of the great arteries
- C. Truncus arteriosus
- D. Ventricular septal defect
- E. Hypoplastic left ventricle

**Answer: D.** An isolated VSD is acyanotic.

### 6. The following are expected findings in a child diagnosed with tetralogy of Fallot EXCEPT:
- A. Overriding aorta
- B. Right ventricular hypertrophy
- C. Ventricular septal defect
- D. Pulmonary stenosis
- E. Atrial septal defect

**Answer: E.** The four features are VSD, pulmonary stenosis, overriding aorta and right ventricular hypertrophy.

### 7. In the management of a child with tetralogy of Fallot who has an acute cyanotic spell, which medication is NOT used?
- A. Propranolol
- B. Sodium bicarbonate
- C. Morphine
- D. Phenobarbitone
- E. Oxygen

**Answer: D.** Phenobarbitone has no role in a tet spell.

### 8. A 1-year-old child who was born preterm has a machinery murmur at the right upper sternal border, bounding pulses and a wide pulse pressure. The most likely cardiac lesion is:
- A. Ventricular septal defect
- B. Aortic regurgitation
- C. Pulmonary regurgitation
- D. Patent ductus arteriosus
- E. Aortic regurgitation

**Answer: D.** A continuous machinery murmur with bounding pulses is a patent ductus, and prematurity is a risk factor. (The classic site is the left upper sternal border. Options B and E are repeated in the paper.)

### 9. A 10-year-old girl with rheumatic heart disease has difficulty breathing, swollen legs and face, a gallop rhythm, a loud murmur of mitral regurgitation and a tender enlarged liver. The most appropriate next step is:
- A. Do intense exercise to help with breathing and quicken recovery
- B. Advise a diet high in salt
- C. Start oxygen and diuretics
- D. Start antibiotics
- E. Do a lumbar puncture

**Answer: C.** This is heart failure, treated with oxygen, rest and diuretics, then anti-inflammatory therapy.

### 10. In a child with coarctation of the aorta, the following signs are expected EXCEPT:
- A. Radio-femoral delay
- B. Blood pressure difference between upper and lower limbs
- C. Severe central cyanosis
- D. Weak pulses in the lower limbs
- E. Signs of circulatory failure in the lower limbs

**Answer: C.** Coarctation is an acyanotic lesion.

### 11. Which of the following signs is found in a patient with aortic regurgitation?
- A. Loud P2
- B. Pistol shots at the brachial pulse
- C. Single S1
- D. Continuous machinery murmur at the apex
- E. Corrigan sign

**Answer: E.** Corrigan sign is the visible forceful carotid pulsation of a collapsing pulse. The pistol-shot sound is over the femoral artery, so option B is loosely worded.

### 12. Medication that should be given to keep the patent ductus open in children with a duct-dependent lesion includes:
- A. Prostaglandin E1
- B. Prostacyclin
- C. Prostaglandin I2
- D. Prostaglandin E2
- E. Indomethacin

**Answer: A.** Alprostadil (PGE1) keeps the ductus open. Indomethacin and ibuprofen close it.

### 13. A 1-week-old preterm baby with a birth weight of 1.2 kg has a haemodynamically significant patent ductus arteriosus. Which is the most correct step in management?
- A. The patient can be observed for a period of 1 year, then the ductus can be closed
- B. The ductus should be closed immediately since it is haemodynamically significant
- C. A trial of ibuprofen or indomethacin should be given to see if the ductus shall close
- D. There is no role for fluid restriction in this case
- E. Diuretics are dangerous at this age and should not be tried

**Answer: C.** Ibuprofen or indomethacin often closes the duct in preterm babies. Fluid restriction and diuretics also help, and surgical or device closure is kept for drug failure.

### 14. Wairimu, 4 years, has a 2-day history of lower abdominal pain, painful micturition and a low-grade fever. The most likely urinalysis findings are:
- A. Red blood cell casts, 2+ protein, pyuria and red cells too numerous to count
- B. Numerous maltese crosses
- C. 4+ protein, 1+ ketones, 0 to 5 RBC/HPF, 0 to 5 WBC/HPF
- D. RBC and WBC casts, more than 50 RBC/HPF, 20 WBC/HPF and many bacteria
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Painful micturition with fever suggests a urinary tract infection, with white cells and bacteria in the urine. Option A is nephritis and option C is nephrotic syndrome. Option D is the nearest, though casts point higher up the tract.

### 15. An 8-year-old girl has a seizure, a blood pressure of 180/110 mmHg, and several episodes of coca-cola coloured urine a few days ago. She was given an antibiotic for a presumed urinary tract infection, and has since had headaches and ankle swelling. She had a sore throat 2 weeks before. A CT of the head is normal. Choose the truest statement.
- A. An urgent renal biopsy is needed to confirm a diagnosis of nephrotic syndrome
- B. Alport syndrome is a likely diagnosis in this child
- C. Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis is likely to be the diagnosis here
- D. An urgent haemogram and plan to transfuse should be the immediate next step, as the child is most likely in cardiac failure due to anaemia
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** Cola-coloured urine, hypertension, oedema and a recent throat infection fit post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, and the seizure is hypertensive encephalopathy rather than an infection or a bleed.

### 16. Concerning the child in question 15, choose the true fact:
- A. Serotype 49 causes post-streptococcal nephritis after an upper respiratory infection, occurring primarily in the cold season
- B. Serotype 12 causes post-streptococcal nephritis after a skin infection, usually in a warm climate
- C. PSGN usually develops 1 to 3 weeks after an acute infection with specific nephritogenic strains of group A beta-haemolytic streptococcus
- D. The incidence of GN is approximately 50% in persons with pharyngitis and 35% in those with skin infections
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** Nephritis follows pharyngitis by 1 to 2 weeks and skin infection by 3 to 6 weeks. Serotype 12 is the pharyngitis strain and 49 is the skin strain, so A and B are swapped, and only a small proportion of infected people develop nephritis.

### 17. In the management of children with acute post-infectious glomerulonephritis, which statement is true?
- A. Antibiotic therapy with penicillin in the early phase improves the outcomes
- B. Loop diuretics, fluid restriction and antihypertensives have a role
- C. Prognosis in most cases is guarded and the disease takes a prolonged and protracted course
- D. Haematuria resolves in about a week after initiation of treatment and the prognosis is excellent in most cases of AGN
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Treatment is supportive: salt and fluid restriction, a loop diuretic and antihypertensives. Penicillin eradicates the organism but does not change the kidney outcome, and microscopic haematuria can persist for months.

### 18. A 3-year-old boy has oedema and shortness of breath, 3+ proteinuria, a serum albumin of 2 g/dL and 3 g of protein in a 24-hour urine. Light microscopy of a kidney biopsy would most likely reveal:
- A. Diffuse crescent formation
- B. Diffuse thickening of the glomerular basement membrane
- C. Mesangial cell proliferation
- D. Minimal change disease with fusion of podocyte foot processes
- E. All the above

**Answer: D.** Nephrotic syndrome in a young child is usually minimal change disease. (Foot process fusion is seen only on electron microscopy, and light microscopy looks normal.)

### 19. After consultation with a paediatric nephrologist, what would be the most appropriate combination of medications for the above patient?
- A. ACE inhibitor, furosemide and prednisone
- B. Prednisone, ACE inhibitor, albumin
- C. Prednisone, furosemide, albumin
- D. Furosemide, spironolactone, digoxin
- E. Furosemide, ACE inhibitor, IV immunoglobulin

**Answer: C.** Prednisolone treats the disease, and albumin with a loop diuretic treats severe oedema and effusions. ACE inhibitors are for steroid-resistant disease.

### 20. In regard to the pathophysiology of nephrotic syndrome in children, which statement is true?
- A. The predominant presentation is gross oedema, attributed to progressive nephron loss and loss of kidney filtration capacity
- B. Hyperlipidaemia is a common presentation and is associated with coagulation defects
- C. Acute bacterial peritonitis is a common complication in patients with ascites
- D. Hypoalbuminaemia and reduced oncotic pressure are attributable to progressive loss of nephrons
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis is a common complication. Oedema comes from protein loss through leaky glomeruli, not loss of nephrons. Option B is also partly true, so the question is loosely worded.

## Part 1: essay

### 1. Discuss the congenital cardiac conditions under the following topics. (a) List 5 cyanotic congenital heart conditions (5 marks). (b) List 5 acyanotic congenital heart conditions (5 marks). (c) Discuss the presentation and the management of a child with tetralogy of Fallot (20 marks).

**(a) Five cyanotic (right-to-left shunt) lesions** (the five Ts and others)

1. **Tetralogy of Fallot** (the commonest cyanotic lesion beyond infancy).
2. **Transposition of the great arteries.**
3. **Tricuspid atresia.**
4. **Total anomalous pulmonary venous return** (with obstruction).
5. **Truncus arteriosus.**
6. Others: pulmonary atresia, Ebstein anomaly, hypoplastic left heart syndrome, and Eisenmenger syndrome (a late cyanosis).

**(b) Five acyanotic lesions**

*Left-to-right shunts:*
1. **Ventricular septal defect** (the commonest).
2. **Atrial septal defect.**
3. **Patent ductus arteriosus.**
4. **Atrioventricular septal (canal) defect.**

*Obstructive lesions:*
5. **Coarctation of the aorta.**
6. Others: pulmonary stenosis and aortic stenosis.

**(c) Tetralogy of Fallot**

**Anatomy (the four features):** a large **ventricular septal defect**, **right ventricular outflow obstruction** (pulmonary stenosis, which sets the severity), an **overriding aorta**, and **right ventricular hypertrophy**. The cause is anterior displacement of the infundibular septum.

**Presentation**

1. **Cyanosis,** often appearing from 2 to 3 months of age (as the duct closes and the obstruction worsens), and worse on crying, feeding and exercise. Cyanosis is central.
2. **Hypercyanotic (tet) spells:** sudden worsening of cyanosis with fast deep breathing and irritability, usually on waking, crying or feeding, which can lead to loss of consciousness, convulsions or stroke.
3. **Squatting** in older children (to raise the systemic vascular resistance and increase pulmonary flow), and poor exercise tolerance.
4. **Clubbing** of the fingers and toes in older children, and poor growth.
5. **Examination:** a single loud second heart sound, a **harsh ejection systolic murmur** at the left upper sternal edge (from pulmonary stenosis, and **softer** as the obstruction is worse), and a right ventricular heave. There is no heart failure.
6. **Complications:** brain abscess, cerebral thrombosis (especially with anaemia and dehydration), infective endocarditis, polycythaemia and iron deficiency.

**Investigations**

1. **Chest X-ray:** boot-shaped heart (coeur en sabot) with a concave pulmonary bay and oligaemic (dark) lung fields, and a right-sided aortic arch in about 25%.
2. **ECG:** right axis deviation and right ventricular hypertrophy.
3. **Echocardiography:** confirms the diagnosis, shows the VSD, the overriding aorta, the outflow obstruction and the coronary arteries.
4. **Blood count:** polycythaemia (raised haemoglobin), and check for iron deficiency.
5. **Pulse oximetry** (low saturation), and cardiac catheterisation or CT only when needed to plan surgery.

**Management**

*1. General care*
- Keep the child well hydrated, treat iron deficiency, and prevent infections and endocarditis (good dental hygiene, and antibiotic prophylaxis before dental procedures).
- Immunise on the normal schedule, and give advice to parents on recognising a tet spell.

*2. Acute hypercyanotic spell (an emergency)*
1. Calm the child and place in the **knee-chest position** (held on the mother's shoulder).
2. Give **oxygen.**
3. **Morphine** (0.1 mg/kg IM or subcutaneous) to relieve distress and fast breathing.
4. **IV fluids** (a bolus of 10 to 20 ml/kg of normal saline) to raise the filling pressure.
5. **Sodium bicarbonate** for acidosis.
6. **Propranolol** (IV 0.1 mg/kg slowly) or esmolol, to relax the infundibular spasm. Phenylephrine (a vasoconstrictor) if the spell does not settle.
7. Ketamine or intubation and ventilation if the spell continues.

*3. Prevention of spells*
- Oral **propranolol** (1 to 2 mg/kg every 6 to 8 hours) while waiting for surgery.

*4. Definitive treatment: surgery*
- **Complete intracardiac repair** (closure of the VSD with a patch and relief of the pulmonary stenosis), usually at 3 to 12 months of age.
- **Palliation first** when the child is very small, has hypoplastic pulmonary arteries or anomalous coronaries: a **Blalock-Taussig shunt** (subclavian artery to pulmonary artery) to raise pulmonary blood flow, with later complete repair.
- **Lifelong follow-up** for residual lesions, pulmonary regurgitation, arrhythmias and the need for further valve surgery.
