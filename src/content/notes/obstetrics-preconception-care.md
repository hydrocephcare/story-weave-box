# Pre-conception care — Year 4 Obstetrics and Gynaecology Notes

*Based on the Department's pre-conception care lectures. Many serious problems in pregnancy are decided before the woman knows she is pregnant, so this is the cheapest and earliest intervention in obstetrics.*

**The reasoning chain:** which women are at risk before they conceive → what in their history, examination and tests tells you → what can be fixed now (disease control, drugs, vaccines, folic acid, weight, habits) → plan the timing and spacing of the pregnancy.

## 1. What it is, and who it is for

**Pre-conception care** is a set of interventions that **identify and modify biomedical, behavioural and social risks** to a woman's health and to future pregnancies. The target group is **women of reproductive age (15 to 49 years)**, but **men are included** in several parts.

**Objectives**

1. **Health promotion and education** to improve knowledge, attitudes and behaviour of men and women about pregnancy.
2. **Evidence-based screening** for pregnancy risks.
3. **Interventions** to deal with the risks and conditions found.
4. Contribute to **universal coverage of essential obstetric care**.

## 2. Why it matters

- **Early fetal development is the most vulnerable.** The fetus is most susceptible to structural problems in **weeks 4 to 10**, when many women **do not yet know they are pregnant**. By the first antenatal visit (recommended before 16 weeks) most of the damage that could have been prevented is done.
- **In many African settings** poor birth outcomes are driven by things that can be addressed beforehand: **prematurity, birth asphyxia and infection** in newborns; **postpartum haemorrhage** as the leading single cause of maternal death; **HIV** (young women are several times more likely to be infected than young men of the same age); **malaria and anaemia** in pregnancy; **smoking, alcohol and drug misuse**; and **childhood malnutrition, stunting and obesity** that later affect pregnancy.

## 3. Who is at risk? (the reproductive health risk list)

| Risk | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Age and parity** | Under 18 or over 35; **grand multiparity** (gravida 5 and above); short birth interval |
| **Social** | Low socio-economic status, gender-based violence, **FGM**, **early marriage**, harmful food taboos and delays in seeking care |
| **Previous adverse outcomes** | Recurrent miscarriages, **stillbirth**, early neonatal death, a baby with a **congenital abnormality** |
| **Medical conditions** | **Anaemia, malaria, HIV/AIDS, TB, STIs**, heart disease, **diabetes, sickle cell disease**, asthma, **hypertension**, kidney disease, **ABO and rhesus incompatibility**, breast and cervical cancer |
| **Obstetric complications** | Previous **postpartum haemorrhage**, **caesarean section scars**, previous **preterm labour**, previous **pre-eclampsia or eclampsia** |
| **Substances** | Smoking, alcohol, drugs, **teratogenic prescription or over-the-counter medicines**, herbs |

## 4. History: take it in eight parts

1. **Family history:** congenital anomalies, genetic disease, diabetes, hypertension, twins.
2. **Medical history:** diabetes, hypertension, HIV, TB, STIs and RTIs, epilepsy, cancers, breast problems.
3. **Obstetric and gynaecological history:** pregnancy loss, preterm births, **STI/PID**, menstrual problems, prolonged subfertility.
4. **Environmental history:** radiation, chemicals.
5. **Occupational history:** type of work and long working hours (long-distance drivers, athletes, cyclists; for men, heat around the testes).
6. **Nutritional history:** diet quality, anaemia, vegetarian diets (B12), food restrictions.
7. **Social history:** smoking, alcohol, drugs, partner violence, support.
8. **Male partner history:** **mumps, STIs/HIV, substance misuse, tight clothing**, and a semen history if there is infertility.

## 5. Examination and tests

Examine **general health, blood pressure, weight and BMI**, thyroid, heart and lungs, breasts, abdomen and pelvis, with a **cervical screen** if due.

**Minimum investigations:**

| Test | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Full blood count** | Anaemia, sickle cell trait signs, haemoglobinopathy |
| **Random blood sugar** | Diabetes (a major cause of malformations when poorly controlled) |
| **Syphilis test** | Congenital syphilis can be prevented |
| **HIV test** | Start treatment before pregnancy to prevent mother-to-child transmission |
| **Blood group and rhesus** | Plan for rhesus incompatibility |
| **Urinalysis** | Infection, protein, sugar |

Add rubella immunity, hepatitis B, haemoglobin electrophoresis, thyroid function and genetic tests **as the history suggests**.

## 6. Interventions

**Health education and counselling:** a **reproductive life plan** (healthy timing and spacing: wait **at least 24 months** after a live birth before the next conception), **family planning**, **prenatal diagnosis and genetic counselling** (Down syndrome, sickle cell disease, thalassaemia), virological screening (**TORCH**), and avoiding unnecessary medicines.

**Lifestyle:** healthy diet, a **BMI between 18.5 and 25**, stop **alcohol, tobacco and drugs**, regular exercise and adequate rest. **Timing of intercourse:** explain the fertile days of the ovulatory cycle; regular intercourse two to three times a week through the cycle is the best advice.

**Prophylaxis:**

- **Folic acid for every woman trying to conceive**, started **at least one month (ideally three) before conception** and continued to 12 weeks, to prevent **neural tube defects**. A **higher dose** is used for women with a **previous neural tube defect, epilepsy on anticonvulsants, diabetes or obesity**.
- **Iron, zinc, iodine, calcium and vitamin A** (in moderation, as vitamin A in excess is teratogenic) according to health status.
- **Vaccines:** rubella, hepatitis B, tetanus toxoid, and others as indicated.

**Pre-existing medical problems:** **stabilise and optimise** them before conception (**diabetes control, blood pressure, epilepsy, HIV treatment, TB treatment**), **review every drug** for safety in pregnancy (for example, switch **ACE inhibitors, warfarin, valproate, methotrexate** to safer options) and for effects on sperm (cytotoxics, radiation, smoking, alcohol), and **refer** for specialist care when needed.

## 7. Delivery: put it where women already go

Pre-conception services must **not stand alone**. Integrate them into **comprehensive care clinics**, **family planning clinics**, **school health programmes**, **VCT centres** and services that **reach men**.

## Practice questions

**1.** The fetus is most vulnerable to structural malformations during:
**A. Weeks 4–10** · B. Weeks 1–3 · C. Weeks 12–16 · D. Weeks 20–24 · E. The third trimester
*Answer: A. Many women do not know they are pregnant yet.*

**2.** Which supplement reduces the risk of neural tube defects?
A. Vitamin C · B. Iron · **C. Folic acid** · D. Vitamin A in high dose · E. Calcium
*Answer: C.*

**3.** Which statement about pre-conception care is TRUE?
A. It only concerns women · B. It starts at the first antenatal visit · C. It only needs blood group testing · **D. It includes the male partner and can be integrated into existing clinics** · E. It is not needed if the woman is healthy
*Answer: D.*

**4.** A woman with epilepsy on valproate wants to conceive. The best advice is:
A. Stop all drugs · B. Continue valproate unchanged · C. Wait until the second trimester to review · D. Start vitamin A · **E. Review the drug with a specialist before conception and take high-dose folic acid**
*Answer: E.*
