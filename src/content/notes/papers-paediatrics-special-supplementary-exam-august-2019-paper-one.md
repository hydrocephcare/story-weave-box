## Multiple choice questions

### 1. The following are signs of circulatory shock EXCEPT:
- A. Cold extremities
- B. Slow capillary refill
- C. Strong peripheral pulse
- D. Responds only to voice
- E. Weak peripheral pulse

**Answer: C.** Shock gives a weak, fast pulse, cold hands, slow capillary refill (over 3 seconds) and a depressed level of consciousness.

### 2. New parents ask how to reduce the chance of sudden infant death syndrome. You tell them to place the child in which position for sleep?
- A. Supine position
- B. Prone position
- C. Seated position
- D. Trendelenburg position
- E. A hammock

**Answer: A.** Back-sleeping (supine) lowers the risk of SIDS.

### 3. Which is true about hepatitis A?
- A. Children commonly recover spontaneously
- B. Diagnostic laboratory tests should include IgM antibodies to hepatitis A
- C. The diagnosis should not be considered if contacts have a history of jaundice
- D. The vaccine is approved for children older than 2 years
- E. The incubation period is 10 to 14 days

**Answer: A.** Most children recover fully without treatment. B is also true (IgM anti-HAV is the diagnostic test). The incubation period is 2 to 6 weeks, and the vaccine can be given from 1 year.

### 4. The following are true regarding Streptococcus pneumoniae EXCEPT:
- A. The bacterium is identified in culture by partial (alpha) haemolysis
- B. Children with sickle cell disease are susceptible to pneumococcal infection
- C. Virulent strains are resistant to phagocytosis by alveolar macrophages
- D. The anti-phagocytic properties of the capsule contribute to its virulence
- E. Nasopharyngeal carriage consistently induces systemic immunity

**Answer: E.** Carriage does not reliably produce protective immunity, which is why vaccination is used.

### 5. Which finding is unlikely in the syndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion?
- A. Serum sodium below 135 mEq/L
- B. Urine osmolality above 100 mOsm/kg
- C. Serum osmolality above 290 mOsm/L
- D. Euvolaemic hyponatraemia
- E. Excess urine sodium with normal intake

**Answer: C.** In SIADH the serum is dilute (osmolality low, under 275), while the urine is relatively concentrated.

### 6. A 12-month-old has knob-like swellings on the anterior chest wall, soft cranial bones and delayed motor milestones. Which findings are expected?
- A. High serum calcium
- B. Low alkaline phosphatase
- C. High inorganic phosphate
- D. Decreased osteoblastic activity
- E. High alkaline phosphatase

**Answer: E.** This is rickets. Alkaline phosphatase is high, calcium low to normal and phosphate low.

### 7. An 11-month-old girl has had cough and difficulty breathing for 1 day. She is in respiratory distress and the chest is hyperresonant. Which is FALSE?
- A. Oxygen treatment is recommended
- B. RSV is most likely the cause
- C. Her prognosis is good
- D. IV antibiotics should not be commenced
- E. She should be started on aminophylline

**Answer: E.** This is bronchiolitis (or a first wheeze). Treatment is supportive with oxygen and feeding support, and aminophylline does not help.

### 8. Onyango, 3 years, has had a cough for 2 days, a temperature of 39 degrees, a respiratory rate of 66 and chest indrawing. There is no cyanosis and he is well nourished. Which is true?
- A. A Mantoux test is recommended
- B. A course of ceftriaxone is recommended
- C. He should be put on oxygen with a non-rebreather mask
- D. He should be isolated
- E. A course of meropenem is recommended

**Answer: B.** Chest indrawing makes this severe pneumonia, which needs admission and injectable antibiotics (benzylpenicillin with gentamicin, or ceftriaxone). The other options are not indicated.

### 9. A 6-month-old is HBsAg positive, anti-HBs negative, anti-HBc positive and HBeAg positive. Which statement about HBV infection or its treatment in this child is true?
- A. Active and passive immunisation should begin immediately
- B. Active replication of HBV is not occurring
- C. Exposure to HBV occurred in the past but the infection has cleared
- D. The infection was transmitted horizontally
- E. Treatment with interferon-alpha may eradicate the virus

**Answer: E.** HBsAg and HBeAg together mean active, highly infectious disease, probably from mother-to-child transmission. Interferon-alpha is a recognised treatment for children and can clear the virus in some. Vaccination now cannot prevent an established infection.

### 10. Jason, 7 years, is HIV-infected with nuchal rigidity and a positive CSF India ink stain. The acute phase of treatment consists of:
- A. Amphotericin B
- B. Fluconazole
- C. Itraconazole
- D. Amphotericin B and flucytosine
- E. Pentamidine

**Answer: D.** This is cryptococcal meningitis. Induction is amphotericin B with flucytosine, followed by fluconazole.

### 11. Which is a differential diagnosis for a child with fever persisting for more than 21 days?
- A. Previous drug administration
- B. Psychosomatic illness in young infants
- C. Occult infection
- D. Munchausen disease
- E. Systemic lupus erythematosus

**Answer: E.** Lupus is a classic cause of prolonged fever (with TB, endocarditis and malignancy). The other options are less usual or loosely worded, though drug fever and occult infection also occur.

### 12. A 2-year-old has rhinorrhoea, cough and fever with non-purulent conjunctivitis, and 7 days later a maculopapular rash. Which is NOT true?
- A. Generalised lymphadenopathy is a feature
- B. Transaminases are elevated
- C. The rash spreads cranio-caudally
- D. Leucocytosis is characteristic
- E. Lymphopenia is characteristic

**Answer: D.** This is measles, which causes leucopenia, not leucocytosis.

### 13. The earliest adolescent change in boys is:
- A. Laryngeal enlargement
- B. Development of pubic hair
- C. Appearance of facial hair
- D. Growth spurt
- E. Testicular growth

**Answer: E.** Testicular enlargement is the first sign of puberty in boys.

### 14. Regarding X-linked recessive conditions:
- A. They mostly affect females
- B. They can be passed from father to son
- C. They are not passed from father to son
- D. Females with a linked recessive condition will have a 50% chance of having an affected son
- E. Sickle cell disease is inherited in an X-linked recessive pattern

**Answer: C.** A father passes his Y chromosome to his sons, so he cannot pass on an X-linked disease to a son. Option D is true only for a carrier mother, and sickle cell is autosomal recessive.

### 15. Regarding mitochondrial inheritance, the following are true EXCEPT:
- A. Mitochondrial disorders show maternal inheritance
- B. A woman with a mitochondrial disorder can have affected offspring of either sex
- C. An affected father cannot pass on the disease to his offspring
- D. Mitochondrial DNA mutations are often deletions or point mutations
- E. Detection of a mitochondrial genome mutation does not require sampling of the affected tissue

**Answer: E.** The mutation load varies between tissues, so the affected tissue usually has to be tested.

### 16. Regarding Down syndrome, which statement is true?
- A. Robertsonian translocation is the commonest cause
- B. The risk increases with maternal age above 35 years
- C. Affected individuals are not more prone to congenital heart defects
- D. Social development is severely affected
- E. Life expectancy is 70 years

**Answer: B.** Non-disjunction (about 95%) is the commonest cause and its risk rises with maternal age. Heart defects are common, and social skills are relatively preserved.

### 17. A 10-day-old newborn has intermittent stridor. The most common cause is:
- A. Viral croup
- B. Laryngomalacia
- C. Foreign body obstruction
- D. Acute bronchiolitis
- E. Choanal atresia

**Answer: B.** Laryngomalacia is the commonest cause of stridor in infants. It is worse with crying and feeding, and the infant is otherwise well.

### 18. A child with Down syndrome has an increased risk of all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Hydrocephalus
- B. Duodenal atresia
- C. Leukaemia
- D. Hypothyroidism
- E. Congenital heart disease

**Answer: A.** Down syndrome is associated with a small head, not hydrocephalus.

### 19. The following are associated with a false-negative Mantoux test EXCEPT:
- A. Very young age
- B. Severe malnutrition
- C. Immunosuppression
- D. Overwhelming TB
- E. Obesity

**Answer: E.** Obesity does not suppress the reaction. BCG vaccination causes a false positive.

### 20. A class two pupil presents with acute loss of consciousness and weight loss over two months. Random blood sugar is 26.8 mmol/L. One of the following is true:
- A. The condition can be avoided by exclusive breastfeeding
- B. The condition is associated with metabolic alkalosis
- C. Hyperkalaemia is a common finding
- D. Obesity is a key risk factor
- E. Hypothyroidism occurs in 40% of affected patients

**Answer: C.** This is diabetic ketoacidosis in type 1 diabetes. The acidosis shifts potassium out of cells, so the measured potassium is often high even though total body potassium is low. It causes metabolic acidosis, not alkalosis.

### 21. A 2-month-old born to an HIV-positive mother is followed in the comprehensive care centre. Which therapy should be considered?
- A. Monthly evaluation for Kaposi sarcoma
- B. Prophylaxis against Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia
- C. Vitamin C supplementation
- D. Oral polio vaccine
- E. Bone marrow transplantation

**Answer: B.** Co-trimoxazole prophylaxis starts at 6 weeks in HIV-exposed infants.

### 22. Which of the following infections is LEAST likely to cause arthritis?
- A. Malaria
- B. HIV
- C. Streptococcus
- D. Staphylococcus
- E. Salmonella

**Answer: A.** Malaria does not infect joints. Staphylococcus, streptococcus and Salmonella (especially in sickle cell disease) cause septic arthritis.

### 23. A 4-year-old from Siaya has painful knuckles and a temperature of 38.5 degrees, with no trauma. Which condition is MOST likely?
- A. Thrombotic crisis of sickle cells
- B. Malaria
- C. Septic arthritis
- D. Haemophilia
- E. Juvenile idiopathic arthritis

**Answer: A.** Painful swollen hands and feet (dactylitis) in a young child from a high-prevalence area suggest a sickle cell crisis, which can also bring fever.

### 24. In asymmetrical intrauterine growth restriction, which organ is spared?
- A. Brain
- B. Liver
- C. Subcutaneous tissue
- D. Heart
- E. Spleen

**Answer: A.** Blood is diverted to protect the brain, so the head is relatively large and the abdomen and fat are small (brain-sparing).

### 25. Risk factors for neural tube defects include the following EXCEPT:
- A. Pre-conceptual diet of the mother
- B. Mother's age
- C. Post-conceptual diet of the mother
- D. Geographic region of residence of the mother
- E. Family history of the same illness

**Answer: C.** The neural tube closes by about 28 days, so it is the diet before and in the first weeks of conception (folate) that matters, not the diet after conception. The question is loosely worded.

### 26. Which statement is true?
- A. In congenital laryngomalacia the infant is usually happy and growing
- B. The goal of oxygen therapy is a saturation above 80%
- C. The most common cause of bronchiolitis is respiratory syncytial virus
- D. Bronchial asthma always presents with wheeze
- E. The side effects of salbutamol include bradycardia and hypertension

**Answer: C.** RSV causes most bronchiolitis. The first option is also true, but the oxygen target is 90% or more, asthma can present as cough only, and salbutamol causes tachycardia.

### 27. Which is the most common type of astrocytoma in children?
- A. Pilocytic astrocytoma
- B. Diffuse astrocytoma
- C. Craniopharyngioma
- D. Anaplastic astrocytoma
- E. Glioblastoma multiforme

**Answer: A.** Pilocytic astrocytoma (often in the cerebellum) is the commonest and has a good outlook.

### 28. A lesion in the frontal lobe causes:
- A. Apraxia
- B. Broca's (expressive) aphasia
- C. Cortical blindness
- D. Homonymous hemianopia
- E. Visuospatial neglect

**Answer: B.** Broca's area is in the dominant frontal lobe. Cortical blindness and hemianopia are occipital, and neglect is parietal.

### 29. Bundi, a 10-month-old, has had 2 days of fever and convulsions, with no loss of consciousness between. All are true EXCEPT:
- A. Family history is relevant
- B. The first episode is an indication for CSF studies
- C. Peak age is 3 months
- D. It is the commonest cause of seizures in childhood
- E. Convulsions cannot be prevented by using an antipyretic

**Answer: C.** This is a febrile convulsion. The peak age is about 18 months (range 6 months to 5 years). Antipyretics do not prevent recurrences, and a first episode under 12 months should lead to considering a lumbar puncture.

### 35. Which statement applies to a 2-month-old baby with gastroenteritis who is 10% clinically dehydrated?
- A. He has blood and mucus in his stools
- B. He has a full fontanelle
- C. He has a low packed cell volume
- D. The urea level is elevated
- E. He should not be given milk orally for at least 48 hours

**Answer: D.** Dehydration raises the urea and the packed cell volume, and the fontanelle is sunken. Feeding continues.

### 36. Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome occurs in:
- A. Tuberculous meningitis
- B. Medulloblastoma
- C. Fulminant meningococcaemia
- D. Diabetes insipidus
- E. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia

**Answer: C.** It is adrenal haemorrhage and shock from fulminant meningococcal sepsis.

### 37. Juliet, 10 months, has abdominal pain and diarrhoea with pink staining, episodes of crying with drawing up of the legs, and a curved firm mass 10 cm long in the right iliac fossa. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Inborn errors of metabolism
- B. Constipation or encopresis
- C. Intussusception
- D. Polycystic kidney disease
- E. Malignancy (neuroblastoma, Wilms, lymphoma)

**Answer: C.** Colicky pain, redcurrant jelly stool and a sausage-shaped mass are classic.

### 38. A well-nourished 4-year-old has a firm bimanually palpable mass in the left lumbar region. The following are associated with this condition EXCEPT:
- A. Asymmetry of body parts
- B. Absent iris
- C. Hypertension
- D. Simian palmar crease
- E. Haematuria

**Answer: D.** This is Wilms tumour, linked to hemihypertrophy, aniridia, hypertension and haematuria. A simian crease is a feature of Down syndrome.

### 39. A 4-month-old with birth weight 3.5 kg now weighs 3.7 kg and has visible wasting. Which is an UNLIKELY immediate life-threatening problem?
- A. Hypothermia
- B. Hypoglycaemia
- C. Dehydration
- D. Hyperkalaemia
- E. Infection

**Answer: D.** Malnourished children develop low potassium (hypokalaemia), not high. Hypothermia, hypoglycaemia, dehydration and infection kill.

### 40. A 12-year-old has had popping eyes, muscle wasting and heat intolerance. Positive examination findings would include:
- A. Cold and moist skin
- B. Bradycardia
- C. Hyperactivity and tremors
- D. Poor or low appetite
- E. Non-pitting oedema

**Answer: C.** This is thyrotoxicosis (Graves disease): warm sweaty skin, tachycardia, tremor, increased appetite with weight loss, and exophthalmos.

### 41. Which is the correct diagnostic criterion for severe acute malnutrition?
- A. Weight for age below 70% of expected
- B. Weight for age more than 3 standard deviations below average
- C. Height for age 85% of expected
- D. Weight for age below 60% of expected plus hair and skin changes
- E. Weight for length 3 standard deviations below average

**Answer: E.** Severe acute malnutrition is a weight-for-length (or height) Z score below minus 3, a MUAC under 11.5 cm, or bilateral pitting oedema.

### 42. Which is NOT a recommendation of the Alma-Ata Declaration of Primary Health Care?
- A. Provision of safe water and basic sanitation
- B. Appropriate treatment of common childhood diseases
- C. Provision of essential drugs
- D. Increased immunisation against yellow fever
- E. Provision of health education

**Answer: D.** The declaration asks for immunisation against the major infectious diseases, and yellow fever is not singled out.

### 43. Neuroblastoma:
- A. Is an embryonic tumour
- B. Usually has a familial incidence
- C. Metastasises most commonly to the lungs
- D. Is easily eradicated by radiotherapy
- E. Is a non-secreting tumour

**Answer: A.** It arises from primitive neural crest cells. It is mostly sporadic, spreads to bone marrow, bone and liver, and secretes catecholamines.

### 44. A complete unilateral facial hemiparesis is most likely caused by:
- A. An intracranial tumour
- B. Birth injury
- C. Phenothiazine toxicity
- D. Myasthenia gravis
- E. Cerebellar atrophy

**Answer: B.** Forceps or pressure injury to the facial nerve at birth is the usual cause of complete one-sided facial weakness in an infant.

### 45. Polyhydramnios is often associated with which foetal abnormality?
- A. Renal agenesis
- B. Tracheo-oesophageal fistula
- C. Hydrocephalus
- D. Coarctation of the aorta
- E. Cleft lip and palate

**Answer: B.** A foetus with oesophageal atresia cannot swallow amniotic fluid. Renal agenesis causes too little fluid (oligohydramnios).

### 46. Conditions associated with an increased risk of malignancy in childhood include:
- A. Aniridia
- B. Ataxia telangiectasia
- C. Fanconi anaemia
- D. Asthma
- E. Loeffler syndrome

**Answer: C.** Fanconi anaemia raises the risk of leukaemia and tumours. Ataxia telangiectasia and aniridia (Wilms tumour) also do, so the question has several defensible options.

### 47. Antidiuretic hormone (ADH):
- A. Is stored in the anterior pituitary gland
- B. Release is stimulated by hypernatraemia
- C. Release is inhibited by hypotension
- D. Release is inhibited by mannitol
- E. Leads to water reabsorption by increasing water permeability of the ascending loop of Henle

**Answer: B.** A rising plasma osmolality triggers ADH. It is stored in the posterior pituitary, hypotension stimulates its release, and it acts on the collecting duct.

### 48. A 2-year-old has a palpable abdominal mass. The following renal pathologies should be considered EXCEPT:
- A. Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease
- B. Obstructive hydronephrosis
- C. Wilms tumour
- D. Nephroblastoma
- E. Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease

**Answer: E.** Adult-type polycystic disease rarely produces a mass at this age. Wilms tumour and nephroblastoma are the same condition. The question is loosely worded.

### 49. Which is associated with posterior urethral valve syndrome?
- A. Polyhydramnios
- B. Bilateral hydronephrosis on antenatal ultrasound
- C. Hypospadias
- D. Undescended testis
- E. Adult polycystic disease

**Answer: B.** Obstruction at the bladder outlet causes bilateral hydronephrosis and low amniotic fluid (oligohydramnios).

### 50. In Hirschsprung disease the following are correct EXCEPT:
- A. Abdominal distension in the newborn period may be the presenting feature
- B. The diagnosis can be made by rectal examination
- C. The affected segment is grossly dilated on barium enema
- D. The male to female ratio is 4:1
- E. There is an increased familial incidence in long-segment disease

**Answer: C.** The aganglionic segment is narrow, and the bowel above it is dilated.

### 51. In the diarrhoea of cholera toxin, there is activation of which enzyme system?
- A. Guanylate cyclase
- B. ATP
- C. Adenylate cyclase
- D. Sodium-glucose co-transporter
- E. Na+/K+ ATPase pump

**Answer: C.** Cholera toxin raises cyclic AMP, and chloride and water pour into the gut.

### 52. Which statement is characteristic of acute hepatitis B infection?
- A. Most patients present with splenomegaly
- B. It confers immunity to hepatitis A
- C. It commonly presents with distal joint arthritis
- D. There is increased infectivity in the presence of the e antigen
- E. Pruritus is an important early symptom

**Answer: D.** HBeAg marks high viral replication and high infectivity.

### 53. Which is true regarding Giardia lamblia?
- A. It can cause intestinal malabsorption
- B. It often causes bloody diarrhoea
- C. It is a cause of haemolytic uraemic syndrome
- D. It can be excluded by a single stool microscopy
- E. It is treated with co-trimoxazole

**Answer: A.** Giardia causes fatty, offensive stools and malabsorption. It is not bloody, one stool can miss it, and metronidazole treats it.

### 54. Which would NOT be expected in an 18-month-old with poorly controlled hypothyroidism?
- A. High plasma TSH
- B. Delayed bone age
- C. Umbilical hernia
- D. Diarrhoea
- E. Delayed developmental milestones

**Answer: D.** Hypothyroidism causes constipation, not diarrhoea.

### 55. Which statement about puberty is correct?
- A. In girls the first sign is the appearance of pubic hair
- B. In boys the first sign is enlargement of the testes
- C. Precocious puberty is more common in boys
- D. Once the epiphyses fuse there is no further growth in stature
- E. In girls menarche coincides with the peak of the growth spurt

**Answer: B.** Girls start with breast buds, and precocious puberty is commoner in girls. Option D is also true, but B is the clearer fact. Menarche comes after the growth peak.

### 56. A lesion of the frontal lobe causes:
- A. Apraxia
- B. Broca's (expressive) aphasia
- C. Cortical blindness
- D. Homonymous hemianopia
- E. Visuospatial neglect

**Answer: B.** This is the same question as Q28.

### 57. Two weeks after a viral illness, a 9-year-old girl has a drooping left mouth and cannot close her left eye. Her smile is asymmetric, and the rest of the examination is normal. She likely has:
- A. Guillain-Barre syndrome
- B. Botulism
- C. Cerebrovascular accident
- D. Brainstem tumour
- E. Bell palsy

**Answer: E.** A lower motor neurone facial palsy (the forehead is also weak, so the eye will not close) after a viral illness is Bell palsy.

### 58. A 5-month-old has a generalised tonic-clonic seizure of about 30 minutes that stops with diazepam. The most helpful information to gather from the mother is:
- A. Whether the child has had congestion without fever for 3 days
- B. Whether the child is developmentally normal, as are his siblings
- C. Whether the mother has been diluting the infant's formula to make it last longer
- D. The number of pets at home
- E. Whether the mother works as a secretary in an energy trading firm

**Answer: C.** Over-diluted formula causes water intoxication and low sodium, a classic cause of seizures at this age.

### 59. The current recommendation for breastfeeding is that:
- A. Exclusive breastfeeding should continue to 6 months, followed by supplementation with additional foods
- B. Exclusive breastfeeding should continue to 4 months, followed by supplementation
- C. Colostrum is the best food for a newborn but is best avoided in the first 2 days
- D. The baby should be allowed to breastfeed till one year
- E. Breastfeeding is contraindicated in all HIV-infected mothers

**Answer: A.** Exclusive for 6 months, then complementary foods with breastfeeding continued to 2 years or beyond.

### 60. The following are part of standard management of congestive cardiac failure EXCEPT:
- A. Oral digoxin
- B. Oral ACE inhibitors
- C. Beta blockers
- D. Fluid restriction
- E. No added salt diet in older children

**Answer: C.** Diuretics, digoxin, ACE inhibitors, fluid and salt restriction are the usual acute measures. Beta blockers are used only in selected stable cases.

### 61. Regarding the treatment of pneumonia, which statement is true?
- A. Oral amoxicillin 40 mg/kg 12-hourly is an effective treatment
- B. Cavitation on chest X-ray is mainly due to Staphylococcus epidermidis
- C. IV chloramphenicol 50 mg/kg 6-hourly is an alternative treatment
- D. Oxygen via a nasopharyngeal catheter is preferable
- E. Unhumidified oxygen therapy with prongs may worsen the clinical picture

**Answer: A.** High-dose oral amoxicillin is effective for non-severe pneumonia. Cavitation is typically due to Staphylococcus aureus (or TB).

### 62. Dave, 5 months, has recurrent vomiting after feeds and has been treated twice for pneumonia, with the radiograph always showing upper lobe involvement. The following are true EXCEPT:
- A. Surgical management has a role
- B. Pulmonary tuberculosis should be considered
- C. Positioning in the sitting position and diet modification suffice in most cases
- D. Severity of the symptoms rises to a peak at 1 year before declining
- E. He has a higher than average chance of having a congenital anomaly of the diaphragm

**Answer: D.** Reflux symptoms peak at about 4 months and settle by 12 to 18 months, so a peak at 1 year is wrong.

### 63. The following suggest child abuse EXCEPT:
- A. The child is promptly rushed to hospital even for minor injuries
- B. A history that does not tally with the severity of the injury
- C. Multiple fractures in different parts of the body
- D. Injuries in a child with severe mental handicap
- E. Isolated fractures in a 2-month-old baby

**Answer: A.** Prompt presentation for even a minor injury is the normal response of a caring parent. Delay in seeking care is the warning sign.

### 64. The KEPI yellow fever vaccine is administered at:
- A. 6 months
- B. 7 months
- C. 8 months
- D. 9 months
- E. 10 months

**Answer: D.** In the Kenyan schedule yellow fever is given at 9 months with measles-rubella in the targeted areas.

### 65. A 3-year-old has swallowed an organophosphate insecticide. Which is true?
- A. Gastric lavage is contraindicated
- B. He is likely to have urinary retention
- C. He is likely to have dilated pupils
- D. Pralidoxime is an effective antidote
- E. Atropine is not useful

**Answer: D.** Pralidoxime reactivates cholinesterase. Atropine is also used. The child has small pupils (miosis), incontinence and excess secretions.

### 66. An 11-month-old cannot stand unsupported, has no teeth and has been admitted several times with pneumonia. Which is true about the condition?
- A. Premature birth is associated
- B. It is due to vitamin C deficiency
- C. Serum phosphate is elevated
- D. Blood gas analysis may be necessary as a useful diagnostic test
- E. The serum alkaline phosphatase is usually below 200 IU/dL

**Answer: A.** This is rickets, which is commoner in preterm babies. It is due to vitamin D deficiency, phosphate is low and alkaline phosphatase is high.

### 67. An example of passive immunisation is:
- A. Colostrum transfer of antibodies
- B. That following a natural infection
- C. That resulting from vaccine-derived polio
- D. That induced by a live attenuated vaccine
- E. That following a conjugate vaccine

**Answer: A.** Passive immunity is borrowed antibody (from the mother or an injection). The rest are active.

### 68. In children with Down syndrome:
- A. The infant is usually hypertonic
- B. Asthma is more common
- C. Most mothers are over 35 years of age
- D. The incidence of atrioventricular septal defects is 90%
- E. Brachycephaly is characteristic

**Answer: E.** The head is short and broad. Babies are hypotonic, most are born to mothers under 35 (because younger mothers have more babies overall), and heart defects occur in about 40 to 50%.

### 69. Oral rehydration therapy:
- A. Works by active absorption of glucose
- B. Is effective in 50% of moderately dehydrated infants
- C. Should not be used in hypernatraemic dehydration
- D. Contains 5 mmol/L of potassium
- E. Can be made up safely at home using salt and sugar

**Answer: E.** A home-made salt and sugar solution is safe if measured carefully. Option A is loosely true (sodium-glucose co-transport), but ORS works in most children with mild or moderate dehydration, is fine in hypernatraemia, and contains 20 mmol/L potassium.

### 70. The mother of a 3-month-old girl brings her because of episodes of crying at the same time every day. You suspect colic. Which statement is true?
- A. It starts at 3 months and peaks at about 6 months
- B. Almost all cases are due to lactose intolerance
- C. The episodes typically occur in the late afternoon or evening
- D. Hyoscine should generally be prescribed
- E. Patients with any degree of abdominal distension should have a CT scan

**Answer: C.** Colic starts at about 2 to 3 weeks, peaks at 6 weeks and is gone by 3 to 4 months, and has no single cause. Episodes are in the late afternoon or evening.

### 71. A previously well 7-month-old has had a 2-day history of fever, cough, difficulty breathing and wheezing. Which statement is true?
- A. A trial of bronchodilators should not be given at casualty
- B. A chest X-ray is likely to show segmental consolidation
- C. Influenza virus is the most likely causative agent
- D. The first-line treatment is a slow intravenous infusion of aminophylline
- E. Corticosteroids are not indicated in the management of this condition

**Answer: E.** This is bronchiolitis, mostly from RSV. The X-ray shows hyperinflation, and steroids are not helpful.

### 72. A 2-year-old has a 2-day history of cough, runny nose and a fever of 38 degrees, with a respiratory rate of 36, an inflamed throat and clear lung fields. Which is true?

- B. A full blood count is mandatory for diagnosis
- C. Sputum culture is positive in more than 70% of cases
- D. The commonest causative organism is Mycoplasma pneumoniae
- E. The major cause of this condition is viruses

**Answer: E.** This is an upper respiratory tract infection, which is mostly viral and needs no antibiotics.

### 73. Which drug or toxin is NOT matched with its antidote?
- A. Morphine and naloxone
- B. Warfarin and protamine
- C. Iron and deferoxamine
- D. Carbon monoxide and oxygen
- E. Carbamate and atropine

**Answer: B.** Warfarin is reversed with vitamin K (and plasma if bleeding). Protamine reverses heparin.

### 74. A baby born weighing 3.0 kg has a head circumference of 45 cm at birth. Choose the correct response.
- A. This finding is a normal variation
- B. A cyst in the fourth ventricle is likely to be a cause
- C. Furosemide is useful in management
- D. Folic acid deficiency in the mother is a contributing factor
- E. Spontaneous cure usually occurs

**Answer: B.** A head of 45 cm is macrocephaly (normal is about 34 to 35 cm). A Dandy-Walker cyst of the fourth ventricle causes obstructive hydrocephalus, and treatment is a shunt.

### 75. A 16-year-old girl has a late apical systolic murmur preceded by a click. Her mother also has a murmur. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Atrial septal defect
- B. Aortic stenosis
- C. Tricuspid regurgitation
- D. Mitral valve prolapse
- E. Ventricular septal defect

**Answer: D.** A mid-systolic click with a late systolic murmur is mitral valve prolapse, which runs in families.

### 76. Regarding pneumococcal conjugate vaccine, choose the correct statement.

- B. It is routinely given at 6 months of life
- C. It helps protect against pneumonia and meningitis
- D. It is usually administered orally
- E. Four doses are routinely given

**Answer: C.** It is an injection, introduced in Kenya in 2011, and given in three doses at 6, 10 and 14 weeks.

### 77. Choose the correct statement regarding measles.
- A. The commonest complication is laryngotracheobronchitis
- B. Immunity after the vaccine lasts 3 years
- C. The disease may precipitate acute malnutrition
- D. The rash is usually vesicular starting on the trunk
- E. Corneal lesions are unlikely in this disease

**Answer: C.** Measles causes appetite loss, diarrhoea and protein loss, which can tip a child into severe malnutrition. The commonest complications are pneumonia and diarrhoea. The rash is maculopapular, starting behind the ears and face, and corneal ulceration and blindness do occur.

### 78. An 18-month-old has recurrent cough. Which would make recurrent aspiration more likely?
- A. Cerebral palsy
- B. History of coughing not associated with feeds
- C. Presence of dextrocardia
- D. Presence of failure to thrive
- E. Coughing spasms ending in vomiting

**Answer: A.** Poor swallowing and reflux in a child with cerebral palsy is the classic setting for recurrent aspiration. Dextrocardia suggests ciliary dyskinesia, and coughing spasms ending in vomiting suggest pertussis.

### 79. A normal infant sits briefly leaning forward on her hands, reaches for and grasps a cube, and transfers it from hand to hand. She babbles but cannot wave bye-bye nor grasp with finger and thumb. Her age is likely to be:
- A. 4 months
- B. 7 months
- C. 10 months
- D. 14 months
- E. 18 months

**Answer: B.** Tripod sitting, transfer between hands and babbling fit about 6 to 7 months. A pincer grasp and waving come at about 9 to 10 months.

### 80. A 17-year-old girl has eaten a strict vegetarian diet for 5 years. You order a complete blood count because you are concerned about:
- A. Lymphocytosis
- B. Macrocytic anaemia
- C. Microcytic anaemia
- D. Normocytic anaemia
- E. Thrombocytosis

**Answer: C.** A diet low in absorbable iron leads to iron-deficiency (microcytic) anaemia. Macrocytosis occurs with a strict vegan diet lacking B12.

### 81. A 4-month-old with birth weight 3.5 kg now weighs 3.7 kg and has visible wasting. Which is an UNLIKELY immediate life-threatening problem?

**Answer: D.** This is the same question as Q39. Hypokalaemia, not hyperkalaemia, is the problem.

### 82. A mother says her 5-month-old, who weighs 6 kg and weighed 3 kg at birth, is "getting fat". What do you explain?

- B. The baby is prone to obesity
- C. The baby will have an eating disorder

**Answer: A.** Birth weight doubles by 4 to 5 months, so this is normal growth.

### 83. Regarding a patient with rheumatic chorea:
- A. The purposeless movements are evident in sleep
- B. It follows kernicterus
- C. Haloperidol is useful in treatment
- D. Boys are three times more affected than girls
- E. Hysteria is a common manifestation

**Answer: C.** Sydenham chorea can be controlled with haloperidol, sodium valproate or carbamazepine. The movements stop during sleep, girls are affected more, and it follows streptococcal infection.

### 84. In a patient with infective endocarditis:
- A. Intravenous antibiotics are given for 14 days
- B. Microscopic haematuria is a common finding
- C. A blood culture is usually an unnecessary investigation
- D. Streptococcus pyogenes is the causative organism
- E. Fever usually occurs in late presentation

**Answer: B.** Emboli and immune complexes cause microscopic haematuria. Treatment lasts 4 to 6 weeks, blood cultures are essential, and the usual organisms are viridans streptococci and staphylococci.

### 85. Clubbing of fingers and toes is a presenting feature in:
- A. Severe pneumonia
- B. Patent ductus arteriosus
- C. Subacute bacterial endocarditis
- D. Coarctation of the aorta
- E. Atrial fibrillation

**Answer: C.** Clubbing occurs with subacute endocarditis, cyanotic heart disease, bronchiectasis and cystic fibrosis. The other conditions do not cause it.

### 86. Job, 10 years, has had a sickle cell crisis for the second time since being diagnosed at 2 years. The following are true:
- A. Hydroxyurea should be commenced immediately
- B. His peripheral blood film will show a hypochromic microcytic picture
- C. Haemoglobin electrophoresis should be done annually
- D. Good adherence to vaccinations and medicines may have reduced the incidence of crises
- E. Permanent sequelae in other patients with sickle cell and frequent crises are rare

**Answer: D.** Vaccines, penicillin prophylaxis, folic acid and malaria prophylaxis reduce crises. Hydroxyurea is for frequent severe crises, the film shows sickle cells and target cells, and the diagnosis needs electrophoresis only once.

### 87. Which commonly occurs in congenital rubella syndrome?
- A. Lung hypoplasia
- B. Choanal atresia
- C. Posterior urethral valves
- D. Lissencephaly
- E. Patent ductus arteriosus

**Answer: E.** The triad is cataracts, deafness and heart disease (patent ductus arteriosus or pulmonary artery stenosis).

### 88. A cyanotic newborn is suspected of congenital heart disease. The ECG shows left axis deviation and left ventricular hypertrophy. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Transposition of the great arteries
- B. Truncus arteriosus
- C. Tricuspid atresia
- D. Tetralogy of Fallot
- E. Persistent foetal circulation

**Answer: C.** Cyanosis with left axis deviation and left ventricular hypertrophy is the signature of tricuspid atresia. Most other cyanotic lesions show right ventricular hypertrophy.

### 89. A 4-year-old boy with asthma presents with respiratory distress and wheeze. Which statement is true?
- A. Intravenous dexamethasone is the most potent in relieving bronchospasm
- B. A chest X-ray should be done to evaluate severity and rule out pneumonia

- D. This state may be triggered by strenuous exercise

**Answer: D.** Exercise is a known asthma trigger. The other statements are wrong: inhaled salbutamol (by spacer or nebuliser) works fast and is the first treatment, steroids take hours, and a chest X-ray is not routine. The question is weak.

### 90. A 12-year-old has had popping eyes, muscle wasting and heat intolerance. Positive examination findings would include:

### 91. A 2-year-old with diarrhoea and vomiting is severely wasted, weighs 6 kg, and has warm extremities. The fluid used to replenish his body fluids should contain:
- A. High levels of sodium
- B. No sugar
- C. Lower levels of potassium
- D. Deionised water
- E. More potassium

**Answer: E.** In severe malnutrition rehydration uses ReSoMal, which has less sodium and more potassium than standard ORS, because these children have low body potassium and excess sodium.

### 92. The following public health measures would reduce the incidence of iron-deficiency anaemia:
- A. Using cow's milk from 6 months of age
- B. Giving young children tea rather than fruit juice
- C. Delaying the introduction of mixed feeding until 9 months of age
- D. Giving elemental iron to all preterm babies

**Answer: D.** Preterm babies have low iron stores and need iron supplements (about 2 to 4 mg/kg/day) from 4 to 6 weeks. Cow's milk and tea reduce iron absorption, and late weaning delays iron-rich foods.
