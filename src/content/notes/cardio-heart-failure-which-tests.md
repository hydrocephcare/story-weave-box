# Heart failure: which tests, and why — Year 4 Internal Medicine (Cardiovascular) Notes

*Clinical reasoning notes. The point of an investigation is the question it answers.*

**The reasoning chain:** is it really heart failure → what type (reduced or preserved ejection fraction) → what caused it → what tipped the patient into this episode → what is it doing to other organs.

## 1. Which tests?

| Test | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **NT-proBNP or BNP** | Is this heart failure? **A normal level makes it unlikely** |
| **Echocardiogram** | Confirms the diagnosis; **ejection fraction** (reduced or preserved); the **valves** (rheumatic heart disease); the cause |
| **ECG** | Ischaemia, **arrhythmia (atrial fibrillation)**, left ventricular hypertrophy |
| **Chest X-ray** | **Pulmonary oedema**, cardiomegaly, pleural effusions |
| **Full haemogram** | **Anaemia**, which worsens or can cause heart failure |
| **Urea, creatinine, electrolytes** | Kidney function before starting diuretics or ACE inhibitors; potassium |
| **Liver tests, thyroid tests, glucose, lipids** | Congestive liver disease; a thyroid cause; risk factors |
| **Troponin** | Is an acute coronary syndrome the trigger? |
| **HIV test** | **HIV cardiomyopathy** |

## 2. Then ask the two questions that matter

**What caused it?** Hypertension, **rheumatic heart disease**, ischaemic heart disease, cardiomyopathy (including **HIV**, peripartum, alcohol, and dilated cardiomyopathy of unknown cause), anaemia, thyroid disease, pericardial disease.

**What precipitated this episode?** Infection, **missed drugs**, **arrhythmia (new atrial fibrillation)**, a **heavy salt load**, NSAIDs, anaemia, a new ischaemic event.

## 3. Features to recognise

- **Left heart failure:** breathlessness, orthopnoea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea, basal crackles, third heart sound.
- **Right heart failure:** raised JVP, ankle swelling, tender enlarged liver, ascites.
- Look for the **cause on examination**: a murmur, an irregular pulse, goitre, pallor.

## 4. Management in outline

1. **Treat the congestion:** loop diuretics, oxygen if hypoxic, sit the patient up.
2. **Treat the cause and the trigger:** control blood pressure, rate or rhythm, treat infection, correct anaemia, repair or replace a diseased valve.
3. **Long-term therapy for reduced ejection fraction** (each class reduces deaths or admissions): **ACE inhibitor or ARB**, **beta-blocker**, **mineralocorticoid antagonist**, and an **SGLT2 inhibitor** where available.
4. **Avoid** NSAIDs, and be careful with fluids and with some calcium channel blockers.
5. **Teach** daily weights, salt and fluid advice, and adherence. Follow up in a heart failure clinic.

## 5. Where tests can mislead

- **BNP is raised in kidney failure, atrial fibrillation, older age and pulmonary embolism**, and lower in obesity. Interpret it with the picture.
- A normal chest X-ray does not exclude heart failure.
- Check the **creatinine and potassium again 1 to 2 weeks** after starting an ACE inhibitor or a mineralocorticoid antagonist.

## Practice questions

**1.** The investigation that best separates reduced from preserved ejection fraction is:
A. Chest X-ray · B. ECG · **C. Echocardiogram** · D. BNP · E. Troponin
*Answer: C.*

**2.** Which investigation gives a clue that a patient with new heart failure may have a treatable cause such as HIV?
A. Troponin · B. Lipid profile · C. Urinalysis · D. Peak flow · **E. HIV test**
*Answer: E.*

**3.** Which drug should be avoided in heart failure because it causes fluid retention?
A. Furosemide · **B. Ibuprofen** · C. Bisoprolol · D. Enalapril · E. Spironolactone
*Answer: B.*
