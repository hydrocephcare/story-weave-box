## Section A: Short answer questions

### Question 1. Ovarian cysts

**(a) What are ovarian cysts? (2 marks)**
Fluid-filled sacs in or on the ovary. They may be physiological (functional) or pathological, and range from simple cysts to complex neoplastic masses.

**(b) The 3 main broad classifications (3 marks)**
1. **Functional (physiological) cysts**
2. **Benign neoplastic cysts**
3. **Malignant cysts and tumours**

**(c) One type of each (3 marks)**
- Functional: **follicular cyst** (or corpus luteum cyst).
- Benign: **serous cystadenoma** (or mature cystic teratoma, endometrioma).
- Malignant: **serous cystadenocarcinoma** (or dysgerminoma).

**(d) Ultrasound: 2 benign and 3 malignant features (5 marks)**
- **Benign:** unilocular, thin wall, smooth regular margins, anechoic content, no solid area, no ascites, unilateral, and little or peripheral blood flow.
- **Malignant:** solid components, thick irregular septa, papillary projections, **ascites,** bilateral masses, and central blood flow on Doppler.

### Question 2. Abnormal menstruation

**(a)** Bleeding that departs from the normal pattern in frequency, regularity, duration or volume (normal: cycle 24 to 38 days, flow up to 8 days, loss under 80 ml).

**(b) Two examples**
1. **Menorrhagia:** regular cycles with heavy bleeding over 80 ml or over 7 days.
2. **Polymenorrhoea:** cycles shorter than 21 days. (Oligomenorrhoea, over 35 days, is another example.)

### Question 3. Congenital abnormality

**(a) Define a congenital abnormality (2 marks)**
A structural or functional defect present at birth.

**(b) What is a teratogen? (2 marks)**
An agent that disturbs normal embryonic or fetal development and causes a malformation, for example thalidomide, alcohol or rubella.

**(c) What is a mutagen? (2 marks)**
An agent that changes the genetic material (DNA or chromosomes) and causes mutations, for example ionising radiation or certain chemicals.

**(d) Describe Klinefelter syndrome (3 marks)**
**47,XXY** karyotype from non-disjunction. Features: tall stature with long legs, small firm testes, gynaecomastia, sparse body hair, **infertility (azoospermia),** and learning difficulties. Hormones: raised FSH and LH with low testosterone. Management: testosterone replacement and fertility counselling.

### Question 4. Pelvic inflammatory disease

**(a) What is PID? (2 marks)**
Infection and inflammation of the upper genital tract, involving the uterus (endometritis), fallopian tubes (salpingitis), ovaries and pelvic peritoneum, usually from ascending infection.

**(b) Known causes (4 marks)**
1. **Chlamydia trachomatis**
2. **Neisseria gonorrhoeae**
3. **Anaerobes and organisms of bacterial vaginosis** (Gardnerella, Bacteroides)
4. Mycoplasma genitalium, and Gram-negative organisms and streptococci, often after instrumentation, childbirth, abortion or IUCD insertion.

**(c) Treatment options for a pelvic abscess (6 marks)**
1. **Admit** and give IV fluids and analgesia.
2. **Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics** covering gonococcus, chlamydia and anaerobes (for example ceftriaxone plus doxycycline plus metronidazole, or clindamycin plus gentamicin).
3. **Drainage:** ultrasound-guided percutaneous drainage, or drainage through the vagina (colpotomy) for a pouch of Douglas abscess.
4. **Surgery:** laparoscopy or laparotomy for rupture, failure of antibiotics after 48 to 72 hours, or a large abscess.
5. **Treat partners** and screen for HIV and other STIs.
6. Follow up, with counselling about infertility and ectopic risk.

## Section B: Multiple choice questions

### 1. While treating gonorrhoea, treatment must also be given for:
- A. Bacterial vaginosis
- B. Chlamydia
- C. Herpes
- D. Syphilis

**Answer: B.** Chlamydia often co-exists with gonorrhoea.

### 2. Which will not present with acute pelvic pain?
- A. Candida albicans infection
- B. Tubo-ovarian abscess
- C. Mittelschmerz
- D. Septic abortion

**Answer: A.** Candidiasis causes itching and discharge.

### 3. Which is unlikely to be a feature of ovarian torsion?
- A. Presents usually after sustained immobility
- B. Usually ipsilateral
- C. Can be reduced by oral contraceptives
- D. Can be caused by previous abdominal surgery

**Answer: A.** Torsion presents with sudden severe unilateral pain, usually after sudden movement or exertion, in a woman with a mobile ovarian mass. Prolonged immobility is not a feature.

### 4. You will suspect pelvic inflammatory disease in all EXCEPT:
- A. Early menarche
- B. Previous abortion
- C. Previous contraceptive use
- D. Previous ectopic pregnancy

**Answer: A.** The risk factor is early coitarche (first intercourse), not early menarche.

### 5. During the menstrual cycle, which is true?
- A. The proliferative phase is relatively constant
- B. Ovulation coincides with menstruation
- C. Anovulatory cycles are characteristically painless
- D. Menstrual blood loss is usually 150 ml

**Answer: C.**

### 6. Milky white discharge with a fishy odour and no itching:
- A. Bacterial vaginosis
- B. Candida albicans
- C. Trichomonas
- D. Malignancy

**Answer: A.**

### 7. Amenorrhoea after treatment for incomplete miscarriage:
- A. Post-abortion endometritis
- B. Pelvic inflammatory disease
- C. Asherman syndrome
- D. Kallmann syndrome

**Answer: C.**

### 8. The main cause of abnormal uterine bleeding in a 13-year-old:
- A. Trauma
- B. Ectopic pregnancy
- C. Anovulation
- D. Polycystic ovarian disease

**Answer: C.**

### 9. Emergency contraceptive:
- A. Low-dose combined contraceptive
- B. Levonorgestrel
- C. Ergometrine
- D. Depo-Provera

**Answer: B.**

### 10. The following apply to a pelvic abscess EXCEPT:
- A. Can be caused by Neisseria gonorrhoeae
- B. Can cause multiple organ failure
- C. Can cause chronic pelvic pain
- D. Can cause amenorrhoea

**Answer: D.** A pelvic abscess causes pain, fever, sepsis, infertility and chronic pelvic pain, but not amenorrhoea.
