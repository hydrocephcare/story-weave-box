# Acute rheumatic fever and rheumatic heart disease — Year 4 Internal Medicine (Cardiovascular) Notes

*Clinical reasoning notes. Rheumatic heart disease is the commonest cause of valve disease in young people across much of Africa and Asia.*

**The reasoning chain:** was there a recent group A streptococcal infection → do the Jones criteria fit → how badly is the heart involved → treat the attack, then prevent the next one → follow the valves for life.

## 1. What it is

**Acute rheumatic fever (ARF)** is an autoimmune reaction, 2 to 4 weeks after a **group A streptococcal throat infection** (and in some settings skin infection), mostly in children 5 to 15 years old. Antibodies against the streptococcus cross-react with heart, joint, brain and skin tissue. **Repeated attacks scar the heart valves** and cause **rheumatic heart disease (RHD)**, most often **mitral stenosis**, then mitral regurgitation and aortic valve disease.

## 2. Jones criteria (2015) for the first attack

**Evidence of a preceding group A streptococcal infection** (raised **ASO or anti-DNase B**, positive throat culture or rapid antigen test, or recent scarlet fever) **plus**:

- **2 major criteria**, or
- **1 major and 2 minor criteria**.

| Major criteria | Minor criteria |
|---|---|
| **Carditis** (including silent carditis seen on echocardiography) | **Fever** |
| **Arthritis** (a migratory polyarthritis of large joints) | **Arthralgia** (joint pain without swelling) |
| **Sydenham chorea** (jerky, purposeless movements) | **Raised ESR or CRP** |
| **Erythema marginatum** (pink rings on the trunk, not the face) | **Prolonged PR interval** on ECG |
| **Subcutaneous nodules** (painless, over bony prominences) | |

In **high-risk populations** the thresholds for fever, joint involvement and inflammatory markers are lower, and **subclinical carditis on echocardiography counts** as a major criterion.

## 3. Investigate with purpose

| Test | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **ASO titre, anti-DNase B** | Was there a recent streptococcal infection? |
| **ESR and CRP** | Is there systemic inflammation (a minor criterion)? |
| **ECG** | Is the PR interval prolonged? Any heart block? |
| **Echocardiography** | Is there valve disease or silent carditis? How severe is it? |
| **Blood cultures** | Is this endocarditis instead? |
| **Throat swab** | Is the organism still there? |

## 4. Treatment

1. **Rest** while there is carditis, and **treat heart failure** if present.
2. **Eradicate the streptococcus** with a course of **penicillin** (a macrolide if penicillin-allergic).
3. **Anti-inflammatory therapy:** aspirin or another anti-inflammatory for arthritis; **corticosteroids** for severe carditis.
4. **Chorea:** reassurance and, if severe, a drug such as valproate or carbamazepine.

## 5. Secondary prevention: the part that saves hearts

**Long-term penicillin prophylaxis** (a monthly injection of benzathine penicillin, or oral penicillin) prevents recurrences and further valve damage. The duration depends on carditis and valve disease: at least 5 years or until age 21 for those without carditis, longer if the heart is involved, and often for life in severe disease.

**Primary prevention:** treat streptococcal sore throats properly.

## 6. Complications

Heart failure, **mitral stenosis** with atrial fibrillation and **stroke**, infective endocarditis, and in pregnancy a risk of decompensation. Severe valve disease needs **balloon valvotomy or valve surgery**.

## 7. Where tests can mislead

- A **normal ASO does not exclude ARF** (it may be low late in the illness or after starting antibiotics): repeat it, and check anti-DNase B.
- **Arthritis improves within hours of aspirin**, which can hide the diagnosis if given before you have seen the joints.
- Silent carditis can only be found by echocardiography.

## Practice questions

**1.** A 9-year-old has a migrating polyarthritis, fever and a raised ASO two weeks after a sore throat. The diagnosis needs a second major criterion. Which is one?
A. Raised ESR · **B. Carditis** · C. Arthralgia · D. Fever · E. Prolonged PR interval
*Answer: B. ESR, arthralgia, fever and a long PR interval are minor criteria.*

**2.** The valve most often damaged in rheumatic heart disease is:
A. Tricuspid · B. Pulmonary · C. Aortic · D. Eustachian · **E. Mitral**
*Answer: E.*

**3.** The aim of long-term penicillin prophylaxis after rheumatic fever is to:
A. Treat chorea · B. Reduce fever · C. Treat arthritis · **D. Prevent recurrent attacks and further valve damage** · E. Cure existing valve disease
*Answer: D.*
