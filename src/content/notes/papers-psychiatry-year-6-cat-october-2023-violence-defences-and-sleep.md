## Multiple choice questions

### 1. All of the following factors are significantly associated with increased risk of violence among those with schizophrenia EXCEPT:
- A. Comorbid substance use
- B. Comorbid personality disorder
- C. Acute psychotic symptoms
- D. Non-compliance with treatment
- E. Comorbid depression

**Answer: E.** Substance misuse, personality disorder, active psychosis and not taking treatment raise the risk. Depression does not.

### 2. A 35-year-old lesbian was referred to the outpatient clinic with a 4-month history of low mood, particularly in the morning, difficulty sleeping, poor appetite, weight loss and poor concentration. Which statement about her psychiatric history and prognosis is correct compared with a heterosexual female?
- A. A higher risk of suicide
- B. Less likely to be psychologically distressed
- C. Less likely to have alcohol problems
- D. More likely to have self-harmed
- E. Poorer response to antidepressant

**Answer: D.** Lesbian and bisexual women have higher rates of depression, alcohol and drug misuse and self-harm than heterosexual women, from the stress of stigma. Rates of attempted suicide are higher too, and so A may also be argued, but self-harm is the best-documented finding. The question is loosely worded.

### 3. The concept of symptoms assessed by descriptive psychopathology has both form and content as its components. Which of the following is true?
- A. Form provides sufficient information for management
- B. Form provides sufficient information for severity
- C. Form is more important than content
- D. Form provides sufficient information for diagnosis
- E. Content is more important than form

**Answer: D.** The form of a symptom points to the diagnosis. The content reflects the person's culture and life.

### 4. A 35-year-old unmarried lesbian was unaware of her hatred towards her mother. However, she often felt inexplicably resentful towards older women in positions of authority. She could not understand why she had felt like this for several years. Which of the following can explain this situation?
- A. Altruism
- B. Displacement
- C. Idealisation
- D. Projection
- E. Projective identification

**Answer: B.** In displacement, feelings towards one person (the mother) are shifted on to a safer substitute (older women in authority).

### 5. A 76-year-old man with a history of falls was brought to the clinic because of recent deterioration in his self-care. He appeared malnourished, confused and somewhat unkempt. There was a significant but mild cognitive impairment, and incontinence. What is the most likely diagnosis?
- A. Alzheimer's disease
- B. Chronic subdural haematoma
- C. Multi-infarct dementia
- D. Normal pressure hydrocephalus
- E. Pick's disease

**Answer: B.** A recent fall followed by confusion and a rapid decline in an older man suggests a chronic subdural haematoma. Normal pressure hydrocephalus (gait disturbance, incontinence and dementia) is also possible, so the question has more than one defensible option. A CT scan decides.

### 6. Which of the following is NOT a common feature of schizophrenic auditory hallucinations?
- A. Being multiple
- B. Male voice
- C. Speaks in one's mother tongue
- D. Often continuously present
- E. Has a different accent

**Answer: D.** In schizophrenia the voices are usually several, commenting or talking about the patient, in the patient's own language. They come and go, and are not usually continuous.

### 7. Which of the following can cause visual hallucinations?
- A. Occipital lobe tumours
- B. Post-concussion states
- C. Hepatic failure
- D. Dementia
- E. All of the above

**Answer: E.** Visual hallucinations are organic until proved otherwise.

### 8. According to Freud's psychic apparatus, which of the following is concerned with mediating between basic desires and the judicial part of punishment?
- A. Collective unconscious
- B. Dreams
- C. Ego
- D. Id
- E. Super-ego

**Answer: C.** The ego balances the instinctive drives of the id (basic desires) against the moral demands of the superego (the judging, punishing part) and against reality.

### 9. Which of the following drugs on withdrawal produce disturbed proprioceptive perceptions?
- A. Cannabis
- B. Amphetamines
- C. LSD
- D. Benzodiazepines
- E. Nicotine

**Answer: D.** Benzodiazepine (and alcohol) withdrawal causes perceptual distortions, such as a sense that the floor is moving.

### 10. Which one of the following is NOT matched correctly?
- A. Form of thought: loosening of association
- B. Content of thought: persecutory belief
- C. Stream of thought: poverty of speech
- D. Form of thought: obsessions
- E. Form of thought: circumstantiality

**Answer: D.** Obsessions are intrusive, unwanted thoughts (a disorder of possession or content of thought), and not a disorder of its form.

### 11. Which one of the following is more common in manic rather than schizophrenic speech disturbance?
- A. Clanging
- B. Derailment
- C. Thought blocking
- D. Tangentiality
- E. Poverty of content of speech

**Answer: A.** Clanging (words linked by sound) is typical of mania.

### 12. A patient looks dishevelled, with unkempt hair and dirty, unwashed clothes. Self-neglect is commonly noted in all of the following EXCEPT:
- A. Alcoholism
- B. Chronic schizophrenia
- C. Depression
- D. Dementia
- E. Social phobia

**Answer: E.** Social phobia causes avoidance, not self-neglect.

### 13. A 57-year-old lady with many previous hospitalisations is brought by police to casualty. She is wearing full make-up, green lipstick, shiny green nail polish and green jewellery. She asks everyone at the admission unit to call her Ms Green. Which diagnosis is most consistent with this presentation?
- A. Mania
- B. Depression
- C. Panic attacks
- D. Obsessive compulsive disorder
- E. Learning disability

**Answer: A.** Flamboyant dress, a theme and over-familiarity point to mania.

### 14. Which of the following is the major difference between mood and affect in the mental state examination?
- A. Mood is short-lived while affect is longer lasting
- B. Mood is objective while affect is subjective
- C. Affect is transient but difficult to interpret
- D. Affect is transient and self-reported
- E. None of the above

**Answer: E.** Mood is the sustained, subjective emotional state. Affect is the observed, moment-to-moment expression. Options A, B and D reverse this.

### 15. On physical examination, you notice that a person with a history of substance misuse has piloerection, dilated pupils, rhinorrhoea and is yawning frequently. Withdrawal from which of the following substances can cause this presentation?
- A. Cocaine
- B. Opiate
- C. Cannabis
- D. Alcohol
- E. Amphetamines

**Answer: B.** Opioid withdrawal: yawning, goose flesh, a runny nose, dilated pupils, cramps and diarrhoea.

### 16. A 28-year-old school teacher is admitted for constipation and acute abdominal pain. She experiences visual and tactile hallucinations with intense anxiety. She develops motor weakness of her legs on administration of hypnotics and diclofenac. Which laboratory test is indicated?
- A. Serum lipid levels
- B. Serum folate
- C. Urine glucose
- D. Urine porphyrins
- E. Serum ceruloplasmin

**Answer: D.** Acute intermittent porphyria, precipitated by the drugs.

### 17. Which of the following is the most clinically useful method of diagnosing Alzheimer's disease?
- A. Clinical interview
- B. CT scans
- C. Functional MRI
- D. SPECT
- E. Lumbar puncture

**Answer: A.** The diagnosis is clinical. Scans exclude other causes.

### 18. The most powerful predictors of recurrence of depressive episodes among the following are:
- A. Previous episodes of depression and presence of residual symptoms
- B. Non-bipolar diagnosis and later age of onset
- C. Female gender and earlier age of onset
- D. Higher degree of life events and family history of affective disorders
- E. Male gender and past history of psychiatric admission

**Answer: A.** The number of previous episodes and residual symptoms are the strongest predictors.

### 19. The most powerful predictor of overall functional outcome in children with autism is given by:
- A. Family history of autism
- B. Autistic symptom count
- C. Presence of soft neurological signs
- D. IQ level
- E. Non-verbal skill

**Answer: D.** A higher IQ (together with early language) predicts a better outcome. The question is loosely worded, as language ability is also a strong predictor.

### 20. Which of the following statements about sleep is correct?
- A. As the day progresses, the circadian wake drive increases and homeostatic sleep decreases, until a tipping point is reached, and the ventrolateral preoptic sleep promoter is triggered to release GABA in the tuberomammillary nucleus, which increases wakefulness
- B. Homeostatic sleep drive increases with a longer period of sleep and decreases with a period in the awake state
- C. Sleep consists of multiple phases which recur in a cyclical manner known as the ultradian cycle
- D. The diminishing of the circadian wake drive is the result of input to the suprachiasmic nucleus
- E. There is a single process regulating sleep

**Answer: C.** Sleep cycles through NREM and REM stages about every 90 minutes (the ultradian cycle). In the two-process model, the sleep drive builds up with time awake and falls with sleep, and the VLPO releases GABA to inhibit the wake-promoting tuberomammillary nucleus (so it reduces wakefulness).
