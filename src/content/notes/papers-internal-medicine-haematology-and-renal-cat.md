## Section A: Multiple choice questions

### 1. Which statement on anaemia is INCORRECT?
- A. A patient with a reticulocyte count of 5% has haemolysis
- B. Hypoproliferative anaemia is characterised by a reticulocyte count of less than 2.5%
- C. Acute blood loss is associated with reticulocyte changes
- D. Sideroblastic anaemia is associated with microcytic anaemia
- E. Anaemia of chronic disease is associated with reduced erythropoietin responsiveness

**Answer: A.** A high reticulocyte count shows the marrow is responding, which happens in haemolysis but also after bleeding or treatment of deficiency. It does not prove haemolysis. The other statements are true.

### 2. Which statement is FALSE?
- A. In iron deficiency anaemia the soluble transferrin receptor is increased
- B. In anaemia of chronic disease the transferrin saturation is increased
- C. Transferrin levels may be reduced in anaemia of chronic illness
- D. Ferritin is reduced in iron deficiency anaemia
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** In anaemia of chronic disease iron is locked in stores by hepcidin, so serum iron and transferrin saturation are low or normal, not raised.

### 3. All are associated with megaloblastic anaemia EXCEPT:
- A. Haemolysis
- B. Anticonvulsants
- C. ARVs
- D. Primary bone marrow disorders
- E. Atrophic gastritis

**Answer: A.** Haemolysis causes a high MCV through reticulocytes, but it is megaloblastic only if folate runs out. Anticonvulsants (folate), zidovudine, myelodysplasia and atrophic gastritis (B12) all cause it.

### 4. Which statement is TRUE?
- A. Alcohol is associated with neutrophil hypersegmentation
- B. Liver disease is typically associated with megaloblastic macrocytosis
- C. A ferritin of 200 mcg/L is likely with iron deficiency anaemia
- D. In cobalamin deficiency homocysteine levels rise
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Without B12, homocysteine and methylmalonic acid both rise. Alcohol and liver disease cause non-megaloblastic macrocytosis. A ferritin of 200 argues against iron deficiency.

### 5. Findings consistent with haemolysis EXCEPT one:
- A. Raised serum haptoglobin
- B. Raised unconjugated bilirubin
- C. Microspherocytes
- D. Raised serum LDH
- E. Decreased urine urobilinogen

**Answer: A.** Haptoglobin is used up and falls in haemolysis. Option E is also wrong, because urine urobilinogen rises, so the question has two candidates. Haptoglobin is the classic one.

### 6. Which is the odd one out concerning haemochromatosis?
- A. May be associated with thyroid dysfunction
- B. It is an autosomal dominant disorder
- C. Association with HFE
- D. Low hepcidin is associated with severe iron overload
- E. Sepsis is a likely feature

**Answer: B.** Hereditary haemochromatosis is autosomal recessive (HFE C282Y). Low hepcidin drives the overload, and iron-loving bacteria such as Vibrio and Yersinia cause sepsis.

### 7. A 15-year-old girl has bilateral haemarthrosis of the elbows and a tender fluctuant right knee. She has bled for 24 hours from a venepuncture site. Most likely diagnosis?
- A. Classical haemophilia
- B. Christmas disease
- C. Von Willebrand's disease
- D. Disseminated intravascular coagulopathy
- E. Protein C deficiency

**Answer: C.** Haemophilia A and B are X-linked and almost all affected people are male. A girl with joint bleeds and prolonged venepuncture bleeding fits severe (type 3) von Willebrand disease.

### 8. A strict vegetarian has malaise, exertional dyspnoea and pallor. The film shows segmented neutrophils and MCV 110 fL. Which statement is TRUE?
- A. Gum hypertrophy occurs
- B. Serum ferritin levels are low
- C. Bone marrow Prussian blue stain is negative
- D. Paraesthesias are common
- E. The condition is invariably fatal

**Answer: D.** This is vitamin B12 deficiency with hypersegmented neutrophils. B12 deficiency damages the dorsal columns and peripheral nerves, causing paraesthesia. It is treatable.

### 9. Statements on haemostasis are true EXCEPT:
- A. Bleeding time assesses platelet function
- B. Prothrombin time evaluates the extrinsic pathway
- C. Partial thromboplastin time evaluates the extrinsic pathway
- D. Partial thromboplastin time evaluates the intrinsic pathway
- E. Thrombin time evaluates conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin

**Answer: C.** APTT tests the intrinsic and common pathways. PT tests the extrinsic pathway.

### 10. Identify the INCORRECT statement.
- A. Petechiae are more common in coagulation factor disorders than in platelet disorders
- B. Bleeding after small cuts is more common in platelet disorders than in coagulation disorders
- C. Haemarthrosis is extremely rare in platelet disorders
- D. Ecchymosis is associated with both platelet and coagulation factor disorders
- E. None of the above

**Answer: A.** Petechiae are typical of platelet disorders. Coagulation factor disorders cause deep bleeds: haemarthrosis and large haematomas.

### 11. A 47-year-old teacher has poor concentration, fatigue, difficulty lifting and rising, tingling in the fingers, a positive Babinski sign and absent reflexes. Hb 10 g/dL, MCV 103 fL. Most likely diagnosis?
- A. Hypothyroidism
- B. Vitamin B12 deficiency
- C. Folic acid deficiency
- D. Liver disease
- E. Alcohol toxicity

**Answer: B.** Macrocytic anaemia with neurological signs (extensor plantar and absent reflexes) is B12 deficiency with subacute combined degeneration. Folate deficiency does not cause these.

### 12. A 55-year-old man has malaise, fatigue and weight loss. Hb 12 g/dL, MCV 90 fL, platelets 250, WBC 10, serum iron low (10 µmol/L), TIBC 40 µmol/L, ferritin 160 µg/L. Most likely diagnosis?
- A. Thalassaemia
- B. Iron deficiency anaemia
- C. Anaemia of chronic disease
- D. Macrocytic anaemia
- E. Aplastic anaemia

**Answer: C.** Mild normocytic anaemia with low iron and a normal or high ferritin is anaemia of chronic disease. In iron deficiency the ferritin is low and TIBC is high. His weight loss suggests a chronic illness.

### 13. A 29-year-old vegetarian woman with weakness drinks much tea. CRP is below 5. Best investigation of her iron stores?
- A. Bone marrow biopsy
- B. Serum ferritin
- C. Serum transferrin
- D. Total iron-binding capacity
- E. Serum iron

**Answer: B.** Ferritin is the best blood test of iron stores, and a normal CRP means it is not falsely raised by inflammation. Marrow iron is the gold standard but is not the practical first step.

### 14. A 65-year-old man has malaise, breathlessness and paraesthesia in the hands. Pulse 115. The Schilling test is positive, the blood film is macrocytic and Coombs is negative. Most likely diagnosis?
- A. Iron deficiency anaemia
- B. Haemorrhage
- C. Anaemia of chronic disease
- D. Pernicious anaemia
- E. Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia

**Answer: D.** Macrocytic anaemia with neuropathy and a Schilling test that corrects with intrinsic factor is pernicious anaemia.

### 15. A 21-year-old woman has frequency, nocturia, constipation, polydipsia and left flank pain. Urine dipstick is normal. Most appropriate investigation?
- A. Serum phosphate
- B. Serum calcium
- C. Parathyroid hormone
- D. Plasma glucose
- E. Serum potassium

**Answer: B.** Polyuria, polydipsia, constipation and flank pain (stone) suggest hypercalcaemia ("bones, stones, groans"). A normal dipstick rules out glucose. Measure calcium first, then PTH.

### 16. A 19-year-old with newly diagnosed type 1 diabetes asks about future kidney complications. Which investigation is most appropriate for early signs of renal impairment?
- A. Blood pressure
- B. Microalbuminuria
- C. Serum creatinine
- D. Serum electrolytes
- E. Urine dipstick for glucose

**Answer: B.** Microalbuminuria is the earliest sign of diabetic nephropathy, long before creatinine rises.

### 17. A 58-year-old with CKD has altered mental status and asterixis. GFR 20 mL/min. A chest X-ray shows large bilateral pleural effusions and the ECG shows diffuse ST elevation with PR depression. All are indications for haemodialysis EXCEPT:
- A. Altered mental status and asterixis
- B. GFR of 20 mL/min
- C. Diffuse ST elevation and PR depression
- D. Low albumin level
- E. Bilateral pleural effusions

**Answer: D.** The urgent indications are encephalopathy, pericarditis (diffuse ST elevation, PR depression) and fluid overload (effusions). A low albumin is not an indication. A GFR of 20 alone would not trigger dialysis, but here it comes with uraemic complications.

### 18. A 55-year-old with renal colic has a 4 mm stone. All of these predispose to stones EXCEPT:
- A. Crohn's disease
- B. High sodium diet
- C. Short bowel syndrome
- D. Sarcoidosis
- E. Hypercitraturia

**Answer: E.** Citrate protects against stones. Low citrate (hypocitraturia) is the risk. Crohn's and short bowel cause hyperoxaluria, sarcoidosis causes hypercalcaemia, and salt raises urinary calcium.

### 19. Which best depicts beta-thalassaemia major?
- A. Decreased MCV, increased haemoglobin A2, increased haemoglobin F
- B. Decreased MCV, decreased haemoglobin A2, increased haemoglobin F
- C. Increased MCV, increased haemoglobin A2, increased haemoglobin F
- D. Increased MCV, decreased haemoglobin A2, increased haemoglobin F
- E. Increased MCV, increased haemoglobin A2, decreased haemoglobin F

**Answer: A.** Microcytic cells with raised HbF and raised HbA2.

### 20. Which is NOT true of acute interstitial nephritis?
- A. Can present with eosinophilia
- B. Mostly associated with non-oliguric kidney injury
- C. Always associated with rapid renal impairment
- D. Dacryoadenitis may be a presentation
- E. Urinalysis is often sterile

**Answer: C.** "Always" is wrong. Many cases are mild or non-oliguric. It often follows drugs (NSAIDs, antibiotics) with sterile pyuria and WBC casts.

### 21. Immune-mediated causes of chronic interstitial nephritis include all EXCEPT:
- A. SLE
- B. Tacrolimus
- C. Sarcoidosis
- D. Sjögren's syndrome
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Tacrolimus injures the kidney by vasoconstriction and direct toxicity, not by an immune mechanism.

### 22. All are true of dialysis EXCEPT:
- A. In peritoneal dialysis fluid and diet are less restricted than in haemodialysis
- B. Failure to grow is an indication for dialysis in CKD
- C. About 12 litres of dialysate are used for 4 to 6 hours in continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis
- D. Dialysis aims at reducing BUN by one third during the first dialysis
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** CAPD uses about 2 litres per exchange, four times a day (around 8 litres a day), with each dwell of 4 to 6 hours. The "12 litres" figure is wrong.

### 23. All describe focal segmental glomerulosclerosis EXCEPT:
- A. Must always have crescent formation
- B. Often associated with systemic disease
- C. Small vessel vasculitis
- D. Responds to immunosuppressants
- E. None of the above

**Answer: A.** FSGS shows segmental scarring, not crescents. Option C is also not FSGS, so the question has two weak options. A is the clearest.

### 24. Correct statements about hypoproliferative anaemias EXCEPT:
- A. Inflammation is a key cause
- B. There is erythroid hyperplasia in the bone marrow
- C. Iron deficiency anaemia is a key cause
- D. Microcytosis is not always the case
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** In hypoproliferative anaemia the marrow fails to respond (no erythroid hyperplasia and a low reticulocyte count).

### 25. Which is NOT true about urolithiasis?
- A. Spinach can be a cause
- B. Hydrochlorothiazide reduces calcium excretion
- C. Vitamin C supplementation reduces oxalate excretion
- D. Vitamin C supplementation increases oxalate excretion
- E. Alkalinisation discourages cystine formation

**Answer: C.** Vitamin C is converted to oxalate, so it raises oxalate excretion. C and D contradict each other.

### 26. Which stone size would pass spontaneously in over 90% of cases?
- A. 1 mm
- B. 3 mm
- C. 2 mm
- D. None of the above
- E. All of the above

**Answer: E.** Stones of 4 mm or less pass in about 90%.

### 27. Which statement about acute kidney injury is the ODD one out?
- A. Prerenal AKI: renal hypoperfusion
- B. Prerenal AKI: renal hyperperfusion
- C. Prerenal AKI: NSAIDs
- D. Prerenal AKI: ARBs
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Prerenal injury comes from hypoperfusion, never hyperperfusion.

### 28. Which statement is TRUE of azotaemia?
- A. FeNa above 1% may be seen in acute tubular necrosis
- B. FeNa below 1% may be seen in acute tubular necrosis
- C. RBCs in urine suggest interstitial nephritis
- D. Muddy brown casts in urine suggest vasculitis
- E. None of the above

**Answer: A.** In ATN the tubules cannot retain sodium, so FeNa is above 1% (below 1% suggests prerenal). Muddy brown casts mean ATN. RBC casts mean glomerulonephritis.

### 29. A 54-year-old has malaise, easy fatiguability and haematuria for weeks, with dysmorphic RBCs and proteinuria over 500 mg/24 h. All are other likely findings EXCEPT:
- A. Anti-GBM antibody
- B. Hepatitis C
- C. HIV
- D. WBC casts
- E. Hepatitis B

**Answer: D.** Dysmorphic RBCs and proteinuria show glomerulonephritis. WBC casts point to interstitial nephritis or pyelonephritis. The viruses and anti-GBM are glomerular causes.

### 30. Which is correctly matched with the K/DOQI classification of chronic kidney disease?
- A. Stage 2: GFR 60 to 79 mL/min/1.73 m²
- B. Stage 1: GFR above 80 mL/min/1.73 m²
- C. Stage 3: GFR 40 to 59 mL/min/1.73 m²
- D. Stage 4: GFR 15 to 39 mL/min/1.73 m²
- E. None of the above

**Answer: E.** The real stages are 1: 90 or more, 2: 60 to 89, 3: 30 to 59, 4: 15 to 29 and 5: under 15. Every listed range is wrong.

## Section B: Short answer questions

### 1. Alouch, 25, has protein excretion of 3.9 g per day. List 6 likely causes (6 marks)

Heavy proteinuria of this size means nephrotic-range glomerular disease:

1. **Minimal change disease** (commonest in children).
2. **Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis** (HIV, obesity, heroin).
3. **Membranous nephropathy** (hepatitis B, malaria, lupus, drugs such as NSAIDs).
4. **Diabetic nephropathy.**
5. **Lupus nephritis** (class V and others).
6. **Amyloidosis.**
7. Others: membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis, post-infectious glomerulonephritis, IgA nephropathy and HIV-associated nephropathy.

### 2. Describe the processes that produce a stable haemostatic plug after vessel injury (4 marks)

1. **Vasoconstriction:** the injured vessel contracts to reduce blood flow.
2. **Platelet adhesion:** platelets stick to exposed collagen through von Willebrand factor (GPIb).
3. **Platelet activation and aggregation:** platelets release ADP and thromboxane A2 and join through fibrinogen (GPIIb/IIIa) to form the primary plug.
4. **Coagulation:** the cascade, ending in thrombin, turns fibrinogen into fibrin, which strengthens the plug into a stable clot.
5. **Regulation and repair:** antithrombin, protein C and fibrinolysis (plasmin) limit the clot.

### 3. Five features that separate platelet-disorder bleeding from coagulation-factor bleeding (5 marks)

| Feature | Platelet disorder | Coagulation-factor disorder |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | Petechiae and small ecchymoses | Large ecchymoses and deep haematomas |
| Joints and muscle | Haemarthrosis rare | Haemarthrosis and muscle bleeds common |
| Mucosa | Epistaxis, gum, menorrhagia | Less common |
| After a small cut | Bleeds immediately and stops with pressure only after a while | Delayed bleeding, restarts after a few hours |
| Sex | Both | Usually male (haemophilia A and B are X-linked) |
| Tests | Low platelets or abnormal bleeding time; normal PT and APTT | Prolonged PT or APTT; normal platelets |
