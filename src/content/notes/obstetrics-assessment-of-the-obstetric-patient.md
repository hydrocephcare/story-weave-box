# Assessment of the obstetric patient

*Week 1, Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology (lecture by Dr Ritho). Rewritten as clinical notes: what to ask, what to examine, and what each finding means on the ward.*

**The reasoning chain:** who is she and how risky is this pregnancy → how old is the pregnancy → what happened in earlier pregnancies → what is she complaining of today → is the mother well and is the baby well → what do the abdominal and pelvic findings say about lie, presentation and progress.

## 1. Take the history in this order

### Identification and the chief complaint

Name, **age** (risk goes up below 20 years and above 35 years), marital status, **address** (a long distance from a health facility is itself a risk), religion, occupation, date of admission, ward and bed. She may come for routine antenatal care or with a complaint. Write each complaint with its **duration**, then explore it (for pain use **SOCRATES**) with associated symptoms, possible causes and **fetal well-being**.

### Antenatal history of this pregnancy

- **LNMP** (last normal menstrual period) and **EDD** (expected date of delivery). **Naegele's rule:** LNMP − 3 months + 7 days. Then work out the gestational age in completed weeks and days.
- **Quickening** (first fetal movement felt): about **18–20 weeks in a first pregnancy, 16–18 weeks in later pregnancies**. It helps to date the pregnancy when the LNMP is not known.
- Antenatal care elsewhere: where, how many visits, and what was done (supplements, vaccines, tests, ultrasound scans).
- Complications so far: bleeding, hyperemesis, urinary tract infection. Was the pregnancy **unplanned, unwanted or unsupported**?
- **Always ask about danger symptoms:** vaginal bleeding, severe headache, blurred vision, epigastric or severe abdominal pain, profuse vaginal discharge, reduced or absent fetal movement, fever, persistent vomiting. If present, add them to the presenting complaint.

### Obstetric history

All previous pregnancies and outcomes.

- **Gravidity:** the number of pregnancies of any kind (term, preterm, stillbirth, abortion, ectopic, molar).
- **Parity:** pregnancies delivered after **28 weeks** (Kenya and the UK), or **20 weeks or more** by WHO definition. An **abortion** is a pregnancy loss before viability.

**Worked example:** one term delivery, one set of twins born at 32 weeks and two abortions = **gravida 5, para 2 + 2**, with three living children.

**Four-number system (term–preterm–abortions–living):** the same woman is **para 1-1-2-3**.

For **each previous pregnancy**, in order: date, **length of gestation** (abortion below 28 weeks, preterm below 37 completed weeks, term 37 to 42 weeks, post-term above 42 weeks), medical problems in that pregnancy (hypertension, antepartum haemorrhage, diabetes), onset of labour (spontaneous or induced), presentation, duration of labour, **mode of delivery** (vaginal, instrumental, caesarean section with its indication, destructive delivery), **fetal outcome** (alive or dead, sex, weight, malformations, current health) and **postpartum complications** such as postpartum haemorrhage.

### Gynaecological history

Family planning (method, duration, side effects), **sexual history** for the risk of STIs and HIV, past operations (laparotomy, D&C, evacuation, manual vacuum aspiration, myomectomy) and the **menstrual history**: menarche, cycle length, duration and flow. **Normal:** cycle 21–35 days, flow 10–80 mL, 1–8 days, dark red without clots.

### Past medical, surgical, drug, family and social history

- **Diabetes, hypertension, thyroid disease:** they affect pregnancy or worsen in it.
- **Blood transfusion:** matters for haemolytic disease of the newborn.
- **Drugs:** teratogenicity and allergies. **Maternal infection:** the **TORCH** group.
- **Social:** education, **alcohol, smoking, drugs**, occupation (radiation, halothane, chemical exposure) and income (low socio-economic status is linked to **pre-eclampsia and preterm labour**).
- **Family history:** diabetes, hypertension, **multiple pregnancy**, genetic disorders.

## 2. Examination: the rules before you start

Examine in a **private room** with a **chaperone**. Explain what you will do before, during and after. The patient should have **emptied her bladder** and be properly positioned. Use warm hands and instruments, good light, gloves and swabs, and keep eye contact.

### General examination and vital signs

Look at the **conjunctiva, sclera, teeth and buccal mucosa** for pallor, jaundice, mucosal congestion and dental caries.

- **Blood pressure**: Use **left lateral with 30° tilt** (or sitting) to avoid the **supine hypotensive syndrome**
- **Pulse**: Rises by 10–15 beats per minute
- **Respiratory rate**: Rises by 1–4 breaths per minute
- **Weight**: A gain of **more than 1 kg a week is abnormal** (fluid retention, pre-eclampsia)
- **Height**: Below **150 cm** may be constitutional but is a risk factor (contracted pelvis)

**Breasts:** nipple retraction, pigmentation, lumps, discharge, colour change.

**Heart and lungs:** the steps are the same as in a non-pregnant woman, but the following are **normal in pregnancy**: reduced diaphragmatic movement (the uterus pushes the diaphragm up), a point of maximum impulse shifted to the left, a **third heart sound** and a **functional systolic murmur**.

**Skin:** pigmentation of breasts, lower abdomen, midline and genitalia is normal; so are **spider naevi and palmar erythema**.

## 3. The abdominal examination

### Inspection

- **Linea nigra:** a dark midline line caused by melanocyte-stimulating hormone.
- **Striae gravidarum:** stretch marks that are purplish when new and white when old, caused by stretching.
- The **umbilicus** may be inverted, flat or everted. Look for **scars**, distended veins, flank fullness and visible fetal movement.

### Palpation

**Superficial** (rigidity, tenderness, superficial masses, abdominal wall defects), then **deep** (masses, organs, tenderness), then **obstetric palpation (Leopold's manoeuvres).**

**Fundal height:** first correct any tilt of the uterus, then measure.

- **Finger method:** one finger above the umbilicus is 2 weeks, one finger below is 1 week. The uterus is felt at the **symphysis at 12 weeks**, at the **umbilicus at 20 weeks** and at the **xiphisternum at term**.
- **Tape measure:** symphysis to fundus in centimetres. Between **18 and 34 weeks** it is accurate to within 2 weeks of the real gestational age.

![Fundal height by gestational age. Mikael Häggström (derivative), CC BY-SA 3.0](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/11/Fundal_height_by_gestational_age.jpg)

### The four Leopold manoeuvres

- **1st: fundal**: Measure the fundal height. A **soft, irregular, bulky** mass at the fundus is the **breech**; a **hard, round, ballotable** mass is the **head**
- **2nd: lateral**: Finds the **lie** (longitudinal, transverse, oblique) and, in a longitudinal lie, **which side the back is on**. The back is linear, rigid and smooth; the limbs are small, irregular and bulky. The fetal heart is best heard over the back
- **3rd: pelvic**: Finds the **presentation**: head (cephalic), breech or shoulder. In a cephalic presentation, descent is judged by the **rule of fifths**: **5/5 floating, 4/5 fixed, 2/5 engaged**. Together with the 2nd manoeuvre it shows the **attitude**: extended (the head prominence is on the same side as the back), flexed (the opposite side), or military (both sides at the same level)
- **4th: Pawlik grip**: The **only manoeuvre done with one hand**; it checks the presenting part

![Leopold's manoeuvres, from the original figures by Christian Gerhard Leopold. Public domain](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/eb/Handgriffe.JPG)

**Percussion:** shifting dullness and flank dullness, fluid thrill (ascites).

### Auscultation

The fetal heart is first heard over the back at **16–18 weeks in a woman who has delivered before** and **18–20 weeks in a first pregnancy**. In a **complete breech** it is heard **above the umbilicus**; in a **cephalic** presentation **below it**; in an **occipito-posterior** position **in the flanks**.

## 4. Genitourinary and pelvic examination

Check for costovertebral and suprapubic tenderness. A **pelvic examination in pregnancy** is done only twice, or in complications or suspected labour: to diagnose a **contracted pelvis**, to find the **Bishop score**, and in labour to assess **cervical dilatation and effacement**, membranes and the colour of the liquor, the presenting part, **station and position**, **moulding**, **caput** and clinical pelvimetry.

**External genitalia:** pubic hair (inverted triangle in a woman), labia, ulcers, swellings, discharge, the hymen.

**Speculum:** vaginal colour (normally pink), septum, rugae, fornices, discharge, scars, lacerations. **The cervix:** colour, the **os (pin-point in a nullipara, slit-like in a woman who has delivered)**, dilatation, effacement, bleeding or a mass.

**Bimanual examination:**

- **Cervix:** closed normally, moves 2–4 cm without pain, smooth, with the consistency of the **tip of the nose**.
- **Uterus:** non-tender, mobile, **9 cm long, pear-shaped, smooth and firm** when not pregnant.
- **Adnexa:** normally not palpable except in a thin woman with a soft abdomen. Describe any mass.

## 5. Summarise the case like a clinician

End with a one-line summary (age, gravida and para, gestational age, the main problem), **the problem list**, a **differential diagnosis**, the **investigations** you want and the **plan**. An examiner asks "what worries you about this patient?", so say the risk out loud: extremes of age, grand multiparity, previous caesarean section, pre-eclampsia, bleeding, a small woman, a long distance to the hospital.

## Practice questions

**1.** The expected date of delivery by Naegele's rule is:
**A. LNMP − 3 months + 7 days** · B. LNMP + 9 months − 7 days · C. LNMP + 280 days − 7 days · D. LNMP + 40 weeks + 7 days · E. Conception + 9 months
*Answer: A.*

**2.** A woman had one term delivery, one set of twins at 32 weeks and two abortions. She is:
A. Gravida 4 para 2 · **B. Gravida 5 para 2+2** · C. Gravida 5 para 3 · D. Gravida 3 para 2 · E. Gravida 6 para 2
*Answer: B. Twins count as one pregnancy; abortions count towards gravidity and are shown after the plus sign.*

**3.** On palpation the head is 2/5 palpable above the symphysis. The head is:
A. Floating · B. Fixed · C. Not descended · **D. Engaged** · E. Crowning
*Answer: D.*

**4.** Which manoeuvre is performed with one hand?
A. First · B. Second · **C. Fourth (Pawlik grip)** · D. Third · E. All of them
*Answer: C.*

**5.** The fetal heart is best heard over:
A. The fetal limbs · B. The fundus always · C. The symphysis always · D. The flank in all presentations · **E. The fetal back**
*Answer: E.*
