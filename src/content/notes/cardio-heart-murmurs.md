# Heart murmurs — Year 4 Internal Medicine (Cardiovascular) Notes

*Clinical reasoning notes for the bedside. Describe every murmur the same way, then ask what is causing it.*

**The reasoning chain:** time it (systolic or diastolic) → place it (where is it loudest, where does it go) → grade it → use manoeuvres → name the lesion → ask what caused it and how bad it is.

## 1. What is a murmur?

A **murmur** is the extra sound made by turbulent blood flow across a valve or defect. A systolic murmur falls between the first and second heart sounds; a diastolic murmur falls after the second. **Diastolic murmurs are always pathological.** Many soft systolic murmurs in children and in pregnancy are harmless (flow murmurs).

## 2. Describe every murmur by

**Timing** (systolic or diastolic), **site** where it is loudest, **radiation**, **grade**, **pitch and shape**, and the **effect of manoeuvres**.

**Levine grading:** systolic murmurs are graded **1 to 6** (grade 4 and above has a **thrill**); diastolic murmurs 1 to 4.

## 3. Manoeuvres

- **Right-sided murmurs are louder on inspiration** (more blood returns to the right heart). Left-sided murmurs are louder on **expiration**. A simple way to remember it: right = in, left = out.
- Lean the patient **forward in expiration** to hear the early diastolic murmur of aortic regurgitation.
- Roll the patient to the **left lateral position** and use the **bell** to hear the mid-diastolic rumble of mitral stenosis.

## 4. The lesions

| Lesion | Murmur | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Mitral stenosis** | **Mid-diastolic rumble** at the apex | **Loud first sound and opening snap**; best with the bell, left lateral, in expiration |
| **Mitral regurgitation** | **Pansystolic** at the apex | **Radiates to the axilla** |
| **Aortic stenosis** | **Ejection systolic**, right 2nd intercostal space | **Radiates to the carotids**; **slow-rising pulse** |
| **Aortic regurgitation** | **Early diastolic**, left sternal edge | Patient sitting forward, in expiration; **wide pulse pressure** |
| **Tricuspid stenosis / regurgitation** | Diastolic / pansystolic | **Louder on inspiration**; raised JVP |
| **Pulmonary stenosis / regurgitation** | Ejection systolic / early diastolic | Left 2nd intercostal space |

## 5. Peripheral signs of aortic regurgitation

A big pulse pressure produces a collection of named signs. Know them:

- **Collapsing (water-hammer) pulse**
- **Corrigan's sign:** visible "dancing" carotid pulsations
- **de Musset's sign:** the head bobs with each heartbeat
- **Quincke's sign:** pulsation of the nail-bed capillaries
- **Traube's sign:** a "pistol-shot" sound over the femoral arteries
- **Duroziez's sign:** a to-and-fro murmur over the femoral artery
- **Müller's sign:** pulsation of the uvula
- **Becker's sign:** pulsation of the retinal arteries
- **Hill's sign:** the leg systolic pressure is much higher than the arm

## 6. Always ask what is causing it

In many low- and middle-income countries, including Kenya, **rheumatic heart disease** is the leading cause of valve disease in young people. Mitral stenosis is nearly always rheumatic. Other causes: **degenerative calcific aortic stenosis** (older patients), a **bicuspid aortic valve**, **infective endocarditis**, **mitral valve prolapse**, and **ischaemia** (papillary muscle rupture after a heart attack). See the note on rheumatic fever for the Jones criteria.

## 7. Where the examination can mislead

- The **loudness of a murmur does not equal the severity**: a very severe mitral stenosis can have a soft rumble, and in severe aortic stenosis with heart failure the murmur may be quiet.
- Atrial fibrillation (common in mitral stenosis) makes timing difficult: take your time, and feel the carotid pulse while you listen.
- A new murmur plus fever means **endocarditis until proven otherwise**.

## Practice questions

**1.** A mid-diastolic rumble at the apex with an opening snap is characteristic of:
A. Mitral regurgitation · **B. Mitral stenosis** · C. Aortic stenosis · D. Aortic regurgitation · E. Tricuspid regurgitation
*Answer: B.*

**2.** A pansystolic murmur at the apex radiating to the axilla indicates:
A. Aortic stenosis · B. Pulmonary stenosis · **C. Mitral regurgitation** · D. Mitral stenosis · E. Aortic regurgitation
*Answer: C.*

**3.** Which finding is a peripheral sign of aortic regurgitation?
A. Slow-rising pulse · **B. Head bobbing synchronous with the pulse (de Musset's sign)** · C. Pulsus paradoxus · D. Pulsus alternans · E. Raised JVP
*Answer: B. A slow-rising pulse belongs to aortic stenosis.*

**4.** Which murmur becomes louder on inspiration?
A. Mitral regurgitation · B. Aortic stenosis · **C. Tricuspid regurgitation** · D. Mitral stenosis · E. Aortic regurgitation
*Answer: C. Right-sided murmurs are louder on inspiration.*
