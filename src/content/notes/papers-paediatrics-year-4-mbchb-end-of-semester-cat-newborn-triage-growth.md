## Multiple choice questions

### 1. A 4-year-old has acute diarrhoea, cannot drink, has weak pulses and is not alert. The most correct statement is:
- A. A weak pulse supports a diagnosis of some dehydration
- B. The extremities are likely to be warm
- C. This is likely severe dehydration
- D. Boluses of 20 ml/kg of normal saline are recommended
- E. ORS will be sufficient for fluid replacement

**Answer: C.** Unable to drink, weak pulse and altered consciousness are signs of severe dehydration with shock. The extremities are cold, and ORS alone is not enough. (Bolus size in the protocol depends on whether the child is also severely malnourished.)

### 2. Which is the most correct statement about a 4-day-old neonate who is hot and feeding poorly?
- A. Neonatal sepsis is among the likely diagnoses
- B. Klebsiella has not been implicated
- C. A viral infection is an unlikely cause
- D. C-reactive protein has no role in management
- E. Give oral antibiotics and allow home

**Answer: A.** Fever and poor feeding in a neonate is sepsis until proved otherwise. It needs admission, blood culture and IV antibiotics. Klebsiella is a common cause.

### 3. Of pathological jaundice in neonates:
- A. The onset is after 24 hours of life
- B. It presents as severe hyperbilirubinaemia
- C. The baby is otherwise well
- D. It only occurs when mother and neonate have ABO or rhesus incompatibility
- E. IV immunoglobulin has no role in management

**Answer: B.** Pathological jaundice starts within 24 hours, rises quickly and is usually high. The baby is often unwell, causes include sepsis and G6PD deficiency as well as incompatibility, and IV immunoglobulin has a role in immune haemolysis.

### 4. Which childhood statistic for Kenya, from the Demographic and Health Survey (KDHS), is correct?
- A. Infant mortality: 22 per 1000 live births
- B. Neonatal mortality: 32 per 1000 live births
- C. Under-5 mortality: 114 per 1000 live births
- D. 61% of infants are exclusively breastfed
- E. Full vaccination coverage: 73%

**Answer: D.** The 2014 survey gave 61% exclusive breastfeeding in infants under 6 months. The other figures do not match any recent survey. The answer depends on the survey year.

### 5. Which is an emergency sign in triage?
- A. Diarrhoea with sunken eyes
- B. Restless
- C. History of convulsions
- D. Tiny baby
- E. High temperature of 40 degrees

**Answer: C.** Convulsions are an emergency sign (when occurring now). Restlessness, a tiny baby (under 2 months) and a very high temperature are priority signs, and diarrhoea with sunken eyes is only an emergency sign if combined with the other signs of severe dehydration. The question is loosely worded.

### 6. A 5-month-old passes bloody stools and then starts bilious vomiting. You feel a mass in the abdomen, and the rectum is empty.
- A. This is dysentery
- B. Ultrasound has no role
- C. Surgical review is indicated immediately
- D. Give antibiotics and allow home
- E. If the diagnosis is delayed, mortality is not increased

**Answer: C.** This is intussusception (redcurrant jelly stool, sausage-shaped mass, vomiting). Ultrasound confirms it, and urgent reduction (air enema or surgery) is needed.

### 7. Akinyi, 3 years, 8 kg, has visible severe wasting:
- A. IV benzylpenicillin and gentamicin have a role
- B. A weight-for-height Z score of more than minus 3 supports the diagnosis
- C. Her weight for age is likely above 80% of expected
- D. She should be started on a high protein diet immediately
- E. Her total body potassium is increased

**Answer: A.** In complicated severe acute malnutrition, IV antibiotics are given. Severe wasting is a weight-for-height Z score below minus 3, weight-for-age is below 60 to 80%, feeding starts with low-protein F-75, and total body potassium is low.

### 8. Concerning evaluation of an infant with suspected congenital heart disease, which statement is FALSE?
- A. The absence of a murmur rules out the possibility of congenital heart disease
- B. Confirmation of the diagnosis is by echocardiogram
- C. Cyanosis is determined by physical examination
- D. A chest X-ray has a role in diagnosis
- E. Some infants are asymptomatic

**Answer: A.** Many serious defects (for example transposition) have no murmur.

### 9. Matala, a 2-year-old with diarrhoea and vomiting, has cold peripheries, weak pulses and is unresponsive. His MUAC is 9 cm. Which is correct in managing his dehydration?
- A. Give 20 ml/kg of Ringer's lactate to run over 15 minutes
- B. Give 20 ml/kg of Ringer's lactate with 5% dextrose to run over 2 hours
- C. Give ReSoMal 1 ml/kg/hour
- D. Give Ringer's lactate with 5% dextrose 30 ml/kg over 30 minutes, then 70 ml/kg over 2.5 hours
- E. Give 20 ml/kg of normal saline with 5% dextrose over 2 hours

**Answer: B.** A MUAC of 9 cm (under 11.5) means severe acute malnutrition. Shock in SAM is treated more slowly with Ringer's lactate with 5% dextrose (the standard dose is 15 ml/kg over 1 hour, then reassess). Fast boluses can cause heart failure. The nearest option is B.

### 10. The components of tetralogy of Fallot include all EXCEPT:
- A. Ventricular septal defect
- B. Transposition of the great arteries
- C. Pulmonary stenosis
- D. Overriding aorta
- E. Right ventricular hypertrophy

**Answer: B.** The four are VSD, pulmonary stenosis, overriding aorta and right ventricular hypertrophy.

### 11. GW is a 12-month-old child. You expect him to:
- A. Speak 10 to 15 words
- B. Stare at his own hands
- C. Build a tower of 6 blocks
- D. Run
- E. Draw a circle

**Answer: A.** None of these is a typical 12-month milestone. At 12 months a child says 1 to 3 words, stands and walks with support, and builds a tower of 2 blocks. Speaking several words is the closest, so the question is faulty.

### 12. The clinical features of Down syndrome include all EXCEPT:
- A. Hypotonia
- B. Low-set ears
- C. Mental retardation
- D. Cleft lip and palate
- E. Atrioventricular septal defect

**Answer: D.** Cleft lip and palate is not typical. Hypotonia, flat face, upslanting eyes, learning disability and AV septal defect are.

### 13. The following organisms cause meningitis in infants aged 0 to 2 years EXCEPT:
- A. Streptococcus pneumoniae
- B. Haemophilus influenzae
- C. Entamoeba histolytica
- D. Neisseria meningitidis
- E. Listeria monocytogenes

**Answer: C.** Entamoeba histolytica causes amoebic dysentery and liver abscess, not meningitis.

### 14. Which is NOT an absolute contraindication to lumbar puncture in a child?
- A. Bulging fontanelle
- B. Infection at the lumbar puncture site
- C. Lateralising signs
- D. Active cardiopulmonary resuscitation
- E. Unequal pupils

**Answer: A.** A bulging fontanelle is common in meningitis, and a puncture can be done with care. Infection at the site, focal signs, unequal pupils and an unstable child are reasons to defer it.

### 15. In unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia, the risk of kernicterus increases with the use of:
- A. Ceftriaxone
- B. Phenobarbitone
- C. Ampicillin
- D. Sulphonamides
- E. Metronidazole

**Answer: D.** Sulphonamides displace bilirubin from albumin, so more free bilirubin reaches the brain. Ceftriaxone also does this, but sulphonamides are the classic answer.

### 16. All of the following may be required in a one-hour-old infant with severe birth asphyxia EXCEPT:
- A. Glucose
- B. Dexamethasone
- C. Calcium gluconate
- D. Normal saline
- E. Therapeutic cooling

**Answer: B.** Steroids do not help brain injury from asphyxia. Glucose, calcium, fluids and cooling do.

### 17. All of the following are features of prematurity EXCEPT:
- A. No creases on the sole
- B. Abundant lanugo
- C. Thick ear cartilage
- D. Empty scrotum
- E. Thin transparent skin

**Answer: C.** Preterm ear cartilage is soft and thin. A term baby has firm cartilage that recoils.

### 18. A 25-year-old had premature rupture of membranes and delivered a boy who was lethargic and apnoeic on day 1 and went into shock. A vaginal swab grew beta-haemolytic colonies of Gram-positive cocci. The most likely organism is:
- A. Streptococcus pyogenes
- B. Streptococcus agalactiae
- C. Peptostreptococci
- D. Enterococcus faecium

**Answer: B.** Group B streptococcus (S. agalactiae) is the leading cause of early-onset neonatal sepsis.

### 19. A newborn drools after feeds, has respiratory distress and froths at the mouth. The diagnosis is:
- A. Tracheo-oesophageal fistula
- B. TOF (tracheo-oesophageal fistula)
- C. Respiratory distress syndrome
- D. Meconium aspiration syndrome
- E. None of the above

**Answer: A.** Oesophageal atresia with a tracheo-oesophageal fistula presents with drooling, choking on feeds and froth. Options A and B are the same, so the question is faulty.

### 20. Full-term small-for-dates babies are at high risk of:
- A. Hypoglycaemia
- B. Intraventricular haemorrhage
- C. Bronchopulmonary dysplasia
- D. Hyperthermia
- E. Hyperglycaemia

**Answer: A.** They have little glycogen and fat, so hypoglycaemia and hypothermia are the main risks. Intraventricular haemorrhage and bronchopulmonary dysplasia are problems of prematurity.

### 21. Breast milk at room temperature can be stored for:
- A. 8 hours
- B. 12 hours
- C. 24 hours
- D. 4 hours
- E. 1 hour

**Answer: A.** Up to 8 hours at room temperature, and longer in a refrigerator.

### 22. The following are radiographic features of rickets EXCEPT:
- A. Rickety rosary
- B. Decreased bone density
- C. Subperiosteal bleeding
- D. Increased width of the growth plate
- E. Cupping

**Answer: C.** Subperiosteal bleeding is a feature of scurvy. Rickets shows widened, cupped, frayed growth plates and thin bones.

### 23. The composition of breast milk per 100 ml is about:
- A. 67 kcal, 3.5 g protein, 3.5 g fat, 4.5 g carbohydrate
- B. 67 kcal, 1.2 g protein, 4 g fat, 4.5 g carbohydrate
- C. 67 kcal, 1.2 g protein, 4 g fat, 7 g carbohydrate
- D. 100 kcal, 3.5 g protein, 4 g fat, 7 g carbohydrate
- E. 100 kcal, 3.5 g protein, 4.5 g fat, 5 g carbohydrate

**Answer: C.** Mature human milk gives about 67 kcal, 1.1 to 1.2 g protein, 4 g fat and 7 g lactose per 100 ml.

### 24. The length of a newborn is about:
- A. 100 cm
- B. 75 cm
- C. 50 cm
- D. 30 cm
- E. 20 cm

**Answer: C.** A term newborn is about 50 cm long.

### 25. Breathing and swallowing movements are seen in the foetus by:
- A. 13 to 14 weeks
- B. 18 to 24 weeks
- C. 15 to 18 weeks
- D. 30 to 36 weeks
- E. 28 to 32 weeks

**Answer: A.** Foetal breathing movements and swallowing begin in the first part of the second trimester (as early as 10 to 14 weeks). The earliest option is best.

### 26. The birth weight of a newborn doubles by 5 months. Birth length doubles by:
- A. 1 year
- B. 2 years
- C. 3 years
- D. 4 years

**Answer: D.** Length doubles by about 4 years, and weight triples by 1 year.

### 27. Preference for one hand (handedness) is evident by:
- A. 6 months
- B. 1 year
- C. 2 years
- D. 3 years
- E. 6 years

**Answer: C.** A hand preference emerges around 2 years and is fixed by 3 to 4 years. The sources differ, so the question is loosely worded.

### 28. The order of development of secondary sexual characteristics in boys is:
- A. Testicular enlargement, pubic hair, axillary hair, beard
- B. Pubic hair, testicular enlargement, axillary hair, beard
- C. Testicular enlargement, axillary hair, pubic hair, beard
- D. Axillary hair, beard, testicular enlargement, pubic hair
- E. Pubic hair, axillary hair, testicular enlargement, beard

**Answer: A.** Puberty in boys starts with testicular enlargement, then pubic hair, penis growth, axillary hair and facial hair.

### 29. The most common cause of cholestatic jaundice in the newborn is:
- A. Hypoplasia of the biliary tract
- B. Neonatal hepatitis
- C. Choledochal cyst
- D. Physiological jaundice
- E. Schistosomiasis

**Answer: B.** Neonatal hepatitis syndrome is the commonest cause, followed by biliary atresia.

### 30. Visual acuity reaches adult level at:
- A. 7 years
- B. 9 years
- C. 3 years
- D. 1 year
- E. 10 years

**Answer: C.** Acuity reaches 6/6 by about 3 to 5 years. Three years is the nearest option.

## Section B: short answer questions

### 1. List the priority signs in triage of children (5 marks).

Priority signs (the child is seen next, ahead of the queue). A useful memory aid is **TPR MOB**:

1. **T**iny baby (under 2 months).
2. **T**emperature very high.
3. **T**rauma or other urgent surgical condition.
4. **P**allor (severe).
5. **P**oisoning.
6. **P**ain (severe).
7. **R**espiratory distress (not severe).
8. **R**estless, continuously irritable or lethargic.
9. **R**eferral (urgent).
10. **M**alnutrition: visible severe wasting.
11. **O**edema of both feet.
12. **B**urns (major).

### 2. Outline the investigations you would order in a neonate presenting with jaundice (6 marks).

1. **Serum bilirubin**, total and direct (conjugated), to confirm the level and its type.
2. **Maternal and baby blood groups** (ABO and rhesus) and the **direct Coombs test** to look for immune haemolysis.
3. **Full blood count** with a **blood film** (haemoglobin, reticulocytes, spherocytes) for haemolysis.
4. **G6PD level** (screen for G6PD deficiency).
5. **Septic screen** if the baby looks unwell: blood culture, C-reactive protein, urine microscopy and culture, and lumbar puncture if indicated.
6. **Liver function tests** and an **abdominal ultrasound** if the jaundice is conjugated, prolonged (over 14 days) or the stools are pale (biliary atresia).
7. **Thyroid function tests** in prolonged jaundice, and TORCH screen and urine reducing substances (galactosaemia) when the history points to them.

### 3. Describe the features of Down syndrome (6 marks).

**Cause:** an extra chromosome 21, in about 95% from non-disjunction (risk rises with maternal age), and the rest from translocation or mosaicism.

1. **Face and head:** flat face, flat nasal bridge, upslanting palpebral fissures with epicanthic folds, Brushfield spots in the iris, small low-set ears, protruding tongue and a small round head (brachycephaly).
2. **Neck and hands:** short neck with excess skin, a single palmar (simian) crease, short broad hands with clinodactyly of the fifth finger, and a wide gap between the first and second toes.
3. **Tone and growth:** marked hypotonia, hyperextensible joints and short stature.
4. **Learning:** intellectual disability, with delayed milestones.
5. **Heart:** congenital heart disease in about 40 to 50%, most often atrioventricular septal defect, and also VSD, ASD and patent ductus.
6. **Other associations:** duodenal atresia and Hirschsprung disease, hypothyroidism, leukaemia, hearing and eye problems (cataract, squint), atlantoaxial instability and recurrent chest infections.

### 4. Outline the developmental milestones in late childhood, 6 to 11 years (6 marks).

1. **Gross motor:** runs, jumps, climbs and skips well, rides a bicycle, and has improving coordination and balance in sports.
2. **Fine motor:** ties shoelaces, writes fluently and draws detailed pictures.
3. **Language:** reads and writes, with a large vocabulary and full grammar by 8 to 10 years, and understands jokes and word play.
4. **Cognition:** moves into concrete operational thinking, so can classify, order and understand conservation, and tells the time and uses money.
5. **Social and emotional:** friendships with the same sex, group games with rules, a growing sense of fairness and a sense of competence at school, and the first signs of puberty at the end of the period (girls earlier than boys).
6. **School:** learns to cope with schoolwork, and independence in dressing, washing and feeding is complete.

### 5. Discuss essential newborn care (8 marks).

Essential newborn care is the set of simple steps given to every baby at and soon after birth:

1. **Clean delivery and cord care:** clean hands and surfaces, delayed cord clamping (after 1 to 3 minutes) and clean cord stump care with no harmful applications (7.1% chlorhexidine digluconate gel where recommended).
2. **Immediate drying and warmth:** dry the baby at once and remove the wet cloth, then keep the baby warm with skin-to-skin contact and a hat and blanket, and delay the first bath for 24 hours. This prevents hypothermia.
3. **Assess breathing:** a baby who is not breathing is stimulated by drying, and if still not breathing needs airway clearing and bag-and-mask ventilation (within the first minute, the golden minute).
4. **Early breastfeeding:** start within the first hour with colostrum, with exclusive breastfeeding and no prelacteal feeds.
5. **Eye care:** tetracycline 1% eye ointment to prevent ophthalmia neonatorum.
6. **Vitamin K:** 1 mg IM to prevent haemorrhagic disease of the newborn.
7. **Immunisation:** BCG and oral polio (birth dose), with hepatitis B where on the schedule.
8. **Examination and weighing:** a head-to-toe check for abnormalities, and identification of babies at risk (low birth weight, preterm, sepsis risk).
9. **Prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV:** infant prophylaxis (nevirapine) for babies of HIV-positive mothers.
10. **Counselling and follow-up:** keep skin-to-skin (kangaroo care for small babies), danger signs for mothers to bring the baby back, and postnatal visits (first week and 6 weeks).
