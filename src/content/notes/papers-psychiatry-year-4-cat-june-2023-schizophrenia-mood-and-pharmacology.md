## Multiple choice questions

### 1. Which one of the symptoms is NOT typical of schizophrenia?
- A. Thought alienation
- B. Paranoid delusions
- C. Disorientation
- D. Hallucination
- E. Disorganised speech

**Answer: C.** In schizophrenia consciousness is clear and orientation is kept. Disorientation points to delirium or dementia.

### 2. A patient presents with acute symptoms of bizarre behaviour, delusions, hallucinations and disorganised speech. What is the most likely diagnosis?
- A. Schizophrenia
- B. Brief psychotic disorder
- C. Schizoaffective disorder
- D. Schizophreniform disorder
- E. Substance-induced psychosis

**Answer: B.** A sudden psychotic episode lasting less than a month is brief psychotic disorder. Schizophreniform disorder lasts 1 to 6 months, and schizophrenia more than 6 months. The duration is not given, so the question is loosely worded.

### 3. A 35-year-old lady reports hearing voices in her head. Which of the following differences between hallucination and pseudo-hallucination is true?
- A. In true hallucinations insight is often retained
- B. True hallucinations are often identified to be originating from self
- C. True hallucinations occur in subjective space
- D. True hallucinations are sought in other modalities by the patient
- E. True hallucinations cannot occur in two modalities simultaneously

**Answer: D.** A person with a true hallucination looks for the source and checks it with other senses. In pseudo-hallucinations the person knows it comes from inside the mind.

### 4. A young man has recently been diagnosed with paranoid schizophrenia and asks about his symptoms. All the following are negative symptoms of schizophrenia EXCEPT:
- A. Affective flattening
- B. Avolition
- C. Anhedonia
- D. Asociality
- E. Aggressiveness

**Answer: E.** Negative symptoms are a lack of normal function: flat affect, poverty of speech, avolition, anhedonia and social withdrawal. Aggression is not one.

### 5. A 24-year-old woman who gave birth 3 days ago behaves bizarrely on the obstetrics ward, accusing staff of spying on her and harming her baby. She has not slept for 24 hours and is overactive and aggressive. All the following are risk factors for postpartum psychosis EXCEPT:
- A. Primiparity
- B. Bipolar affective disorder
- C. Obstetric complications
- D. Gender of the child
- E. Single marital status

**Answer: D.** The sex of the baby has no effect. A history of bipolar disorder is the strongest risk factor, and a first baby, complications and being single add to it.

### 6. Haloperidol is more potent than chlorpromazine. The potency of a therapeutic formulation refers to:
- A. Strength of binding to receptors
- B. Duration of action at receptors
- C. Size of the dose required to produce an effect
- D. Elimination half-life of a drug
- E. Proportion of available receptors occupied by a drug

**Answer: C.** A more potent drug produces its effect at a lower dose. Potency is not the same as how well it works (efficacy).

### 7. Absorption of orally administered drugs is affected by which of the following?
- A. Intestinal transit
- B. Co-administered drugs
- C. P-glycoprotein
- D. Presence of food
- E. All of the above

**Answer: E.** Gut motility, other drugs, efflux pumps such as P-glycoprotein and food all change absorption.

### 8. Which of the following postpartum disorders is correctly matched with its time of onset?
- A. Postpartum blues: within a few months of delivery
- B. Postpartum depression: first week of delivery
- C. Postpartum psychosis: within 2 weeks of delivery
- D. Postpartum pituitary apoplexy: 12 months after delivery
- E. All of the above are correct

**Answer: C.** Postpartum psychosis starts abruptly in the first 2 weeks.

### 9. Two clinicians using the same checklist to aid clinical description come up with the same diagnosis. Which property of the checklist is involved in this outcome?
- A. Validity of the checklist
- B. Reliability of the checklist
- C. Sensitivity of the checklist
- D. Specificity of the checklist
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Agreement between different raters is reliability. Validity is whether it measures what it claims to.

### 10. By definition, the nature of delirium that differentiates it from dementia includes which of the following?
- A. Insidious onset
- B. Acute onset
- C. Deteriorating course
- D. Familial onset
- E. Irreversible progression

**Answer: B.** Delirium comes on over hours to days, fluctuates, and is usually reversible. Dementia is insidious and progressive.

### 11. The best option for preventing dementia available currently is:
- A. Regular NSAIDs
- B. Vitamin E
- C. Low salt diet
- D. Early retirement
- E. None of the above

**Answer: E.** No drug or supplement has been shown to prevent dementia. Control of vascular risk factors, exercise and mental activity may lower the risk.

### 12. Dementia secondary to which of the following is NOT reversible?
- A. Nutritional deficiencies
- B. Hypothyroidism
- C. Stroke
- D. Normal pressure hydrocephalus
- E. Depression

**Answer: C.** Vascular dementia after stroke has permanent brain damage. The other causes can improve with treatment.

### 13. A 45-year-old man develops auditory hallucinations that are initially fragmented but later turn into second-person derogatory voices. The most important aspect of personal history in this case is:
- A. Stimulant use
- B. Alcohol use
- C. Relationship difficulties
- D. Psychosexual history
- E. Employment history

**Answer: B.** Second-person, derogatory voices in clear consciousness are typical of alcoholic hallucinosis. Stimulant psychosis is also possible, so the question has more than one defensible option, but alcohol is the classic.

### 14. Which clinical feature of schizophrenia adds support to a neurodevelopmental hypothesis?
- A. Age of onset
- B. Stress-induced relapses
- C. Increased incidence among migrants
- D. Association with cannabis
- E. Response to antipsychotics

**Answer: A.** Onset in late adolescence and early adulthood, as the brain finishes its development, supports a neurodevelopmental origin.

### 15. A 32-year-old man has circumscribed areas of alopecia. He admits to recurrent pulling of his hair, especially at times of stress, and feels relief after the act. He has a normal IQ and no other stereotyped behaviour. The most appropriate diagnosis is:
- A. OCD
- B. Tourette syndrome
- C. Trichotillomania
- D. Autism
- E. Factitious disorder

**Answer: C.** Recurrent hair pulling with tension before and relief after is trichotillomania.

### 16. A 20-year-old man repeatedly cross-dresses in privacy. He experiences sexual arousal during cross-dressing but has a normal sexual relationship with his girlfriend otherwise. Which is the appropriate diagnosis?
- A. Disorder of gender identity
- B. Disorder of sexual preference
- C. Disorder of sexual orientation
- D. Disorder of chromosomal sex
- E. Sexual dysfunction of arousal phase

**Answer: B.** Fetishistic transvestism (cross-dressing for arousal, with no wish to change sex) is classified among the disorders of sexual preference.

### 17. A 19-year-old boy shows recent-onset avolition, flat affect and preoccupation with religion and philosophy. He preferred being solitary most of his childhood. The most probable diagnosis includes:
- A. Simple schizophrenia
- B. Paranoid schizophrenia
- C. Hebephrenic schizophrenia
- D. Residual schizophrenia
- E. Schizoaffective disorder

**Answer: C.** Hebephrenic schizophrenia starts in the teens with shallow affect, aimless behaviour, and preoccupation with abstract themes such as religion and philosophy, on a shy solitary personality. Simple schizophrenia is also possible, so the question is loosely worded.

### 18. Which of the following is the endocrine abnormality most commonly seen in depression?
- A. Hypercortisolaemia
- B. Hypocortisolaemia
- C. Hypothyroidism
- D. Hypopituitarism
- E. Hypoprolactinaemia

**Answer: A.** About half of patients with severe depression have a raised cortisol (a failure of dexamethasone suppression).

### 19. Which of the following endocrine abnormalities is suspected to be associated with rapid cycling bipolar disorder?
- A. Hypercortisolaemia
- B. Hypocortisolaemia
- C. Hypothyroidism
- D. Hypopituitarism
- E. Hyperprolactinaemia

**Answer: C.** Hypothyroidism (often lithium-induced) is linked to rapid cycling.

### 20. Which of the following is NOT a predictor of good outcome in schizophrenia?
- A. Florid positive symptoms at onset
- B. Prominent affective symptoms
- C. Acute onset
- D. Older age of onset
- E. Long first episode

**Answer: E.** A good outcome is predicted by acute onset, florid positive and affective symptoms, a later onset and good social support. A long first episode is a poor sign.

### 21. A 17-year-old girl is admitted to a medical unit following a prolonged period of repeated bingeing and vomiting. She induces vomiting at least six times a day but does not use laxatives or diuretics. Which laboratory finding is most likely?
- A. Low urea levels
- B. High potassium levels
- C. Low bicarbonate levels
- D. Increased thyroxine levels
- E. High amylase levels

**Answer: E.** Repeated vomiting raises the salivary amylase. It also causes low potassium (not high) and a high bicarbonate (a metabolic alkalosis).

### 22. Naloxone can be life-saving in cases of opioid toxicity. The commonest route of administration of naloxone for this purpose is:
- A. Subcutaneous
- B. Intramuscular
- C. Transtracheal
- D. Intrathecal
- E. Intravenous

**Answer: E.** In hospital the intravenous route gives the fastest effect. The intramuscular and intranasal routes are used when there is no vein, so the question has more than one defensible option.

### 23. The volume of distribution of a drug depends on all of the following EXCEPT:
- A. Protein binding
- B. Lipid solubility
- C. Tissue binding
- D. Half-life
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** The half-life depends on the volume of distribution and the clearance, and it does not determine the volume.

### 24. A patient with gastritis is prescribed several psychotropic medications. She wants to know which part of her body will absorb most of these orally administered drugs. The correct answer is:
- A. Oral mucosa
- B. Large intestine
- C. Oesophagus
- D. Stomach
- E. Small intestine

**Answer: E.** The small intestine has a very large surface area, so most oral drugs are absorbed there.

### 25. A 50-year-old woman being treated for depression presents with acute-onset, severe headache described as the worst headache of her life. She insists on switching off the lights. Her blood pressure is 150/90 mmHg, and she complains of neck stiffness. An emergency CT scan is normal. The next most appropriate step is:
- A. Change her antidepressant
- B. Obtain an MRI scan
- C. Obtain a urine drug screen
- D. Prepare for lumbar puncture
- E. Prescribe haloperidol 2.5 mg only

**Answer: D.** A thunderclap headache with neck stiffness and photophobia is a subarachnoid haemorrhage until proved otherwise. A normal CT does not exclude it, so a lumbar puncture (to look for xanthochromia) is needed.

### 26. Phineas Gage is a celebrated case in neuropsychiatry. This 25-year-old railroad foreman sustained an extraordinary brain injury, after which he had a significant change in his personality, characterised by childishness, stubborn and obstinate behaviour with frequent use of profanities. Which of the following brain regions was damaged significantly?
- A. Inferior parietal cortex
- B. Superior temporal cortex
- C. Orbitofrontal cortex
- D. Hippocampus
- E. Hypothalamus

**Answer: C.** Damage to the orbitofrontal and ventromedial frontal cortex causes disinhibition and a change in personality.

### 27. A 65-year-old woman believes that she is already dead. Similar delusions are reported in which of the following diseases?
- A. Schizophrenia
- B. Depressive psychosis
- C. Late-onset depression
- D. Organic disorders
- E. All of the above

**Answer: E.** Cotard's (nihilistic) delusion occurs in severe depression and in schizophrenia and organic brain disease.

### 29. Overvalued ideas are NOT noted in the core symptoms of which one of the following disorders?
- A. Body dysmorphic disorder
- B. Anorexia nervosa
- C. Morbid jealousy
- D. Trans-sexualism
- E. PTSD

**Answer: E.** In PTSD the core symptoms are re-experiencing, avoidance and hyperarousal. Overvalued ideas are central to body dysmorphic disorder, anorexia nervosa, morbid jealousy and the wish to change sex.

### 30. Which of the following is a psychopathology of familiarity?
- A. Deja vu
- B. Confabulation
- C. Pseudologia
- D. Parapraxis
- E. Paraphasia

**Answer: A.** Deja vu (a false feeling of having been here before) is a disorder of familiarity. Confabulation and pseudologia are disorders of memory and lying, and paraphasia is a language disorder.
