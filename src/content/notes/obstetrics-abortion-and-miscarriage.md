# Abortion and miscarriage — Year 4 Obstetrics and Gynaecology Notes

*Built from the two abortion lecture decks (the Department's "Abortions" slides and the "Abortion and Miscarriage" slides). Doses are left out on purpose: use your hospital protocol.*

**The reasoning chain:** bleeding in early pregnancy → is the pregnancy still alive → is the cervix open → has tissue passed → is she infected or bleeding heavily → empty the uterus safely if needed → look for the cause, especially if this is not the first loss → give anti-D and counselling.

## 1. Definitions

**Abortion** is the loss of a pregnancy **between conception and viability**. The cut-off differs: **WHO uses 20–22 weeks (or a fetus under 500 g); Kenya uses 28 weeks.** It may be **spontaneous (miscarriage)** or **induced**.

- **Miscarriage** is common: **10–20% of clinically recognised pregnancies**, and **about 75% occur before 16 weeks**.
- **Recurrent miscarriage** (habitual abortion) is **three or more consecutive** spontaneous losses, affecting about **1–2% of couples** (the lecture quotes 2–5%).

**Classification**

- **Spontaneous:** threatened, inevitable, incomplete, complete, **missed**, **septic**, recurrent.
- **Induced:** legal (therapeutic, under the law) or illegal/unsafe.

## 2. Causes of spontaneous abortion

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Fetal (about half of early losses)** | **Chromosomal abnormalities** (50% of early miscarriages): trisomies (the commonest is trisomy 16), triploidy, monosomy X; structural defects; **multiple pregnancy**; degeneration of villi |
| **Maternal: endocrine and metabolic (10–15%)** | **Poorly controlled diabetes**, thyroid disease, luteal phase defect, PCOS (raised LH) |
| **Maternal: anatomical (10–15%)** | **Müllerian anomalies** (septate, bicornuate, double uterus), **submucous fibroids**, polyps, **intrauterine adhesions (Asherman syndrome)**, **cervical incompetence** |
| **Infections (about 5%)** | **TORCH** (toxoplasma, rubella, CMV, herpes), HIV, **syphilis**, malaria, *Ureaplasma*, *Chlamydia*, brucellosis, listeria |
| **Immunological (5–10%)** | **Antiphospholipid syndrome** (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin antibodies), alloimmune factors |
| **Environmental and lifestyle** | **Smoking, alcohol, drugs**, heavy caffeine, some drugs, radiation |
| **Other maternal illness** | Cyanotic heart disease, haemoglobinopathies (sickle cell), kidney disease, SLE |
| **Advancing maternal age** | The risk rises from under 2% below 30 to above 10% over 40 and about 50% at 45 (more chromosomal errors) |
| **Unexplained** | **40–60%** |

## 3. The five types and how to tell them apart

| Type | Bleeding and pain | Cervical os | Tissue passed | Ultrasound | Plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Threatened** | Small bleed, little or no pain | **Closed** | None | **Viable** pregnancy (heartbeat) | Rest and reassure; 80% reach term |
| **Inevitable** | **Heavy bleed, colicky pain** | **Open** | None yet | Pregnancy in the uterus, may be failing | Empty the uterus |
| **Incomplete** | Persistent bleeding and pain | **Open** | **Some tissue passed, some retained** | Retained products | Evacuate |
| **Complete** | **Pain and bleeding settle** | **Closed** | **All passed** | **Empty uterus** | Observe, support |
| **Missed** | Often none, or old brown spotting; symptoms of pregnancy fade | **Closed** | None | **No heartbeat**, or an empty sac | Medical or surgical evacuation |
| **Septic** | Fever, pain, foul discharge | Open, purulent | May be retained | Retained products | Antibiotics then evacuate |

### Threatened abortion

**Definition:** the process has started, but recovery is still possible. **History:** small, often painless bleeding (mild back or lower abdominal ache), pregnancy symptoms still present. **Examination:** uterus **size equals dates**, cervix **soft with a closed os**.

**Ultrasound** is diagnostic (a live intrauterine pregnancy); **avoid a pelvic examination** when ultrasound is available. **Differentials:** cervical ectopy, polyp, cervical cancer, **ectopic pregnancy**, **molar pregnancy**.

**Management and outlook:** rest until the bleeding stops, simple pain relief, reassurance, and **anti-D if she is rhesus-negative**. **About 80% of threatened abortions continue to term**; with a **live fetus on ultrasound, over 95% continue**. Pregnancies that continue carry **more preterm labour, placenta praevia and growth restriction**.

### Inevitable abortion

Bleeding is **heavier**, pain **colicky**, and the **internal os is open** with products felt at it. The pregnancy cannot continue. **Aims:** speed up the expulsion and keep **strict asepsis**. **Under 12 weeks:** suction evacuation. **Over 12 weeks:** oxytocin or prostaglandin to induce expulsion. Control heavy bleeding with **uterotonics**, give **fluids and blood** as needed.

### Incomplete abortion

Some tissue has passed, **some remains**. Bleeding and pain **persist**, the uterus is **smaller than dates** and the **os is open**. **Retained products** cause **bleeding, sepsis and placental polyp**. **Treatment:** evacuate the retained products (suction curettage or **manual vacuum aspiration, MVA**; **misoprostol** is an alternative in early cases), give **prophylactic antibiotics**, and send tissue for histology.

### Complete abortion

All the tissue has gone. Pain stops, bleeding becomes trace or absent, the **os is closed**, and **ultrasound shows an empty uterus**. Be sure it was not an **ectopic pregnancy** with a pseudosac.

### Missed abortion

The fetus is **dead and retained**. The **uterus is smaller than dates**, the **os is closed**, **pregnancy symptoms fade** and the pregnancy test turns negative. **Ultrasound:** a **crown-rump length of 7 mm or more without a heartbeat**, or an **empty sac above 25 mm** (an **anembryonic pregnancy**). **Complications:** **sepsis** and rarely **DIC**, especially over 16 weeks. **Treatment:** **misoprostol** (expulsion in about 48 hours) or **suction evacuation / D&E**; later in pregnancy, repeated misoprostol with antibiotics.

### Septic abortion

**Any abortion with clinical infection of the uterus and its contents:** fever (38 °C or more for 24 hours), **offensive purulent discharge**, lower abdominal pain and tenderness. **Commonest cause: an unsafe induced abortion** by an untrained person; spontaneous cases are usually mild.

| Grade | Spread |
|---|---|
| **I** | Infection **confined to the uterus** (commonest, usually spontaneous) |
| **II** | Spread to the **parametrium, tubes, ovaries or pelvic peritoneum** |
| **III** | **Generalised peritonitis, endotoxic shock, jaundice or acute kidney injury** |

**Clues to spread:** a pulse above 100 to 120 matters more than fever. **Examination:** tender uterus, open os, foul discharge; guarding means peritonitis.

**Investigations:** full blood count, **urea, creatinine and electrolytes**, **high vaginal swab**, **blood cultures**, **pelvic ultrasound** (retained products), **abdominal X-ray** if bowel injury is suspected (free air), chest X-ray for breathing difficulty.

**Complications:** haemorrhage, **injury to the uterus, bowel and bladder**, **peritonitis, endotoxic shock (often *E. coli*)**, **DIC**, **acute kidney injury**, thrombophlebitis and death.

**Management:** **resuscitate** (oxygen, **IV crystalloid**, blood), **broad-spectrum IV antibiotics** (a combination such as **ampicillin, gentamicin and metronidazole** is typical), and **evacuate the uterus within 4 to 6 hours of starting antibiotics**. Laparotomy for bowel injury or uncontrolled sepsis. Give **tetanus prophylaxis** after an unsafe abortion and counsel on contraception.

## 4. Investigations: tie each to a question

| Test | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Transvaginal ultrasound** | Is the pregnancy in the uterus, alive, and complete? |
| **β-hCG** (serial) | Is the level rising or falling appropriately? (Ectopic or molar pregnancy?) |
| **Full blood count, group and crossmatch, rhesus** | How much blood lost? Does she need anti-D? |
| **Blood cultures, high vaginal swab, urea and creatinine** | Septic abortion and its effect on the kidneys |
| **Chromosomes of the products** | Recurrent loss: was it a chromosomal error? |

## 5. Recurrent miscarriage: find the cause

Three or more consecutive losses. **No cause is found in about 50%.** Work it up:

- **Genetic (3–5%):** parental **balanced translocation** (risk of loss over 25%; the commonest cause of first-trimester loss). **Karyotype both parents.**
- **Antiphospholipid syndrome (about 15%):** **lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin and anti-β2 glycoprotein antibodies** on two occasions 12 weeks apart. **Treat with low-dose aspirin and heparin in the next pregnancy.**
- **Endocrine:** diabetes (control it), **thyroid antibodies and thyroid function**, PCOS.
- **Inherited thrombophilia:** protein C and S deficiency, factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation.
- **Infections:** syphilis, TORCH.
- **Uterine factors:** **septate or double uterus** (hysteroscopic resection of the septum), **Asherman syndrome** (hysteroscopic division of adhesions), **submucous fibroids** (myomectomy). **Transvaginal or 3D ultrasound** and **hysterosalpingography** or **hysteroscopy** make the diagnosis.
- **Cervical insufficiency:** see below.

### Second-trimester loss and cervical insufficiency

**Painless dilatation of the cervix** with ballooning of the membranes into the vagina, rupture and expulsion of a live-looking fetus, usually at **16 to 24 weeks**. **Causes:** congenital weakness and uterine anomalies; **acquired** (commoner) after **D&C, dilatation and evacuation, difficult operative deliveries through an undilated cervix, and cone biopsy or cervical amputation**; multiple pregnancy and previous preterm birth.

**Diagnosis:** a history of **repeated painless mid-trimester losses**. **Outside pregnancy:** a **Hegar dilator (number 6 to 8) passes the internal os without resistance**, or **funnelling** on hysterosalpingography. **In pregnancy:** **transvaginal ultrasound shows a cervix under 25 mm with funnelling**.

**Treatment: cervical cerclage** (a non-absorbable tape or stitch around the cervix at the level of the internal os), usually at **12 to 14 weeks**, with 80–90% success.

- **History-indicated:** three or more previous second-trimester losses.
- **Ultrasound-indicated:** short cervix or funnelling after one or two losses.
- **Rescue (emergency) cerclage:** the cervix is already dilated.
- **Methods:** **McDonald** (purse-string, commonest and simplest), **modified Shirodkar** (submucosal tape, via an anterior vaginal incision), **transabdominal** (rare; for repeated failure).
- **Contraindications:** intrauterine infection, ruptured membranes, bleeding, uterine irritability, cervical dilatation over 4 cm.

## 6. After a miscarriage

- **Anti-D immunoglobulin** to every **rhesus-negative** woman.
- **Contraception** and counselling about when to try again.
- **Support:** grief is real and often neglected. Offer follow-up and a clear explanation: most losses are **not caused by anything the mother did**.
- **Recurrent loss** needs an investigation clinic, not just reassurance.

## Practice questions

**1.** A 24-year-old at 9 weeks has a small painless bleed. The cervical os is closed and ultrasound shows a fetal heartbeat. The diagnosis is:
A. Missed abortion · B. Inevitable abortion · **C. Threatened abortion** · D. Incomplete abortion · E. Ectopic pregnancy
*Answer: C.*

**2.** The commonest cause of early (first-trimester) miscarriage is:
A. Cervical incompetence · B. Infection · **C. Chromosomal abnormality of the fetus** · D. Diabetes · E. Uterine septum
*Answer: C.*

**3.** Painless dilatation of the cervix at 20 weeks with expulsion of a live-looking fetus suggests:
A. Threatened abortion · B. Missed abortion · **C. Cervical insufficiency** · D. Placenta praevia · E. Molar pregnancy
*Answer: C. Treat the next pregnancy with a cervical cerclage at 12–14 weeks.*

**4.** After an unsafe abortion a woman has fever, tender uterus, a pulse of 120 and a foul vaginal discharge. The most important management is:
A. Reassurance · B. Oral antibiotics only · **C. Resuscitation, IV broad-spectrum antibiotics and evacuation of the uterus** · D. Hysterectomy at once · E. Bed rest
*Answer: C.*

**5.** Which test is most useful in a woman with three consecutive first-trimester miscarriages and a history of a deep vein thrombosis?
A. Pap smear · **B. Antiphospholipid antibodies (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin)** · C. HIV test only · D. Ultrasound of the kidneys · E. Serum ferritin
*Answer: B.*
