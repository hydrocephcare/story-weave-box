# Diagnosis of pregnancy — Year 4 Obstetrics and Gynaecology Notes

*Based on the Department lecture (Dr Ritho). The point on the ward: confirm the pregnancy, say how far along it is, and be sure where it is.*

**The reasoning chain:** symptoms and signs raise the suspicion → a pregnancy test confirms → ultrasound shows it is in the uterus, alive, and how many weeks → think of what else can look like pregnancy.

## 1. Trimesters

- **First trimester:** the first 12 weeks
- **Second trimester:** 13 to 28 weeks
- **Third trimester:** 29 to 40 weeks

## 2. First trimester: symptoms

| Symptom | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Amenorrhoea** | The commonest symptom: a sudden stop of regular periods. It can also occur in lactation, illness, stress or endocrine disease; and **bleeding can occur in early pregnancy** (threatened abortion, or slight bleeding at the expected period in the first 12 weeks, **Hartman's sign**) |
| **Morning sickness** | Nausea with or without vomiting, about **6 weeks** after the LMP, usually gone by about **12–14 weeks** |
| **Frequency of micturition** | From congestion and pressure on the bladder; it returns late in pregnancy when the head descends |
| **Breast symptoms** | Enlargement, fullness, tingling and tenderness |
| **Appetite changes, sleepiness, fatigue** | Common |

## 3. First trimester: signs

### Breast signs

Increased **size and vascularity**, increased **pigmentation of the nipple and primary areola**, a **secondary areola**, **Montgomery's tubercles**, and **colostrum** that can be expressed. **Breast signs are only diagnostic in a first pregnancy**, because earlier pregnancies leave similar changes.

### Pelvic and uterine signs

| Sign | What you find |
|---|---|
| **Uterine enlargement** | Enlarged, globular, soft; remains a pelvic organ until **12 weeks**, when it can just be felt above the symphysis |
| **Hegar's sign** | On bimanual examination, the two fingers in the anterior fornix can nearly touch the abdominal hand behind the **softened lower uterine segment** (6–10 weeks) |
| **Palmer's sign** | Rhythmic uterine contractions felt on bimanual examination |
| **Jacquemier's (Chadwick's) sign** | **Bluish or violet** discolouration of the vestibule and anterior vaginal wall, from vascular congestion (about **8 weeks**) |
| **Osiander's sign** | Increased **pulsation felt through the lateral fornices** |
| **Goodell's sign** | **Softening of the cervix** from about **6 weeks** ("like the lips of the mouth", not "the tip of the nose") with bluish colour on speculum examination |
| **Vagina** | Violet, moist and warm with more discharge and increased acidity |

## 4. Investigations: pregnancy tests

**Human chorionic gonadotrophin (hCG)** is made by the **trophoblast** only, so it appears only in pregnancy (and in trophoblastic disease). It is **detectable in maternal serum about 8 to 11 days after conception**, rises, **peaks at 8 to 12 weeks**, then falls to a plateau.

| Test | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Urine pregnancy test (dipstick)** | Rapid, simple, based on enzyme-labelled monoclonal antibodies; detects low hCG from about **the time of the missed period**. **False negatives** (too early, dilute urine, hook effect in very high hCG) and **false positives** (some drugs, hCG-secreting tumours, recent miscarriage) occur |
| **Latex agglutination (inhibition) tests** | The older form |
| **Serum β-hCG (quantitative)** | More sensitive and can follow the **rise** (doubling about every 48 hours early in a normal pregnancy). Essential for **ectopic pregnancy, molar pregnancy and threatened miscarriage** |
| **ELISA and radio-immunoassay** | Laboratory methods |

### Ultrasound: what you see and when

Counted from the **last menstrual period (weeks of amenorrhoea)**:

| Finding | When |
|---|---|
| **Gestational sac** | About **4 to 5 weeks** (transvaginal) |
| **Yolk sac** | About **5 weeks** |
| **Fetal pole and cardiac activity** | About **6 weeks** |
| **Embryonic movements** | About **7 weeks** |
| **Fetal heart by Doppler** | About **10 weeks** |

**Ultrasound also tells you where the pregnancy is** (excluding ectopic), **how many embryos there are**, **how old the pregnancy is** (crown-rump length in the first trimester) and whether it is alive.

## 5. Second trimester (13 to 28 weeks)

- **Symptoms:** continued amenorrhoea, **morning sickness and urinary frequency settle**, **quickening** (the mother first feels the baby move) at **18–20 weeks in a first pregnancy** and **16–18 weeks if she has delivered before**, and abdominal enlargement.
- **Skin signs:** **chloasma (melasma), linea nigra and striae gravidarum**.
- **Breast signs** become obvious.
- **Uterine signs:** the uterus is felt through the abdomen; **Braxton Hicks contractions** (painless, intermittent, irregular tightening) are felt on palpation.
- **Fetal signs:** **internal ballottement** at about **16 weeks** (tap the fetal part through the anterior fornix and feel it rebound) and **external ballottement** at about **20 weeks**; **fetal movements and fetal parts** are felt; **the fetal heart** is heard with a **Pinard stethoscope at about 20 weeks** and by Doppler from about 10–12 weeks.

## 6. Third trimester (29 to 40 weeks)

The fundus rises to the **xiphisternum at term (36 weeks)** and may **drop (lightening)** as the head engages in a first pregnancy. **Leopold's manoeuvres** show lie, presentation and position; **fetal movements and heart sounds** are clear; **ultrasound** estimates growth and well-being. Reduced fetal movement is an **urgent symptom**.

## 7. Summary of the signs

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Presumptive** (felt by the woman; many causes) | Amenorrhoea, nausea and vomiting, breast changes, frequency, fatigue, quickening, skin changes |
| **Probable** (found by the examiner; other causes possible) | Enlarged uterus, Hegar's, Goodell's, Chadwick's signs, Braxton Hicks contractions, ballottement, **positive pregnancy test** |
| **Positive (certain)** | **Fetal heart heard or seen, fetal movements felt by the examiner, fetal parts palpated, and ultrasound of the fetus** |

## 8. What else can mimic pregnancy?

- **Pseudocyesis** (false pregnancy): amenorrhoea, a swollen abdomen and the belief of being pregnant, with a **negative test**.
- **Hydatidiform mole** and other **trophoblastic disease** (positive test, but the uterus is larger than dates and the scan shows no fetus).
- **Fibroids, ovarian tumours**, **ascites**, a full bladder and **obesity**.
- **Secondary amenorrhoea** from PCOS, thyroid disease, hyperprolactinaemia, premature ovarian failure, stress or weight change.

## Practice questions

**1.** Bluish discolouration of the vestibule and anterior vaginal wall in early pregnancy is:
A. Hegar's sign · B. Goodell's sign · C. Osiander's sign · D. Palmer's sign · **E. Jacquemier's (Chadwick's) sign**
*Answer: E.*

**2.** Softening of the cervix from about 6 weeks is:
A. Chadwick's sign · **B. Goodell's sign** · C. Hegar's sign · D. Piskacek's sign · E. Ladin's sign
*Answer: B.*

**3.** Which of the following is a POSITIVE (certain) sign of pregnancy?
A. Amenorrhoea · B. Positive urine test · **C. Fetal heart seen on ultrasound** · D. Breast tenderness · E. Enlarged uterus
*Answer: C.*

**4.** Quickening in a woman in her first pregnancy occurs at about:
A. 8–10 weeks · B. 12–14 weeks · C. 24–26 weeks · **D. 18–20 weeks** · E. 30 weeks
*Answer: D.*

**5.** A woman has amenorrhoea, a large uterus for dates and a very high hCG with no fetus on ultrasound. The diagnosis is:
**A. Hydatidiform mole** · B. Twin pregnancy · C. Ectopic pregnancy · D. Pseudocyesis · E. Missed abortion
*Answer: A.*
