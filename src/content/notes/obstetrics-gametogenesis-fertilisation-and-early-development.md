# Gametogenesis, fertilisation and the first two weeks of development

*From the Department's gametogenesis and early embryology slides (photographed), rewritten as a clinical story: how the gametes are made, how they meet, and how the embryo implants. The clinical payoff is infertility, ectopic pregnancy, molar pregnancy and early pregnancy loss.*

**The reasoning chain:** meiosis halves the chromosomes (46 → 23) in the testis and the ovary → one sperm penetrates one secondary oocyte in the ampulla of the tube → the zygote cleaves and becomes a blastocyst → the blastocyst implants in the posterior upper wall of the uterus by about day 7 → the bilaminar disc forms in week 2 and the placental circulation begins. **A fault at any step gives infertility, an ectopic pregnancy or an early loss.**

## 1. Gametogenesis: making sperm and eggs

**Gametogenesis** converts diploid germ cells (46 chromosomes, 2N) into haploid gametes (23, 1N) by **meiosis**. The primordial germ cells arise in the **wall of the yolk sac** in week 4 and reach the gonadal ridges by week 6.

### Male: spermatogenesis (from puberty, continuous)

Three phases:

1. **Spermatocytogenesis:** at puberty, primordial germ cells become **type A spermatogonia (46, 2N)**. Some type A cells divide by **mitosis** to keep a **continuous stem-cell supply for the whole reproductive life**; others become **type B spermatogonia (46, 2N)**, which enter meiosis as **primary spermatocytes**.
2. **Meiosis:** a primary spermatocyte (46, 4C) completes **meiosis I** to form **two secondary spermatocytes (23, 2N)**, and **meiosis II** to give **four haploid spermatids (23, 1N)**.
3. **Spermiogenesis:** each spermatid **matures into a sperm** (acrosome forms, nucleus condenses, tail develops, excess cytoplasm is shed). **No cell division** occurs here.

The whole process takes about **64–74 days** in the seminiferous tubules, supported by **Sertoli cells** (blood-testis barrier, nutrition) and driven by **FSH, LH and testosterone from Leydig cells**. **Sperm are stored and matured in the epididymis** (about 2 weeks) and gain motility there. **One primary spermatocyte gives four sperm.**

### Female: oogenesis (starts before birth, finishes at fertilisation)

- **Fetal life:** oogonia multiply by mitosis, become **primary oocytes**, and **start meiosis I but stop in prophase I (diplotene) from about month 5 of fetal life until puberty.** About **7 million** germ cells at 20 weeks fall to about **1–2 million at birth** and **about 400,000 at puberty**; only **about 400–500** are ovulated.
- **At puberty:** each cycle **5–15 primary oocytes begin to mature, usually only one completes maturation.**
- **Just before ovulation (triggered by the LH surge):** the primary oocyte **completes meiosis I**, giving a **secondary oocyte (23, 2N)** and the **first polar body**, which degenerates. The secondary oocyte starts **meiosis II** and **stops at metaphase II**.
- **Meiosis II is completed only if a sperm penetrates**, producing the **mature ovum** and the **second polar body**.
- **One primary oocyte gives one ovum** (and three polar bodies). **Advancing maternal age** raises the chance of **non-disjunction** (for example **trisomy 21**), because the oocyte has been arrested for decades.

- **Starts**: At puberty — In fetal life
- **Meiotic arrest**: None — Prophase I (until puberty), then metaphase II (until fertilisation)
- **Product of one germ cell**: **4 sperm** — **1 ovum + polar bodies**
- **Duration**: Continuous, 64–74 days — Years, and ends at fertilisation
- **Cell supply**: Renewable (stem cells) — **Fixed at birth**

## 2. Week 1: fertilisation, cleavage and the blastocyst

**Where:** in the **ampulla of the uterine tube**, within **12–24 hours of ovulation**. Sperm remain able to fertilise for **about 3–5 days**.

**Three phases of fertilisation**

1. **Penetration of the corona radiata** with the help of the **sperm's enzymes and tail movements** (**capacitation** in the female tract first).
2. **Binding and penetration of the zona pellucida:** sperm **glycosyltransferases bind to ZP3 receptors**; binding triggers the **acrosome reaction** (fusion of the outer acrosomal and the sperm cell membranes, releasing **acrosin and other enzymes**), which digests a path through the zona.
3. **Fusion of the sperm and oocyte membranes** triggers the **cortical reaction**: **cortical granules (lysosomes)** release enzymes that **change the zona** and **inactivate the sperm receptors**. This is the **zona (polyspermy) block**: **no second sperm can enter**.

**Results of fertilisation:** the secondary oocyte **completes meiosis II**; the **male and female pronuclei fuse** into the **zygote (46)**; **sex is determined** (an X-bearing sperm gives a girl, a Y-bearing sperm a boy); **cleavage begins**.

**Clinical points:** failure to fertilise causes infertility (IVF and ICSI bypass it). **Dispermy gives a triploid conception** (partial mole). **An empty egg fertilised by a duplicated sperm gives a complete mole (46,XX of paternal origin).**

**Cleavage and the blastocyst:** the zygote divides (2, 4, 8 cells) with **no growth**, forms the **morula (16 cells, about day 3)** and becomes a **blastocyst (about day 4–5)**, with an **inner cell mass (embryoblast)** and an **outer trophoblast**; it **hatches from the zona pellucida**. **If the zona is defective or the tube is damaged, the embryo may implant in the tube (ectopic pregnancy).**

## 3. Implantation (about day 6–7)

- The blastocyst **implants in the endometrium, usually in the posterior superior wall of the uterus, about day 7 after fertilisation.**
- It happens in the **functional layer of the endometrium** during the **secretory (progestational) phase** of the cycle.
- **Trophoblast differentiates into two layers:** the **cytotrophoblast** (inner, mitotic) and the **syncytiotrophoblast** (outer, multinucleated, invasive; it **secretes hCG**).
- **hCG** from the syncytiotrophoblast **keeps the corpus luteum** producing progesterone, which is why **pregnancy tests turn positive about 8–10 days after fertilisation**.
- **Clinical:** implantation low in the uterus gives **placenta praevia**; implantation in the tube, ovary, cervix or caesarean scar gives an **ectopic pregnancy**; **bleeding at implantation** can be mistaken for a light period.

## 4. Week 2 (days 8 to 14)

### The embryoblast: the bilaminar disc

- It splits into two layers: a **dorsal epiblast** (columnar cells) and a **ventral hypoblast** (cuboidal cells). Together they form a flat oval disc, the **bilaminar embryonic disc**.
- **Clefts appear in the epiblast and join to form the amniotic cavity.**
- **Hypoblast cells migrate** and line the inner surface of the cytotrophoblast to form the **exocoelomic (Heuser's) membrane**, which encloses the **exocoelomic cavity (primitive yolk sac)**.
- At the future site of the mouth, hypoblast cells become columnar and fuse with the epiblast to form a **circular midline thickening, the prochordal plate**.

### The trophoblast and the start of the placental circulation

- The **syncytiotrophoblast invades the endometrium**, eroding **blood vessels and glands**.
- **Lacunae** appear in the syncytiotrophoblast and fill with **maternal blood and glandular secretions**.
- **Decidual cells** at the implantation site fill with **glycogen and lipids** and nourish the embryoblast.
- The lacunae fuse into a **lacunar network**, and **maternal blood flows through it**, establishing the **early uteroplacental circulation** (about **day 12**). At this stage the embryo is fed by **diffusion** only.
- By the end of week 2 **primary chorionic villi** begin to form.

**Clinical pearls**

- **The first two weeks are the "all-or-none" period:** a toxic insult either kills the embryo or leaves it unharmed; **structural malformations arise from weeks 3–8**.
- **Failure of the trophoblast to invade properly (week 2 and later)** underlies **pre-eclampsia**; **over-invasion** gives **placenta accreta**.
- **Chromosomal errors** at fertilisation are the commonest cause of **early miscarriage** (see the notes on abortion).

**1.** In a woman, primary oocytes are arrested in:
A. Prophase of meiosis II · B. Metaphase of meiosis I · **C. Prophase (diplotene) of meiosis I** · D. Anaphase of mitosis · E. Interphase
*Answer: C. They stay arrested from fetal life until the LH surge.*

**2.** Meiosis II of the oocyte is completed:
A. At birth · **B. When a sperm penetrates the secondary oocyte** · C. At puberty · D. At ovulation · E. At implantation
*Answer: B.*

**3.** The usual site of fertilisation is:
A. The uterine cavity · B. The cervix · C. The ovary · **D. The ampulla of the uterine tube** · E. The fimbriae
*Answer: D.*

**4.** The cortical reaction:
A. Starts the acrosome reaction · B. Forms the morula · C. Produces hCG · D. Forms the amniotic cavity · **E. Blocks polyspermy by altering the zona pellucida**
*Answer: E.*

**5.** The cells of the trophoblast that make hCG are the:
**A. Syncytiotrophoblast** · B. Cytotrophoblast · C. Epiblast · D. Hypoblast · E. Decidual cells
*Answer: A.*
