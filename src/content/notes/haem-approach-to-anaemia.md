# Approach to anaemia — Year 4 Internal Medicine (Haematology) Notes

*Clinical reasoning notes. Anaemia is a sign, not a diagnosis: always ask why.*

**The reasoning chain:** confirm anaemia → classify by red cell size (MCV) → decide whether the marrow is making too few cells or the body is losing or destroying them → find the cause (including the local ones: malaria, hookworm, HIV, sickle cell, TB) → treat the cause and replace what is missing.


![Human blood from a case of iron deficiency anemia Giemsa stain. Dr Graham Beards, CC BY-SA 3.0](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/Iron_deficiency_anemia_blood_film.jpg/960px-Iron_deficiency_anemia_blood_film.jpg)

## 1. Define

Anaemia is a **haemoglobin below normal for age and sex**: roughly below **13 g/dL in men, 12 g/dL in women, 11 g/dL in pregnancy and young children** (WHO). Symptoms: tiredness, breathlessness, palpitations, headache, pallor, and angina or heart failure when severe.

## 2. Classify by MCV, then by mechanism

| Type | MCV | Common causes |
|---|---|---|
| **Microcytic** | below 80 fL | **Iron deficiency** (commonest), **thalassaemia**, anaemia of chronic disease, sideroblastic anaemia, lead |
| **Normocytic** | 80–100 fL | Anaemia of chronic disease, **acute blood loss**, **haemolysis**, **chronic kidney disease**, **marrow failure**, mixed deficiencies |
| **Macrocytic** | above 100 fL | **Vitamin B12 or folate deficiency**, alcohol, hypothyroidism, liver disease, drugs (zidovudine, methotrexate, hydroxycarbamide), myelodysplasia |

**Then ask: is the reticulocyte count high or low?**

- **High:** the marrow is responding: **blood loss or haemolysis**.
- **Low:** the marrow is **not making enough**: deficiency, chronic disease, marrow failure or infiltration.

## 3. Investigate with purpose

| Test | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Full haemogram with MCV and red cell indices** | Is it anaemia, and what type? Are other lines affected? |
| **Blood film** | Hypochromic cells (iron deficiency), target cells (thalassaemia), sickle cells, spherocytes, schistocytes, parasites, blasts |
| **Reticulocyte count** | Is the marrow responding? |
| **Ferritin, iron studies** | Iron deficiency (low ferritin); a **raised ferritin can hide deficiency** in inflammation |
| **B12 and folate** | Macrocytic anaemia |
| **Bilirubin, LDH, haptoglobin, direct Coombs test** | Haemolysis? Autoimmune? |
| **Haemoglobin electrophoresis** | Sickle cell disease, thalassaemia |
| **Malaria test, HIV test, stool for ova (hookworm)** | Local causes |
| **Urea, creatinine, liver and thyroid tests** | Chronic kidney, liver or thyroid disease |
| **Bone marrow aspirate and biopsy** | Marrow failure, infiltration, myelodysplasia, leukaemia, myeloma |
| **Endoscopy, colonoscopy, or stool occult blood** | **In an adult with iron deficiency, find the bleeding site** until proved otherwise |

**Where results can mislead:** **ferritin is an acute-phase reactant**, so a "normal" level in an inflamed patient may still mean deficiency (use transferrin saturation); the **haemoglobin falls late after an acute bleed** (the concentration is unchanged at first); **mixed deficiencies** (iron plus B12) can give a normal MCV.

## 4. Causes by mechanism: the local list

- **Blood loss:** hookworm and schistosomiasis, peptic ulcer, heavy menstrual bleeding, postpartum haemorrhage, colorectal cancer.
- **Haemolysis:** **malaria** (including blackwater fever), **sickle cell disease**, G6PD deficiency (after drugs such as primaquine, dapsone or fava beans), autoimmune haemolysis, thalassaemia.
- **Under-production:** iron, folate or B12 deficiency, **HIV**, TB, chronic kidney disease, malignancy, aplastic anaemia.

## 5. Management

1. **Treat the cause** (deworm, stop the bleeding, treat malaria and infection, start ART, treat the underlying disease).
2. **Replace what is missing:**
   - **Iron deficiency:** oral iron (continue 3 months after the haemoglobin normalises, to refill stores); IV iron when oral iron fails or is not tolerated, in pregnancy, or in kidney disease.
   - **B12 deficiency:** B12 injections (oral for dietary lack only). **Give folate only if B12 is replaced or absent**, or neurological damage can worsen.
   - **Folate deficiency:** folic acid.
3. **Transfuse only when needed:** severe anaemia with **heart failure, shock, ongoing bleeding, or very low haemoglobin with symptoms**. **In chronic anaemia, transfuse slowly** (with a diuretic) to avoid overload.
4. **Special groups:** sickle cell disease (hydroxycarbamide, folate, vaccines, prompt treatment of crises), pregnancy (iron and folate supplements), chronic kidney disease (erythropoietin).

## 6. When to worry

Anaemia **with fever, bleeding, bruising or abnormal white cells or platelets** suggests **leukaemia or marrow failure**: get a film and urgent haematology help.

## Practice questions

**1.** A woman has MCV 65 fL, low ferritin and heavy periods. The most likely diagnosis is:
**A. Iron deficiency anaemia** · B. Thalassaemia trait · C. Anaemia of chronic disease · D. B12 deficiency · E. Aplastic anaemia
*Answer: A.*

**2.** Raised reticulocytes, raised indirect bilirubin and low haptoglobin indicate:
A. Iron deficiency · **B. Haemolysis** · C. Marrow failure · D. B12 deficiency · E. Chronic kidney disease
*Answer: B.*

**3.** A man of 62 has a haemoglobin of 8 g/dL and iron deficiency. The most important next step is:
A. Iron tablets only · B. Folate · **C. Look for a source of gastrointestinal blood loss** · D. Blood transfusion · E. Reassure
*Answer: C.*

**4.** Which drug can cause haemolysis in G6PD deficiency?
A. Amoxicillin · B. Paracetamol · C. Amlodipine · **D. Primaquine** · E. Omeprazole
*Answer: D.*
