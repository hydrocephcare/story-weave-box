# Hepatitis B and the approach to hepatitis

*Clinical reasoning notes. Treatment thresholds change with new guidance: check your current national guidelines before treating.*

**The reasoning chain:** what is the pattern of liver injury → what is the cause (virus, drug, autoimmune, alcohol) → which virus and which phase → does this patient need treatment → what could go wrong later (cirrhosis, liver cancer).

## 1. Presentation

Jaundice, fatigue, right upper quadrant pain, nausea and vomiting, fever, dark urine and pale stools. Many patients, especially with chronic hepatitis B, have **no symptoms** until the liver is badly scarred.

## Anatomy and physiology you need  

- **Hepatocytes – main parenchymal cells, contain the sodium‑taurocholate cotransporting polypeptide (NTCP) that HBV uses as its entry receptor**: HBV binds to NTCP on the basolateral membrane → internalisation of the nucleocapsid — Damage to hepatocytes raises ALT/AST; loss of synthetic function (albumin, clotting factors) signals advanced disease
- **Sinusoids – low‑pressure capillary network lined by fenestrated endothelium and Kupffer cells**: Allows free passage of virions from portal blood to hepatocytes; Kupffer cells produce cytokines that modulate viral clearance — Inflammation of sinusoids (lobular hepatitis) produces the “ground‑glass” appearance on biopsy and correlates with ALT spikes
- **Bile canaliculi – tiny ducts that drain bile into the intra‑hepatic bile ducts**: HBV does not infect cholangiocytes, but cholestasis can develop from hepatocellular swelling that compresses canaliculi — Predominant cholestatic LFT pattern (↑ALP, GGT) suggests an alternative or superimposed pathology
- **Portal triad (portal vein, hepatic artery, bile duct) – supplies blood and bile outflow**: Portal inflow delivers HBV‑containing lymphocytes; arterial supply sustains immune surveillance — Fibrosis begins in the portal tract; progression to bridging fibrosis and cirrhosis is staged by the extent of portal‑centric scarring
- **Liver‑associated lymphoid tissue (LALT) – intra‑hepatic CD8⁺ T‑cells, NK cells, dendritic cells**: Cytotoxic T‑cells recognise HBV core antigen presented on HLA‑I; successful clearance requires a robust CD8 response — Weak cellular immunity → chronic infection; high HBV DNA despite normal ALT suggests immune tolerance phase
- **Hepatic stellate cells (Ito cells) – quiescent vitamin‑A‑storing cells that become myofibroblasts when activated**: Persistent inflammation releases TGF‑β, PDGF → activation of stellate cells → collagen deposition — Fibrosis progression is the key determinant of treatment indication and prognosis
- **Spleen & reticulo‑endothelial system – filters blood, produces anti‑HBs antibodies after vaccination**: Provides the source of anti‑HBs after successful immunisation or natural recovery — Presence of anti‑HBs with anti‑HBc IgG confirms immunity after infection; anti‑HBs alone indicates vaccine‑derived immunity

## Pathophysiology  

**HBV infection → intracellular events → host response → clinical picture**

1. **Virion attachment** → HBV surface antigen (HBsAg) binds NTCP on hepatocyte → endocytosis.  
2. **Nuclear import** → relaxed circular DNA (rcDNA) → repaired to covalently closed circular DNA (cccDNA) → stable episome.  
3. **Transcription/translation** → production of viral proteins (HBsAg, HBeAg, core, polymerase).  
4. **Virion assembly & secretion** → high circulating HBsAg (immune‑complex formation).  
5. **Innate immune activation** → Kupffer cells release IL‑6, TNF‑α → hepatocyte injury → ↑ALT/AST.  
6. **Adaptive response**  
   - **CD8⁺ cytotoxic T‑cells** recognise HBcAg → kill infected hepatocytes → necro‑inflammatory lobular hepatitis (symptomatic phase).  
   - **B‑cell production of anti‑HBs** → neutralising antibodies → seroconversion (HBsAg → anti‑HBs).  
7. **Chronicity** – when cccDNA persists and immune response is inadequate:  
   - **Immune‑tolerant phase**: high HBV DNA, normal ALT, minimal inflammation.  
   - **Immune‑active phase**: fluctuating ALT, hepatic necrosis, fibrosis initiation.  
8. **Fibrogenesis** – activated stellate cells deposit type I collagen → peri‑portal fibrosis → bridging → cirrhosis.  
9. **Oncogenesis** – integration of HBV DNA into host genome → insertional mutagenesis, HBx protein dysregulates p53 → hepatocellular carcinoma risk.  

**Resulting signs**: jaundice (bilirubin accumulation), right‑upper‑quadrant pain (capsular stretch), coagulopathy (↓ clotting factor synthesis), ascites/portal hypertension (cirrhosis), elevated ALT/AST (hepatocellular injury), elevated ALP/GGT if cholestasis supervenes.

## 2. Start with the pattern: liver function tests

- **Hepatocellular**: **ALT and AST** high — Viral hepatitis, drugs, alcohol, autoimmune hepatitis
- **Cholestatic**: **ALP and GGT** high — Gallstones in the bile duct, tumour, primary biliary cholangitis
- **Synthetic function**: **Low albumin, prolonged INR** — How badly the liver is failing (more serious than ALT)

## 3. Find the cause

**Viral:** hepatitis **A, B, C and E**.

**Drugs and toxins:** **anti-TB drugs (isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide)**, co-amoxiclav, **paracetamol overdose**, **herbal remedies** and **alcohol**. Always take a drug history, including traditional medicines.

**Autoimmune:** **ANA**, **anti-smooth muscle antibody** and **anti-LKM1** for autoimmune hepatitis; **antimitochondrial antibody** for primary biliary cholangitis.

**Always do:** a toxicology and drug review, and **viral screening**.

## 4. Hepatitis B serology: read it like a story

- **HBsAg**: The patient has the virus now (acute or chronic). Positive for more than 6 months means chronic infection
- **Anti-HBc IgM**: **Acute** (recent) infection
- **Anti-HBc IgG**: Past or chronic infection
- **Anti-HBs**: **Immunity**: from vaccination (anti-HBs alone) or after recovery (with anti-HBc IgG)
- **HBeAg**: The virus is actively replicating: high infectivity
- **HBV DNA (viral load)**: How much virus there is: the key number for the decision to treat

## Investigations: what each test answers  

- **HBsAg**: Is the patient currently infected? — Positive = infection (acute or chronic)
- **Anti‑HBc IgM**: Is the infection recent (<6 mo)? — Positive = acute infection
- **Anti‑HBc IgG**: Has the patient been exposed in the past? — Positive = past or chronic infection
- **HBeAg**: Is the virus replicating vigorously? — Positive = high infectivity, high HBV DNA
- **Anti‑HBe**: Has replication waned? — Positive = lower infectivity, often seroconversion
- **HBV DNA (quantitative PCR)**: How much virus is circulating? — Detectable → guides treatment threshold; undetectable → viral suppression
- **ALT / AST**: Is there active hepatocellular injury? — Elevated → necro‑inflammation; trend monitors response
- **ALP / GGT**: Is there cholestasis? — Predominant rise suggests biliary obstruction or drug injury
- **INR / Albumin**: How well is synthetic function preserved? — Prolonged INR / low albumin = advanced disease
- **Liver ultrasound (with Doppler)**: Is there cirrhosis, focal lesion, portal hypertension? — Nodular surface, splenomegaly, or HCC lesion → further imaging
- **FibroScan (transient elastography)**: What is the degree of fibrosis? — Stiffness > 9 kPa (approx) = significant fibrosis; > 12 kPa = cirrhosis
- **AFP (alpha‑fetoprotein)**: Is there a marker for HCC? — Elevated in many HCCs but not specific; used with imaging
- **Full blood count & platelets**: Is there hypersplenism or marrow suppression? — Thrombocytopenia may indicate portal hypertension
- **Anti‑HBs**: Does the patient have immunity? — Positive alone = vaccine immunity; with anti‑HBc IgG = natural immunity

## 5. Chronic hepatitis B: who needs treatment?

Classic thresholds (a plain-language rule to remember):

- **HBeAg positive** with **HBV DNA above 20,000 IU/mL** and a raised ALT or significant fibrosis
- **HBeAg negative** with **HBV DNA above 2,000 IU/mL** and a raised ALT or significant fibrosis
- **Cirrhosis:** treat if the DNA is detectable at all

Newer WHO guidance has widened who is eligible for treatment, so check what is current in your country.

## 6. Drugs: why this one and not that one

- **Tenofovir**: First line — Very high barrier to resistance; check kidney function and bone health
- **Entecavir**: First line — High barrier to resistance
- **Lamivudine**: Cheap but no longer first line — Resistance develops through the **YMDD mutation** in the viral reverse transcriptase
- **Pegylated interferon-α**: A finite course in selected patients — Many side effects; not for cirrhosis with decompensation
- **Ribavirin**: **Hepatitis C**, not hepatitis B — A classic exam trap: do not confuse the two viruses' drugs

## 7. Supportive care, and why

- **Vomiting:** antiemetics (such as metoclopramide or ondansetron) and fluids.
- **Fever or pain:** **paracetamol is acceptable at a reduced dose**; the liver clears it more slowly.
- **Avoid NSAIDs such as diclofenac:** low clotting factors raise the risk of bleeding, they can bleed the stomach, and they injure the kidneys.
- **Avoid aspirin:** bleeding, and **Reye's syndrome in children**.
- Stop alcohol and any suspected hepatotoxic drug.

## 8. Complications and surveillance

**Cirrhosis, liver failure and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).** In chronic hepatitis B and in cirrhosis, screen for HCC with **ultrasound, with or without AFP, every 6 months**. A suspicious lesion is characterised with **triple-phase CT of the abdomen** (arterial enhancement then washout is typical).

## 9. Prevention

**Vaccinate** all infants and health workers, give **hepatitis B immunoglobulin plus vaccine** to babies of infected mothers, screen blood and contacts, and counsel on safe sex and needles.

## Differential diagnosis and how to tell them apart  

| Condition | Key clinical / lab pointers | Distinguishing feature vs HBV |
|---|---|---|
| Acute hepatitis A | Recent travel/food‑borne outbreak, IgM anti‑HAV, normal HBsAg | Anti‑HAV IgM positive, HBsAg negative |
| Autoimmune hepatitis | Female, ANA/ASMA high, IgG ↑, interface hepatitis on biopsy | Autoantibodies present, no HBV DNA, response to steroids |
| Drug‑induced liver injury (e.g., paracetamol) | Temporal relation to overdose, high ALT >1000 U/L, normal viral serology | History of toxic exposure, acetaminophen level, absence of HBV markers |
| Primary biliary cholangitis | Pruritus, AMA positive, cholestatic LFT pattern, middle‑aged women | Anti‑mitochondrial antibody, ALP >> ALT, no HBsAg |
| Non‑alcoholic steatohepatitis | Metabolic syndrome, ultrasound fatty liver, ALT modestly ↑ | Imaging shows steatosis, absence of viral markers, normal HBV DNA |
| Acute alcoholic hepatitis | Recent binge, AST:ALT ratio >2, elevated GGT, history of heavy drinking | History of alcohol, AST predominance, no HBV serology |
| Hepatitis C (chronic) | Risk factors (IVDU), anti‑HCV positive, HCV RNA detectable, ALT mildly ↑ | Anti‑HCV positive, HCV RNA present, HBsAg negative |

## Clinical case  

**Case vignette**  
A 28‑year‑old male construction worker presents to the outpatient clinic with a 3‑week history of fatigue, mild right‑upper‑quadrant discomfort and intermittent low‑grade fever. He denies jaundice, nausea or vomiting. He reports occasional unprotected sex with multiple partners and a history of a childhood vaccination series, but he cannot recall receiving a hepatitis B booster. Physical examination shows a soft abdomen with mild tenderness over the liver edge; no scleral icterus or stigmata of chronic liver disease are evident. Laboratory results: ALT ≈ 350 U/L, AST ≈ 300 U/L, normal ALP and bilirubin, INR = 1.0, albumin = 42 g/L. Serology: HBsAg positive, anti‑HBc IgM negative, anti‑HBc IgG positive, HBeAg positive, HBV DNA detectable at a moderate level. Transient elastography shows liver stiffness of 6 kPa.

**Questions**

1. **Interpret the serological pattern and explain the phase of infection.**  
   *Model answer:* The presence of HBsAg with anti‑HBc IgG and absence of IgM indicates a chronic infection. Positive HBeAg and detectable HBV DNA denote the immune‑tolerant/early immune‑active phase, where viral replication is high but hepatic inflammation is modest, consistent with the modest ALT rise.

2. **What are the key factors that would prompt initiation of antiviral therapy in this patient?**  
   *Model answer:* Treatment is considered when (a) HBV DNA is ≥ 20 000 IU/mL in HBeAg‑positive individuals, (b) ALT is persistently > 2 × ULN, and/or (c) there is evidence of significant fibrosis (≥ F2) on elastography or biopsy. In this vignette, the ALT is only mildly elevated and liver stiffness is < 7 kPa, so immediate therapy is not mandatory; close monitoring is appropriate.

3. **Outline the examiner’s “thinking pathway” when presented with a hepatitis B case.**  
   *Model answer:*  
   - **Pattern of injury:** hepatocellular (ALT/AST ↑) → consider viral, drug, autoimmune.  
   - **Serology:** construct the infection timeline (HBsAg, anti‑HBc IgM/IgG, HBeAg, anti‑HBs).  
   - **Virology:** quantify HBV DNA, assess replication status.  
   - **Liver disease severity:** evaluate ALT, synthetic function, fibrosis (elastography/biopsy).  
   - **Treatment decision:** apply guideline thresholds (DNA level, ALT, fibrosis, cirrhosis).  
   - **Complication surveillance:** schedule HCC screening if fibrosis ≥ F3 or cirrhosis, vaccinate contacts, counsel on risk reduction.

## Practice questions

**1.** HBsAg negative, anti-HBs positive, anti-HBc negative. The patient is:
A. Acutely infected · B. Chronically infected · C. A carrier · **D. Immune through vaccination** · E. Immune after natural infection
*Answer: D. Anti-HBs alone means vaccine immunity.*

**2.** The mutation that makes hepatitis B resistant to lamivudine is:
A. T790M · B. V600E · **C. YMDD** · D. BRCA1 · E. K103N
*Answer: C.*

**3.** Which drug is used for hepatitis C but not hepatitis B?
A. Tenofovir · **B. Ribavirin** · C. Entecavir · D. Lamivudine · E. Pegylated interferon-α is used for neither
*Answer: B.*

**4.** The best way to screen a patient with cirrhosis for hepatocellular carcinoma is:
**A. Ultrasound with or without AFP every 6 months** · B. A liver biopsy every year · C. Chest X-ray · D. ALT every month · E. No screening is needed
*Answer: A.*
