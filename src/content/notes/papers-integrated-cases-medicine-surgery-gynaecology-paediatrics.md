## Case 1 (50 marks). A 50-year-old housewife with 20 lb weight gain in a year, fatigue, dizziness, memory loss, slow speech, a deepening voice, dry skin, constipation and cold intolerance. Temperature 96.8 °F, pulse 58 regular, BP 110/60, moderately obese, slow speech, puffy face, pale cool dry thick skin, thyroid not palpable, delayed reflex relaxation

### (a) Likely diagnosis (1 mark)
**Primary hypothyroidism,** most likely autoimmune (Hashimoto) thyroiditis with an atrophic gland.

### (b) Symptoms that point to it (5 marks)
Weight gain, fatigue, memory loss, slow speech and deepening voice, dry skin, constipation, and cold intolerance.

### (c) Supporting physical findings (5 marks)
Low temperature and **bradycardia** (58), **puffy face,** pale, cool, dry, thick skin, obesity, slow speech, and **delayed relaxation of the tendon reflexes.**

### (d) Additional history and examination to help diagnosis (10 marks)
- **History:** family history of thyroid or autoimmune disease (type 1 diabetes, pernicious anaemia); previous thyroid surgery, radioactive iodine or neck irradiation; drugs (amiodarone, lithium, antithyroid drugs, interferon); menstrual changes (menorrhagia) and infertility; hair loss, hoarseness, hearing loss, carpal tunnel symptoms, depression and ankle swelling; iodine intake and place of residence.
- **Examination:** goitre or neck nodes, hair and nail changes, carpal tunnel signs (Tinel, Phalen), pericardial effusion, ascites, ankle oedema, mental state and the ankle reflex relaxation time.

### (e) Tests to confirm (5 marks)
1. **TSH** (raised) and **free T4** (low).
2. **Anti-thyroid peroxidase antibodies** to confirm autoimmune thyroiditis.
3. Full blood count (anaemia), **lipid profile,** urea, electrolytes (hyponatraemia), glucose, and creatine kinase.
4. ECG (bradycardia, low voltage) and thyroid ultrasound if nodular.
5. If TSH is low with a low free T4 (central hypothyroidism), do pituitary hormones and MRI.

### (f) Treatment and drug mechanism (10 marks)
- **Levothyroxine** (synthetic T4) lifelong. Usual dose is about 1.6 micrograms per kg per day. Start at 25 to 50 micrograms in older patients or with heart disease, and increase every 4 to 6 weeks. Check TSH after 6 to 8 weeks and aim for a normal range.
- **Mechanism:** T4 is converted to T3, which binds nuclear thyroid hormone receptors and raises gene transcription, metabolic rate, heart rate and thermogenesis.
- Take on an empty stomach, apart from iron and calcium.
- **Myxoedema coma:** IV levothyroxine (with or without liothyronine), IV hydrocortisone, warming, and supportive care.
- Surgery is not a treatment for hypothyroidism, except for a compressive goitre.

### (g) Types of thyroid surgery (5 marks)
1. **Lobectomy (hemithyroidectomy):** removal of one lobe.
2. **Isthmusectomy**
3. **Subtotal thyroidectomy:** a small remnant is left to keep thyroid function.
4. **Near-total thyroidectomy**
5. **Total thyroidectomy** (with central or lateral neck dissection for cancer)

### (h) Complications of surgery (5 marks)
1. **Haemorrhage and neck haematoma,** which can compress the airway.
2. **Recurrent laryngeal nerve injury:** hoarseness, and stridor if bilateral.
3. **Hypoparathyroidism** with **hypocalcaemia.**
4. **Superior laryngeal nerve injury** (voice fatigue and loss of pitch).
5. Thyroid storm, wound infection, scar, hypothyroidism, and tracheomalacia.

### (i) Structures in the same fascial plane as the thyroid (4 marks)
The **pretracheal (visceral) fascia** encloses the thyroid and parathyroid glands, **trachea, oesophagus,** larynx and pharynx. The **infrahyoid (strap) muscles** (sternohyoid, sternothyroid, omohyoid, thyrohyoid) lie just in front of it, and the recurrent laryngeal nerves run in the tracheo-oesophageal groove behind the gland.

## Case 2 (25 marks). A patient with infertility: outline your management before diagnosis

**Definition:** failure to conceive after 12 months of regular unprotected intercourse (6 months if the woman is over 35). Assess **both partners** together.

**1. History**
- Duration, coital frequency and timing, cycle length and regularity, previous pregnancies, and dyspareunia.
- Previous pelvic infection or STI, abdominal or pelvic surgery, contraception history, and medical conditions (thyroid disease, diabetes).
- Male: mumps, cryptorchidism, hernia surgery, erectile and ejaculatory problems, drugs, alcohol, smoking and occupational heat or toxins.

**2. Examination**
- BMI, hirsutism, thyroid, breast (galactorrhoea), abdominal and pelvic examination.
- Male: testicular volume, varicocele, penile and secondary sex characteristics.

**3. Investigations**
- **Ovulation:** mid-luteal (day 21) progesterone, cycle charting or LH kits.
- **Hormones:** TSH, prolactin, FSH, LH and oestradiol (day 2 to 5), and AMH or antral follicle count for ovarian reserve.
- **Tubal patency:** hysterosalpingography, sono-hysterosalpingography, or laparoscopy with dye test.
- **Pelvic ultrasound** for PCOS, fibroids and endometriomas, and **hysteroscopy** if the uterine cavity is suspected to be abnormal. Screen for chlamydia.
- **Male:** **semen analysis** (WHO lower limits: volume 1.4 ml, concentration 16 million/ml, total motility 42%, morphology 4%), and hormones, karyotype and scrotal ultrasound if abnormal.

**4. Treatment according to cause**
- **General:** stop smoking, reduce alcohol, optimise weight, folic acid, and counsel on timed intercourse (every 2 to 3 days).
- **Anovulation:** clomiphene or letrozole, metformin in PCOS, gonadotrophins, and cabergoline for hyperprolactinaemia.
- **Tubal disease:** tubal surgery in selected cases, or IVF.
- **Endometriosis and fibroids:** laparoscopic surgery.
- **Male factor:** treat varicocele, surgical sperm retrieval, intrauterine insemination for mild disease, and ICSI for severe disease.
- **Unexplained:** intrauterine insemination, then IVF.
- Offer counselling, donor gametes or adoption where treatment fails.

## Case 3. If she is considered for IVF

### Preconception care (5 marks)
1. **Folic acid** 400 micrograms (higher dose if risk factors) before conception.
2. **Immunity and screening:** rubella and varicella immunity, cervical screening, HIV, hepatitis B and C and syphilis screening.
3. **Lifestyle:** stop smoking and alcohol, optimise BMI, and review all medicines.
4. **Work-up:** ovarian reserve (AMH, antral follicle count), semen analysis, and uterine cavity assessment.
5. **Counselling** on costs, success rates, risks and emotional stress, and vitamin D.

### Risks of IVF
1. **Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome**
2. **Multiple pregnancy** and its complications (preterm birth)
3. **Ectopic and heterotopic pregnancy**
4. **Miscarriage,** preterm birth and low birth weight
5. Bleeding, infection or organ injury at egg retrieval, **anaesthetic complications,** ovarian torsion, treatment failure, financial and psychological stress

## Case 4 (5 marks each). After IVF, at 37 weeks she has a constant trickle of clear fluid for 8 hours, scanty first-trimester bleeding, reassuring fetal wellbeing, no contractions, soft abdomen, afebrile

At 37 completed weeks this is **premature rupture of membranes at term (PROM).** (Rupture before 37 weeks is PPROM.)

### (a) How to confirm
1. **Sterile speculum examination:** pooling of liquor in the posterior fornix, or fluid leaking when she coughs.
2. **Nitrazine paper** turns blue (alkaline) and **ferning** on microscopy.
3. **Placental alpha-microglobulin-1 or IGFBP-1 test** where available.
4. **Ultrasound** for amniotic fluid volume. **Avoid digital vaginal examination** to prevent infection.

### (b) Other diagnoses to consider
1. **Urinary incontinence** or leakage of urine
2. **Vaginal infection or discharge** (bacterial vaginosis, candidiasis)
3. **Cervical mucus, show** or semen
4. Early labour, and a bladder infection

### (c) Complications
1. **Chorioamnionitis** and maternal sepsis
2. **Cord prolapse** and cord compression from oligohydramnios
3. **Placental abruption**
4. **Neonatal sepsis,** fetal distress and the need for operative delivery
5. Onset of labour and, if earlier, preterm birth and pulmonary hypoplasia

### (d) Treatment plan
1. **Admit** and assess maternal temperature, pulse and uterine tenderness, and monitor fetal heart rate with CTG.
2. Take high vaginal and rectal swabs for group B streptococcus.
3. At term, **induce labour** (oxytocin or prostaglandin) within about 24 hours, as the risk of infection rises with time.
4. **Antibiotics** if there are signs of infection, if GBS-positive, or if rupture exceeds 18 hours.
5. Monitor in labour, avoid unnecessary vaginal examinations, and have the neonatal team review the baby.

## Case 5 (30 marks). A neonate born by caesarean section develops fever, tachypnoea, tachycardia and crepitations at 42 hours, with raised procalcitonin and CRP: neonatal sepsis

### (a) Define early- and late-onset neonatal sepsis (2 marks)
- **Early-onset:** begins within the first **72 hours** (some definitions use the first 7 days) and is acquired from the mother (before or during birth).
- **Late-onset:** begins after 72 hours (or 7 days) up to 28 days and is acquired from the hospital or community.

### (b) Risks and aetiology (8 marks)
- **Maternal and obstetric risks:** premature rupture of membranes for more than 18 hours, chorioamnionitis, maternal fever, urinary tract infection or genital infection, GBS colonisation, prolonged or unclean delivery, and prematurity.
- **Neonatal risks:** low birth weight, birth asphyxia, invasive procedures and lines, delayed or absent breastfeeding, and poor hygiene.
- **Organisms (early-onset):** group B streptococcus, *Escherichia coli,* *Klebsiella* and *Listeria.* **Late-onset:** *Staphylococcus aureus,* coagulase-negative staphylococci, *Klebsiella,* *Pseudomonas* and *Candida.*

### (c) Stepwise diagnosis (10 marks)
1. **Clinical features:** temperature instability (fever or hypothermia), tachypnoea, grunting, apnoea, tachycardia, poor feeding, lethargy or irritability, seizures, jaundice, vomiting, abdominal distension, mottled skin and hypoglycaemia.
2. **Blood culture before antibiotics** (the gold standard).
3. **Full blood count:** white cells under 5 or over 20, neutropenia, immature-to-total neutrophil ratio over 0.2, thrombocytopenia.
4. **CRP** and **procalcitonin,** blood glucose, urea and electrolytes, and blood gas.
5. **Lumbar puncture** when the baby is stable (cells, protein, glucose and culture) to exclude meningitis, and urine culture in late-onset sepsis.
6. **Imaging:** chest X-ray for pneumonia, abdominal X-ray if necrotising enterocolitis is suspected, and cranial ultrasound if there is meningitis.

### (d) Treatment of a 2.8 kg baby who cannot feed (10 marks)
1. **Admit** to the newborn unit, keep **warm** (kangaroo care or incubator), and monitor temperature, SpO2 and glucose.
2. **Antibiotics immediately after cultures:** IV **ampicillin or benzylpenicillin plus gentamicin** (gentamicin about 5 mg/kg once daily, so about 14 mg). Treat for 7 to 10 days, and for 14 to 21 days if meningitis.
3. **IV fluids** since she cannot feed: 10% dextrose at about 80 ml/kg per day on day 2 (about 225 ml per day), increased over the following days, with electrolytes added after the first 48 hours.
4. **Oxygen** if SpO2 is under 90%, and treat hypoglycaemia and seizures.
5. Give vitamin K if not given, **start expressed breast milk by nasogastric tube** as soon as she is stable, and support the mother.
6. Review at 48 to 72 hours with the culture results, and change antibiotics if there is no improvement.

## Case 6 (30 marks). At 4 years, Gathei is very anxious when her big brother leaves for school, refuses to feed, refuses day care if he is not taking her, and has tummy aches when sleeping in a different room from him

### (a) Most likely diagnosis, with DSM-5 criteria (10 marks)
**Separation anxiety disorder.**

DSM-5 requires:
1. **Developmentally inappropriate and excessive fear or anxiety** about separation from the people to whom the child is attached, shown by at least **3** of the following:
   - recurrent excessive distress on separation,
   - persistent worry about losing the attachment figure or harm to them,
   - persistent worry about an event (for example kidnapping) that leads to separation,
   - persistent reluctance or refusal to go out (to school or day care) because of separation,
   - persistent reluctance to be alone,
   - reluctance to sleep away from home or without the attachment figure,
   - repeated nightmares about separation,
   - repeated physical complaints (headache, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting) on separation.
2. Duration of at least **4 weeks** in children.
3. Clinically significant **distress or impairment** (here feeding, day care and sleep).
4. Not better explained by another disorder (for example autism or generalised anxiety disorder).

Gathei fits: refusal to attend day care without her brother, reluctance to sleep apart, distress when he leaves, and somatic complaints.

### (b) Risk factors for anxiety disorders (5 marks)
1. **Family history** of anxiety or depression (genetic) and parental anxiety.
2. **Temperament:** behavioural inhibition and shyness.
3. **Parenting:** overprotective or controlling styles, insecure attachment.
4. **Life events:** bereavement, moving, school change, abuse or neglect.
5. Chronic illness, family conflict, low socio-economic status and adverse childhood experiences.

### (c) Management (10 marks)
1. **Assess** for other disorders and for stressors, and explain the diagnosis to the parents.
2. **Psychoeducation** and **parent training:** consistent routines, brief goodbyes, praise for brave behaviour, and avoiding reinforcing avoidance.
3. **Cognitive behavioural therapy** (age-adapted, with play and graded exposure to separation), and parent-child interaction therapy.
4. **Liaise with the day care or school** and plan a gradual return.
5. **Family therapy** and treatment of parental anxiety.
6. **Medication** is only for severe or refractory cases and is rarely used at 4 years: an SSRI as the first choice, with close monitoring.
7. Follow up regularly.

### (d) Three drugs and their mechanism (5 marks)
1. **Sertraline or fluoxetine (SSRIs):** block the serotonin reuptake transporter, raising synaptic serotonin and reducing anxiety over weeks.
2. **Buspirone:** a 5-HT1A partial agonist with an anxiolytic effect and no dependence.
3. **Benzodiazepines** (short-term only, for example lorazepam): enhance GABA-A receptor chloride channel opening. Avoid long-term use in children because of tolerance and dependence.
