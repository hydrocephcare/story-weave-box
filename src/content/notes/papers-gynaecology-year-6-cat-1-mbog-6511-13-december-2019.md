## Multiple choice questions

### 1. You will suspect pelvic inflammatory disease in all EXCEPT:
- A. Early coitarche
- B. Previous abortion
- C. Previous contraceptive use
- D. Previous ectopic pregnancy

**Answer: C.** Early first intercourse, a previous abortion and a previous ectopic pregnancy (a sign of past tubal damage or PID) point to PID. Contraceptive use (condoms, the pill) is protective, and it is not a reason for suspicion.

### 2. Which of the following does NOT develop from the endodermal embryonic layer?
- A. Lining of the urethra and bladder
- B. Reproductive system
- C. Liver
- D. Thymus

**Answer: B.** The gonads and the genital ducts arise from mesoderm (the urogenital ridge). The bladder and urethral lining (urogenital sinus), liver and thymus are endodermal.

### 3. Regarding vulval intraepithelial neoplasia (VIN), which is true?
- A. Presents usually after sustained immobility
- B. Usually ipsilateral
- C. Can be reduced by oral contraceptives
- D. It is commonly asymptomatic

**Answer: D.** Many women have no symptoms and the lesion is found by chance, though itching is the commonest symptom when there are any. The other options are not features of VIN.

### 4. Regarding fertilisation, which of the following is incorrect?
- A. The process lasts 24 hours
- B. A zonal reaction occurs when sperm comes into contact with the corona radiata
- C. The secondary oocyte completes its second meiotic division after entry of the sperm head
- D. The zygote is formed from the fusion of male and female pronuclei

**Answer: B.** The zona reaction (which blocks other sperm) is triggered when the first sperm fuses with the oocyte membrane, after it has crossed the corona radiata and the zona pellucida.

### 5. During the menstrual cycle, the following is correct:
- A. The proliferative phase is relatively constant
- B. Ovulation coincides with menstruation
- C. Anovulatory cycles are characteristically painless
- D. Menstrual blood loss is usually 150 ml

**Answer: C.** Without ovulation there is no luteal phase and little prostaglandin, so periods are painless (ovulatory dysmenorrhoea needs ovulation). The proliferative phase varies (the luteal phase is the constant one), ovulation is mid-cycle, and normal loss is 30 to 80 ml.

### 6. Choose the correct answer. Mucinous cysts of the vulva:
- A. Originate from the urogenital sinus
- B. Are lined by stratified squamous epithelium
- C. Are usually symptomatic requiring early intervention
- D. Are malignant

**Answer: A.** Mucinous (vestibular) cysts arise from the urogenital sinus remnants and are lined by mucus-secreting epithelium. They are benign and usually have no symptoms.

### 7. A 30-year-old woman has primary infertility and dysmenorrhoea, and ultrasound shows a 6 cm endometrioma in the left ovary. What is the most appropriate initial management?
- A. Gonadotrophin releasing hormone agonist for six months
- B. In-vitro fertilisation
- C. Laparoscopic drainage of the endometrioma
- D. Laparoscopic excision of the endometrioma

**Answer: D.** For a large endometrioma with pain and infertility, laparoscopic cystectomy (excision of the cyst wall) gives better pain relief and fertility than drainage, which recurs. Medical treatment does not remove the cyst.

### 8. In regard to genital herpes, identify the correct statement:
- A. Can be caused by HSV 1
- B. Present as painful vesicles
- C. Treatment is via administration of trichloroacetic acid
- D. Relapses are progressively symptomatic

**Answer: A.** Genital herpes is caused by HSV-2 and, increasingly, HSV-1. The lesions are painful vesicles that quickly ulcerate, but the best single true statement is the cause. Treatment is aciclovir (trichloroacetic acid is for warts), and relapses are usually milder.

### 9. Which of the following does NOT form part of Amsel's criteria for the diagnosis of bacterial vaginosis?
- A. Vaginal pH above 4.5
- B. Positive amine (whiff) test
- C. Identification of Tzanck bodies on microscopy
- D. Vaginal discharge

**Answer: C.** Amsel's four criteria are a thin homogeneous discharge, a pH above 4.5, a positive whiff test and clue cells. Tzanck cells belong to herpes.

### 10. Which of the following is NOT a common source of toxic shock syndrome?
- A. Contaminated surgical site
- B. Long-term tampon use
- C. Skin abscess
- D. Contaminated tattoo site

**Answer: D.** Toxic shock syndrome follows staphylococcal or streptococcal toxin production at a wound, tampon, abscess or surgical site. A tattoo site is an uncommon source.

### 11. The following apply to a pelvic abscess EXCEPT:
- A. Can be caused by Neisseria gonorrhoeae
- B. Can cause multiple organ failure
- C. Can cause chronic pelvic pain
- D. Can cause amenorrhoea

**Answer: D.** A pelvic (tubo-ovarian) abscess causes fever, pain and discharge, and can lead to sepsis, organ failure and chronic pain, but it does not cause amenorrhoea.

### 12. The following statements refer to uterine fibroids. Determine which is true:
- A. Infertility is a common presentation
- B. Around 1% of fibroids are leiomyosarcomata which may result in malignant change
- C. 25% are asymptomatic
- D. Can cause amenorrhoea

**Answer: B.** Malignant change is rare (well under 1%, so "around 1%" is the nearest option). Heavy menstrual bleeding is the commonest symptom, infertility is uncommon, most fibroids (about half or more) have no symptoms, and fibroids cause heavier periods rather than amenorrhoea. The question is loosely worded.

### 13. Regarding galactorrhoea, which statement is false?
- A. Lactation requires oestrogen and progesterone
- B. Diagnosis is aided by microscopy showing fat globules and scanty cellular material
- C. Mammography is useful in making a diagnosis
- D. Is treatable by thyroid hormone replacement

**Answer: C.** Galactorrhoea is diagnosed by the history, the finding of milk (fat globules on microscopy) and a prolactin level, and not by a mammogram. When it is caused by hypothyroidism, thyroid hormone treats it.

### 14. A patient has amenorrhoea. Her FSH and LH levels are elevated. What structure is most likely responsible?
- A. Thyroid
- B. Ovaries
- C. Pituitary
- D. Hypothalamus

**Answer: B.** High FSH and LH mean the ovaries are not responding (primary ovarian failure), so the feedback from oestrogen is lost. A pituitary or hypothalamic cause gives low or normal levels.

### 15. What is oligomenorrhoea?
- A. Prolonged or excessive uterine bleeding that occurs at regular intervals
- B. Irregular menstrual bleeding
- C. Regular bleeding that occurs more frequently than every 21 days
- D. Menstrual cycle lengths greater than 35 days

**Answer: D.** Oligomenorrhoea is infrequent periods, with cycles longer than 35 days. Option A is menorrhagia, and option C is polymenorrhoea.

### 16. A 34-year-old woman complains of heavy periods. She is trying to get pregnant, so you suggest mefenamic acid, knowing it is very effective in reducing blood flow. What type of drug is this?
- A. Cyclo-oxygenase inhibitor
- B. Gonadotropin releasing hormone agonist
- C. Plasminogen activator inhibitor
- D. Synthetic steroid hormone

**Answer: A.** Mefenamic acid is an NSAID, which blocks prostaglandin production and reduces menstrual loss by about 30%. It does not stop ovulation, so it suits a woman trying to conceive.

## Section A: short answer questions

### 1. Question 1. (a) What is pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) (2 marks)? (b) Describe briefly the Gainesville classification of PID (5 marks). (c) List protective factors against PID (3 marks).

**(a) Pelvic inflammatory disease**

An **infection of the upper female genital tract:** the uterus (endometritis), fallopian tubes (salpingitis), ovaries and the pelvic peritoneum. It spreads upwards from the cervix and vagina, and it is a polymicrobial infection caused by *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* and *Chlamydia trachomatis* with anaerobes, *Gardnerella* and enteric bacteria. It causes lower abdominal pain, discharge, fever and painful intercourse, and the late effects are infertility, ectopic pregnancy and chronic pelvic pain.

**(b) The Gainesville (Monif) classification**

| Stage | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Stage 1 | Acute salpingitis **without peritonitis** (mild, uncomplicated disease with endometritis) |
| Stage 2 | Acute salpingitis **with peritonitis** (pelvic peritonitis) |
| Stage 3 | Acute salpingitis with **superimposed tubal occlusion or a tubo-ovarian abscess** (pyosalpinx, with an unruptured abscess) |
| Stage 4 | **Ruptured tubo-ovarian abscess** (generalised peritonitis, with sepsis) |

Stages 1 and 2 are treated with antibiotics, and stages 3 and 4 often need admission, drainage or surgery.

**(c) Protective factors**

1. **Consistent condom use** (barrier contraception), which prevents sexually transmitted infections.
2. **Hormonal contraception:** the combined pill and progestogen methods thicken the cervical mucus, which lowers the risk of ascending infection.
3. **Delaying first intercourse, a single faithful partner,** and avoiding many partners.
4. **Screening and early treatment** of sexually transmitted infections, with **treatment of partners.**
5. **Safe insertion practice:** screening for infection before inserting an IUD or doing an operation on the womb.
6. Avoiding vaginal douching, and good hygiene after delivery and abortion.

### 2. Question 2. (a) Define recurrent miscarriage (2 marks). (b) List 3 fetal causes of miscarriage (3 marks).

**(a) Recurrent miscarriage**

The loss of **three or more consecutive pregnancies** before 20 weeks of gestation (some guidelines, such as the American society, say two or more).

**(b) Fetal causes of miscarriage**

1. **Chromosomal abnormalities** (the commonest cause, in about half of early losses): trisomies, monosomy X and triploidy.
2. **Structural congenital malformations:** for example neural tube defects and heart defects.
3. **Fetal and intrauterine infection:** for example TORCH infections, listeriosis and syphilis.
4. Others: single-gene disorders, and defects of the placenta and the trophoblast.

### 3. Question 3. (a) Describe Spiegelberg's criteria for ovarian ectopic pregnancy (4 marks). (b) Describe 2 serological test findings that are suggestive of an ectopic pregnancy (2 marks). (c) What investigation is regarded as the gold standard for the diagnosis of ectopic pregnancy and why (2 marks)?

**(a) Spiegelberg's criteria for an ovarian pregnancy**

1. The **fallopian tube on the affected side is intact** and normal (not involved).
2. The **gestational sac is in the position of the ovary.**
3. The **sac is connected to the uterus by the ovarian ligament.**
4. **Ovarian tissue can be shown in the wall of the sac** on histology.

**(b) Serological findings suggesting an ectopic pregnancy**

1. **Serum beta-hCG** that rises **less than expected** (a rise of under about 53 to 66% in 48 hours), plateaus or falls slowly. A normal early pregnancy roughly doubles every 48 hours. A level above the discriminatory zone (about 1500 to 3500 IU/L) with **no intrauterine sac on transvaginal ultrasound** is also suspicious.
2. **Low serum progesterone** (below about 5 ng/ml suggests a failing pregnancy, and values under about 25 ng/ml are suspicious), while normal pregnancies have a level above 25 ng/ml.

**(c) Gold standard: diagnostic laparoscopy**

It lets the surgeon **see the tube and the pelvis directly** and confirm the diagnosis, and it allows **immediate treatment** (salpingectomy or salpingostomy) in the same procedure. (In practice, transvaginal ultrasound with serial hCG is used first.)

### 4. Question 4. (a) What is abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB) (2 marks)? (b) List structural causes of AUB (4 marks). (c) Briefly describe the medical treatment options for AUB (5 marks).

**(a) Abnormal uterine bleeding**

Bleeding from the uterus that is **abnormal in regularity, frequency, duration or volume,** in a woman who is not pregnant. By the FIGO definition, the normal values are a cycle of 24 to 38 days, a period of up to 8 days, and a loss of up to 80 ml.

**(b) Structural causes (PALM)**

1. **P**olyps (endometrial and cervical).
2. **A**denomyosis.
3. **L**eiomyoma (fibroids), particularly the submucosal type.
4. **M**alignancy and premalignant disease (endometrial hyperplasia, endometrial and cervical cancer).

(The non-structural causes are COEIN: coagulopathy, ovulatory dysfunction, endometrial, iatrogenic and not yet classified.)

**(c) Medical treatment options**

1. **Levonorgestrel intrauterine system (Mirena):** the first-line treatment, reducing loss by 70 to 95%.
2. **Tranexamic acid** (antifibrinolytic) during the period, reducing loss by about half.
3. **NSAIDs** (mefenamic acid) during the period, reducing loss by about 30%. They also relieve pain.
4. **Combined oral contraceptive pill,** or cyclical oral progestogens (norethisterone) and depot medroxyprogesterone, to regulate the cycle.
5. **GnRH analogues** (goserelin) for up to 6 months, for example to shrink fibroids before surgery, and **ulipristal acetate** or danazol in selected cases.
6. **Iron supplements** for anaemia, and treatment of any underlying disorder (thyroid disease, bleeding disorder).
7. If medical treatment fails: **endometrial ablation, uterine artery embolisation, myomectomy or hysterectomy.**
