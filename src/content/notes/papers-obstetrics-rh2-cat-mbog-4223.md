## Question 1. Mrs X in labour

**Given:** admitted at 14:00. Contractions 2 in 10 minutes, each 20 seconds. Head 5/5 above the brim, FHR 130/min. Cervix 2 cm, membranes intact, no moulding. BP 110/70, pulse 78, temperature 36.6 °C, urine 100 ml, protein and acetone negative. At 18:00: FHR 140/min, membranes ruptured with clear liquor, no moulding, cervix 5 cm, head 3/5 above the brim, contractions 3 in 10 minutes each 50 seconds, BP 105/70, pulse 80, temperature 37 °C.

### (i) Plot the findings on the partograph (8 marks)

How to plot (the partograph itself cannot be reproduced here):
1. **Fetal heart rate:** plot 130 at 14:00 and 140 at 18:00 on the FHR grid, within the normal band of 110 to 160.
2. **Liquor and moulding:** record **I** (intact) at 14:00, then **C** (clear) and moulding **0** at 18:00.
3. **Cervical dilatation (X):** the active phase starts at 4 cm, so plot the 5 cm finding at 18:00 and draw the **alert line** from 4 cm (rising 1 cm per hour) and the **action line** 4 hours to its right. Plot head descent (O) at 3/5.
4. **Contractions:** shade 3 squares (3 in 10 minutes) with the 20 to 40 second band at 14:00 and the more than 40 second band at 18:00.
5. **Maternal condition:** record pulse, BP and temperature, and urine volume and tests.
6. Record oxytocin and drugs (none), and the time every 30 minutes for FHR and contractions and every 4 hours for the cervix.

### (ii) At 22:00 she is still 5 cm. Most likely diagnosis (1 mark)
**Prolonged active phase of the first stage (arrested active phase),** with failure of cervical dilatation. The cervix has not progressed over the 4 hours since 18:00, so she has crossed the action line.

### (iii) Five complications of the diagnosis (5 marks)
1. **Obstructed labour** and uterine rupture
2. **Fetal distress** and birth asphyxia
3. **Maternal exhaustion, dehydration and ketosis**
4. **Chorioamnionitis** and sepsis after prolonged ruptured membranes
5. **Postpartum haemorrhage** from uterine atony
6. Obstetric fistula (VVF/RVF) from pressure necrosis, and neonatal infection

### (iv) Most appropriate management (1 mark)
Assess the cause (the 3 Ps: power, passage, passenger). If there is no cephalopelvic disproportion, correct hydration and augment with oxytocin. If dilatation fails, or there is disproportion or fetal distress, **deliver by caesarean section.**

### (v) Five complications of the management in (iv) (5 marks)
Complications of caesarean section:
1. Haemorrhage
2. Infection (wound, endometritis)
3. Venous thromboembolism
4. Injury to bladder, ureter or bowel
5. Anaesthetic complications, and uterine scar problems in later pregnancies (rupture, placenta praevia or accreta)

## Question 2. Mrs R, para 3+0 G4 at 38 weeks, liquor draining, lower abdominal pain, fundal content a hard ballotable mass, longitudinal lie, FHR 134 regular, presenting part firm and engaged

### (i) What presentation is this? (1 mark)
**Breech presentation.** The hard ballotable mass in the fundus is the fetal head, and the firm, engaged presenting part in the pelvis is the breech, with a longitudinal lie.

### (ii) Five complications of this presentation (5 marks)
1. **Cord prolapse**
2. **Birth asphyxia** and birth trauma
3. **Head entrapment** by an incompletely dilated cervix
4. **Fetal injury:** fractures, brachial plexus injury, intracranial haemorrhage
5. **Premature rupture of membranes**, preterm delivery and obstructed labour
6. **Increased caesarean section** and perinatal mortality

### (iii) On vaginal examination, a soft mass with a rope-like pulsatile structure next to it, cervix 6 cm. Diagnosis (1 mark)
**Umbilical cord prolapse.**

### (iv) Five risk factors for the rope-like structure in the vaginal canal (5 marks)
1. **Malpresentation** (breech, transverse lie) and an unengaged presenting part
2. **Premature rupture of membranes** and artificial rupture with a high head
3. **Polyhydramnios**
4. **Prematurity,** low birth weight and multiple pregnancy
5. **Long cord**, cephalopelvic disproportion, high parity, placenta praevia

### (v) Management (8 marks)
Cord prolapse with a cervix at 6 cm is an obstetric emergency.
1. **Call for help** and prepare theatre. Do not push the cord back.
2. **Relieve cord compression:** insert a hand to lift the presenting part off the cord, or fill the bladder with 500 ml of saline through a catheter, and put the mother in knee-chest or exaggerated Sims position.
3. **Handle the cord minimally,** or keep it warm and moist, and avoid spasm.
4. **Assess the fetus:** confirm fetal heart activity.
5. **Immediate delivery:** emergency **caesarean section** if the cervix is not fully dilated and the fetus is alive.
6. If **fully dilated,** deliver quickly by vaginal route: breech extraction here or forceps or vacuum for a cephalic baby.
7. **If the fetus is dead,** manage expectantly and let labour proceed.
8. **Neonatal resuscitation** must be ready, and give the mother postnatal counselling.
