## Multiple choice questions

### 1. Radiological signs of rickets include all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Splaying
- B. Cupping
- C. Fraying
- D. Increased bone density
- E. Pathological fractures

**Answer: D.** Rickets thins the bones (osteopenia). The ends of the long bones show splaying, cupping and fraying.

### 2. Risk factors for developing rickets include all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Lack of exposure to sunshine
- B. Chronic diarrhoea
- C. Taking a diet rich in dairy
- D. Chronic kidney disease
- E. Chronic liver disease

**Answer: C.** Dairy foods give calcium and vitamin D. The other four reduce vitamin D making, absorption or activation.

### 3. Investigations that can be done at the bedside in a newborn with respiratory distress, lethargy and hypothermia include all EXCEPT:
- A. CRP
- B. Full blood count
- C. Absolute neutrophil count with band counts
- D. Lumbar puncture
- E. CT scan of the head

**Answer: E.** A CT scan is not a bedside test and is not part of a septic screen.

### 4. Characteristics of kwashiorkor include all EXCEPT:
- A. Oedema
- B. Hypopigmented hair
- C. Dermatitis
- D. Mental challenge
- E. Hunger and eagerness to eat

**Answer: E.** Children with kwashiorkor have a poor appetite and are apathetic and miserable. Hunger is a feature of marasmus.

### 5. Which vitamin prevents neural tube defects?
- A. Folic acid
- B. Thiamine
- C. Pyridoxine
- D. Riboflavin
- E. Vitamin E

**Answer: A.** Folic acid before conception and in early pregnancy cuts the risk.

### 6. Which nutrient reduces morbidity and mortality in children with severe measles?
- A. Vitamin C
- B. Vitamin E
- C. Vitamin A
- D. Folic acid
- E. Vitamin K

**Answer: C.** Vitamin A is given to every child with measles (two doses on consecutive days).

### 7. Mariam is a 24-month-old girl. She is expected to do all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Run well
- B. Put 3 words together (subject, verb, object)
- C. Make a tower of 7 cubes
- D. Handle a spoon well
- E. Hop on one foot

**Answer: E.** Hopping on one foot comes at about 4 years. At 2 years a child runs, builds a tower of 6 or 7 cubes and joins words.

### 8. The following are part of gross motor development in a child EXCEPT:
- A. Sits without support
- B. Scribbles
- C. Runs
- D. Walks alone
- E. Pulls to sit with no head lag

**Answer: B.** Scribbling is fine motor development.

### 9. By what age should a child's weight be expected to triple?
- A. 6 months
- B. 9 months
- C. 1 year
- D. 18 months
- E. 2 years

**Answer: C.** Birth weight doubles by 5 months and triples by 1 year.

### 10. In terms of language development in a child, which statement is true?
- A. Expressive language precedes receptive language
- B. Receptive language precedes expressive language
- C. Toddlers do not enjoy polysyllabic jargoning
- D. By 15 months the average child cannot point to major body parts
- E. Infants speak their first word around 15 months

**Answer: B.** A child understands before he can speak. The first word comes at about 12 months, and by 15 months most can point to body parts.

### 11. A 12-year-old girl has sparse, lightly pigmented straight hair on the pubic region and an elevated small mound of the breast and papilla. In which stage of sexual maturity is she?
- A. 1
- B. 2
- C. 3
- D. 4
- E. 5

**Answer: B.** Tanner stage 2 has breast buds and sparse straight pubic hair.

### 12. A severely malnourished child has diarrhoea, fever and cannot feed. The following should be addressed on the first day of treatment EXCEPT:
- A. Hypoglycaemia
- B. Hypothermia
- C. Dehydration
- D. Start iron supplement
- E. Correct electrolyte imbalance

**Answer: D.** Iron is withheld in the first (stabilisation) phase because it can feed infection. It is started in the rehabilitation phase.

### 13. Which is an expected laboratory finding in the malnourished child above?
- A. Hyperglycaemia
- B. Hypernatraemia
- C. Hyperkalaemia
- D. Hyponatraemia
- E. Normal haemoglobin

**Answer: D.** Total body sodium is high but the serum level is low from dilution, and potassium and glucose are low and anaemia is common.

### 14. Primitive neonatal reflexes include all EXCEPT:
- A. Grasp
- B. Moro
- C. Suck
- D. Root
- E. Knee jerk

**Answer: E.** The knee jerk is a deep tendon reflex that persists throughout life.

### 15. Breast milk is rich in all the following EXCEPT:
- A. Lactalbumin
- B. Calcium
- C. Vitamin D
- D. Immunoglobulin A
- E. Lactose

**Answer: C.** Breast milk is low in vitamin D, and babies need sunlight (or a supplement).

### 16. The correct order for examining a child is:
- A. Palpation, auscultation, percussion, inspection
- B. Inspection, percussion, palpation, auscultation
- C. Inspection, palpation, percussion, auscultation
- D. Percussion, palpation, auscultation, inspection
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** The usual order is inspection, palpation, percussion, auscultation. (In the abdomen, auscultation comes before palpation.)

### 17. A normal 3-year-old should be able to:
- A. Copy a triangle
- B. Separate from the primary caregiver with ease
- C. Skip
- D. Tell his or her gender
- E. Walk down the stairs one foot at a time without help

**Answer: D.** A 3-year-old knows whether he is a boy or a girl. Copying a triangle comes at 5 years, skipping at 5, and walking down stairs one foot per step at 4 years.

### 18. The sense that is not fully developed at birth is:
- A. Hearing
- B. Smell
- C. Taste
- D. Touch
- E. Vision

**Answer: E.** Visual acuity is poor at birth and matures over the first few years.

### 19. Which of the following is NOT a cranial nerve?
- A. Facial
- B. Pudendal
- C. Vagus
- D. Hypoglossal
- E. Oculomotor

**Answer: B.** The pudendal nerve is a sacral nerve.

### 20. Which of the following regarding childhood tuberculosis is true?
- A. Pulmonary cavitation is common
- B. The Mantoux test is useful even with a previous history of BCG vaccination
- C. The diagnostic yield of Mycobacterium from gastric aspirates is more than 40%
- D. Streptomycin, rifampicin and isoniazid are the recommended initial therapy
- E. Meningitis may be the first presentation

**Answer: B.** A positive Mantoux still counts after BCG, with the size and the clinical picture guiding interpretation. Cavities are uncommon in young children, gastric aspirate yield is about 30 to 40%, and the initial regimen is RHZE. Option E is also true in young children, so the question is loosely worded.

### 21. What is the expected average height and weight of a 4-year-old?
- A. Weight 19 kg, height 95 cm
- B. Weight 16 kg, height 101 cm
- C. Weight 16 kg, height 70 cm
- D. Weight 14 kg, height 95 cm
- E. Weight 18 kg, height 105 cm

**Answer: B.** Weight is about 2 x (age + 4) = 16 kg, and height at 4 years is about double the length at 1 year, about 100 cm.

### 22. A 7-year-old girl has cough, difficulty breathing and wheezing, no fever, and a strong family history of allergy. The following can be part of treatment EXCEPT:
- A. Steroids
- B. Sedatives
- C. Theophyllines
- D. Terbutaline
- E. Oxygen

**Answer: B.** Sedatives can suppress breathing and are avoided in asthma.

### 23. A 2-year-old has inspiratory stridor, a hoarse voice and a barking cough, preceded by a runny nose and low-grade fever for 3 days. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Viral croup
- B. Laryngomalacia
- C. Foreign body obstruction
- D. Acute bronchiolitis
- E. Choanal atresia

**Answer: A.** Barking cough, hoarseness and stridor after a cold is laryngotracheobronchitis (croup).

### 24. The most common site of aspiration in a child lying supine is:
- A. The lower lobe of the left lung
- B. The lower lobe of the right lung
- C. The upper lobe of the left lung
- D. None of the above
- E. All of the above

**Answer: B.** The right main bronchus is wider and straighter. In the supine position the posterior segments of the right lung (the right lower lobe superior segment and right upper lobe) are most often affected.

### 25. Which should we expect of a 7-month-old infant?
- A. Crawling
- B. Social smile
- C. Control of bowel and bladder function
- D. Sitting unsupported
- E. Raising the head while prone

**Answer: D.** Sitting without support comes at about 6 to 8 months. A social smile comes at 6 weeks, crawling at 9 months and continence at 2 years or more.

### 26. Baby John was born at term. His mother had a difficult labour with thick meconium-stained liquor. John has respiratory distress soon after resuscitation. Which statement is true?
- A. The baby most likely has respiratory distress syndrome
- B. The baby most likely has pneumonia
- C. The prevalence is affected by the gender of the baby
- D. Specific procedures before delivery would have prevented this
- E. Prior antibiotic treatment of the mother is beneficial in this condition

**Answer: A.** The picture is meconium aspiration syndrome, a cause of respiratory distress in a term baby. Of the options offered, respiratory distress is the nearest, so the question is faulty.

### 27. In a 3-week-old baby with a serum bilirubin of 275 micromol/L, which finding supports a possible diagnosis of biliary atresia?
- A. Absence of dark urine and pale stools
- B. Fevers
- C. Presence of dark urine and pale stools
- D. Tender hepatomegaly
- E. Ultrasound is able to visualise the gall bladder

**Answer: C.** Conjugated bilirubin spills into the urine (dark), and none reaches the gut, so the stool is pale. The gall bladder is often small or not seen on ultrasound.

### 28. Recognised consequences of ABO incompatibility do NOT include:
- A. Coombs-negative haemolysis
- B. Increased severity in further pregnancies
- C. Jaundice on the first day of life
- D. Reduced haemoglobin on the first day of life
- E. Spherocytes in the blood

**Answer: B.** ABO disease does not worsen with later pregnancies, unlike rhesus disease. D is also a rhesus-type feature (the haemoglobin is usually normal at birth in ABO disease), so the question is loosely worded.

### 29. Which is the best way to screen for wasting?
- A. Weight-for-age Z score
- B. Height-for-age Z score
- C. Weight-for-height Z score
- D. Mid-upper arm circumference
- E. Skin thickness

**Answer: C.** Wasting means low weight for height. Height for age shows stunting and weight for age shows underweight.

### 30. Mary, 10 months, has cough, wheezing and fever. She had sneezing and watery rhinorrhoea, and no history of atopy. The most probable diagnosis is:
- A. Pneumonia
- B. Tuberculosis
- C. Acute bronchiolitis
- D. Asthma
- E. Foreign body aspiration

**Answer: C.** A viral cold followed by wheeze in an infant under 1 year is bronchiolitis.

### 31. About kangaroo mother care, which statement is true?
- A. It should only be started if the weight of the newborn is above 3 kg
- B. The mother is the only person who can provide kangaroo care
- C. The baby should be placed between the mother's breasts in an upright position
- D. The baby and mother should be fully clothed so that the skins do not touch
- E. It is the same as baby wearing

**Answer: C.** The baby is held skin to skin, upright between the breasts. Fathers and others can also give it, and it is for small and preterm babies.

### 32. Concerning epiglottitis, which statement is true?
- A. The main cause is respiratory syncytial virus
- B. A radiograph of the neck shows the steeple sign
- C. It affects only children
- D. The airway has to be secured as early as possible
- E. It is preceded by a flu-like illness for 2 to 3 days

**Answer: D.** It is caused by Haemophilus influenzae type b, comes on over hours, and can obstruct the airway suddenly. The steeple sign is croup (the thumb sign is epiglottitis).

### 33. Risk factors for neonatal sepsis include all EXCEPT:
- A. Prolonged labour
- B. Prolonged rupture of membranes
- C. Treating maternal infections in the third trimester
- D. Poor maternal hygiene
- E. Septic techniques during delivery

**Answer: C.** Treating maternal infections lowers the risk.

### 34. The correct regimen for management of miliary tuberculosis in children is:
- A. 2RHZES 4RE
- B. 2RHZE 4RH
- C. 1RHZES 2RHZE 5RHE
- D. 2RHZE 8RH
- E. 2RHZE 10RH

**Answer: B.** Miliary TB is treated like other severe TB with 2 months of RHZE and 4 months of RH. Tuberculous meningitis and bone TB take 10 months of continuation (2RHZE 10RH).

### 35. A 4-year-old was playing with small beads and suddenly developed a persistent cough. On auscultation there are unilateral rhonchi. The most likely cause is:
- A. Pneumonia
- B. Acute bronchitis
- C. Foreign body aspiration
- D. Croup
- E. Asthma

**Answer: C.** Sudden cough with a one-sided wheeze after play with small objects is a foreign body.

### 36. To improve rates of exclusive breastfeeding in hospitals, the following are useful policies EXCEPT:
- A. Give nothing to drink or eat to exclusively breastfeeding infants except medicines or vitamins
- B. Hospitals should have a breastfeeding policy
- C. Mother and baby should be together at all times unless very sick
- D. Babies should be fed formula after delivery so the mother can relax
- E. Dummies, pacifiers and artificial nipples should not be given before breastfeeding is established

**Answer: D.** Formula top-ups undermine breastfeeding and are not part of the baby-friendly steps.

### 37. Which is a characteristic of late adolescence?
- A. Preoccupied with changing body
- B. Increased interest in sexual anatomy
- C. Increased bid for independence
- D. Self-conscious about appearance and attractiveness
- E. Future-oriented with a sense of perspective

**Answer: E.** Early adolescence is about body changes, middle about independence and peers, and late about identity and future goals.

### 38. To prevent haemorrhagic disease of the newborn, which vitamin should all newborns be given at birth?
- A. Vitamin D
- B. Vitamin C
- C. Vitamin K
- D. Vitamin B
- E. Vitamin A

**Answer: C.** Vitamin K 1 mg IM at birth.

### 39. Preterm babies are at risk of hypothermia by losing heat through the following ways EXCEPT:
- A. Radiation
- B. Conduction
- C. Evaporation
- D. Cooling
- E. Convection

**Answer: D.** The four routes of heat loss are conduction, convection, radiation and evaporation. "Cooling" is not one of them.

### 40. Common causes of respiratory distress within the first hour of birth include the following EXCEPT:
- A. Transient tachypnoea of the newborn
- B. Respiratory distress syndrome
- C. Cyanotic congenital heart disease
- D. Pneumonia
- E. Rheumatic heart disease

**Answer: E.** Rheumatic heart disease is a disease of older children and follows streptococcal infection.

### 41. Differential diagnosis for a 3-year-old with a chronic cough includes all EXCEPT:
- A. Pulmonary tuberculosis
- B. Asthma
- C. Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease
- D. Foreign body aspiration
- E. Bronchiolitis

**Answer: E.** Bronchiolitis is an acute illness of infants, lasting days to 2 weeks.

### 42. Relevant specific investigations for pulmonary tuberculosis include:
- A. Full blood count
- B. Urea and electrolytes
- C. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate
- D. Sputum for microscopy and GeneXpert
- E. C-reactive protein

**Answer: D.** Microscopy and GeneXpert (or a gastric aspirate in younger children) are specific. The others are not.

### 43. Added sounds in the lungs on auscultation include all EXCEPT:
- A. Rales
- B. Murmurs
- C. Rhonchi
- D. Wheezes
- E. Crepitations

**Answer: B.** Murmurs are heart sounds.

### 44. Phototherapy should be initiated in newborns with jaundice caused by the following EXCEPT:
- A. ABO blood group incompatibility
- B. Rhesus factor incompatibility
- C. Biliary atresia
- D. Neonatal sepsis
- E. Cephalohaematoma

**Answer: C.** Phototherapy breaks down unconjugated bilirubin. Biliary atresia causes conjugated jaundice, and the treatment is surgical (Kasai operation).

### 45. Which statement is true about marasmus?
- A. It is caused by lack of proteins only in the diet
- B. Affected children are dull and gloomy
- C. Affected children have oedema
- D. There is wasting of muscles and subcutaneous fat
- E. It has a poorer prognosis than kwashiorkor

**Answer: D.** Marasmus is a lack of total energy (and nutrients), with severe wasting and no oedema. Children are alert and hungry.

### 46. The recommended newborn cardiopulmonary resuscitation for a baby born with no signs of life and one rescuer is:
- A. 1 breath to 3 cardiac compressions
- B. 1 cardiac compression to 15 breaths
- C. 1 cardiac compression to 3 breaths
- D. 2 breaths to 30 chest compressions
- E. 2 breaths to 15 chest compressions

**Answer: A.** The newborn ratio is 3 compressions to 1 breath (3:1). The wording "1 breath to 3 compressions" is the nearest option.

### 47. Complications that can occur in preterm babies include all EXCEPT:
- A. Anaemia
- B. Intracranial haemorrhage
- C. Rickets
- D. Meconium aspiration
- E. Retinopathy

**Answer: D.** Meconium aspiration affects term and post-term babies. Anaemia, intraventricular haemorrhage, rickets of prematurity and retinopathy of prematurity occur in preterm babies.

### 48. A child on treatment for TB meningitis passes red-coloured urine. The medication causing this is:
- A. Ethambutol
- B. Rifampicin
- C. Isoniazid
- D. Streptomycin
- E. Pyrazinamide

**Answer: B.** Rifampicin turns urine, tears and sweat orange-red, and it is harmless.

### 49. A 1-week-old neonate has fever and refuses to breastfeed. The most common organism causing this infection is:
- A. Neisseria meningitidis
- B. Group B streptococcus
- C. Streptococcus pneumoniae
- D. Haemophilus influenzae
- E. Staphylococcus aureus

**Answer: B.** Group B streptococcus and Escherichia coli are the leading causes of early neonatal sepsis and meningitis.
