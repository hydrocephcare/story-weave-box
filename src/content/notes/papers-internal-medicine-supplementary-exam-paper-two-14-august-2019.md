## Section A: Multiple choice questions

### 1. Which of the following drugs is likely to cause a lupus-like syndrome?
- A. Baclofen
- B. Insulin
- C. Procainamide
- D. Isoniazid

**Answer: C.** Procainamide (and hydralazine) are the strongest causes. Isoniazid is weaker.

### 2. Which antibodies are useful in making a diagnosis of systemic lupus erythematosus, except:
- A. ANA
- B. dsDNA
- C. Anti-centromere antibodies
- D. Anti-histone antibodies

**Answer: C.** Anti-centromere antibodies belong to limited systemic sclerosis (CREST). Anti-histone antibodies point to drug-induced lupus.

**Answer: D.** Pancytopenia is a blood count pattern, not a cause of small red cells.

### 8. A patient with low MCV has a high serum ferritin and low TIBC. What is the most likely cause of the anaemia?
- A. Iron deficiency
- B. Anaemia secondary to inflammation
- C. Thalassaemia
- D. Haemoglobinopathy

**Answer: B.** High ferritin with low TIBC means iron is locked in stores (chronic disease). In iron deficiency, ferritin is low and TIBC high.

**Answer: D.** Aspirin (with a statin and blood pressure control) is the standard secondary prevention for an ischaemic stroke. Warfarin is used if the cause is atrial fibrillation.

## Section B: Short answer questions (10 marks each, answer all)

### 1. A 58-year-old alcoholic presents with an upper GI bleed to casualty. List important differential diagnoses and the investigations necessary to make a diagnosis.

- **Differential diagnosis:** bleeding oesophageal or gastric varices (portal hypertension), peptic ulcer (NSAIDs, H. pylori), erosive gastritis, Mallory-Weiss tear, oesophagitis, gastric cancer, portal hypertensive gastropathy, and a coagulopathy from liver disease.
- **Investigations:** full blood count (Hb), group and crossmatch, clotting profile (INR), urea (raised), electrolytes and creatinine, liver function tests and albumin, hepatitis B and C and HIV tests, **urgent upper GI endoscopy** (to diagnose and treat), ultrasound of the abdomen (cirrhosis, portal hypertension, ascites), and an ECG and chest X-ray for fitness.

### 2. A 48-year-old female presents with an acute confusional state. What history, investigations and probable diagnosis could explain the cause of the confusion?

- **History:** onset and course, fever and headache, head injury, diabetes and insulin, drug and alcohol use, new medicines (sedatives, steroids), vomiting and diarrhoea, urinary symptoms, HIV status, liver, kidney or heart disease, and past psychiatric illness.
- **Investigations:** bedside glucose first; full blood count, urea, electrolytes (sodium, calcium), creatinine, liver tests, ammonia, thyroid tests, blood and urine cultures, malaria test, HIV test; arterial blood gas; toxicology if suspected; ECG; CT head, then lumbar puncture if meningitis or encephalitis is suspected; EEG if seizures.
- **Probable diagnoses:** metabolic (hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia, uraemia, hepatic encephalopathy, hypercalcaemia), infection (meningitis, sepsis, cerebral malaria, urinary infection), drugs and alcohol, stroke or head injury, and thyroid disease.

### 3. List the Jones criteria for making a diagnosis of rheumatic fever.

Diagnosis needs **evidence of a preceding group A streptococcal infection** (raised ASO or anti-DNase B titre, a positive throat culture or rapid test, or recent scarlet fever) plus:

- **2 major criteria**, or **1 major and 2 minor criteria**.
- **Major:** carditis, polyarthritis (migratory), Sydenham's chorea, erythema marginatum, and subcutaneous nodules.
- **Minor:** fever, arthralgia, raised ESR or CRP, and a prolonged PR interval on ECG (and previous rheumatic fever).

### 4. A diabetic patient with anasarca is admitted for haemodialysis. What are the clear indications that warrant dialysis, and what complications can arise due to dialysis?

- **Indications (AEIOU):** **A**cidosis that is severe and does not respond to treatment; **E**lectrolytes: refractory hyperkalaemia; **I**ntoxication (dialysable poisons); **O**verload: fluid overload with pulmonary oedema that does not respond to diuretics; **U**raemia with encephalopathy, pericarditis or bleeding. In chronic kidney disease, start when the GFR falls below 15 with symptoms.
- **Complications of dialysis:** hypotension and cramps, disequilibrium syndrome, bleeding (heparin), infection (catheter or access site, peritonitis in peritoneal dialysis), air embolism, anaemia, access thrombosis, hypoglycaemia in diabetics, amyloidosis in long-term patients, and cardiac arrhythmias.

### 5. A 28-year-old male presents with cough, night sweats and weight loss. What investigations are necessary to make a diagnosis of pulmonary tuberculosis, and list the medication used for its treatment, including duration and possible side effects of the drugs?

- **Investigations:** sputum **GeneXpert MTB/RIF** and smear for AFB, with culture; chest X-ray; HIV test; full blood count and ESR; liver and kidney tests before starting.
- **Treatment:** 6 months. The **intensive phase** is 2 months of rifampicin (R), isoniazid (H), pyrazinamide (Z) and ethambutol (E). The **continuation phase** is 4 months of rifampicin and isoniazid. Give pyridoxine with isoniazid.
- **Side effects:** rifampicin: orange urine and sweat, hepatitis, flu-like illness and drug interactions; isoniazid: peripheral neuropathy and hepatitis; pyrazinamide: hepatitis, gout and joint pain; ethambutol: optic neuritis (colour vision loss).

## Section C: Essay questions (15 marks each, answer 2 only)

### 1. A 45-year-old woman presents with easy fatigability, headache and dizziness. Full haemogram reveals Hb of 7 g/dL and MCV is 72 fL. What are the probable differential diagnoses, investigations and management of this patient?

**Probable diagnosis:** microcytic anaemia, most likely **iron deficiency anaemia** (heavy menstrual bleeding, poor diet or worms).

**Differential diagnosis:** thalassaemia trait, anaemia of chronic disease, sideroblastic anaemia, lead poisoning.

**Investigations:** blood film (hypochromic microcytic, pencil cells), reticulocyte count, serum ferritin (low), serum iron (low) and TIBC (high), transferrin saturation; haemoglobin electrophoresis if thalassaemia is likely; stool for ova and occult blood; pregnancy test; pelvic ultrasound for fibroids; upper and lower GI endoscopy in an older woman or if no cause is found; coeliac serology; urea and creatinine.

**Management:** assess severity (Hb of 7 with symptoms: admit if unstable or give a transfusion only if there are signs of heart failure or instability). Treat the cause (deworming, stop NSAIDs, treat the ulcer, control menstrual bleeding with tranexamic acid or hormones). Give oral ferrous sulphate 200 mg three times a day with vitamin C for 3 to 6 months after the Hb is normal; check the Hb after 2 to 4 weeks. Use IV iron if oral iron fails or is not tolerated. Give dietary advice (red meat, green vegetables, avoiding tea with meals) and follow up.

### 2. A 25-year-old man from prison is brought in for evaluation of a persistent cough with difficulty in breathing. The chest X-ray reveals a massive left-sided effusion. What are the probable differential diagnoses, investigations and management of this patient?

**Probable diagnosis:** tuberculous pleural effusion (high prison TB risk).

**Differential diagnosis:** parapneumonic effusion or empyema, malignant effusion, pulmonary embolism, heart failure (transudate), HIV-related effusion, and a hepatic or renal cause.

**Investigations:** chest X-ray and ultrasound; **diagnostic pleural tap** (protein, LDH, glucose, pH, cell count with lymphocyte predominance, ADA, Gram stain, culture, GeneXpert and AFB, cytology); Light's criteria; sputum GeneXpert; HIV test; full blood count, ESR; pleural biopsy or thoracoscopy if the cause is unclear; CT chest if needed.

**Management:** therapeutic drainage if the effusion is massive and causing breathlessness (remove no more than 1.5 L at a time). If TB is confirmed (or strongly suspected with lymphocytic exudate and high ADA), start the 6-month regimen (2RHZE/4RH) with pyridoxine; consider steroids in selected cases. Treat empyema with antibiotics and a chest drain. Test and treat HIV, counsel, trace and screen contacts, notify, and give nutrition and infection control.

### 3. A 34-year-old lady is brought in with diarrhoea and vomiting for 4 days and is now confused. She was diagnosed with HIV 4 years ago and is on HAART. What history is important to elicit to make a diagnosis, and what are the investigations and management of this patient?

**Probable diagnosis:** severe dehydration with electrolyte disturbance (hyponatraemia or hypoglycaemia) and acute kidney injury, possibly **lactic acidosis** from her drugs, or infection (sepsis, meningitis, CNS opportunistic infection).

**Important history:** drugs and adherence (which ARVs: zidovudine and stavudine can cause lactic acidosis), duration and type of diarrhoea (watery, bloody), fever, headache and neck stiffness, urine output, recent CD4 and viral load, TB treatment and co-trimoxazole, other medicines, alcohol and recent contact with sick people, and any history of seizures.

**Investigations:** blood glucose, full blood count, urea, electrolytes and creatinine, lactate and blood gas, liver tests, malaria test, blood cultures, stool microscopy and culture (including for cryptosporidium and C. difficile), CD4 count and viral load, CSF analysis and cryptococcal antigen if meningeal signs, CT head, chest X-ray and ECG.

**Management:** ABC, check glucose and treat hypoglycaemia. **Rehydrate** with IV fluids (Ringer's lactate or saline), correct electrolytes carefully (sodium and potassium), and monitor urine output. Treat the cause: antibiotics for bacterial diarrhoea or sepsis, and treat meningitis or opportunistic infection as indicated. Hold or change the offending ARV if lactic acidosis or renal injury is the cause, after discussion with the HIV specialist, and restart ART once stable. Give nutritional support, treat anaemia, and counsel about adherence.
