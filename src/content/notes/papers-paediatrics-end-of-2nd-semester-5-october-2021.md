## Multiple choice questions

### 1. Ann, 3 years old, has a 3-day cough and fever, lower chest wall indrawing, cannot feed or drink, and a chest X-ray shows right lower lobe opacities. Choose the best option:
- A. She has pneumonia according to WHO and should be given amoxicillin and reviewed in 24 hours
- B. She needs oxygen at 10 to 15 litres by nasal prongs
- C. She should be admitted and given IV crystalline penicillin and oxygen
- D. The disease is on the rise because of lack of vaccines

**Answer: C.** Chest indrawing with inability to drink is **severe pneumonia** (a danger sign). She needs admission, parenteral penicillin (with gentamicin) and oxygen if hypoxic.

### 2. A 10-year-old has difficulty breathing, bilateral diffuse wheeze, no fever, and responds well to a fast-acting bronchodilator. Most likely condition:
- A. Severe pneumonia
- B. Bronchiolitis
- C. Asthma
- D. Foreign body aspiration
- E. Tuberculosis

**Answer: C.** Reversible wheeze without fever.

### 3. Causes of pulmonary haemorrhage include the following EXCEPT:
- A. Foreign bodies in the airway
- B. Tuberculosis
- C. Systemic lupus erythematosus
- D. Obstructive sleep apnoea
- E. Factor VIII deficiency

**Answer: D.** Foreign bodies, TB cavities, lupus (diffuse alveolar haemorrhage) and bleeding disorders can all cause pulmonary bleeding.

### 4. Causes of interstitial lung disease in infants include all EXCEPT:
- A. Surfactant protein B deficiency
- B. Surfactant protein A deficiency
- C. Surfactant protein C deficiency
- D. ATP-binding cassette (ABCA3) protein deficiency
- E. Neuroendocrine cell hyperplasia of infancy

**Answer: B.** SP-B, SP-C, ABCA3 and NKX2-1 defects and neuroendocrine cell hyperplasia of infancy cause childhood ILD. SP-A deficiency is not a recognised infant cause.

### 5. The following are commonly expected in a child with cystic fibrosis EXCEPT:
- A. Failure to thrive
- B. Vitamin A, D, E and K deficiency
- C. Chronic lung disease
- D. Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency
- E. Severe vitamin B and C deficiency

**Answer: E.** Fat-soluble vitamins are malabsorbed, not vitamins B and C.

### 6. Cystic fibrosis is inherited as:
- A. Autosomal dominant
- B. X-linked recessive
- C. Autosomal recessive
- D. Autosomal dominant
- E. Multifactorial

**Answer: C.** Mutations in the CFTR gene on chromosome 7.

### 7. John's parents are both cystic fibrosis carriers. Which is true?
- A. There is a 50% probability of each child having the disease
- B. The probability of having an affected child is 25%
- C. Each sibling has a 100% chance of being affected
- D. The condition is not inherited
- E. All males in the family will have cystic fibrosis

**Answer: B.** Two carriers: 25% affected, 50% carriers, 25% unaffected non-carriers for each pregnancy.

### 8. Concerning pulmonary haemorrhagic syndromes, which is true?
- A. The bronchial circulation is low capacitance and supplies the conducting airways
- B. The pulmonary circulation is high capacitance and supplies the respiratory airways
- C. Infections rarely cause pulmonary haemorrhage and should not be suspected
- D. It is important to stop the bleeding immediately with surgery
- E. Foreign bodies can cause localised bleeding in the airways

**Answer: A.** The bronchial circulation is a high-pressure, low-capacitance systemic supply to the conducting airways, and bleeding from it is brisk. B and E are also broadly correct, so this question is loosely worded. C and D are wrong: infections are a common cause, and treatment is of the cause.

### 9. Regarding the prognosis of children with interstitial lung disease:
- A. Children with surfactant protein B deficiency have a very good prognosis
- B. Children with Heiner syndrome due to cow's milk allergy do very well once cow's milk is withdrawn
- C. The prognosis of immune-mediated vasculitis is fair and resolves without treatment
- D. Immunosuppressants should not be used because they suppress immunity
- E. Heart-lung transplant improves 5-year survival to almost 100%

**Answer: B.** Heiner syndrome is a milk-induced pulmonary hypersensitivity that resolves on a milk-free diet. SP-B deficiency is usually fatal without transplant.

### 10. The diagnosis of cystic fibrosis in the work-up is supported by which result?
- A. Sweat chloride of 30 mmol/L
- B. Sweat chloride of 120 mmol/L
- C. Stool faecal elastase of 500 µg/g of stool
- D. Sweat sodium of 140 mmol/L
- E. Sweat sodium of 20 mmol/L

**Answer: B.** A sweat chloride above 60 mmol/L is diagnostic. 30 or below is normal, and a normal faecal elastase (over 200) argues against pancreatic insufficiency. The first words of the stem are cut off in the scan.

### 11. In the management of cystic fibrosis, which statement is false?
- A. There is a role for timely use of antibiotics with good outcomes
- B. Physiotherapy improves lung function and is key in management
- C. All children develop diabetes mellitus and need insulin by 5 years of age
- D. Enzyme replacement therapy improves weight gain and stool consistency
- E. The type of organisms that colonise the child's airway matters and plays a role in reducing lung health if not treated well

**Answer: C.** Cystic-fibrosis-related diabetes appears mostly in adolescence and adulthood, and not in all patients.

### 12. Which wave on the ECG represents atrial repolarisation?
- A. R wave
- B. P wave
- C. T wave
- D. J wave
- E. U wave

**Answer: none is correct.** Atrial repolarisation (the Ta wave) is buried inside the QRS complex and is normally not seen. The P wave is atrial depolarisation, the T wave is ventricular repolarisation, and the U wave is thought to be late repolarisation of the Purkinje fibres or papillary muscles. The question appears flawed.

### 13. What is the ideal interval of the PR duration on the ECG?
- A. 1.2 seconds
- B. 3 seconds
- C. 1 minute
- D. 5 seconds
- E. 0.2 seconds

**Answer: E.** The normal PR interval is 0.12 to 0.20 seconds (3 to 5 small squares).

### 14. What does the QRS complex represent?
- A. Atrial repolarisation
- B. Ventricular repolarisation
- C. Ventricular depolarisation
- D. Atrial depolarisation
- E. Both atrial and ventricular repolarisation

**Answer: C.** The QRS complex is ventricular depolarisation. The T wave is ventricular repolarisation.

### 15. Identify the rhythm below. (ECG strip: a regular, fast, wide, sine-wave-like rhythm with no distinct P waves.)
- A. Supraventricular tachycardia
- B. Ventricular fibrillation
- C. Ventricular tachycardia
- D. Atrial fibrillation
- E. Sinus tachycardia

**Answer: C.** A fast, regular, wide-complex rhythm with no P waves is ventricular tachycardia. Ventricular fibrillation is chaotic and irregular, and SVT has narrow complexes. The strip is described, not shown.

### 16. A 2-year-old child is brought to the emergency with a very high fever and a high heart rate. The ECG (lead II) shows regular narrow complexes, each preceded by a P wave. The most likely rhythm is:
- A. Sinus bradycardia
- B. Sinus tachycardia
- C. Ventricular tachycardia
- D. Supraventricular tachycardia
- E. Ventricular fibrillation

**Answer: B.** Fever raises the heart rate. A P wave before every QRS at a fast rate is sinus tachycardia, not SVT, where the rate is usually above 220 per minute in infants without P waves.

### 17. About Hirschsprung's disease, which statement is false?
- A. The classic triad is neonatal bilious vomiting, abdominal distension and delayed passage of meconium (over 48 hours)
- B. A small proportion of cases are diagnosed in infancy with severe chronic constipation
- C. Rectal biopsy may be indicated if growth faltering or abdominal distension persist
- D. It will often present within the first 3 days of life with feed intolerance

**Answer: D.** Most babies present with delayed passage of meconium (over 24 to 48 hours) and distension, often with vomiting, and the diagnosis is made by rectal biopsy. The statement about "feed intolerance within 3 days" is the weakest of the four.

### 18. Risk factors for dehydration in acute gastroenteritis are the following EXCEPT:
- A. Age under 12 months, because of the large surface area to volume ratio
- B. Osmotic load of an exclusive milk diet
- C. Bottle feeding predominantly
- D. Frequent stools (more than 5 per day) and vomiting (more than twice a day)

**Answer: B.** Infants under a year, more than 5 watery stools and more than 2 vomits a day, and failure to tolerate fluids all raise the risk. The "osmotic load of milk diet" is not a recognised risk factor.

### 19. Cardinal signs of hypovolaemic shock include all EXCEPT:
- A. Weak or absent pulse
- B. AVPU below A, or any alteration in the level of consciousness
- C. Cold hands with a temperature gradient
- D. Capillary refill over 3 seconds
- E. Sunken eyes

**Answer: E.** Sunken eyes are a sign of dehydration. The others are signs of shock.

### 20. What is the common presentation of acute pancreatitis?
- A. Right hypochondrial pain that radiates to the back
- B. Severe abdominal pain that is initially epigastric and later generalised, with rebound tenderness and guarding
- C. Left hypochondrial pain with radiation to the neck
- D. Severe abdominal pain predominantly in the umbilical quadrant, with generalisation and rebound tenderness
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Of these, B is closest: severe epigastric pain (often radiating to the back) that can become diffuse with tenderness and guarding.

### 21. All are true about gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GERD) EXCEPT:
- A. GERD is a benign illness without any life-threatening complications
- B. Many infants with GERD can be treated successfully with conservative measures alone
- C. Symptoms are frequently non-specific, such as failure to thrive and irritability
- D. H2-blocking drugs (such as ranitidine) have a role
- E. Surgery should be done for severe cases with life-threatening aspirations

**Answer: A.** GERD can cause aspiration, apnoea, strictures and failure to thrive.

### 22. A 2-month-old boy has vomiting and no bowel movement for 4 days, with difficulty passing stools since birth, a distended abdomen, and stool palpable above the pubis. The rectum is empty on examination with no fissures, and he passes stool when you remove your finger. Which statement is TRUE about the cause of his constipation?
- A. Caused by neurotoxins produced by bacteria in the GI tract
- B. Caused by an absence of parasympathetic ganglion cells in the intestine
- C. Likely due to a combination of behavioural and environmental factors
- D. Caused by poorly functioning endocrine glands

**Answer: B.** The empty rectum with a gush of stool on withdrawal, onset from birth and distension describe Hirschsprung disease, where ganglion cells are missing from the bowel wall.

### 23. A 3-year-old girl has fever, vomiting and abdominal pain. She has right upper quadrant tenderness and hepatomegaly. Total and direct bilirubin are only slightly above normal and transaminases are high. Several children in her day care are sick. Which statement is FALSE?
- A. Most patients with this illness do not develop fulminant or chronic disease
- B. Jaundice is common in young children with this illness
- C. Highly effective vaccines exist against this disease
- D. Most commonly transmitted by faecal-oral contact
- E. None of the above

**Answer: B.** This is hepatitis A. Young children are usually anicteric or have mild illness. Jaundice is more common in older children and adults.

### 24. A 5-year-old boy has abdominal pain. Potential causes include:
- A. Pneumonia
- B. Streptococcal throat infection
- C. Testicular torsion
- D. Diabetic ketoacidosis
- E. All of the above

**Answer: E.** In children, abdominal pain has many extra-abdominal causes.

### 25. Among the hepatitis viruses, hepatitis B virus (HBV) is unique because:
- A. Liver injury is mediated through the immune system
- B. The presence of antibodies to HBV indicates protective immunity
- C. It has similarities to human retroviruses because it is a DNA virus that replicates through an RNA intermediate
- D. It is the only agent to cause fulminant hepatic failure
- E. It has a glycoprotein coat enclosing a viral nucleocapsid

**Answer: C.** HBV is a DNA virus that replicates by reverse transcription of an RNA intermediate.

### 26. Recognised complications of acute hepatitis A include all EXCEPT:
- A. Fulminant hepatic failure
- B. Hepatic adenocarcinoma
- C. Chronic hepatitis
- D. Cholestatic hepatitis
- E. Triggering autoimmune hepatitis

**Answer: C.** Hepatitis A never becomes chronic. Hepatic adenocarcinoma is also not a complication, so two options are wrong. Chronic hepatitis is the classic teaching point.

### 27. Common causes of secretory diarrhoea in children include all EXCEPT:
- A. Vibrio cholerae infection
- B. Clostridium (as printed)
- C. Giardia lamblia
- D. Cow's milk protein allergy
- E. Oral dextrose rehydration solutions

**Answer: D.** Cholera toxin and other enterotoxins cause secretory diarrhoea. Cow's milk protein allergy causes an inflammatory (allergic) colitis. The options are poorly printed, and Giardia and oral rehydration are also not classic secretory causes.

### 28. In the management of acute diarrhoeal disease in children:
- A. The preferred IV rehydration fluid is Ringer's lactate
- B. Zinc sulphate is very important to help restore gut flora
- C. Intravenous fluids are the fastest and preferred solution for rehydration
- D. IV metronidazole is recommended to treat severe cases admitted to hospital
- E. All the above

**Answer: A.** Ringer's lactate is the recommended IV fluid for severe dehydration (WHO). Zinc shortens the illness, but not by restoring flora. ORS is the preferred route, and IV fluids are for severe dehydration or shock. Metronidazole is not routine.

### 29. Bacterial causes of dysentery include all EXCEPT:
- A. Shigella dysenteriae
- B. Entamoeba coli
- C. Enterohaemorrhagic Escherichia coli
- D. Salmonella typhi
- E. Campylobacter jejuni

**Answer: B.** Entamoeba coli is a harmless commensal and a protozoan. Entamoeba histolytica is the one that causes amoebic dysentery.

### 30. The causes of chronic diarrhoea in malnutrition include all of the following EXCEPT:
- A. Bacterial overgrowth in the gut
- B. Villous atrophy with loss of functional absorptive surface
- C. Osmotic diarrhoea due to high-calorie replacement feeds
- D. Lowered overall immunity that prevents acquisition of infection
- E. Defective nutrient uptake

**Answer: D.** Poor immunity makes infection more likely, not less.

### 31. Acute infection with hepatitis B is ascertained by the following serological markers EXCEPT:
- A. Positive HBsAg
- B. Positive anti-hepatitis B core antibody
- C. Positive anti-hepatitis B surface antibody
- D. IgM anti-hepatitis B core antibody
- E. None of the above

**Answer: C.** Anti-HBs appears when the infection has resolved or after vaccination. Acute infection shows HBsAg and IgM anti-HBc.

### 32. Chronic hepatitis B infection is indicated by:
- A. Positive anti-HBc IgM antibody
- B. Positive HBsAg for more than 6 months
- C. Positive anti-HBs IgM antibody
- D. Positive anti-HBc and anti-HBe
- E. All of the above

**Answer: B.** HBsAg that persists for more than 6 months defines chronic infection.

### 33. The stigmata of chronic liver disease in childhood include all of the following EXCEPT:
- A. Spider naevi and deep jaundice
- B. Abdominal distension and ascites and hepatosplenomegaly
- C. Palmar erythema and gynaecomastia
- D. Hirsutism and gynaecomastia
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Chronic liver disease causes loss of body hair rather than hirsutism.

### 34. Regarding Salmonella:
- A. It is part of the Enterobacteriaceae family, a Gram-negative non-flagellated organism
- B. Infants are not susceptible to non-typhoidal Salmonella enteritis
- C. Transmission is strictly faecal-oral
- D. The common source is food contamination, especially milk and fruits
- E. It causes a mild self-limiting infection in children

**Answer: E.** Non-typhoidal Salmonella enteritis is usually mild and self-limiting in children. Salmonella is flagellated, infants are very susceptible, and spread is not strictly faecal-oral (animals, poultry and eggs are sources). Option D is partly true, so the question is loosely worded.

### 35. Choose the true fact about Vibrio cholerae:
- A. Cholera diarrhoea is osmotic, attributed to activation of cAMP by the enterotoxin
- B. The diarrhoea is attributed to massive ischaemic necrosis of the intestinal villi by the enterotoxin
- C. The enterotoxin causes malabsorption and hence diarrhoea
- D. Vibrio cholerae has an effective injectable vaccine
- E. It is a secretory diarrhoea due to increased cAMP and CFTR activity with massive sodium and chloride losses

**Answer: E.** The toxin raises cyclic AMP, which opens the CFTR channel, so chloride and water are secreted into the gut. The villi are not damaged, and the vaccine is oral.

### 36. Which statement is correct about persistent diarrhoea?
- A. It is of presumed infectious aetiology, starts acutely and lasts for more than 14 days
- B. It includes chronic or recurrent diarrhoeal disorders such as tropical sprue and gluten-sensitive enteropathy
- C. It is the passage of 3 or more watery stools per day for more than 2 weeks in a child who fails to gain weight or loses weight
- D. All the above facts are true
- E. None of the above facts is correct

**Answer: A.** Persistent diarrhoea begins as an acute infection and lasts 14 days or more. It does not include the chronic malabsorption disorders.

### 37. Red flags in a child with chronic diarrhoea include all of the above EXCEPT:
- A. Bloody diarrhoea
- B. Picky feeding
- C. Accompanying systemic symptoms like fever and skin rashes
- D. Continuous diarrhoea with both day and night losses
- E. Drastic weight loss

**Answer: B.** Picky eating is a behaviour issue and not a warning sign of organic disease.

### 38. In regard to ulcerative colitis versus Crohn disease, which statement is true?
- A. Crohn disease involves the rectum only and has continuous lesions with a high risk of malignant transformation
- B. Crohn disease involves the mucosa of the entire GIT with a low risk of malignant transformation
- C. Ulcerative colitis has transmural lesions that involve the entire GIT with a high risk of malignant transformation
- D. Ulcerative colitis has rectal involvement only and a low risk of malignant transformation
- E. A and C are true

**Answer: B.** Crohn disease can affect any part of the gut from the mouth to the anus, in patches, and its cancer risk is lower than that of ulcerative colitis. The question is poorly worded (Crohn lesions are transmural).

### 39. Regarding acute gastroenteritis, all the facts are true EXCEPT:
- A. In 50% of cases there is no identifiable cause
- B. Rotavirus and adenovirus are the leading causes of viral enteritis
- C. Diarrhoeal diseases kill half a million children globally every year
- D. Most diarrhoea episodes require medical interventions as they can potentially complicate with dehydration
- E. Low-osmolarity oral rehydration salts and oral zinc sulphate are all you need to manage most of the cases

**Answer: D.** Most episodes are mild and self-limiting and are managed at home with fluids, feeding and zinc.

### 40. All of the following can contribute to gastro-oesophageal reflux in children EXCEPT:
- A. Dysfunctional lower oesophageal sphincter
- B. Impaired oesophageal mucosal integrity
- C. Hiatal hernia
- D. Transient relaxation of the lower oesophageal sphincter
- E. Thickened pylorus

**Answer: E.** A thickened pylorus (pyloric stenosis) causes projectile vomiting from outflow obstruction, and it is not a cause of reflux.

### 41. Non-gastrointestinal complications of GERD include:
- A. Oesophagitis
- B. Gastric ulcerations
- C. Sandifer syndrome
- D. Persistent retrosternal chest pain
- E. Duodenal ulcerations

**Answer: C.** Sandifer syndrome (head and neck posturing) is a neurological-looking sign of reflux. The others are gastrointestinal. The stem is partly cut off in the scan.

### 42. About the treatment of GERD, choose the FALSE statement:
- A. Lifestyle modification will improve the symptoms in most cases
- B. Histamine receptor antagonists and proton pump inhibitors suppress gastric acid secretion
- C. Metoclopramide and erythromycin are prokinetics
- D. Sucralfate and dimethicone also suppress gastric acid secretion
- E. Surgical intervention is not frequently preferred for children

**Answer: D.** Sucralfate coats the mucosa and dimethicone is an anti-foaming agent. Neither suppresses acid secretion.

### 43. Lifesaving interventions that have a high impact in children include all of the following EXCEPT:
- A. Antibiotic treatment for acute intestinal infections
- B. Zinc supplementation for gastroenteritis and acute respiratory infections
- C. Low-osmolarity oral rehydration salts for acute diarrhoeal episodes
- D. Access to clean water and environmental sanitation
- E. Hand washing at the five critical times

**Answer: A.** Most acute intestinal infections are viral or self-limiting, and antibiotics are kept for dysentery, cholera with severe disease and a few others.

### 44. About the procedure of intraosseous cannulation, which statement is true?
- A. It is a lifesaving procedure that is an alternative for IV cannulation in children with severe dehydration
- B. It is performed on the anteromedial surface of the tibia shaft 2 cm below the tibial tuberosity
- C. It is performed on the anteromedial surface of the fibula a centimetre below the tuberosity
- D. All the above statements are true
- E. None of the above statements is true

**Answer: B.** The needle goes into the flat anteromedial surface of the proximal tibia, 2 to 3 cm below the tibial tuberosity. The fibula is not used. Option A is partly true, since it is used for shock when a vein cannot be found, but the wording "severe dehydration" alone is too broad.

### 45. Which statement is true regarding irritable bowel syndrome?
- A. It is a very rare condition that often affects the elderly
- B. Food-related triggers rarely cause flares
- C. Abdominal pain, distension and episodes of diarrhoea alternating with constipation are the common presentations
- D. Stool microscopy will always reveal the offending organism
- E. It hardly presents in early childhood

**Answer: C.** IBS is common, food triggers are typical, and there is no organism to find. It can occur in school-age children.

### 46. The extra-gastrointestinal manifestations of cystic fibrosis are:
- A. Steatorrhoea
- B. Pancreatic insufficiency
- C. Meconium ileus
- D. Chronic pulmonary dysfunction with recurrent acute respiratory infections
- E. All of the above

**Answer: D.** Steatorrhoea, pancreatic insufficiency and meconium ileus are gastrointestinal. The chest disease is the extra-gastrointestinal one. Option E is therefore wrong, and the question is loosely worded.

### 47. Which statement is true?
- A. Coeliac sprue and tropical sprue are one and the same disease entity
- B. Coeliac sprue is a gluten enteropathy attributed to inability to tolerate gliadin
- C. Tropical sprue is triggered by inability to tolerate gliadin in diets rich in gluten
- D. Dental erosions and brittle nails are extra-intestinal complications of tropical sprue
- E. Both coeliac and tropical sprue are self-limiting

**Answer: B.** Coeliac disease is an autoimmune reaction to gliadin. Tropical sprue is a separate, infection-related malabsorption seen in the tropics.

### 48. The diagnosis of tuberculosis can be identified using the following tests EXCEPT:
- A. ZN stain
- B. GeneXpert
- C. Bactec culture
- D. Gram stain
- E. Fluorescent microscopy

**Answer: D.** Mycobacteria do not stain well with the Gram stain. They need the acid-fast Ziehl-Neelsen or fluorescent stains.

### 49. A child has a 3-day history of a barking cough, rhinorrhoea and low-grade fever. The X-ray shows a steeple sign narrowing of the airway. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Epiglottitis
- B. Croup
- C. Severe pneumonia
- D. Tonsillopharyngitis
- E. Adenoid hypertrophy

**Answer: B.** The steeple sign of subglottic narrowing is croup.

### 50. Stephanie, 6 years, has a dramatic history of high fevers and drooling and difficulty in breathing. An X-ray after stabilisation shows a thumb sign. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Acute pharyngitis
- B. Acute nasopharyngitis
- C. Acute epiglottitis
- D. Laryngo-tracheobronchitis
- E. Severe bronchiolitis

**Answer: C.** The thumb sign is a swollen epiglottis, caused by Haemophilus influenzae type b.
