## Multiple choice questions

### 1. A 24-year-old G2P1 had her last menstrual period 9 weeks ago. She presents with bleeding and passage of tissues per vagina. The bleeding is associated with lower abdominal pain. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Threatened abortion
- B. Inevitable abortion
- C. Incomplete abortion
- D. Twin pregnancy
- E. Ectopic pregnancy

**Answer: C.** Passage of some tissue with continued bleeding and pain means that part of the products of conception has been expelled and part remains (an incomplete abortion).

### 2. Diabetic control is important before conception to reduce the incidence of:
- A. Maternal nephropathy
- B. Diabetic ketoacidosis
- C. Congenital anomalies
- D. Maternal retinopathy
- E. Caesarean section

**Answer: C.** High sugar levels during the first weeks of organ formation cause congenital malformations (neural tube, heart and sacral defects), so good control before conception lowers the risk.

### 3. Cardiac output is highest:
- A. In the first trimester
- B. In the second trimester
- C. In the third trimester
- D. During labour
- E. Immediately after delivery

**Answer: E.** Cardiac output rises through pregnancy, climbs further during labour, and peaks just after delivery when the contracted uterus returns about 500 ml of blood to the circulation. The paper's last option reads "during delivery". The question is loosely worded.

### 4. Perinatal mortality rate is defined as:
- A. The number of perinatal deaths per thousand live births and stillbirths
- B. Deaths in the first week after birth
- C. All stillbirths plus deaths in the first week after birth

**Answer: A.** It is the number of stillbirths plus deaths in the first week, per 1000 total births (live births plus stillbirths). Options B and C give only the numerator.

### 5. A patient has her last menstrual period 4 weeks ago. She denies symptoms such as nausea, fatigue, urinary frequency or breast tenderness. She thinks she may be pregnant because she has not had her period yet. She is very anxious to find out because she has a history of a previous ectopic pregnancy and wants early prenatal care. Which action is most appropriate at this time?
- A. No action is needed because the patient is asymptomatic, has not missed her period, and cannot be pregnant
- B. Order a serum quantitative pregnancy test
- C. Listen for fetal heart tones by Doppler equipment
- D. Perform an abdominal ultrasound
- E. Perform a bimanual pelvic examination to assess uterine size

**Answer: B.** A quantitative hCG is positive within days of a missed period and is the best test for a woman with a past ectopic pregnancy. Ultrasound and fetal heart tones are too early, and the examination is not reliable at 4 weeks.

### 6. An 18-year-old G2P1001 with the first day of her last menstrual period on May 7 presents for her first visit at 10 weeks. What is her estimated date of delivery?
- A. February 10 of the next year
- B. February 14 of the next year
- C. December 10 of the same year
- D. December 14 of the same year
- E. December 21 of the same year

**Answer: B.** Naegele's rule: add 7 days and subtract 3 months (or add 9 months). May 7 plus 7 days is May 14, and minus 3 months is February 14.

### 7. Passage of some but not all placental tissue through the cervix at 9 weeks gestation. Match the description with the correct type of abortion.
- A. Complete abortion
- B. Incomplete abortion
- C. Threatened abortion
- D. Missed abortion
- E. Inevitable abortion

**Answer: B.** Some products have been passed and some remain.

### 8. Fetal death at 15 weeks gestation without expulsion of any fetal or maternal tissue for at least 8 weeks. Match the description with the correct type of abortion.
- A. Complete abortion
- B. Incomplete abortion
- C. Threatened abortion
- D. Missed abortion
- E. Inevitable abortion

**Answer: D.** A dead fetus retained in the womb with a closed cervix is a missed abortion.

### 9. Uterine bleeding at 7 weeks gestation without any cervical dilation. Match the description with the correct type of abortion.
- A. Complete abortion
- B. Incomplete abortion
- C. Threatened abortion
- D. Missed abortion
- E. Inevitable abortion

**Answer: C.** Bleeding with a closed cervix and a viable pregnancy is a threatened abortion.

### 10. Uterine bleeding at 12 weeks gestation accompanied by cervical dilation without passage of tissue. Match the description with the correct type of abortion.
- A. Complete abortion
- B. Incomplete abortion
- C. Threatened abortion
- D. Missed abortion
- E. Inevitable abortion

**Answer: E.** An open cervix with bleeding means that the miscarriage cannot be stopped, though no tissue has yet been passed.

### 11. A 20-year-old G1P0 follows up after suction dilation and curettage for an incomplete abortion. She is asymptomatic, and her examination is normal. The pathology report reveals trophoblastic proliferation and hydropic degeneration with absence of vasculature, and no fetal tissue is identified. A chest X-ray is negative for metastatic disease. What is the best next step?
- A. Weekly human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) titres
- B. Hysterectomy
- C. Single-agent chemotherapy
- D. Combination chemotherapy
- E. Radiation therapy

**Answer: A.** This is a complete hydatidiform mole. After evacuation the hCG is followed weekly until it is normal, and then monthly for 6 months, to detect persistent disease.

### 12. A 22-year-old G1P0 follows up after evacuation of a complete hydatidiform mole. She is asymptomatic and her examination is normal. Which would be an indication to start single-agent chemotherapy?
- A. A rise in hCG titres
- B. A plateau of hCG titres for 1 week
- C. Return of the hCG titre to normal at 6 weeks after evacuation
- D. Appearance of liver metastasis
- E. Appearance of brain metastasis

**Answer: A.** A rising (or persistently plateaued over 3 weeks) hCG means persistent trophoblastic disease, which is treated with single-agent chemotherapy (methotrexate or actinomycin D). A plateau of only 1 week is too short, and liver or brain spread is high-risk disease needing combination chemotherapy.

### 13. A 32-year-old has abdominal pain and vaginal bleeding. Her last menstrual period was 8 weeks ago and her pregnancy test is positive. She is tachycardic and hypotensive, with peritoneal signs, and a bedside ultrasound shows free fluid in the abdominal cavity. She is taken to the operating room for an emergency laparotomy. Which is the most likely diagnosis?
- A. Ruptured ectopic pregnancy
- B. Hydatidiform mole
- C. Incomplete abortion
- D. Missed abortion
- E. Torted ovarian corpus luteum cyst

**Answer: A.** A positive pregnancy test with shock, peritonism and free fluid is a ruptured ectopic pregnancy.

### 14. Which is NOT a risk factor for an ectopic pregnancy?
- A. Fertility treatment
- B. IUD in situ
- C. Endometriosis
- D. Previous tubal infection
- E. Family history

**Answer: E.** A family history is not a recognised risk factor. Tubal damage (infection, surgery, endometriosis), an IUD, and assisted conception are.

### 15. Which is NOT a true statement with regard to a normal pregnancy?
- A. The resting heart rate increases by 15 to 20 beats per minute by the end of the third trimester
- B. The blood volume increases by 45%
- C. Polycythaemia develops
- D. There is a leucocytosis up to 18 000
- E. The bladder becomes an abdominal organ

**Answer: C.** Plasma volume rises more than the red cell mass, so the blood is diluted, causing a physiological anaemia and not polycythaemia.

### 17. Neonatal death is defined as death:
- A. Within the first 24 hours of life
- B. Within the first 4 weeks of life
- C. Within the first 1 year of life
- D. Within the first 2 weeks of life
- E. Of at least 28 weeks gestation

**Answer: B.** A neonatal death is a death of a live-born baby in the first 28 days of life. An early neonatal death is in the first 7 days.

### 18. The following changes occur in blood coagulation factors during normal pregnancy:
- A. Increase in total serum fibrinogen
- B. Increase in factor VIII
- C. Increase in factor IX
- D. All of the above
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Pregnancy is a hypercoagulable state. Fibrinogen and factors VII, VIII, IX and X rise.

### 20. The following changes in acid-base balance occur in normal pregnancy:
- A. Decrease in serum hydrogen concentration
- B. Reduction in plasma bicarbonate
- C. Increase in total base
- D. Acidosis
- E. Increase in organic acids

**Answer: B.** Progesterone makes the woman breathe faster, so the carbon dioxide falls (a respiratory alkalosis), and the kidney compensates by lowering the bicarbonate. Total base is also reduced, and there is no acidosis. Option A is also true of the slightly higher pH, so the question has more than one defensible option.

### 21. Progesterone in pregnancy causes relaxation of the smooth muscle of:
- A. The GI tract
- B. The uterus
- C. The ureters
- D. The blood vessels
- E. All of the above

**Answer: E.** Progesterone relaxes smooth muscle everywhere. This causes constipation and reflux, a quiet uterus, dilated ureters and a lower blood pressure.

### 22. The following changes occur in haemodynamics during normal pregnancy:
- A. Increase in systolic BP
- B. Increase in diastolic BP
- C. Increase in antecubital venous pressure
- D. Increase in femoral venous pressure
- E. All of the above

**Answer: D.** The enlarged uterus presses on the iliac veins and the inferior vena cava, which raises the pressure in the veins of the legs. The blood pressure tends to fall in the second trimester, and the pressure in the arm veins does not change. The question is loosely worded.

### 23. The following changes occur in the digestive tract during pregnancy:
- A. Hypochlorhydria
- B. Increase in gastric motility
- C. Increase in muscle tone
- D. Increase in pepsin secretion
- E. All of the above

**Answer: A.** Gastric acid and pepsin fall slightly, and gastric motility and tone fall too, so heartburn comes from a relaxed lower oesophageal sphincter.

### 24. The following changes occur in the urinary system during normal pregnancy:
- A. Increase in renal blood flow
- B. Increase in glomerular filtration rate
- C. Increase in the capacity of the kidney pelvis
- D. All of the above
- E. None of the above

**Answer: D.** Renal blood flow and GFR rise by 40 to 50%, and the renal pelvis and ureters dilate (more on the right).

### 25. The neonatal death rate is greatest during:
- A. Labour
- B. The first 24 hours of life
- C. The second week of life
- D. The third week of life
- E. The first 3 months of gestation

**Answer: B.** Most neonatal deaths occur in the first day, and about three quarters in the first week.

### 28. Folic acid supplementation should continue until at least ___ weeks of gestation:
- A. 12
- B. 26
- C. 36
- D. 24

**Answer: A.** Folic acid is given from before conception to 12 weeks, the period when the neural tube closes (4 mg a day for women at higher risk).

### 29. Which of the following antenatal tests is NOT routinely done?
- A. Hepatitis C virus
- B. Rhesus D status
- C. Screening for anaemia
- D. Syphilis
- E. HIV

**Answer: A.** Routine antenatal screening covers blood group and rhesus status, haemoglobin, syphilis and HIV (and hepatitis B in many places). Hepatitis C is not part of routine screening.

### 30. Where would you expect the fundus to rise to in a woman at 20 weeks of gestation?
- A. Around the belly button
- B. Halfway between the belly button and the pubic symphysis
- C. Halfway between the belly button and the breasts
- D. Up to the breasts

**Answer: A.** The fundus is at the umbilicus at 20 to 22 weeks. It is halfway to the symphysis at about 16 weeks.

## Section B: short answer questions (20 marks)

### 1. Question 1. Define prenatal care and list 3 reasons why all women should attend prenatal care clinics (5 marks).

**Definition:** Prenatal (antenatal) care is the **planned care a pregnant woman receives from conception until labour begins,** to keep the mother and the fetus healthy, detect and treat problems early, and prepare the family for the birth and the newborn.

**Three reasons every woman should attend**

1. **Early detection and treatment of problems:** anaemia, pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, malaria, syphilis, HIV and urinary infections, a poorly growing fetus, malposition and multiple pregnancy.
2. **Preventive care:** tetanus toxoid, iron and folic acid, intermittent preventive treatment for malaria and insecticide-treated nets, deworming, and prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV.
3. **Education and birth preparedness:** nutrition, danger signs, a birth plan and a skilled birth attendant, breastfeeding and family planning, and the chance to build a relationship with the health facility.

### 2. Question 2. Outline the pillars of safe motherhood (6 marks).

The Safe Motherhood Initiative has **four pillars** (a fifth, postnatal care, is often added):

1. **Family planning:** lets women space and limit their births, and prevents unwanted and high-risk pregnancies (too early, too late, too many, too close).
2. **Antenatal care:** early and regular visits to prevent, detect and treat problems, with education, screening, immunisation and supplements.
3. **Clean and safe delivery:** every birth is attended by a **skilled birth attendant** in a clean environment, with the partograph, active management of the third stage, and essential newborn care.
4. **Essential (emergency) obstetric care:** access to services for complications: antibiotics, oxytocics, anticonvulsants (magnesium sulphate), manual removal of the placenta and assisted delivery (basic care), plus caesarean section and blood transfusion (comprehensive care), with an efficient referral system.
5. **Postnatal care** (often added): care of the mother and baby in the first 6 weeks, including breastfeeding, family planning, immunisation and detection of infection and bleeding.

### 3. Question 3. Write short notes on complication readiness (5 marks).

**Complication readiness** is the planning, done by the woman, her family and the community, so that if a danger sign appears they can **act quickly and reach care without delay.** It is part of birth preparedness, and it targets the **three delays** (deciding to seek care, reaching the facility, and receiving care).

**Elements**

1. **Know the danger signs** in pregnancy, labour and after birth: vaginal bleeding, severe headache or blurred vision, convulsions, severe abdominal pain, fever, leaking fluid, reduced or absent fetal movements, swollen face and hands, and prolonged labour.
2. **Know where to go:** choose a facility able to give emergency care, and know the route.
3. **Arrange transport** beforehand (a vehicle, a motorbike or an ambulance contact), and have a plan for the night.
4. **Save money,** or enrol in health insurance (NHIF and Linda Mama).
5. **Identify blood donors** and a decision-maker who can act if the woman cannot.
6. **Arrange a companion,** and care for the children and the home.
7. **Keep emergency contact numbers,** and have the delivery kit ready.

### 4. Question 4. Outline the role of the male partner in antenatal care (4 marks).

1. **Attending the clinic with her:** for couples counselling and HIV testing, to prevent mother-to-child transmission and to hear the health messages directly.
2. **Emotional and psychological support,** which lowers stress and depression.
3. **Financial and practical support:** paying for care and transport, saving money, and sharing the heavy housework.
4. **Healthy home:** good nutrition and rest for the mother, no alcohol, tobacco or drugs, **safe sex** (faithfulness and condoms) to avoid infections, and protection from violence.
5. **Birth preparedness and complication readiness:** helping to choose a facility and a skilled attendant, arranging transport and blood donors, and **making a quick decision** to seek care in an emergency.
6. **After the birth:** supporting breastfeeding, newborn care, immunisation and family planning.
