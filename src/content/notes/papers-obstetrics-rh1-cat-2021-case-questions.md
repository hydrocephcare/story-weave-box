### Question 1. Patient W, 23, severe lower abdominal pain and vaginal bleeding for a day, 3 weeks late, very pale with abdominal distension, rebound tenderness and guarding

**(a) Most likely diagnosis (1 mark)**
Ruptured ectopic pregnancy with haemoperitoneum.

**(b) Investigations to confirm (3 marks)**
1. **Urine pregnancy test** (or serum beta-hCG).
2. **Transvaginal or abdominal ultrasound:** empty uterus, adnexal mass and free fluid in the pouch of Douglas.
3. **Haemoglobin and group and crossmatch** to guide transfusion.
4. Culdocentesis (non-clotting blood) or diagnostic laparoscopy if ultrasound is unavailable.

**(c) Five risk factors (5 marks)**
1. Previous pelvic inflammatory disease or sexually transmitted infection (especially chlamydia)
2. Previous ectopic pregnancy
3. Previous tubal surgery or tubal sterilisation
4. Intrauterine contraceptive device in situ or progestogen-only pills
5. Assisted reproduction, smoking, endometriosis, or tubal anomalies

**(d) Management (4 marks)**
1. **Resuscitate:** oxygen, two large-bore lines, crystalloids, and send blood for crossmatch and transfuse.
2. **Emergency laparotomy** (or laparoscopy if stable) with **salpingectomy** to stop the bleeding.
3. Give **anti-D immunoglobulin** if Rhesus-negative.
4. Counsel on future fertility, contraception and the recurrence risk, and give postoperative care.

### Question 2. Patient W, para 2+0 gravida 3, LMP 5 April 2021, comes for antenatal care

**(a) Due date (1 mark)**
Naegele's rule: LMP + 9 months + 7 days, giving **12 January 2022**.

**(b) Importance of antenatal care (4 marks)**
1. Early detection and treatment of complications such as anaemia, hypertension and infection
2. Identification of high-risk pregnancies and planned delivery
3. Prevention: tetanus toxoid, IPTp-SP, iron and folate, nets
4. Prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV
5. Education, birth preparedness and monitoring of fetal growth

**(c) Components of antenatal care (4 marks)**
1. **History and risk assessment:** obstetric, medical, family and social history.
2. **Examination:** weight, BP, pallor, breasts, fundal height, lie, presentation, fetal heart.
3. **Laboratory tests:** haemoglobin, blood group and Rhesus, HIV, VDRL or syphilis, urinalysis, malaria and hepatitis B where indicated.
4. **Preventive interventions:** tetanus toxoid, IPTp-SP, iron and folate, deworming, ITNs.
5. **Ultrasound** where available, counselling, birth plan and danger signs.

### Question 3. Patient X, amenorrhoea 7 weeks, lower abdominal pain, profuse vaginal bleeding, pallor, no abdominal mass, suprapubic tenderness, open cervix

**(a) Most likely diagnosis (1 mark)**
Inevitable or incomplete abortion (open os with bleeding).

**(b) Five possible causes (5 marks)**
1. Chromosomal abnormalities of the embryo (commonest)
2. Maternal infections (malaria, syphilis, brucellosis)
3. Uterine anomalies or fibroids
4. Endocrine and chronic disease: diabetes, thyroid disease, PCOS, antiphospholipid syndrome
5. Drugs, alcohol, smoking, trauma or induced abortion

**(c) Principles of management (6 marks)**
1. **Resuscitate:** IV access, fluids, blood group and crossmatch, transfuse if shocked.
2. **Analgesia** and oxytocic support where needed.
3. **Evacuate the uterus:** manual vacuum aspiration, or misoprostol for stable women.
4. **Broad-spectrum antibiotics** if there is sepsis.
5. **Rhesus-negative:** give anti-D.
6. **Counsel and give family planning** and follow up for the cause.

### Question 4. Pillars of maternal and newborn health in Kenya (6 marks)

1. Family planning and adolescent sexual and reproductive health
2. Focused antenatal care
3. Skilled attendance at birth in a facility
4. Emergency obstetric and newborn care
5. Postnatal care for mother and baby
6. Essential newborn care, immunisation and treatment of newborn illness

### Question 5. Physiological changes in pregnancy

**(a) Genitourinary (3 marks)**
1. Renal blood flow and **GFR rise by 40 to 50%**, so urea and creatinine fall and glycosuria can occur.
2. **Ureteric and pelvicalyceal dilatation,** more on the right, from progesterone and uterine pressure, so pyelonephritis is more likely.
3. **Bladder:** frequency from pressure, and displacement upwards later in pregnancy.
4. **Genital:** the uterus enlarges from about 70 g to 1000 g, the cervix softens, and vaginal discharge and pH change.

**(b) Haematological (3 marks)**
1. **Plasma volume** rises by 40 to 50% and **red cell mass** by 20 to 30%, causing physiological (dilutional) anaemia.
2. **Hypercoagulable state:** fibrinogen and factors VII, VIII, X rise, and fibrinolysis is reduced.
3. **White cell count** rises (neutrophilia) and platelets fall slightly.
4. Iron requirement rises, and ESR is raised.

### Question 6. Define (1 mark each)

- **Near miss:** a woman who nearly died but survived a complication of pregnancy, childbirth or termination within 42 days.
- **Maternal mortality:** death during pregnancy or within 42 days of its end from any cause related to or aggravated by pregnancy or its management, excluding accidental or incidental causes.
- **Perinatal mortality:** stillbirths plus deaths in the first 7 days of life, per 1000 births.
- **Neonatal mortality:** deaths in the first 28 days of life, per 1000 live births.
