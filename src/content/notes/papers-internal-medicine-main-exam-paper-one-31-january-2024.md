## Section A: Multiple choice questions

### 1. Which of the following is TRUE regarding the development of pneumonia?
- a) Pneumonia is an acute inflammatory condition within the parenchyma of the lung
- b) Immunocompromised individuals are at an increased risk of developing pneumonia
- c) Pneumonia often develops as a consequence of bacterial colonisation and microaspiration of upper respiratory tract secretions during a period of impaired host pulmonary defence mechanisms

**Answer: d.** All three statements are true.

### 2. Approximately what percentage of adult cases of community-acquired pneumonia involve both bacterial and viral pathogens?
- a) 10%
- b) 30%
- c) 50%
- d) 80%

**Answer: b.** Mixed infection is found in roughly a quarter to a third of adult cases.

### 3. All of the following are common symptoms of community-acquired pneumonia in adults, EXCEPT:
- a) Fever
- b) Bradycardia
- c) Sputum production

**Answer: b.** Pneumonia causes tachycardia.

## Section B: Short answer questions (10 marks each, answer all)

### 1(a). List likely causes of a pleural effusion (5 marks)

- **Transudates:** heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, hypoalbuminaemia, chronic kidney disease, peritoneal dialysis, hypothyroidism.
- **Exudates:** tuberculosis, pneumonia (parapneumonic effusion, empyema), malignancy (lung, breast, lymphoma, mesothelioma), pulmonary embolism, rheumatoid arthritis and lupus, pancreatitis, asbestos exposure, drugs, chylothorax and haemothorax.

### 1(b). Investigations to diagnose a pleural effusion (5 marks)

- **Chest X-ray** (blunted costophrenic angle, meniscus; over 200 mL) and **ultrasound** (to guide aspiration and show septations); CT of the chest if the cause is unclear.
- **Pleural fluid** (diagnostic tap): appearance, protein, LDH and glucose, pH, cell count and differential, Gram stain, culture, AFB and GeneXpert, ADA, cytology.
- **Light's criteria** to tell exudate from transudate (pleural/serum protein above 0.5, LDH ratio above 0.6, or pleural LDH above two thirds of the upper normal limit).
- **Blood tests:** full blood count, serum protein and LDH, albumin, liver and kidney tests, HIV test, and echocardiography if heart failure is suspected.
- Pleural biopsy or thoracoscopy for an undiagnosed exudate.

### 2(a). Typical presentation of a patient with tuberculosis (2 marks)

Chronic cough (over 2 weeks) with sputum that may be bloody, fever (often evening), night sweats, weight loss, loss of appetite and fatigue. There may also be chest pain and breathlessness, and signs such as cachexia, lymph node enlargement and crackles or signs of an effusion.

### 2(b). Radiological and laboratory tests for pulmonary tuberculosis (5 marks)

- **Chest X-ray:** upper-zone infiltrates, cavities, fibrosis, miliary pattern, hilar nodes, pleural effusion.
- **Sputum:** GeneXpert MTB/RIF (also shows rifampicin resistance), smear for acid-fast bacilli (Ziehl-Neelsen), and culture (the gold standard, slow).
- Tuberculin skin test or interferon-gamma release assay (shows exposure, not active disease).
- Urine LAM in advanced HIV; HIV test; full blood count and ESR; CT if the X-ray is unclear.

### 2(c). Management of a patient diagnosed with pulmonary tuberculosis (2 marks)

A 6-month regimen: 2 months of rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide and ethambutol (RHZE), then 4 months of rifampicin and isoniazid (RH), with pyridoxine. Observe treatment, counsel, trace contacts, test and treat HIV, and monitor sputum, weight and liver function.

### 3(a). Risk factors for infective endocarditis (2 marks)

Pre-existing valve disease (rheumatic heart disease, congenital defects), prosthetic valves, previous endocarditis, intravenous drug use, indwelling lines, dental or other procedures with bacteraemia, immunosuppression and HIV.

### 3(b). Describe Duke's criteria for diagnosing infective endocarditis (5 marks)

- **Major criteria:** (1) positive blood cultures with a typical organism from two separate sets, or persistently positive cultures; (2) evidence of endocardial involvement: vegetation, abscess or new valve regurgitation on echocardiography.
- **Minor criteria:** predisposing heart condition or IV drug use; fever of 38°C or higher; vascular phenomena (emboli, infarcts, Janeway lesions, mycotic aneurysm); immunological phenomena (Osler's nodes, Roth spots, glomerulonephritis, positive rheumatoid factor); positive blood culture not meeting a major criterion.
- **Diagnosis:** definite endocarditis needs 2 major criteria, or 1 major and 3 minor, or 5 minor criteria. Possible endocarditis needs 1 major and 1 minor, or 3 minor criteria.

### 3(c). Management of infective endocarditis (2 marks)

Take at least three sets of blood cultures before antibiotics, then give prolonged IV bactericidal antibiotics (4 to 6 weeks) guided by the organism, such as benzylpenicillin and gentamicin for streptococci or flucloxacillin for Staphylococcus aureus (vancomycin if MRSA). Surgery is needed for heart failure, abscess, persistent infection or large vegetations with emboli.

### 4(a). Risk factors for developing pulmonary embolism (2 marks)

Immobility and bed rest, surgery (especially orthopaedic), trauma, malignancy, pregnancy and the postpartum period, oestrogen (pill or HRT), previous deep vein thrombosis or PE, obesity, thrombophilia, long-haul travel, central lines and heart failure.

### 4(b). Investigations for pulmonary embolism (5 marks)

- Assess pre-test probability (Wells score); **D-dimer** if probability is low or moderate.
- **CT pulmonary angiography** (first choice); or V/Q scan if CT is not possible.
- **Leg compression ultrasound** for deep vein thrombosis.
- **ECG** (sinus tachycardia, S1Q3T3, right heart strain), **chest X-ray** (to exclude other causes), **arterial blood gas**, troponin and BNP, and echocardiography for right heart strain.

### 4(c). Briefly describe management of pulmonary embolism (2 marks)

Oxygen and resuscitation. Start anticoagulation immediately: low-molecular-weight heparin, fondaparinux or a DOAC, then warfarin or a DOAC for at least 3 months (longer if unprovoked). In massive PE with shock, give thrombolysis (alteplase) or embolectomy.

### 5(a). The most common cause of portal hypertension (3 marks)

Cirrhosis of the liver (intrahepatic, sinusoidal). In Africa, schistosomiasis (presinusoidal) is also a common cause.

### 5(b). How are causes of portal hypertension classified? (3 marks)

By the site of the block:

- **Prehepatic:** portal vein thrombosis, splenic vein thrombosis.
- **Intrahepatic:** presinusoidal (schistosomiasis, congenital hepatic fibrosis), sinusoidal (cirrhosis), postsinusoidal (veno-occlusive disease).
- **Posthepatic:** Budd-Chiari syndrome, constrictive pericarditis and right heart failure.

### 5(c). Symptoms of liver disease in portal hypertension (4 marks)

Ascites and abdominal swelling, haematemesis and melaena (varices), splenomegaly, caput medusae, hepatic encephalopathy (confusion, flapping tremor), jaundice, pedal oedema and fatigue, with spider naevi, palmar erythema and wasting in chronic liver disease.

### 6(a). What is haemophilia A? (2 marks)

An X-linked recessive bleeding disorder caused by deficiency of clotting factor VIII, which affects males and is passed on by carrier mothers.

### 6(b). Signs and symptoms of haemophilia A (4 marks)

Spontaneous or prolonged bleeding after injury, haemarthrosis (swollen, painful joints, most often knees and elbows) with later arthropathy, deep muscle haematomas, easy bruising, bleeding after circumcision or dental extraction, haematuria, and in severe cases intracranial bleeding. The prolonged APTT with normal PT and platelets supports the diagnosis.

### 6(c). Physical findings suggesting iron deficiency anaemia (4 marks)

Pallor of the conjunctivae and palms, koilonychia (spoon nails), angular stomatitis, atrophic glossitis (smooth tongue), brittle hair and nails, tachycardia and flow murmurs, with pica and, in severe cases, signs of heart failure.

### 7(a). Complications of leishmaniasis (5 marks)

- **Visceral:** severe anaemia, bleeding (thrombocytopenia), secondary bacterial infections (pneumonia, TB), splenic rupture, malnutrition and wasting, post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis, HIV co-infection, and death if untreated.
- **Cutaneous and mucocutaneous:** scarring and disfigurement, and destruction of the nose and mouth tissue in mucocutaneous disease.

### 7(b). Differential diagnosis of visceral leishmaniasis (5 marks)

Malaria (chronic, with splenomegaly), tropical splenomegaly syndrome, schistosomiasis, typhoid and brucellosis, tuberculosis, HIV and AIDS, lymphoma and leukaemia (chronic myeloid), histoplasmosis, and hepatosplenic causes such as chronic liver disease with portal hypertension.

### 8(a). Causes of prerenal acute kidney injury (5 marks)

- **Hypovolaemia:** haemorrhage, dehydration (vomiting, diarrhoea), burns, and diuretic overuse.
- **Low cardiac output:** heart failure, myocardial infarction, arrhythmias, cardiac tamponade, pulmonary embolism.
- **Vasodilatation:** sepsis and anaphylaxis.
- **Reduced renal perfusion:** hepatorenal syndrome, NSAIDs and ACE inhibitors or ARBs, renal artery stenosis.

### 8(b). Likely causes of intrinsic kidney injury (5 marks)

- **Tubular:** acute tubular necrosis (ischaemia and toxins: aminoglycosides, contrast, rhabdomyolysis, haemolysis).
- **Glomerular:** acute glomerulonephritis, lupus nephritis, vasculitis, anti-GBM disease.
- **Interstitial:** acute interstitial nephritis (drugs such as NSAIDs and antibiotics), infection (pyelonephritis).
- **Vascular:** renal artery or vein thrombosis, vasculitis, thrombotic microangiopathy (HUS and TTP), malignant hypertension.
