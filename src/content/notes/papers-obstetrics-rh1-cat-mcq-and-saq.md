## Section 1: Multiple choice questions

### 1. Fetal nutrition is dependent on:
- A. Maternal nutrient stores
- B. Maternal diet
- C. Placental exchange
- D. Maternal metabolism
- E. All of the above

**Answer: E.** Nutrients reach the fetus only after the mother's stores, diet and metabolism have supplied them and the placenta has transferred them.

### 2. Maternal mortality is lowest in mothers between what age groups?
- A. 10 to 20
- B. 20 to 30
- C. 30 to 40
- D. 40 to 50
- E. 50 to 60

**Answer: B.** Risk follows a J-curve: it is raised in adolescents and rises again after 35 years.

### 3. In a patient with a bicornuate uterus, pregnancy can give all these complications EXCEPT:
- A. Polyhydramnios
- B. Abortion
- C. Preterm labour
- D. Abnormal fetal lie
- E. Retained placenta

**Answer: A.** A bicornuate uterus causes miscarriage, preterm labour, malpresentation and retained placenta. Polyhydramnios is not part of the pattern.

### 4. Which of the following is suggestive of ovulation?
- A. Basal body temperature drop of at least 0.5 °C in the second half of the cycle
- B. Day 21 oestrogen level is elevated
- C. Progesterone level on day 10 of the cycle is elevated
- D. Regular cycle with dysmenorrhoea
- E. Oligomenorrhoea

**Answer: D.** Regular cycles with menstrual pain suggest ovulation, because anovulatory cycles are usually painless. Temperature rises (not falls) after ovulation, and the biochemical test is mid-luteal progesterone, not day 10 progesterone or oestrogen.

### 5. The follicular phase of the menstrual cycle is characterised by:
- A. Endometrial gland proliferation
- B. Decreased ovarian oestradiol production
- C. Progesterone dominance
- D. A fixed length of 8 days
- E. A reduction in aromatase activity

**Answer: A.** Rising oestradiol drives proliferation of endometrial glands and stroma.

### 6. An involuted corpus luteum becomes a hyalinised mass known as a:
- A. Corpus delicti
- B. Corpus granulosa
- C. Graafian follicle
- D. Corpus atretica
- E. Corpus albicans

**Answer: E.** The corpus albicans is the white scar left when the corpus luteum regresses.

### 7. Ovulation occurs:
- A. Immediately after the LH surge
- B. 6 to 8 hours after the LH surge
- C. After the prolactin surge
- D. After follicles ripen in the ovary
- E. 36 hours after the LH surge

**Answer: E.** Ovulation follows the LH surge by about 34 to 36 hours (10 to 12 hours after the LH peak).

### 8. Endometrial changes during the menstrual cycle:
- A. The basal layer is responsive to hormonal stimulation
- B. The functional layer remains intact throughout the cycle
- C. The increased thickness of the endometrium in the proliferative phase is due to oestrogen action
- D. Oestrogen induces secretory changes and reduces mitotic activity
- E. The zona compacta and spongiosa form the basal layer

**Answer: C.** Oestrogen causes proliferation. The functional layer is shed at menstruation, the basal layer is the unresponsive regenerative layer, and secretory change is progesterone's effect.

### 9. The following are presumptive skin signs of pregnancy EXCEPT:
- A. Chloasma
- B. Maculopapular rash
- C. Linea nigra
- D. Stretch marks
- E. Spider telangiectases

**Answer: B.** Pigmentation, striae and vascular spiders are classic skin changes. A maculopapular rash is not.

### 10. All are correct EXCEPT. Pregnancy is associated with:
- A. Increased cardiac output
- B. Increased venous return
- C. Increased peripheral resistance
- D. Increased pulse rate
- E. Increased stroke volume

**Answer: C.** Systemic vascular resistance falls in pregnancy because of progesterone-mediated vasodilatation.

### 11. In normal pregnancy, levels of all of the following hormones increase EXCEPT:
- A. Total thyroxine (T4)
- B. Parathyroid hormone (PTH) in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters
- C. Free cortisol
- D. Prolactin
- E. Oestradiol

**Answer: B.** Total T4 (more TBG), free cortisol, prolactin and oestradiol clearly rise. PTH falls to low-normal early and only returns towards the normal range later, so it is the one not clearly raised.

### 12. The increase in blood volume in normal pregnancy is made up of:
- A. Plasma only
- B. Erythrocytes only
- C. More plasma than erythrocytes
- D. More erythrocytes than plasma
- E. All of the above

**Answer: C.** Plasma rises by about 40 to 50% and red cell mass by about 20 to 30%, so the dilution produces physiological anaemia.

### 13. In the fetus, the most well-oxygenated blood is allowed into the systemic circulation by the:
- A. Ductus arteriosus
- B. Foramen ovale
- C. Right ventricle
- D. Ligamentum teres
- E. Ligamentum venosum

**Answer: B.** Oxygenated blood from the ductus venosus and inferior vena cava passes through the foramen ovale to the left atrium and on to the body.

### 14. During normal pregnancy the glomerular filtration rate can increase by as much as:
- A. 10%
- B. 25%
- C. 50%
- D. 75%
- E. 100%

**Answer: C.** GFR and renal plasma flow rise by up to 50%, so serum creatinine falls.

### 15. Fetal blood is returned to the placenta through:
- A. Hypogastric arteries
- B. Ductus venosus
- C. Portal vein
- D. Inferior vena cava
- E. Foramen ovale

**Answer: A.** The two umbilical arteries are continuations of the internal iliac (hypogastric) arteries and carry deoxygenated blood to the placenta.

### 16. Normally the pregnant woman hyperventilates. This is compensated by:
- A. Increased tidal volume
- B. Respiratory alkalosis
- C. Decreased PCO2 of the blood
- D. Decreased plasma bicarbonate
- E. Decreased serum pH

**Answer: D.** Progesterone drives the hyperventilation and the kidneys compensate by excreting bicarbonate, keeping pH near normal.

### 17. Regarding placental anatomy:
- A. The decidua capsularis forms part of the placenta
- B. The fetal side is divided into 30 to 40 cotyledons
- C. The intervillous space contains fetal blood
- D. The placenta is anatomically fully formed by 30 weeks
- E. Fetal blood vessels develop in the mesenchymal core of the chorionic villi

**Answer: E.** The decidua basalis (not capsularis) forms the maternal part, cotyledons are on the maternal side, the intervillous space holds maternal blood, and the placenta is complete by about 18 to 20 weeks.

### 18. Which of the following does NOT accurately describe the placenta?
- A. 15 to 20 cm in diameter
- B. 2 to 4 cm thick
- C. Weighs about one sixth of the term infant
- D. Derived from maternal and fetal tissue
- E. The umbilical cord originates from the centre of the placenta in almost all cases

**Answer: E.** Cord insertion is central or eccentric in most cases and marginal or velamentous in a minority, so "almost all central" is not accurate.

### 19. A sure (positive) sign of pregnancy is:
- A. Amenorrhoea
- B. Hegar's sign
- C. Nausea and vomiting
- D. Auscultation of the fetal heart
- E. Abdominal distension

**Answer: D.** Positive signs are fetal heart sounds, fetal parts and movements felt by the examiner, and ultrasound visualisation.

### 20. A first-trimester pregnancy may be terminated by:
- A. Prostaglandin inhibitor
- B. Anti-progesterone
- C. Beta-sympathomimetic agonist
- D. Synthetic oestrogen
- E. Medroxyprogesterone

**Answer: B.** Mifepristone is an anti-progestogen, used with misoprostol for medical abortion.

### 21. An ultrasound in the first trimester is done for:
- A. Placental localisation
- B. Detecting fetal weight
- C. Assessment of amniotic fluid volume
- D. Detection of fetal breathing
- E. Dating of the pregnancy

**Answer: E.** Crown-rump length gives the most accurate gestational age.

### 22. The following are normal symptoms of pregnancy EXCEPT:
- A. Backache from increased lumbar lordosis
- B. Lower abdominal and groin pain from stretch of the round ligaments
- C. Visual disturbance
- D. Calf pain from muscle spasm
- E. Increased vaginal discharge

**Answer: C.** Visual disturbance is a warning sign of pre-eclampsia and must be assessed.

### 23. A woman in early pregnancy is worried about several small raised nodules on both areolae. There are no other findings. Your immediate management is:
- A. Reassurance after thorough examination
- B. Needle aspiration of the nodules
- C. Surgical removal of the areola
- D. Mammography
- E. Radical mastectomy

**Answer: A.** These are Montgomery tubercles, normal hypertrophied sebaceous glands.

### 24. The source of progesterone that maintains the pregnancy in early first trimester:
- A. Placenta
- B. Corpus luteum
- C. Corpus albicans
- D. Adrenal glands
- E. Endometrium

**Answer: B.** The corpus luteum, rescued by hCG, until the placenta takes over at 8 to 10 weeks (luteal-placental shift).

### 25. The average weight gain in a normal pregnancy is approximately:
- A. 5 to 10 kg
- B. 10 to 15 kg
- C. 15 to 20 kg
- D. 20 to 30 kg
- E. 30 to 40 kg

**Answer: B.** About 12 kg (11 to 16 kg for a woman of normal BMI).

### 26. Which medication given before and during early pregnancy helps protect against neural tube defects?
- A. Vitamin B6
- B. Iron
- C. Folic acid
- D. Zinc
- E. Magnesium

**Answer: C.** 400 micrograms daily from before conception to 12 weeks (4 to 5 mg for high-risk women).

### 27. A probable sign of pregnancy includes:
- A. Detection of fetal movement
- B. Enlargement of the abdomen
- C. X-ray demonstrating a fetus
- D. Lower abdominal cramps
- E. Nausea in the morning

**Answer: B.** Probable signs are objective changes in the mother: uterine enlargement, Hegar's, Goodell's and Chadwick's signs, ballottement and a positive pregnancy test. Nausea is presumptive, and an X-ray showing a fetus is a positive sign.

### 28. A woman at 14 weeks had an abortion and was told it was complete. Which is true of a complete abortion?
- A. The uterus is usually bigger than dates
- B. The cervical os is open with tissue in the cervix
- C. Evacuation of the uterus is needed
- D. After a complete abortion there is minimal or no pain and minimal or no bleeding
- E. Follow up with beta-hCG for one year

**Answer: D.** All products have been passed, the uterus is contracted and the os is closed, so no evacuation is needed.

### 29. Regarding cervical incompetence, all are true EXCEPT:
- A. Typically causes painful abortions
- B. Typically causes mid-trimester abortions
- C. Treated by Shirodkar suture, best placed early in the second trimester
- D. May lead to premature rupture of membranes
- E. Can occur in a patient with a history of cone biopsy

**Answer: A.** The classical picture is painless cervical dilatation and mid-trimester loss.

### 30. Regarding incomplete abortion, all are true EXCEPT:
- A. There is a history of tissue passed per vagina
- B. The cervix is open on vaginal examination
- C. Ultrasound shows retained products of conception
- D. Ultrasound shows an intact gestational sac with a non-viable fetus
- E. Management includes evacuation

**Answer: D.** An intact sac with a non-viable fetus describes a missed abortion.

### 31. The most common cause of first-trimester abortion:
- A. Chromosomal abnormalities
- B. Syphilis
- C. Rhesus isoimmunisation
- D. Cervical incompetence
- E. Bicornuate uterus

**Answer: A.** About half of first-trimester losses are chromosomally abnormal, most often aneuploidy.

### 32. Of the proposed causes of recurrent pregnancy loss, the least likely is:
- A. Maternal trauma
- B. Maternal balanced translocation
- C. Paternal balanced translocation
- D. Luteal phase deficiency
- E. Autoimmune disease

**Answer: A.** Parental translocations, luteal defects and antiphospholipid disease all cause recurrent loss. Trauma does not.

### 33. Factors affecting the choice of methotrexate for ectopic pregnancy, EXCEPT:
- A. Size of the ectopic
- B. Presence or absence of cardiac activity
- C. Level of beta-hCG
- D. Parity of the patient
- E. Integrity of the tube

**Answer: D.** Selection depends on a stable patient, unruptured tube, small mass (under 3.5 cm), no cardiac activity and hCG below about 5000 IU/L. Parity is irrelevant.

### 34. The most common cause of ectopic pregnancy is:
- A. History of pelvic inflammatory disease
- B. Congenital anomalies of the tube
- C. Endometriosis
- D. Tubal surgery
- E. Previous sterilisation

**Answer: A.** Salpingitis damages the tubal mucosa and cilia. Tubal surgery and failed sterilisation carry the highest individual risk but are far less frequent.

### 35. After evacuation of a molar pregnancy, beta-hCG falls to untraceable levels in about 90% of patients within:
- A. 2 weeks
- B. 4 weeks
- C. 8 weeks
- D. 10 weeks
- E. 12 weeks

**Answer: C.** Most women normalise by 8 weeks (the range extends to about 12). hCG is then followed for 6 months to a year.

### 36. Acceptable management of a possible ruptured ectopic pregnancy includes all EXCEPT:
- A. Exploratory laparotomy
- B. Diagnostic laparoscopy
- C. Partial salpingectomy
- D. Total salpingectomy
- E. Observation followed by methotrexate

**Answer: E.** A ruptured ectopic is a surgical emergency. Expectant and methotrexate management are only for stable, unruptured cases.

### 37. Primordial follicle development is completed at:
- A. 8 weeks of gestation
- B. 12 weeks
- C. 16 weeks
- D. 20 weeks

**Answer: D.** Primordial follicles form from about 16 weeks and the oocyte count peaks at about 20 weeks (6 to 7 million).

### 38. A 29-year-old G2P0 at 19 weeks has heavy vaginal bleeding, a fundal height of 28 cm, a closed cervix, hCG 190,000 mIU/mL, and an ultrasound showing no fetal tissue, diffuse hydropic villi and bilateral large theca lutein cysts. The preferred management is:
- A. Bilateral ovarian cystectomies with ovarian fixation
- B. Dilatation and suction curettage with concurrent bilateral ovarian cystectomies
- C. Total abdominal hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy
- D. Dilatation and suction curettage under ultrasound guidance

**Answer: D.** This is a complete mole. Suction evacuation is the treatment, and the theca lutein cysts regress on their own after hCG falls.

### 39. The most likely karyotype of a partial mole:
- A. 46,XX
- B. 46,XY
- C. Diandric triploidy
- D. Triploidy with two haploid sets of maternal origin

**Answer: C.** A partial mole is usually 69,XXY or 69,XXX: two paternal and one maternal haploid sets. A complete mole is 46,XX of entirely paternal origin.

### 40. In threatened abortion at 15 weeks in a nulliparous patient:
- A. Pain is characteristic
- B. The internal os is often open
- C. Fainting is characteristic
- D. Vaginal bleeding is usually mild
- E. Absence of fetal movements suggests non-viability

**Answer: D.** In threatened abortion the os is closed, bleeding is light and pain is minimal.

## Section 2: Short answer questions (20 marks)

### Question 1. Define safe motherhood and outline its pillars (7 marks)

**Safe motherhood** is an initiative, launched in Nairobi in 1987, to ensure that every woman receives the care she needs to be safe and healthy through pregnancy, childbirth and the postnatal period, so that she and her baby survive and thrive.

**Pillars**
1. **Family planning:** lets women space and limit pregnancies and avoid unintended ones.
2. **Antenatal care:** detects and manages risk, gives IPTp, iron and folate, tetanus immunisation and counselling.
3. **Clean and safe delivery:** skilled birth attendance, partograph use and infection prevention.
4. **Emergency (essential) obstetric care:** basic and comprehensive services including caesarean section and blood transfusion.
5. **Postnatal care:** care of mother and newborn for the first 6 weeks.
6. **Post-abortion care:** management of complications and contraception after abortion.

### Question 2. Define the following terms (1 mark each)

- **(a) Near miss:** a woman who nearly died but survived a complication during pregnancy, childbirth or within 42 days of termination of pregnancy.
- **(b) Maternal mortality:** death of a woman while pregnant or within 42 days of termination of pregnancy, irrespective of duration or site, from any cause related to or aggravated by the pregnancy or its management, but not from accidental or incidental causes.
- **(c) Perinatal mortality:** stillbirths plus deaths in the first 7 days of life, expressed per 1000 total births.
- **(d) Neonatal mortality:** deaths within the first 28 days of life per 1000 live births.

### Question 3. Outline the components of a birth plan (5 marks)

A birth plan is made by the woman and her provider during antenatal care to prepare for birth and for complications.
1. **Place of delivery** and the skilled birth attendant.
2. **Transport:** how she will reach the facility, including emergency transport.
3. **Funds:** savings or insurance (for example SHA) for delivery and emergencies.
4. **Birth companion** and a person to care for the home and other children.
5. **Blood donor** identified in case of haemorrhage.
6. **Items to carry:** clean clothes, baby clothes, sanitary supplies, antenatal card.
7. **Danger signs** to recognise and when to go immediately.
8. **Newborn and postnatal plans:** breastfeeding, immunisation and family planning.

### Question 4. Define preconception care and list 3 reasons why it is important (4 marks)

**Preconception care** is the set of biomedical, behavioural and social interventions given to women and couples before conception to improve health outcomes for the mother and baby.

**Importance (any three)**
1. Folic acid before conception prevents neural tube defects.
2. Optimises chronic conditions (diabetes, hypertension, epilepsy, HIV) before pregnancy.
3. Detects and treats infections such as syphilis and HIV, and updates immunisation.
4. Reduces exposure to teratogens (alcohol, tobacco, harmful drugs).
5. Prevents unintended pregnancy and allows planned birth spacing.
