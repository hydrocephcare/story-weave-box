## Multiple choice questions

### 1. An alcoholic man has pale stools, dark urine, right upper quadrant pain, AST 2000, ALP 100, bilirubin 50, vomiting and diarrhoea. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Drug-induced hepatitis
- B. Viral hepatitis
- C. Obstructive jaundice
- D. Chronic active hepatitis
- E. Alcoholic cirrhosis

**Answer: E.** The history is of a heavy drinker with jaundice, and Q2 on this paper asks about treatment of the same patient (stop alcohol). An AST of 2000 with a normal ALP is really a hepatocellular picture (viral or toxic hepatitis), and the figures look misprinted for a typical alcoholic picture of AST near 200. By the history, the option closest to alcoholic liver disease is E. Obstructive jaundice would give a high ALP.

### 2. A known alcoholic with anorexia and nausea has become jaundiced, with dark urine and pale faeces and discomfort in the right hypochondrium. AST is 2000 IU/L, ALP 100 IU/L and bilirubin 75 µmol/L. The best treatment would be:
- A. Withdrawal of alcohol
- B. Expectant management
- C. Operation to remove obstruction (gallstones, tumour)
- D. Urgent liver biopsy
- E. Endoscopy

**Answer: A.** The picture is alcoholic hepatitis, and stopping alcohol (with nutrition, thiamine and supportive care) is the key treatment.

### 3. Jaundice without bilirubin in the urine is seen in:
- A. Carcinomatosis
- B. Acquired haemolytic anaemia
- C. Obstruction of the common bile duct
- D. Infective hepatitis
- E. Chlorpromazine hepatitis

**Answer: B.** In haemolysis the bilirubin is unconjugated and bound to albumin, so it is not filtered into the urine (acholuric jaundice). Obstruction, hepatitis and drug cholestasis raise conjugated bilirubin, which appears in urine.

### 4. Hepatocellular damage is indicated by raised:
- A. AST
- B. Alkaline phosphatase
- C. Alanine transaminase
- D. A and C
- E. CK

**Answer: D.** AST and ALT leak out of damaged liver cells. Alkaline phosphatase marks cholestasis.

### 5. The double-stranded hepatitis B virus can survive outside the body for:
- A. Less than 4 hours
- B. Six to twelve hours
- C. One to two days
- D. Two to seven days
- E. More than seven days

**Answer: E.** HBV survives on surfaces for at least 7 days, which is why it spreads easily through blood spills and needles.

### 6. Immunological evidence of immunity to hepatitis B is the presence of:
- A. Hepatitis B core antibodies
- B. Hepatitis B core antigen
- C. Hepatitis B surface antibodies
- D. Hepatitis B surface antigen
- E. Any of the above

**Answer: C.** Anti-HBs shows immunity, from vaccine or recovery. Core antibody shows past exposure.

### 7. The most correct statement regarding the Child-Pugh score for liver disease is that:
- A. A high-risk score is not possible with normal aminotransferase levels
- B. A high-risk score is possible without encephalopathy
- C. A prothrombin time greater than 10 seconds above normal confers extra points to the raw score
- D. It has not been validated for non-shunt and non-transplant laparotomies
- E. It was originally developed for patients undergoing hepatic transplantation

**Answer: B.** The score uses bilirubin, albumin, INR, ascites and encephalopathy, so a high-risk score (class C, 10 or more) is possible without encephalopathy. Aminotransferases are not part of it. It was developed for portal-shunt surgery, and it has been validated for other abdominal surgery. The wording of option C is loose.

### 8. A patient with a third episode of gastrointestinal haemorrhage from an unknown site should first be offered:
- A. Early endoscopy
- B. Barium radiology
- C. Laparotomy
- D. Intravenous acid-suppressing therapy
- E. A Sengstaken tube

**Answer: A.** After resuscitation, upper GI endoscopy is the first investigation for a bleed of unknown site, and it is also treatment. Barium studies and laparotomy come later, and a Sengstaken tube is only for variceal bleeding that is not controlled.

### 9. Fat digestion is facilitated by:
- A. Bile juice
- B. Pancreatic juice
- C. Gastric juice
- D. None of these

**Answer: A.** Bile salts emulsify fat so lipase can digest it. Pancreatic lipase does the digesting.

### 10. Pancreatic juice contains:
- A. Trypsin, lipase and maltase
- B. Pepsin, trypsin and maltase
- C. Trypsin, chymotrypsin, amylase and lipase
- D. Trypsin, pepsin and amylase

**Answer: C.** Pepsin comes from the stomach and maltase from the small-bowel brush border.

### 11. A gasless abdomen on X-ray is a sign of:
- A. Ulcerative colitis
- B. Acute pancreatitis
- C. Intussusception
- D. Necrotising enterocolitis

**Answer: C.** A gasless abdomen (bowel loops full of fluid) is described in intussusception and in other fluid-filled obstructions. Ulcerative colitis can show gas (toxic megacolon), and necrotising enterocolitis shows bowel-wall gas. Acute pancreatitis typically gives a sentinel loop or colon cut-off sign. The question is weak.

### 12. The most diagnostic investigation for acute pancreatitis is:
- A. Serum LDH
- B. Serum amylase
- C. Serum lipase
- D. Serum P-isoamylase

**Answer: C.** Lipase is more specific than amylase and stays raised longer.

### 13. A 45-year-old patient is admitted with suspected acute pancreatitis. He reports extreme mid-epigastric pain that radiates to the back, which started last night after eating fast food. What are the two most common causes of acute pancreatitis?
- A. High cholesterol and alcohol abuse
- B. History of diabetes and smoking
- C. Pancreatic cancer and obesity
- D. Gallstones and alcohol abuse

**Answer: D.** Gallstones and alcohol together cause about 80% of cases.

### 14. A patient is admitted with very painful mid-epigastric pain felt in the back, raised glucose, fever and vomiting. On examination you notice bluish discoloration around the belly button. This is called:
- A. Grey-Turner's sign
- B. McBurney's sign
- C. Homan's sign
- D. Cullen's sign

**Answer: D.** Cullen's sign is bruising around the umbilicus, from bleeding in severe pancreatitis. Grey-Turner's sign is the same bruising in the flanks.

### 15. The physician orders a patient with pancreatitis to take a pancreatic enzyme. What assessment finding shows that the enzymes are working properly?
- A. Abdominal girth is decreased
- B. Skin turgor is less than 2 seconds
- C. Blood glucose is 250
- D. Stools appear formed and solid

**Answer: D.** Enzyme replacement stops the fatty, pale, loose stools (steatorrhoea).

### 16. The symptoms are recurrent episodes of variable amounts of watery non-bloody diarrhoea (amounts can be massive). Physical findings show weight loss and severe upper quadrant abdominal pain with biliary tract involvement. Which condition BEST suits this presentation?
- A. HIV enteropathy
- B. Ulcerative colitis
- C. Irritable bowel syndrome
- D. Cryptosporidium protozoan parasites
- E. Giardia parasite

**Answer: D.** Cryptosporidium causes profuse watery diarrhoea and, in AIDS, biliary disease (AIDS cholangiopathy).

### 17. General management emphasises supportive care. A nutritious diet and vitamins (especially B vitamins) are important during the first days of abstinence. Alcohol withdrawal requires benzodiazepines (e.g. diazepam). However, excessive sedation can precipitate which condition that must be avoided?
- A. Bleeding varices
- B. Electrolyte abnormalities
- C. Portal hypertension
- D. Portosystemic encephalopathy

**Answer: D.** Sedatives can tip a cirrhotic patient into hepatic encephalopathy.

### 18. Which of the following is true of hepatitis C infection?
- A. Hepatitis C virus is a DNA virus
- B. Infection is a risk factor for hepatocellular carcinoma
- C. Infection is commoner in women
- D. Commonly transmitted by sexual contact
- E. Alpha-interferon is an effective treatment to cure the infection

**Answer: B.** HCV is an RNA virus spread by blood. Interferon was the old treatment with a low cure rate. Modern direct-acting antivirals cure over 95%.

### 19. In parenchymal liver disease, which liver function test is likely to be decreased from normal?
- A. AST and ALT
- B. Alkaline phosphatase
- C. Albumin
- D. Prothrombin time
- E. Gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase

**Answer: C.** The failing liver makes less albumin. Prothrombin time becomes longer, and the enzymes rise.

### 20. In obstructive liver disease, which liver function test is likely to remain normal?
- A. AST and ALT
- B. Alkaline phosphatase
- C. Albumin
- D. 5'-Nucleotidase
- E. Gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase

**Answer: C.** The liver still makes albumin early in obstruction. ALP, 5'-nucleotidase and GGT rise, and AST and ALT may rise mildly.

### 21. The replication of hepatitis B includes which of the following stages?
- A. Movement of intact virus to the cellular cytoplasm for replication
- B. Conversion of relaxed circular viral DNA in the nucleus to covalently closed circular (cccDNA)
- C. Virions produced in the cytoplasm by cellular DNA polymerase
- D. Oncogenic activity to transform liver cells

**Answer: B.** cccDNA is the template for viral replication and is why the virus is hard to clear.

### 22. Which statement about hepatitis B virus is most accurate?
- A. The most reliable indicator that a person can transmit HBV is the presence of HBsAg in the blood
- B. HBV has a circular, partially double-stranded DNA as its genome and has a DNA polymerase in the virion
- C. HBV infection induces antibody to HBcAg (core antigen) that protects from re-infection by the same strains of HBV
- D. A person in the "window period" can be diagnosed as having been infected by HBV if HBsAg is present

**Answer: B.** The best markers of infectivity are HBeAg and HBV DNA. Anti-HBc does not protect, and the window period is when HBsAg has gone and anti-HBs has not yet appeared.

### 23. Which of the following statements is NOT true about primary biliary cirrhosis?
- A. Ursodeoxycholic acid is the only approved treatment
- B. Liver transplantation is the recommended treatment of choice in decompensated cirrhosis
- C. Median age of 30 years at the time of diagnosis
- D. Strong female preponderance

**Answer: C.** It usually presents around the age of 50. Women are affected about 9 times as often as men. A is also not strictly true (obeticholic acid is approved for some patients), so the question is weak.

### 24. Identify the odd one out concerning liver cirrhosis:
- A. INR above 1.3
- B. AST/ALT above 1
- C. Thrombocytosis
- D. Small liver span

**Answer: C.** Cirrhosis causes a low platelet count (thrombocytopenia, from hypersplenism), not a high count. (The paper prints "thrombocytosis" in the third option.)

### 25. All the following risk factors are commonly associated with non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) except one:
- A. Sleep apnoea
- B. Heredity
- C. Polycystic ovarian syndrome
- D. Large bowel bacterial overgrowth

**Answer: D.** Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (not large bowel) is the linked condition.

### 26. All the following factors are known to modulate the evolution of non-alcoholic fatty liver disease EXCEPT one:
- A. Adiponectin
- B. TNF-alpha
- C. Fatty acids
- D. None of the above

**Answer: D.** All three modulate the disease, so none is an exception.

### 27. The following constitute idiosyncratic causes of bone marrow failure EXCEPT one:
- A. Drug induced
- B. HPV
- C. Commercial solvents
- D. Ionising radiation

**Answer: D.** Radiation damage is dose-dependent and predictable, not idiosyncratic. Drugs, some viruses and solvents such as benzene act unpredictably in a few people.

### 28. One of the following laboratory evaluations is wrongly matched:
- A. Bleeding time: intrinsic pathway
- B. aPTT: intrinsic pathway
- C. PT: extrinsic pathway
- D. Thrombin time: fibrinolytic pathway

**Answer: A.** Bleeding time tests platelet function, not the intrinsic pathway. (Thrombin time tests the last step, fibrinogen to fibrin.)

### 29. Identify the correctly matched statement on haemophilia:
- A. Haemophilia A: 60% of all haemophiliacs
- B. Haemophilia B: prolonged aPTT
- C. Haemophilia B: 50% of all haemophiliacs
- D. None of the above

**Answer: B.** Haemophilia A is about 85% and B about 15% of cases. Both prolong the aPTT with a normal PT.

### 30. Which statement about myelodysplasia is incorrect?
- A. RCMD: dysplasia in 2 or more myeloid lineages
- B. RAEB: good prognosis
- C. 5q- syndrome: poor prognosis
- D. RA: dysplasia in red blood cells only

**Answer: C.** The 5q- syndrome has a good prognosis. RAEB (excess blasts) carries a poor one, so option B is also wrong, and C is the clearer error.

## Short answer questions

### 1. List 5 early complications of acute pancreatitis.

1. **Hypovolaemia and shock** from fluid loss into the abdomen.
2. **Acute respiratory distress syndrome** and pleural effusion.
3. **Acute kidney injury.**
4. **Metabolic upset:** hypocalcaemia, hyperglycaemia and acidosis.
5. **Pancreatic and peripancreatic necrosis and acute fluid collections**, which can become infected.
6. Others: disseminated intravascular coagulation, paralytic ileus, and gastrointestinal bleeding.

### 2. List 5 approaches to treatment of bone marrow failure.

1. **Remove the cause:** stop the offending drug or toxin and treat infections.
2. **Supportive care:** red cell and platelet transfusion, and antibiotics for infection.
3. **Immunosuppression:** anti-thymocyte globulin with ciclosporin (and corticosteroids).
4. **Haemopoietic stem cell (bone marrow) transplantation**, especially for younger patients with a matched donor.
5. **Growth factors:** G-CSF for neutropenia, erythropoietin for anaemia, and thrombopoietin agonists (eltrombopag).
6. Androgens (oxymetholone or danazol) in selected patients.
