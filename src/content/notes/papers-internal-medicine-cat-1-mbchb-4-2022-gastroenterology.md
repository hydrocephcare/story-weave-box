## Multiple choice questions

### 1. An alcoholic man has pale stools, dark urine, right upper quadrant pain, AST 2000, ALP 100, bilirubin 50, vomiting and diarrhoea. The most likely diagnosis is:
- A. Drug-induced hepatitis
- B. Viral hepatitis
- C. Obstructive jaundice
- D. Chronic active hepatitis
- E. Alcoholic cirrhosis

**Answer: E.** The history is of a heavy drinker with jaundice, and Q2 on this paper asks about treatment of the same patient (stop alcohol). An AST of 2000 with a normal ALP is really a hepatocellular picture (viral or toxic hepatitis), and the figures look misprinted for a typical alcoholic picture of AST near 200. By the history, the option closest to alcoholic liver disease is E. Obstructive jaundice would give a high ALP.

### 2. A known alcoholic with anorexia and nausea has become jaundiced, with dark urine and pale faeces and discomfort in the right hypochondrium. AST is 2000 IU/L, ALP 100 IU/L and bilirubin 75 µmol/L. The best treatment would be:
- A. Withdrawal of alcohol
- B. Expectant management
- C. Operation to remove obstruction (gallstones, tumour)
- D. Urgent liver biopsy
- E. Endoscopy

**Answer: A.** The picture is alcoholic hepatitis, and stopping alcohol (with nutrition, thiamine and supportive care) is the key treatment.

### 3. Jaundice without bilirubin in the urine is seen in:
- A. Carcinomatosis
- B. Acquired haemolytic anaemia
- C. Obstruction of the common bile duct
- D. Infective hepatitis
- E. Chlorpromazine hepatitis

**Answer: B.** In haemolysis the bilirubin is unconjugated and bound to albumin, so it is not filtered into the urine (acholuric jaundice). Obstruction, hepatitis and drug cholestasis raise conjugated bilirubin, which appears in urine.

### 4. Hepatocellular damage is indicated by raised:
- A. AST
- B. Alkaline phosphatase
- C. Alanine transaminase
- D. A and C
- E. CK

**Answer: D.** AST and ALT leak out of damaged liver cells. Alkaline phosphatase marks cholestasis.

### 5. The double-stranded hepatitis B virus can survive outside the body for:
- A. Less than 4 hours
- B. Six to twelve hours
- C. One to two days
- D. Two to seven days
- E. More than seven days

**Answer: E.** HBV survives on surfaces for at least 7 days, which is why it spreads easily through blood spills and needles.

### 6. Immunological evidence of immunity to hepatitis B is the presence of:
- A. Hepatitis B core antibodies
- B. Hepatitis B core antigen
- C. Hepatitis B surface antibodies
- D. Hepatitis B surface antigen
- E. Any of the above

**Answer: C.** Anti-HBs shows immunity, from vaccine or recovery. Core antibody shows past exposure.

### 7. The most correct statement regarding the Child-Pugh score for liver disease is that:
- A. A high-risk score is not possible with normal aminotransferase levels
- B. A high-risk score is possible without encephalopathy
- C. A prothrombin time greater than 10 seconds above normal confers extra points to the raw score
- D. It has not been validated for non-shunt and non-transplant laparotomies
- E. It was originally developed for patients undergoing hepatic transplantation

**Answer: B.** The score uses bilirubin, albumin, INR, ascites and encephalopathy, so a high-risk score (class C, 10 or more) is possible without encephalopathy. Aminotransferases are not part of it. It was developed for portal-shunt surgery, and it has been validated for other abdominal surgery. The wording of option C is loose.

### 8. A patient with a third episode of gastrointestinal haemorrhage from an unknown site should first be offered:
- A. Early endoscopy
- B. Barium radiology
- C. Laparotomy
- D. Intravenous acid-suppressing therapy
- E. A Sengstaken tube

**Answer: A.** After resuscitation, upper GI endoscopy is the first investigation for a bleed of unknown site, and it is also treatment. Barium studies and laparotomy come later, and a Sengstaken tube is only for variceal bleeding that is not controlled.

### 9. Fat digestion is facilitated by:
- A. Bile juice
- B. Pancreatic juice
- C. Gastric juice
- D. None of these

**Answer: A.** Bile salts emulsify fat so lipase can digest it. Pancreatic lipase does the digesting.

### 10. Pancreatic juice contains:
- A. Trypsin, lipase and maltase
- B. Pepsin, trypsin and maltase
- C. Trypsin, chymotrypsin, amylase and lipase
- D. Trypsin, pepsin and amylase

**Answer: C.** Pepsin comes from the stomach and maltase from the small-bowel brush border.

### 11. A gasless abdomen on X-ray is a sign of:
- A. Ulcerative colitis
- B. Acute pancreatitis
- C. Intussusception
- D. Necrotising enterocolitis

**Answer: C.** A gasless abdomen (bowel loops full of fluid) is described in intussusception and in other fluid-filled obstructions. Ulcerative colitis can show gas (toxic megacolon), and necrotising enterocolitis shows bowel-wall gas. Acute pancreatitis typically gives a sentinel loop or colon cut-off sign. The question is weak.

### 12. The most diagnostic investigation for acute pancreatitis is:
- A. Serum LDH
- B. Serum amylase
- C. Serum lipase
- D. Serum P-isoamylase

**Answer: C.** Lipase is more specific than amylase and stays raised longer.
