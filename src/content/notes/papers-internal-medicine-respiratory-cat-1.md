## Section A: Multiple choice questions

### 1. Which bacterial pathogen is the most common cause of community-acquired pneumonia in adults?
- A. Staphylococcus aureus
- B. Mycoplasma pneumoniae
- C. Streptococcus pneumoniae
- D. Chlamydophila pneumoniae

**Answer: C.** Pneumococcus is the commonest cause of community-acquired pneumonia worldwide.

### 2. All of the following are risk factors for community-acquired pneumonia EXCEPT:
- A. Female gender
- B. Smoking history
- C. Occupational dust exposure
- D. History of childhood pneumonia

**Answer: A.** Male sex, not female, is the risk factor. Smoking, dust and earlier chest disease all raise the risk.

### 3. Approximately what percentage of adult community-acquired pneumonia cases involve both bacterial and viral pathogens?
- A. 10%
- B. 30%
- C. 50%
- D. 80%

**Answer: B.** Mixed bacterial and viral infection is found in roughly a quarter to a third of adult cases in most studies. The question gives only rough figures, and 80% is too high.

### 4. All of the following are common symptoms of community-acquired pneumonia in adults EXCEPT:
- A. Fever
- B. Bradycardia
- C. Sputum production
- D. Signs of consolidation

**Answer: B.** Pneumonia causes tachycardia, not a slow pulse.

### 5. In adults with community-acquired pneumonia, which is NOT among the minor criteria suggesting ICU admission?
- A. Uraemia
- B. Leukopenia
- C. Decreased respiratory rate
- D. Low PaO2/FiO2 ratio

**Answer: C.** The minor criteria include a respiratory rate of 30 or more (increased), PaO2/FiO2 of 250 or less, multilobar infiltrates, confusion, uraemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, hypothermia and hypotension.

### 6. The following pleural fluid characteristics imply an exudative effusion EXCEPT:
- A. Pleural/serum protein ratio greater than 0.5
- B. Pleural pH of 7.40
- C. Pleural/serum LDH ratio greater than 0.6
- D. WBC content of 6000

**Answer: B.** A normal pH of about 7.40 goes with a transudate. Exudates usually have a lower pH.

### 7. A pleural effusion associated with pulmonary embolism can be:
- A. An exudate
- B. A transudate
- C. Bloody
- D. All of the above

**Answer: D.** Effusions in pulmonary embolism may be either type, and are often blood-stained.

### 8. Pleural effusion in rheumatoid or collagen vascular disease is all of the following EXCEPT:
- A. Often associated with onset of joint symptoms
- B. Has a low pH and glucose
- C. Often bilateral
- D. Empyema can occur in this setting

**Answer: C.** Rheumatoid effusions are usually unilateral, with very low glucose and pH. Lupus effusions are more often bilateral.

### 9. Pleural effusions that occur secondarily to ________ are most often treated with pleurodesis.
- A. Ascites
- B. Congestive heart failure
- C. Nephrotic syndrome
- D. Malignancy

**Answer: D.** Recurrent malignant effusions are the main indication for pleurodesis. The other causes are treated at the source.

### 10. Which classic exudate can present as a transudate?
- A. Malignancy
- B. CHF
- C. Infection
- D. Nephrotic syndrome

**Answer: A.** Malignant effusions are exudates but are occasionally transudates (lymphatic obstruction or collapsed lung). CHF and nephrotic syndrome are classic transudates.

### 11. First-line management of a patient with CHF who presents to the emergency room with bilateral pleural effusion:
- A. Chest tube
- B. Diuretics
- C. Beta blockers
- D. Nitrates

**Answer: B.** Treat the heart failure with diuretics and the effusions usually clear. A chest tube is not needed first.

### 12. Fluid from a pleural effusion shows lymphocyte dominance, indicating:
- A. Acute infection
- B. Cirrhosis
- C. TB
- D. Haemorrhagic pleural effusion

**Answer: C.** A lymphocytic exudate (with high ADA) should make you think of tuberculosis, and also malignancy.

### 13. Which disease is included in the umbrella term COPD?
- A. Emphysema
- B. Chronic bronchitis
- C. Lung cancer
- D. A and B

**Answer: D.**

### 14. In which age group are people most likely to have symptoms of COPD?
- A. 15 to 24
- B. 25 to 34
- C. 35 to 39
- D. 40 and older

**Answer: D.**

### 15. Long-term exposure to which can increase the risk of COPD?
- A. Airborne chemicals
- B. Pollutants
- C. Lung irritants
- D. All of the above

**Answer: D.**

### 16. Which of the following can help improve your health if you have COPD?
- A. Not smoking
- B. Exercising regularly
- C. Drinking alcohol
- D. A and B

**Answer: D.** Stopping smoking is the one intervention that slows decline.

### 17. Environmental risk factors for the development of asthma include:
- A. Socioeconomic status
- B. Allergen exposure
- C. Exposure to secondhand tobacco smoke in infancy
- D. All of the above

**Answer: D.**

### 18. What is a common symptom of asthma?
- A. Wheezing
- B. Whistling
- C. Full breaths
- D. Snoring

**Answer: A.**

### 19. What value is measured by spirometry?
- A. Residual volume
- B. Total lung capacity
- C. Functional residual capacity
- D. Forced expiratory volume

**Answer: D.** Spirometry measures FEV1 and FVC. Residual volume and total lung capacity need body plethysmography or gas dilution.

### 20. What drug therapy should be available to ALL asthma patients (intermittent and persistent)?
- A. Low-dose ICS
- B. PRN SABA
- C. LABA
- D. Leukotriene antagonist

**Answer: B.** A quick-relief inhaler (as-needed short-acting beta agonist) is for everyone. Current GINA advice prefers as-needed ICS-formoterol, but SABA is the expected answer here.

### 21. Workers exposed to asbestos are at risk of which type of cancer?
- A. Mesothelioma
- B. Bladder cancer
- C. Bone cancer
- D. Bronchoalveolar carcinoma

**Answer: A.** Asbestos causes mesothelioma and also lung cancer, especially in smokers.

### 22. A 28-year-old mine worker has dyspnoea and blackish sputum, and has rheumatoid arthritis. Most probable diagnosis?
- A. Adult respiratory distress syndrome
- B. Hyaline membrane disease
- C. Goodpasture syndrome
- D. Caplan syndrome

**Answer: D.** Caplan syndrome is rheumatoid arthritis with pulmonary nodules in coal (or silica or asbestos) workers.

### 23. Which is an indirect injury to the lung?
- A. Fat embolism
- B. Sepsis
- C. Pneumonia
- D. Trauma

**Answer: B.** Sepsis is the commonest indirect cause of ARDS, acting through the blood. Pneumonia is a direct injury. Fat embolism and distant trauma are also indirect, so the question is weak.

### 24. Which is an asymptomatic accumulation of coal pigment in the lung without cellular reaction?
- A. Coal worker's pneumoconiosis
- B. Silicosis
- C. Anthracosis
- D. Asbestosis

**Answer: C.**

### 25. All are true about sarcoidosis EXCEPT:
- A. Characterised by caseating or necrotising granulomas in affected organs
- B. Unknown cause
- C. Affects all races
- D. Affects both sexes equally

**Answer: A.** Sarcoid granulomas are non-caseating.

### 26. Workers who handle unprocessed cotton are at risk of developing:
- A. Coccidioidomycosis
- B. Byssinosis
- C. Pertussis
- D. Sarcoidosis

**Answer: B.**

### 27. All are true about ARDS EXCEPT:
- A. It generally arises in persons with serious comorbidities
- B. It may be life threatening
- C. It is a common complication of anaesthesia
- D. Some patients suffer permanent lung damage

**Answer: C.** ARDS follows sepsis, pneumonia, aspiration and trauma. It is rare after anaesthesia.

### 28. A 40-year-old woman has progressive shortness of breath over 4 months. Examination is normal. The chest X-ray shows bilateral hilar adenopathy and a mild diffuse infiltrate. Most likely diagnosis?
- A. Interstitial pulmonary fibrosis (IPF)
- B. Sarcoidosis
- C. Wegener's granulomatosis
- D. Asbestosis

**Answer: B.** Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy with a normal examination is typical of sarcoidosis.

### 29. In interstitial lung diseases, lung function tests most often show:
- A. Reduced carbon monoxide diffusing capacity (DLCO)
- B. Increased total lung capacity
- C. Airflow obstruction
- D. Elevated arterial PCO2

**Answer: A.** The pattern is restrictive (low TLC and FVC, normal or high FEV1/FVC) with a low DLCO. PCO2 rises only very late.

### 30. Common medications that can cause ILD include all of the following EXCEPT:
- A. Methotrexate
- B. Nitrofurantoin
- C. Amiodarone
- D. Furosemide

**Answer: D.**

## Section B: Short answer questions (10 marks each)

### 1. A 35-year-old man has cough, difficulty breathing and fever for 5 days. List the likely diagnosis, the differential diagnosis and the important investigations.

- **Likely diagnosis:** community-acquired pneumonia (lobar or bronchopneumonia).
- **Differential:** acute bronchitis, tuberculosis (especially with HIV), pulmonary embolism, lung abscess, empyema, asthma or COPD exacerbation, and HIV-related pneumonia (Pneumocystis).
- **Investigations:** chest X-ray (consolidation); full blood count and CRP; blood cultures and sputum for Gram stain, culture and GeneXpert or AFB; urea and electrolytes; pulse oximetry or arterial blood gas; HIV test; pleural fluid analysis if there is an effusion. Severity can be scored with CURB-65.

### 2. A patient has progressive breathlessness and now breathes hard at rest. A chest X-ray shows a pleural effusion. Elucidate likely causes, investigations, and the use of Light's criteria.

- **Likely causes:** transudates: heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome. Exudates: tuberculosis, pneumonia (parapneumonic effusion or empyema), malignancy, pulmonary embolism, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, pancreatitis.
- **Investigations:** chest X-ray and ultrasound (to guide tapping), diagnostic thoracentesis (appearance, protein, LDH, glucose, pH, cell count, ADA, Gram stain, culture, AFB or GeneXpert, cytology), blood tests (protein, LDH, albumin), HIV test, echocardiogram, CT of the chest if the cause is unclear.
- **Light's criteria:** the effusion is an **exudate** if any one is true: pleural/serum protein above 0.5; pleural/serum LDH above 0.6; pleural LDH above two thirds of the upper normal serum limit. Otherwise it is a transudate. It separates the two groups of causes, and about 25% of heart-failure effusions on diuretics are misclassified (check the serum-effusion albumin gradient above 1.2 g/dL).

### 3. A 55-year-old man has had a cough for 3 weeks not responding to antibiotics, with night sweats and weight loss. What is the likely diagnosis? How will you confirm it and manage him?

- **Likely diagnosis:** pulmonary tuberculosis.
- **Confirm:** sputum GeneXpert MTB/RIF (first choice) and sputum smear for AFB with culture; chest X-ray (upper-lobe infiltrates, cavities); HIV test; consider urine LAM in HIV.
- **Management:** notify and register. Give the standard 6-month regimen: 2 months of rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide and ethambutol (RHZE), then 4 months of rifampicin and isoniazid (RH), with pyridoxine. Do direct observed therapy, counsel, trace contacts, start co-trimoxazole and antiretrovirals if HIV positive, give nutritional support, and monitor sputum, weight and liver function.

### 4. A 26-year-old woman has wheezed since childhood and now has difficulty breathing. What is the likely diagnosis? List 4 investigations needed to make the diagnosis.

- **Likely diagnosis:** bronchial asthma (acute exacerbation).
- **Investigations:**
  1. Spirometry with reversibility testing (FEV1 rises by 12% and 200 mL or more after bronchodilator).
  2. Peak expiratory flow monitoring (diurnal variation above 20%).
  3. Chest X-ray, to exclude pneumothorax, pneumonia and other causes.
  4. Allergy testing: skin prick tests or specific IgE, plus eosinophil count.
  5. Others: FeNO and methacholine challenge if the diagnosis is doubtful.

### 5. A 45-year-old man, driving, was hit on the side by a lorry and landed in a ditch. He has severe chest pain and difficulty breathing, no overt bleeding but bruising of the chest. What is the diagnosis? How do you confirm it and what is the management?

- **Likely diagnosis:** blunt chest trauma with a **pulmonary contusion**, rib fractures with flail chest, and possibly a haemothorax or pneumothorax.
- **Confirm:** primary survey (ATLS) with examination for paradoxical chest movement and air entry. Chest X-ray and bedside ultrasound (eFAST). CT of the chest if stable. Arterial blood gas, pulse oximetry and ECG (to exclude cardiac contusion).
- **Management:** airway, breathing and circulation. High-flow oxygen. Two large IV lines and fluids with care (avoid overload in contusion). Analgesia (including regional blocks) to allow deep breathing. Chest drain for haemothorax or pneumothorax. Ventilate (non-invasive or intubation) if respiratory failure or severe flail chest. Admit to a high-dependency unit and monitor for ARDS.
