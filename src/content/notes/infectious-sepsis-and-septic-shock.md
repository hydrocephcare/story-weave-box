# Sepsis and septic shock — Year 4 Internal Medicine (Infectious Disease) Notes

*Clinical reasoning notes. Sepsis kills by delay: time to antibiotics and fluids matters more than the perfect diagnosis.*

**The reasoning chain:** recognise sepsis early → find the source → give the first hour's care (the "Sepsis Six") → choose antibiotics for the likely organism and local resistance → source control → watch for organ failure.


![Patient with severe septic shock and ischemia zones on the skin in microthrombs. Emergency doc, CC BY-SA 4.0](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Sepsis-Mikrothomben1.JPG/960px-Sepsis-Mikrothomben1.JPG)

## 1. Definitions (Sepsis-3)

- **Sepsis:** life-threatening **organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated response to infection**. In practice: suspected infection **plus an acute rise in SOFA score of 2 or more**.
- **Septic shock:** sepsis with **persistent hypotension needing vasopressors to keep the mean arterial pressure at or above 65 mmHg and a lactate above 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluids.**
- **Bedside screen (qSOFA):** two or more of **respiratory rate 22 or more, altered mentation, systolic blood pressure 100 or less**. It flags patients at risk: it is a warning, not a diagnosis.

## 2. Find the source

| Source | Clues |
|---|---|
| **Lungs** | Cough, breathlessness, crackles, consolidation on X-ray |
| **Urine** | Dysuria, loin pain, catheter, pyuria |
| **Abdomen** | Peritonism, jaundice (cholangitis), diarrhoea, perforation, abscess |
| **Skin and soft tissue** | Cellulitis, necrotising fasciitis (pain out of proportion), wounds, injection sites |
| **CNS** | Headache, neck stiffness, rash (meningococcus) |
| **Blood** | Endocarditis, line infection, malaria |
| **Obstetric and gynaecological** | Septic abortion, puerperal sepsis, pelvic inflammatory disease |

**Think local:** **malaria**, **typhoid**, **TB**, **HIV with opportunistic infection**, **meningitis** and **puerperal sepsis** must be on the list in many settings, and are easily missed when the focus is on bacteria alone.

## 3. Investigate with purpose

| Test | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Blood cultures (before antibiotics, two sets)** | Which organism, and which antibiotic works? |
| **Lactate** | How badly is tissue perfusion failing? Above 2 mmol/L is sepsis-range; above 4 is severe |
| **Full haemogram** | White cells high or very low (a bad sign), platelets low (DIC) |
| **Urea, creatinine, electrolytes, liver tests, glucose** | Organ dysfunction; hypoglycaemia is common |
| **Clotting screen** | Disseminated intravascular coagulation |
| **Blood gas** | Acidosis and oxygenation |
| **Urine culture, malaria test, HIV test, chest X-ray** | Source |
| **Lumbar puncture** (if no contraindication) | Meningitis |
| **CT or ultrasound** | Collection or obstruction needing drainage |

**Where results can mislead:** **a normal white count does not exclude sepsis**; elderly, diabetic and immunosuppressed patients may have **no fever**; lactate may rise from **metformin, seizures or liver disease**, not only from shock.

## 4. Management: the first hour (Sepsis Six)

1. **Give oxygen** to keep saturations above 94%.
2. **Take blood cultures** (and other cultures) **before** antibiotics, but **do not delay** antibiotics for them.
3. **Give broad-spectrum IV antibiotics within one hour**, chosen for the likely source and local resistance, then **narrow** when cultures return (**de-escalation**).
4. **Give IV fluids:** a bolus of **balanced crystalloid**, then reassess. Stop when the patient is no longer fluid-responsive. Too much fluid causes pulmonary oedema.
5. **Check lactate** and repeat it to see the response.
6. **Monitor urine output** (catheter) and vital signs closely.

**If the patient stays hypotensive after fluids:** start a **vasopressor (noradrenaline first line)** to keep the mean arterial pressure at or above 65 mmHg, and move to a high-dependency or intensive care area.

**Source control:** drain pus, remove an infected line or catheter, debride dead tissue, relieve obstruction (infected obstructed kidney, cholangitis). **No antibiotic can clear an undrained abscess.**

**Other:** treat hypoglycaemia, give thromboprophylaxis, and consider **hydrocortisone** in refractory shock.

## 5. Antibiotic choice: reason it through

- Match the **likely organisms for the source**, then **local resistance patterns**.
- **Community-onset sepsis:** a third-generation cephalosporin (such as ceftriaxone) is a common start; add cover for **atypical organisms, anaerobes (intra-abdominal sepsis)** or **MRSA** when the source suggests it.
- **Hospital-acquired sepsis** or neutropenia: broader cover (an anti-pseudomonal β-lactam or a carbapenem).
- **Malaria endemic area:** treat severe malaria with **IV artesunate** alongside antibiotics until it is excluded.
- Review at **48 to 72 hours** with culture results, and stop at the shortest effective course.

## 6. Complications

Acute kidney injury, ARDS, DIC, liver dysfunction, hypoglycaemia, adrenal insufficiency, ischaemic bowel, and **multi-organ failure**.

## Practice questions

**1.** A patient has suspected pneumonia, RR 28, BP 85/50 and is confused. The most important next step is:
A. CT chest · B. Wait for culture results · **C. IV antibiotics and fluids within the hour, with blood cultures taken first if quick** · D. Oral antibiotics · E. Discharge
*Answer: C.*

**2.** Septic shock is defined by sepsis plus:
A. Fever above 39 °C · B. A raised white cell count · C. Positive blood culture · **D. Vasopressors needed to keep MAP at 65 mmHg or above, with lactate above 2 mmol/L despite fluids** · E. Two organ failures
*Answer: D.*

**3.** After fluids, an infected obstructed kidney is still septic. What will make the biggest difference?
A. A third antibiotic · B. Steroids · C. More saline · D. Oral fluids · **E. Urgent drainage (stent or nephrostomy)**
*Answer: E. Source control.*

**4.** In sub-Saharan Africa, a febrile, hypotensive patient with a normal-looking chest and urine should also be tested for:
**A. Malaria** · B. Gout · C. Hypothyroidism · D. Osteoporosis · E. Migraine
*Answer: A.*
