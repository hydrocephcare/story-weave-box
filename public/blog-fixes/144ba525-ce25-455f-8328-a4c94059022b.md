## Clinical Biochemistry and Laboratory Testing

Clinical biochemistry involves the analysis of bodily fluids, primarily blood and urine, to diagnose and monitor diseases. Laboratory testing is a critical component of the diagnostic process, providing objective data to support clinical findings.

## Key Areas of Focus

- **Electrolyte and acid-base balance**  
- **Renal function tests** (e.g., Creatinine, Urea, GFR)  
- **Liver function tests** (e.g., ALT, AST, Bilirubin, Albumin)  
- **Endocrine function** (e.g., Thyroid hormones, Glucose, Insulin)  
- **Lipid profiles** (e.g., Cholesterol, Triglycerides)  
- **Cardiac markers** (e.g., Troponin, CK-MB)

## Interpretation of Results

Interpretation of results requires an understanding of reference ranges, biological variation, and the pathophysiology of various disease states. Accuracy and precision in the laboratory are maintained through rigorous quality control and quality assurance protocols.
