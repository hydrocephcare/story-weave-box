# Hypertension

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Cardiology**

> **In one line:** hypertension is a sustained rise in blood pressure (BP) that silently damages the heart, brain, kidneys, eyes and vessels. Over 90% is primary (no single cause, from genes plus environment, via sympathetic and renin-angiotensin-aldosterone activation and salt retention); the rest is secondary and should be sought in the young or in resistant disease. Treat with lifestyle change plus a thiazide, CCB or ACE inhibitor / ARB, aiming below 130/80 mmHg.

## What you must know

- More than **90% of hypertension is primary**; secondary causes are renal, endocrine, drugs, coarctation and pregnancy.
- **Never diagnose on a single reading.** Confirm with repeated office readings, home monitoring or **24-hour ambulatory BP monitoring (ABPM)**; this excludes **white coat hypertension**.
- **Hypertensive urgency:** BP at or above 180/110 with **no** acute end-organ damage. **Emergency:** at or above 180/110 **with** acute end-organ damage.
- ACC/AHA stage 1 is **130 to 139 / 80 to 89**, stage 2 is **140/90 or more**. Target is **below 130/80** (less strict in the very elderly and frail).
- First-line classes: **thiazide, ACE inhibitor/ARB, calcium channel blocker**. Beta blockers are **not** first-line monotherapy without another indication.
- In **black patients** use a thiazide or CCB first; in **diabetes or proteinuric CKD** use an ACE inhibitor or ARB.
- **Resistant hypertension:** uncontrolled on 3 drugs including a diuretic; check adherence, then look for secondary causes and add **spironolactone**.
- Always **measure BP in both arms** (a difference suggests subclavian stenosis, peripheral arterial disease or aortic dissection).

## 1. Definition and physiology

- Blood pressure is the **lateral pressure exerted by blood on vessel walls**.
- It depends on: **arteriolar tone**, **cardiac output** (contractility, systolic pressure), **intravascular volume**, **elasticity of vessels**, and **autonomic, hormonal and neural output**.
- Hypertension matters because it is **very common**, greatly raises the risk of **cardiovascular disease, stroke and other major illness**, and in younger patients may point to an **underlying disease**.

### Ways of classifying hypertension

| Basis | Categories |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | Normal, elevated BP, stage 1, stage 2; (older: mild, moderate, severe) |
| **Crisis** | Urgency, emergency; malignant and accelerated hypertension |
| **Cardiac cycle** | Systolic, diastolic (isolated systolic hypertension in the elderly) |
| **Aetiology** | Primary, secondary |
| **Duration** | Sustained, transient |

## 2. Aetiology and pathophysiology

### Primary hypertension

- More than **90%** of cases. "Essential hypertension" is an old term, from the early 20th century notion that older people living longer had higher BP; it is no longer favoured.
- **Clusters in families**; arises from complex interaction of genetic and environmental factors.
- Genes identified so far regulate **renal salt and water handling**.
- Main mechanisms: activation of the **sympathetic nervous system** and the **renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS)**.
- **Endothelial dysfunction, increased vascular reactivity and vascular remodelling** may be causes as well as consequences. Increased **vascular stiffness** causes isolated systolic hypertension in the elderly.
- Risk factors: obesity, high salt intake, alcohol, low physical activity, smoking, stress, family history, age, and diabetes.

### Secondary hypertension

| Group | Causes |
|---|---|
| **Renal** | Renal parenchymal disease; renal vascular disease (renal artery stenosis) |
| **Endocrine** | **Phaeochromocytoma**, **Cushing's syndrome**, **primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn's)**, glucocorticoid-remediable hyperaldosteronism, hyperparathyroidism, acromegaly, primary hypothyroidism, thyrotoxicosis, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, **Liddle's syndrome** |
| **Drugs** | Oestrogens, anabolic steroids, **NSAIDs**, carbenoxolone, sympathomimetics (also cocaine, liquorice, ciclosporin, steroids) |
| **Others** | Obesity, **coarctation of the aorta**, **pregnancy (pre-eclampsia)**, obstructive sleep apnoea |

### Clinical clues for secondary hypertension

| Clue | Think of |
|---|---|
| Family or personal history of kidney disease; blood in urine, swelling | Renal disease |
| Episodic headache, palpitations, sweating, paroxysmal hypertension | **Phaeochromocytoma** |
| Central obesity, facial rounding and redness, easy bruising, diabetes, muscle weakness; supraclavicular and dorsocervical fat pads, wide purple striae, proximal weakness | **Cushing's syndrome** |
| Hypertension with **hypokalaemia** | **Primary hyperaldosteronism** |
| Low-trauma fracture, kidney stones, anorexia, abdominal pain, psychiatric disturbance ("bones, stones, groans, moans") | Hyperparathyroidism |
| Weight gain, fatigue, cold intolerance, constipation; coarse thick skin, leg swelling, periorbital puffiness | Hypothyroidism |
| Weight loss, palpitations, tremor, heat intolerance, diarrhoea; thyromegaly, fine tremor, sweaty palms | Thyrotoxicosis |
| Onset after 20 weeks of gestation | Pre-eclampsia |
| Abdominal bruit | Renal artery stenosis |
| Drug history | Drug-induced |

> **Exam tip:** think of secondary causes when hypertension starts **young (under 30)**, is **severe or resistant**, has an **abrupt onset**, or is accompanied by hypokalaemia.

## 3. Measuring blood pressure and diagnosis

- Use a **well-calibrated** machine.
- The cuff bladder should cover **at least 80% of the arm circumference**.
- Patient **rested at least 5 minutes**, and **at least 30 minutes** after coffee or smoking.
- **Measure in both arms.** A difference between the arms suggests **subclavian stenosis, peripheral arterial disease or aortic dissection**.
- A single high reading in the clinic does not prove hypertension (**white coat hypertension**): confirm with **home BP monitoring** or **24-hour ABPM**.
- Diagnosis needs **multiple measurements**, except in hypertensive emergency, urgency or malignant hypertension.
- **Screening:** all adults aged 18 and above every year, or every 6 months if there are risk factors or systolic BP 120 to 130.

### Stages (ACC/AHA)

| Category | Systolic | Diastolic |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | below 120 | and below 80 |
| Elevated | 120 to 129 | and below 80 |
| Stage 1 | 130 to 139 | or 80 to 89 |
| Stage 2 | 140 or more | or 90 or more |
| Hypertensive crisis | 180 or more | or 110 or more |

- **Hypertensive urgency:** at least 180/110, **no acute end-organ damage**.
- **Hypertensive emergency:** at least 180/110 **with acute end-organ damage** (encephalopathy, stroke, MI, acute pulmonary oedema, aortic dissection, acute kidney injury, eclampsia).

> **Check:** the revision question about a person with a persistent BP of 150/70 mmHg asymptomatic: the systolic of 150 puts him in **stage 2 hypertension** (isolated systolic hypertension).

## 4. Evaluation

### History

- Previous high BP readings; risk factors; **dietary history** (salt, alcohol); family history of hypertension and cardiovascular disease.
- Previous therapies: BP response and side-effects.
- History of end-organ damage (stroke, angina, heart failure, renal disease, visual change).

### Examination

- Weight, height, BMI; BP in both arms; peripheral pulses (radiofemoral delay in coarctation).
- **Fundoscopy** (hypertensive retinopathy), apical impulse (displaced or heaving), heart sounds (S4, loud A2).
- Abdominal bruits; Cushingoid features.

### Investigations

| Test | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Urea, creatinine, electrolytes | Renal function; hypokalaemia |
| Urinalysis for blood, protein, glucose | Renal disease, diabetes |
| Fasting glucose | Diabetes |
| Total, HDL and LDL cholesterol, triglycerides | Cardiovascular risk |
| ECG | Left ventricular hypertrophy, ischaemia, arrhythmia |
| Echocardiogram | LV hypertrophy and function |

Targeted tests for suspected **secondary** causes: chest X-ray (rib notching in coarctation), renal ultrasound, renal angiography, **plasma or urinary catecholamines/metanephrines**, urinary cortisol and **dexamethasone suppression test**, **plasma renin activity and aldosterone**, thyroid function tests.

## 5. Management

### How low to target?

- Traditional goal: below 140/90. **ACC/AHA recommends below 130/80.**
- JNC 7 used below 130/80 for diabetes and CKD and below 140/90 for others.
- JNC 8 is the writers' opinion and not an officially endorsed NIH guideline (it started therapy in elderly low-risk patients when BP exceeded 150).
- Elderly over 75, especially with limited life expectancy, can have **less strict control**.
- Over-enthusiastic treatment can reduce cognitive ability and increase mortality in the elderly and frail.
- **Diastolic below 70 mmHg is unacceptable in patients with coronary artery disease** (coronary perfusion occurs in diastole).

### Major trials

| Trial | Population | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **SPRINT** | Age 50 or above, high risk (Framingham risk 15% or more), no diabetes | Target below 120 mmHg systolic: **27% lower mortality**, **38% lower heart failure** |
| **ACCORD** | Diabetics | Systolic target **below 130** justified in diabetics at high stroke risk |

### Non-drug (lifestyle) measures: always, with or without drugs

- Reduce **sodium** intake
- **DASH diet**: low-fat dairy, fruit and vegetables
- Potassium supplementation (dietary)
- Maintain normal body weight
- Stop smoking
- Reduce alcohol
- Increase physical activity and exercise

### Drug treatment

| Stage | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Stage 1, low risk (10-year risk under 10%)** | Lifestyle modification alone |
| **Stage 1, high risk (10% or more)** | Start drug: long-acting **CCB, thiazide diuretic, ACE inhibitor or ARB**; switch class if not controlled |
| **Stage 2** | All need drugs. If already **150/90 or more**, start **combination therapy** directly. If uncontrolled: check compliance, switch to a more suitable class, add a third drug (or increase doses) |

- **ABCD** scheme: **A** = ACE inhibitor (or ARB if ACE-intolerant), **C** = calcium channel blocker, **D** = thiazide-type diuretic.
- Common combination: ACE inhibitor/ARB with CCB or thiazide. **Do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB.**

### Special groups

| Patient | Preferred drug |
|---|---|
| **Black patients** | ACEI/ARB less effective as monotherapy: start **thiazide or CCB**; add ACEI/ARB second |
| **Proteinuric CKD** | **ACEI or ARB** first line |
| **Diabetes** | **ACEI or ARB** |
| **HFrEF** | **Beta blocker plus ACEI/ARB** (reduce mortality, especially after ACS) |
| **AF, angina, essential tremor, migraine** | **Beta blocker** (not as monotherapy unless another indication) |
| **BPH** | **Alpha blocker** (not as monotherapy) |
| Pregnancy (general knowledge) | Methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine; avoid ACEI/ARB |

> **Exam tip:** the answer to "which drug is not recommended first-line monotherapy?" among amlodipine, enalapril, atenolol, losartan and hydrochlorothiazide is **atenolol** (a beta blocker). The ACC/AHA target is **below 130/80**.

### Resistant hypertension

Definition: BP uncontrolled on **3 drugs, one of which is a diuretic**.

1. Check the drugs are appropriate and at proper dose.
2. Check **adherence**.
3. Rule out white coat effect, NSAIDs and other interfering drugs.
4. **Work up for secondary causes.**
5. Add an **aldosterone antagonist**: **spironolactone** or **eplerenone**.
6. Add other agents: **labetalol, carvedilol, bisoprolol**; direct vasodilators **hydralazine, minoxidil**; **clonidine**.

## 6. Complications

- Stroke (ischaemic and haemorrhagic), hypertensive encephalopathy
- Coronary artery disease, left ventricular hypertrophy, heart failure, atrial fibrillation
- Chronic kidney disease (nephrosclerosis)
- Retinopathy (Keith-Wagener-Barker grades)
- Peripheral arterial disease, aortic aneurysm and dissection

## Key points to revise

1. Over 90% of hypertension is primary; look for secondary causes in the young, resistant or abrupt cases.
2. Diagnose with repeated, correct measurements (both arms); use ABPM or home monitoring to exclude white coat hypertension.
3. Urgency: 180/110 or more, no organ damage; emergency: with acute organ damage.
4. Target below 130/80 (ACC/AHA); avoid diastolic below 70 in coronary disease; relax targets in frail over-75s.
5. Lifestyle (DASH, salt, weight, alcohol, exercise, smoking) is for everyone.
6. First line: thiazide, CCB, ACEI/ARB; thiazide or CCB in black patients; ACEI/ARB in diabetes and proteinuric CKD.
7. Stage 2 and BP of 150/90 or more: start combination therapy.
8. Resistant hypertension: confirm adherence, screen for secondary causes, add spironolactone.
