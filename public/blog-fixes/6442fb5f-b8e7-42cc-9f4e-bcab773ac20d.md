# Spondyloarthropathies

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Rheumatology**

> **In one line:** the spondyloarthropathies are a family of inflammatory arthritides that share **HLA-B27**, **spine and sacroiliac involvement**, **enthesitis** (inflammation where tendons attach to bone), **asymmetric oligoarthritis**, and a **negative rheumatoid factor** (so they used to be called "seronegative" arthritis).

## What you must know

- The family: **ankylosing spondylitis (axial spondyloarthritis), psoriatic arthritis, reactive arthritis, enteropathic arthritis**.
- Shared features: inflammatory back pain, sacroiliitis, enthesitis, dactylitis ("sausage digit"), asymmetric large-joint arthritis, uveitis, HLA-B27, no rheumatoid factor.
- **Inflammatory back pain:** starts before 40, insidious, **morning stiffness over 30 minutes, better with exercise and worse with rest**, night pain in the second half of the night.
- NSAIDs and exercise are the first line for axial disease. **Methotrexate and sulfasalazine do not help axial disease** (they help peripheral arthritis). Anti-TNF or anti-IL-17 drugs are for those who fail NSAIDs.

## 1. Ankylosing spondylitis (AS)

**Definition.** A chronic inflammatory arthritis mainly of the **axial skeleton** (sacroiliac joints and spine) that can progress to bony **fusion of the spine**. Peripheral joints and extra-articular structures are often involved too.

**Epidemiology.**

- Onset between **20 and 30** years
- Male : female about **3 : 1**
- More than 90% of affected Europeans are HLA-B27 positive; overall prevalence is under 0.5%
- Fewer than 75% are able to stay in employment

### Pathophysiology

- An ill-defined interaction between environmental triggers (including gut microbes) and the immune system in a genetically susceptible person.
- Raised circulating **IL-17 and IL-23** activate T cells at the entheses and synovium. This is why drugs against IL-17 and TNF work.
- **HLA-B27** is an MHC class I molecule. Theories for how it causes disease:
  1. It presents arthritogenic peptides to CD8 T cells.
  2. It readily forms **homodimers** that activate leukocytes.
  3. It **misfolds**, causing endoplasmic reticulum stress and inflammatory cytokine release from macrophages and dendritic cells.

> **Common exam trap:** HLA-B27 is a *risk marker*, not a diagnostic test. Only about 1% of HLA-B27 positive people develop AS, and a few AS patients are negative, especially outside Europe.

### Clinical features

- **Cardinal feature:** inflammatory low back pain and early morning stiffness, radiating to the buttocks and posterior thighs. Worse with inactivity, **better with movement**.
- **Enthesitis:** tenderness over the iliac crest and greater trochanter; plantar fasciitis; Achilles tendinitis.
- Costovertebral joint involvement → **pleuritic chest pain and reduced chest expansion** (under 2.5 cm).
- **Peripheral arthritis in about 40%:** asymmetric, large joints (hips, knees, ankles, shoulders). In 10% it comes before spinal symptoms; in 10% it begins in childhood as oligoarticular JIA.
- Fatigue (from broken sleep and the direct effect of cytokines on the brain).
- Late: **spinal fusion, secondary osteoporosis** (raising vertebral fracture risk), atlanto-axial dislocation.

**Extra-articular features (the "A"s):** **A**nterior uveitis (the commonest, about 25 to 40%), **A**ortic regurgitation and conduction defects, **A**pical lung fibrosis, **A**myloidosis, cauda equina syndrome, IgA nephropathy, osteoporosis.

**Examination:** reduced lumbar flexion (**modified Schober test** under 5 cm), reduced chest expansion, loss of lumbar lordosis, increased thoracic kyphosis, increased occiput-to-wall distance, tender sacroiliac joints.

### Investigations

- **ESR and CRP** are often raised (but may be normal).
- **HLA-B27** positive (supportive only). **Rheumatoid factor and ACPA are negative.**
- Normochromic anaemia of chronic disease may be seen.

**Imaging**

| Finding | Where and what |
|---|---|
| **Sacroiliitis** | Earliest X-ray sign. Blurring and erosion of the joint margins → widening → sclerosis → narrowing → **fusion** |
| **"Squaring" of vertebrae** | Erosion and sclerosis of the anterior corners of thoracolumbar vertebrae (loss of the waist) |
| **Syndesmophytes** | Thin, vertical, symmetric bony bridges between vertebrae, from ossification of the outer fibres of the annulus fibrosus (fine, asymmetrical marginal ones are typical of AS) |
| **"Bamboo spine"** | Advanced disease: fused vertebrae with continuous syndesmophytes |
| **Others** | Erosions at the pubic symphysis, ischial tuberosity and peripheral joints; osteoporosis |

- Early disease can have a **normal X-ray**. If clinical suspicion is high, do an **MRI of the sacroiliac joints** (STIR sequences show bone-marrow oedema). MRI is more sensitive than X-ray.

**Disease activity** is measured with the **BASDAI** (Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index), a patient questionnaire. A BASDAI of **4 or more** (plus spinal pain of 4 or more) is the threshold for starting biological therapy.

### Management

**Aims:** relieve pain and stiffness, keep a maximum range of movement, prevent deformity.

**Non-drug (the cornerstone)**

- Patient education and physiotherapy
- Daily back-extension exercises and a morning warm-up
- Regular breaks from prolonged inactivity; avoid poor posture
- **Swimming** is an ideal exercise
- Stop smoking

**Drugs**

| Drug | Role |
|---|---|
| **NSAIDs** | First line; relieve symptoms and may slow disease. A long-acting dose at night helps morning stiffness |
| **Methotrexate / sulfasalazine** | Only for **peripheral** arthritis; **no effect on axial disease** |
| **Anti-TNF** (adalimumab, infliximab, etanercept) | For those with BASDAI and spinal pain ≥ 4 despite standard therapy. Improves symptoms, but has **not been shown to prevent ankylosis** or change the natural course |
| **Anti-IL-17** (secukinumab) | Alternative biologic |
| **Corticosteroids** | Local injections for plantar fasciitis, enthesitis and peripheral joints; oral or topical steroids for **acute uveitis**. Not for axial disease |
| **Surgery** | For severe hip, knee or shoulder restriction. **Total hip replacement** has largely replaced difficult spinal surgery |

Screen for TB (and hepatitis B) before starting anti-TNF treatment.

## 2. Psoriatic arthritis (PsA)

**Definition.** A chronic inflammatory arthritis associated with **psoriasis**, mainly affecting joints and entheses, and sometimes the spine. Psoriasis itself is an immune-mediated disease with itchy, raised, scaly plaques on the scalp, ears, gluteal fold, umbilicus, perineum, palms, soles, nails and extensor surfaces.

**Epidemiology.** PsA develops in 10 to 30% of people with psoriasis. Men and women are equally affected. Onset is usually at 25 to 40 years. Early PsA may look like axial spondyloarthritis.

**Pathophysiology.**

- Multifactorial: **HLA-B and HLA-C** variants are the strongest genetic risks (plus more than 30 other variants).
- Environmental triggers (infection, trauma) acting through toll-like receptors in a susceptible person release **IL-17, IL-23, interferon-gamma and TNF**. Dendritic cells and T cells are activated; matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) destroy tissue.
- **CD8 T cells** outnumber CD4 cells in the joints, in keeping with the HLA-C and HLA-B association.
- Psoriatic skin shows epidermal hyperplasia, neutrophils in the stratum corneum, more dendritic cells, and CD8 T cells in the epidermis.

### Clinical patterns

Psoriasis comes **before** the arthritis in 60 to 70%, **after** it in 15 to 20%, and at the same time in the rest.

| Pattern | Features |
|---|---|
| **Asymmetric oligoarthritis** (commonest) | Fewer than 5 joints; often abrupt synovitis with periarticular inflammation; knee and ankle; DIP and PIP joints; nail disease |
| **Symmetric polyarthritis** (about 25%) | More often in women. Looks like rheumatoid arthritis but **rheumatoid factor negative** |
| **DIP-predominant** | Classically linked to nail change |
| **Spondylitis / sacroiliitis** | Inflammatory neck and back pain; can lead to ankylosis |
| **Arthritis mutilans** | Destructive; "telescoping" digits from lysis of phalanges and metacarpals |

**Other features**

- **Nail changes:** pitting, horizontal ridging, onycholysis, yellowish discolouration at the margin, dystrophic hyperkeratosis
- **Dactylitis** ("sausage digit"): swelling of the whole finger or toe
- **Enthesitis:** pain and stiffness where tendons insert (e.g. Achilles, plantar fascia)
- **Eye:** conjunctivitis and uveitis in 10 to 30%; aortic valve involvement in about 4%

### Investigations

- ESR and CRP are **not always raised**
- RF and ACPA negative (low-titre RF or ANA in a few); uric acid may be raised
- HLA-B27 usually positive when the spine is involved
- **X-ray:** DIP involvement with the **"pencil-in-cup"** deformity, marginal erosions with new bone formation, small-joint ankylosis, osteolysis of phalanges and metacarpals with telescoping, **asymmetric** sacroiliitis, and non-marginal ("chunky") syndesmophytes

### CASPAR classification criteria

Inflammatory articular disease (joint, spine or entheseal) **plus 3 or more points** from:

1. **Psoriasis** now (2 points), or a past or family history (1 point)
2. **Psoriatic nail dystrophy**
3. **Negative rheumatoid factor**
4. **Dactylitis** now or in the past
5. **Radiographic new bone formation** near the joint in the hand or foot

> The slide says "more than 3 of the 5 criteria". The published CASPAR score is **3 or more points**, with current psoriasis counting 2.

### Treatment by manifestation

| Manifestation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Peripheral arthritis** | NSAIDs; intra-articular steroids; DMARDs (methotrexate, sulfasalazine, leflunomide, ciclosporin); biologics (anti-TNF) |
| **Skin and nail disease** | Topical coal tar, steroids, vitamin D analogues; phototherapy (PUVA / UVB); DMARDs; anti-TNF |
| **Axial disease** | Physiotherapy, NSAIDs, anti-TNF |
| **Dactylitis and enthesitis** | NSAIDs, steroid injection, anti-TNF |

## 3. Reactive arthritis

**Definition.** An acute, **sterile**, non-purulent arthritis triggered by an infection elsewhere in the body, usually enteric or urogenital. About one third of patients present with the classic triad: **arthritis, urethritis (or cervicitis) and conjunctivitis** ("can't see, can't pee, can't climb a tree"). About 75% are HLA-B27 positive.

**Epidemiology.** Uncommon; most often in young and middle-aged adults. The triggering infection varies by region:

- **Urogenital:** *Chlamydia trachomatis*
- **Gastrointestinal:** *Salmonella, Shigella, Yersinia, Campylobacter* and *Clostridioides difficile*

**Pathophysiology.** Genetic susceptibility plus an exaggerated immune response. Triggering organisms share features: they make **lipopolysaccharide (LPS)**, attack mucosal surfaces, invade host cells and survive inside them.

**Clinical features.**

- Acute onset, usually **1 to 4 weeks after** the infection
- Asymmetric **oligoarthritis of the lower limbs**, enthesitis (heel pain), sacroiliitis, back pain, sausage digits
- Genitourinary: urethritis, prostatitis, orchitis, balanitis, vaginitis, cervicitis
- Eye: conjunctivitis, uveitis
- Skin and mucosa: **keratoderma blennorrhagicum** (palms and soles), **circinate balanitis**, mouth ulcers, nail changes

**Investigations.** The diagnosis is **clinical**; no test is definitive.

- **Joint aspiration** to exclude crystal arthritis and septic arthritis (fluid is sterile and inflammatory)
- ESR and CRP raised
- Stool culture is usually negative, except post-*Salmonella*
- Serology for *Yersinia, Salmonella, Chlamydia*; urethral or cervical swab / PCR for chlamydia
- RF, ACPA and ANA negative
- X-ray: periarticular osteoporosis, proliferative erosions, periostitis

**Treatment.**

- **NSAIDs** for symptoms; physiotherapy; intra-articular steroids
- **Antibiotics** only for an active chlamydial infection. For *Chlamydia* urethritis (the notes give rifampicin plus azithromycin, or rifampicin plus doxycycline for 6 months for prolonged cases). Antibiotics do **not** help the arthritis after enteric infections.
- DMARDs (**sulfasalazine**, methotrexate, leflunomide) if NSAIDs are contraindicated or fail
- Anti-TNF drugs for resistant cases
- Most patients recover within 3 to 12 months; some develop chronic disease

## 4. Enteropathic arthritis

Arthritis linked to **inflammatory bowel disease**.

- An acute inflammatory **oligoarthritis** occurs in about **10% of ulcerative colitis** and **20% of Crohn's disease**.
- Large lower-limb joints (knees, ankles, hips) are mainly affected; wrists and small joints can also be involved.
- The arthritis usually **flares with the bowel disease**, sometimes with aphthous ulcers, iritis and **erythema nodosum**.
- Sacroiliitis (about 16%) and AS (about 6%) can occur and are identical to classic AS, but their **activity does not track the bowel disease**.

**Treatment.** Peripheral arthritis improves when the bowel disease is treated, and in ulcerative colitis it can be **cured by total colectomy**. Axial disease does not. **NSAIDs can flare IBD**, so use them with caution. DMARDs (sulfasalazine) or biologics (anti-TNF, which treat both) are sometimes needed.

## Comparing the four

| | AS | Psoriatic | Reactive | Enteropathic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pattern | Axial | Oligo, polyarticular, DIP, axial | Acute asymmetric lower limb | Large lower-limb joints |
| Sacroiliitis | Symmetric | Asymmetric | Asymmetric | Symmetric |
| Special sign | Bamboo spine | Nail pitting, pencil-in-cup | Triad, keratoderma | Erythema nodosum |
| HLA-B27 | 90% | About 50% with spinal disease | About 75% | About 50% with spinal disease |

## Key points to revise

1. Spondyloarthropathies = **HLA-B27**, seronegative, enthesitis, sacroiliitis, uveitis.
2. **Inflammatory back pain: better with movement**, worse with rest.
3. AS: young man, **bamboo spine**, MRI for early sacroiliitis, NSAIDs → anti-TNF; DMARDs do not help the spine.
4. PsA: nail pitting, dactylitis, pencil-in-cup; CASPAR criteria; treat skin and joints together.
5. Reactive arthritis: arthritis after chlamydia or enteric infection; sterile joint; NSAIDs.
6. Enteropathic: tracks the bowel disease; NSAIDs may flare IBD.
