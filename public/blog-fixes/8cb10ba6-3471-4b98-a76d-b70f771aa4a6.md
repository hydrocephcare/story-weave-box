# Adolescent Growth and Development: Puberty and Psychosocial Change

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Adolescent Health and Growth**

> **In one line:** adolescence is the stage between childhood and adulthood, set in motion by hormones and shaped by society. Puberty brings a growth spurt and sexual maturation (rated by Tanner/SMR stages), while the brain matures from the emotional limbic system towards the rational prefrontal cortex, which explains impulsivity, egocentrism and risk-taking.

## What you must know

- Adolescence is imprecisely bounded (society is unclear about roles: no longer a child, not yet an adult) and has **three periods: early, middle, late**.
- **Puberty** lasts typically **3 to 5 years**; onset ranges from **8 to 15 years**; sequence is universal, timing varies.
- **Primary sex characteristics** involve the reproductive organs; **secondary** are visible signs of maturity not involving sex organs directly.
- The **growth spurt**: boys grow about 4.1 inches (10 cm) a year, girls 3.5 inches (9 cm); girls start about **2 years earlier**; boys are taller on average by about age 13.
- First sign: girls **breast enlargement (SMR 2)**; boys **testicular enlargement**.
- Timing depends on **gender, genes and ethnicity, weight, stress**. Malnutrition delays; stress and adiposity advance puberty.
- Adolescent thinking shows **imaginary audience** and **personal fable**.

## 1. Definition and stages

- **Adolescence** is the developmental stage between childhood and adulthood: a time of considerable physical and psychological change.
- Hormones and social structures together set the developmental agenda.

| Stage | Features |
|---|---|
| **Early** | Preoccupied with body image; limited dating and intimacy; start of rapid growth and secondary sexual characteristics |
| **Middle (about 14 to 16)** | Menarche about one year after the growth spurt starts; relationships with parents strained and distant; **intense peer group involvement**; dating is the norm but relationships are superficial; **risk of sexually transmitted infections**; they understand consequences but may not apply them in decisions |
| **Late** | Physical change slows; peer group and its values recede; relationships involve love and commitment; career decisions made; transition to adult roles |

## 2. Puberty and sexual maturation

- Puberty is the period of rapid growth and sexual maturation, beginning **earlier in girls** than boys. Changes: secondary sexual characteristics, adult size, reproductive capacity.
- Some children enter puberty early because of increased weight and adiposity.
- The resulting changes are staged by the **sexual maturity rating (SMR / Tanner stages)**. The components are not perfectly synchronised.
- **Girls:** breast enlargement (thelarche) is usually first (SMR 2). **Boys:** testicular enlargement (measured with an **orchidometer**) is first.
- Boys' growth spurt is around age 12, girls' around age 10.

### Timing of puberty

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Gender | Girls ahead by months to years |
| Genes and ethnicity | Familial and ethnic variation |
| Weight | **Malnutrition delays**; obesity advances |
| Stress | Earlier puberty |

> **Check:** the slide says age of onset ranges from 8 to 15 years. Standard normal onset is 8 to 13 years in girls and 9 to 14 in boys; onset before 8 (girls) or 9 (boys) is precocious, and none by 13 (girls) or 14 (boys) is delayed.

## 3. Physical changes

- **Growth spurt:** a sudden period of rapid growth; sequence of gain is **weight, then height, then muscle**. Females gain more fat; males gain muscle. Heart and lungs enlarge.
- **Lymphoid tissue** (tonsils, adenoids) decreases, so teenagers get fewer colds and less asthma (as stated in the slides).
- **Skin** becomes oilier and sweatier, giving acne.
- Hormones contribute to moodiness, conflict and sexual urges, but social and cultural reactions to body change also play a large role.

## 4. Brain and cognitive development

- The **limbic system** (emotional, impulsive) matures **before the prefrontal cortex** (rational, logical). This partly explains impulsive, emotional behaviour.
- **Myelination of the prefrontal cortex** improves with age, improving decision-making, rational argument and reaction time. Experiences in adolescence have lasting effects.
- **Metacognition** (thinking about one's own thinking and monitoring it) grows, a key reason mental abilities advance.
- **Adolescent egocentrism** (David Elkind): self-absorption, seeing the world from one's own view; critical of authority, unwilling to accept criticism, quick to find fault. It produces two distortions:
  - **Imaginary audience:** believing everyone is watching and judging them; building elaborate scenarios about others' thoughts.
  - **Personal fable:** believing oneself unique and exceptional ("no one understands me"), which promotes **risk-taking**.

## 5. Psychosocial effects of maturation timing

| Group | Risks |
|---|---|
| **Early-maturing girls** | Teasing, early sexual activity, eating disorders, early substance use |
| **Early-maturing boys** | Rebellion, law-breaking, stress and depression linked to relationships |
| **Late-maturing boys** | Difficulty, especially in schools valuing athletics |
| **Late-maturing girls** | Anxiety about when puberty will start |

> **Exam tip:** the "at-risk" groups are early-maturing girls and late-maturing boys (early boys are generally seen as advantaged but still carry the behavioural risks listed above).

## 6. Clinical approach

- Reassure that variation is normal; but investigate when onset is clearly early or late, or when the sequence is abnormal.
- Counsel on nutrition, sleep, mental health, substance use, sexual health (STI and pregnancy prevention, HIV testing) and eating disorders.
- Interview privately and keep confidentiality within limits of safety.

## Key points to revise

1. Adolescence has three periods; puberty lasts 3 to 5 years, onset 8 to 15 years per the slides (normal onset about 8 to 14).
2. Primary sex characteristics are the reproductive organs; secondary are visible signs such as breasts, body hair and voice.
3. Girls start puberty about 2 years earlier and spurt around 10 years; boys around 12 years, and become taller.
4. First sign: breast buds in girls, testicular enlargement in boys; staged by Tanner/SMR.
5. Malnutrition delays puberty; stress and increased adiposity bring it earlier.
6. Limbic system matures before the prefrontal cortex, explaining impulsivity.
7. Imaginary audience and personal fable underlie self-consciousness and risk-taking.
8. Early-maturing girls (eating disorders, substance use, early sex) and late-maturing boys need support.
