# Prolonged and Obstructed Labour

**Year 4 · Obstetrics and Gynaecology · Labour and delivery**

> **In one line:** prolonged labour is slow progress caused by **poor powers, passenger or passage problems**; when a **mechanical barrier** stops descent despite strong contractions it becomes **obstructed labour**, which, if untreated, ends in uterine rupture, sepsis, fistula and death of mother and baby. The rule is relieve obstruction by caesarean section and **never give oxytocin** to an obstructed uterus.

## What you must know

- **Prolonged labour**: total over about 18-24 hours, or failure to progress on the partograph; second stage over **3 hours in primigravidae and 2 hours in multiparae**.
- Causes: **powers** (commonest), **passenger**, **passage**.
- Obstructed labour signs: **Bandl's ring**, tonic tender uterus, severe caput and moulding, oedematous cervix, hot dry vagina, ketosis.
- Management: resuscitate (fluids, analgesia, catheter, antibiotics), then **caesarean section** for a live foetus.
- **No oxytocin in obstructed labour.**
- Complications: **uterine rupture**, PPH, sepsis, **obstetric fistula**, foot drop; foetal asphyxia and stillbirth.

## 1. Prolonged labour

### 1.1 Definition
Labour exceeding about **18-24 hours** in total, or failure to progress against the partograph: cervical dilatation slower than expected in the active phase (the alert line is 1 cm per hour), or a prolonged second stage (over 3 hours in primigravidae, 2 in multiparae).

### 1.2 Classification

1. **Prolonged latent phase** (over 8 hours in the usual teaching, 12 hours in primigravidae in some texts).
2. **Protracted or arrested active phase** (slower than 1 cm/hour, or no change over 4 hours).
3. **Prolonged second stage.**

### 1.3 Causes: the three Ps

| Cause | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Powers** (most common) | Inadequate uterine contractions (primigravida, exhaustion, dehydration, epidural, overdistension) |
| **Passenger** | Malposition (occipitoposterior), malpresentation, macrosomia, hydrocephalus, shoulder or head size, foetal anomalies |
| **Passage** | Contracted pelvis (rickets, trauma, malnutrition in childhood), fibroids, ovarian cysts, cervical dystocia, full bladder |

> **Remember:** a full bladder can obstruct descent. Always empty it.

## 2. Obstructed labour

The foetus cannot descend despite strong contractions, because of a **mechanical barrier**. It is a major cause of maternal death and foetal loss in low-resource settings, including parts of Kenya.

### 2.1 Clinical features

- Prolonged labour; the mother is **exhausted, dehydrated and febrile** with **ketosis** and tachycardia.
- Uterus **tonically contracted and tender**, with a rising **Bandl's (retraction) ring**, an oblique groove at or above the umbilicus. This is the thin lower segment separating from the thick contracted upper segment, and a warning of **impending rupture**.
- **Severe caput and moulding**, an **oedematous cervix**, a foul vaginal discharge and meconium, and an **empty, oedematous vagina** that is hot and dry.
- The foetal heart is absent or distressed.
- The bladder is distended or contains bloody urine (pressure on the base).

### 2.2 Why harm occurs
Prolonged pressure of the presenting part on the soft tissues cuts off the blood supply (pressure necrosis), causing fistula; the thinned lower segment tears (rupture); membranes ruptured for long give ascending infection.

## 3. Management

1. **Resuscitate**: IV fluids, correct dehydration and ketosis, analgesia, bladder catheterisation, and **antibiotics** (ampicillin, gentamicin, metronidazole).
2. **Relieve the obstruction** by the quickest safe route:
   - **Caesarean section** for a live foetus (the main option).
   - Foetus dead: **destructive delivery** (craniotomy or decapitation) by a skilled person only if the cervix is fully dilated; otherwise caesarean.
   - **Symphysiotomy** is an option where caesarean is unavailable.
3. **Never give oxytocin** in obstructed labour (risk of uterine rupture). Oxytocin is only for hypocontractile labour **after** excluding obstruction.
4. Manage postpartum haemorrhage and prevent sepsis and thrombosis after delivery.

> **Exam tip:** before any oxytocin, confirm no cephalopelvic disproportion, a normal presentation and a reassuring foetal heart.

## 4. Simple delay in the active phase (no obstruction)

If there is no obstruction and the foetal heart is normal:
1. Ensure **hydration** and **pain relief**.
2. **Rupture the membranes** (amniotomy).
3. Give **oxytocin infusion** for slow progress due to poor contractions.
4. Review after 2-4 hours; **caesarean section** if there is still no progress.

## 5. Complications

| Maternal | Foetal |
|---|---|
| **Uterine rupture** | Asphyxia |
| Postpartum haemorrhage (atony, trauma) | Infection |
| Sepsis | Birth trauma |
| **Obstetric fistula** (vesicovaginal or rectovaginal from pressure necrosis; continuous leakage of urine or faeces) | Intrapartum stillbirth |
| Foot drop (pressure on the lumbosacral plexus or peroneal nerve) | |
| Maternal death | |

## 6. Prevention

Antenatal screening of high-risk women, **partograph** use, timely referral, skilled attendance, and access to caesarean section. Delay in reaching care (the three delays) is the commonest underlying factor.

## Key points to revise

1. Delay in labour: powers, passenger, passage.
2. Second stage prolonged if over 3 hours (primigravida) or 2 hours (multipara).
3. Bandl's ring, tonic uterus, severe moulding and caput suggest obstruction.
4. Resuscitate with fluids, analgesia, catheter and antibiotics, then deliver (caesarean for a live baby).
5. No oxytocin in obstructed labour.
6. Dead foetus with full dilatation: destructive delivery by a skilled operator.
7. Fistula and uterine rupture are the dreaded complications.
