## Summary
This document provides a comprehensive overview of platelet disorders, covering their production, structure, and function, as well as the mechanisms, diagnosis, and treatment of thrombocytopenia and thrombocytosis. It delves into specific conditions like ITP, TTP, and HUS, distinguishing their key features and management strategies. Qualitative platelet disorders, both inherited and acquired, are also detailed, alongside essential laboratory diagnostics and high-yield facts.

## Key Points
- Platelets are produced in the bone marrow by megakaryocyte fragmentation, with thrombopoietin (TPO) as the major regulator, and they perform adhesion, aggregation, and release functions crucial for hemostasis.
- Thrombocytopenia can arise from decreased production, altered distribution (e.g., splenomegaly), or increased destruction, which includes immune-mediated conditions like ITP and consumption disorders such as TTP and HUS.
- Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) is characterized by a deficiency of ADAMTS13, leading to the accumulation of ultra-large VWF multimers, causing microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, severe thrombocytopenia, and neurological/renal dysfunction.
- Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS) shares features with TTP but typically affects children, is often triggered by E. coli O157:H7 toxin, and is dominated by severe renal failure with normal ADAMTS13 levels.
- Qualitative platelet disorders, exemplified by von Willebrand Disease (vWD), Bernard-Soulier syndrome, and Glanzmann's thrombasthenia, involve normal platelet counts but impaired function due to defects in adhesion, aggregation, or granule release.
- Laboratory diagnosis of platelet disorders relies on blood counts, film review for morphology, bleeding time, platelet aggregation studies, and specific assays for VWF, ADAMTS13, or platelet glycoproteins.
- Platelet transfusions are generally contraindicated in TTP and HUS as they can exacerbate the thrombotic process, but they are crucial for life-threatening bleeding or for prophylactic measures in other thrombocytopenic conditions.

## Detailed Notes

### SECTION 1: PLATELET BASICS

### Production

- Produced in bone marrow by **fragmentation of megakaryocyte cytoplasm**
- Megakaryocyte matures by **endomitotic synchronous replication** — DNA replicates without nuclear or cytoplasmic division → nucleus enlarges in multiples of two
- Early invaginations of plasma membrane form the **demarcation membrane** → evolves into highly branched network
- Each megakaryocyte gives rise to **1,000–5,000 platelets**
- Platelets released through **endothelium of vascular niches** of marrow
- Stem cell → platelet production: **averages 10 days**

### Thrombopoietin (TPO)

- **Major regulator** of platelet formation
- **95% produced by the liver**
- **50% constitutive** — plasma level depends on removal by binding to **c-MPL receptors** on platelets and megakaryocytes
- **50% regulated** in response to platelet destruction:
  - Aging platelets lose surface **sialic acid** → exposes **galactose** → binds **Ashwell-Morell receptor** in liver → signals new TPO production
- TPO ↑ → ↑ number and maturation rate of megakaryocytes via c-MPL
- TPO **high** in thrombocytopenia from marrow aplasia (no platelets to mop it up)
- TPO **low** in raised platelet counts
- Platelet levels start rising **6 days** after TPO therapy starts
- TPO itself not clinically available → **thrombomimetic agents** (bind c-MPL) used instead

### Platelet Structure

- Small discoid cells: **3.0 × 0.5 μm**

**Key surface glycoproteins:**
| Glycoprotein | Function | Defect in |
|---|---|---|
| **GPIa** | Adhesion to collagen | — |
| **GPIb** | Binds VWF → adhesion to subendothelium | **Bernard-Soulier syndrome** |
| **GPIIb/IIIa** (αIIb/β3) | Binds VWF + fibrinogen → aggregation | **Glanzmann's thrombasthenia** |

**Internal structures:**
| Granule | Contents |
|---|---|
| **α granules** (more common) | Clotting factors, VWF, PDGF, fibrinogen |
| **Dense granules** (less common) | ADP, ATP, serotonin, calcium |
| **Lysosomes** | Hydrolytic enzymes |

- **Open canalicular system** — plasma membrane invaginates into platelet interior → large reactive surface for coagulation proteins
- **Membrane phospholipids (Platelet Factor 3)** — crucial for:
  - **Tenase complex**: IXa + VIIIa + X → Xa
  - **Prothrombinase complex**: Xa + Va + prothrombin → **thrombin**
- **PDGF** (from α granules) → stimulates vascular SMC proliferation → hastens vascular healing

### Platelet Function

**Three major functions: Adhesion → Aggregation → Release + Amplification**

**1. Adhesion:**
- Platelet adheres to exposed subendothelium via **VWF bridge** (GPIb receptor) and **collagen** (GPIa)
- VWF = large glycoprotein; multimers of 2–50 dimeric subunits
- VWF synthesised by **endothelial cells** (stored in Weibel-Palade bodies) and **megakaryocytes** (stored in α granules)
- Ultra-large VWF multimers cleaved by **ADAMTS13** metalloprotease → smaller multimers

**2. Aggregation:**
- Cross-linking of platelets via active **GPIIb/IIIa** with **fibrinogen bridges**
- Resting GPIIb/IIIa does NOT bind fibrinogen → activation needed first

**3. Release reaction + Amplification:**
- Activation → release of α and dense granule contents
- **ADP** (from dense granules) → major positive feedback → recruits more platelets
- **TXA2** (thromboxane A2):
  - Formed de novo via **cytosolic PLA2** activation
  - ↓ platelet cAMP → initiates release reaction
  - Potentiates aggregation + causes **vasoconstriction**
- **Prostacyclin (PGI2)** — from endothelial cells → ↑ cAMP → **inhibits** aggregation + vasodilation
- **Nitric oxide (NO)** — from endothelial cells, macrophages, platelets → inhibits platelet activation + vasodilation
- **CD39 (ectonucleotidase/ADPase)** — degrades ADP → prevents aggregation on intact endothelium

### Normal Values

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Normal platelet count | 150–400 × 10⁹/L |
| Normal platelet lifespan | 10 days |
| Platelet size | 3.0 × 0.5 μm |
| Splenic sequestration (normal) | Up to one third |
| Splenic sequestration (massive splenomegaly) | Up to 90% |

### SECTION 2: THROMBOCYTOPENIA

**Definition:** Platelet count **<150 × 10⁹/L**

**Clinical bleeding thresholds:**
| Count | Risk |
|---|---|
| <60 × 10⁹/L | Bleeding in surgery |
| <30 × 10⁹/L | Petechial bleeding |
| <20 × 10⁹/L | Spontaneous bleeding |
| <5 × 10⁹/L | CNS haemorrhage risk |

**Clinical features:** Petechiae · ecchymoses · epistaxis · gingival bleeding · menorrhagia · mucosal haemorrhage

### Causes — Master Table

| Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|
| **↓ Production** | Leukaemia, aplastic anaemia, MDS, myelofibrosis, marrow infiltration, cytotoxics, viral infections, megaloblastic anaemia, HIV |
| **↑ Destruction (Immune)** | ITP, drug-induced, post-transfusion purpura, feto-maternal alloimmune, TTP, DIC |
| **Abnormal distribution** | Splenomegaly (liver disease, myeloproliferative disorders) |
| **Dilutional** | Massive transfusion |
| **Preanalytical** | Sample clotting, platelet satellitism |

### Preanalytical/Sample Issues

- Coagulation samples → **blue top tubes** (sodium citrate)
- Tube must be **≥90% full**; inverted **3–4 times** for proper mixing
- Failure → small clots form → **falsely low platelet count**
- **Platelet satellitism** — platelets adhere around neutrophils in EDTA samples → falsely low count
  - Fix: redraw in **sodium citrate** → rerun on automated counter

### Decreased Production

**Most common cause** of thrombocytopenia — usually part of generalised BM failure

| Cause | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Leukaemia | Blast cells crowd out normal marrow |
| Megaloblastic anaemia | Pancytopenia — all cell lines ↓ |
| Cytotoxic drugs/chemotherapy | Interfere with cell cycle |
| Viral (CMV, EBV, varicella, rubella) | **Megakaryocytic suppression** |
| Bacterial infections | **Direct toxicity** to circulating platelets |
| Aplastic anaemia · MDS · myelofibrosis | General BM failure |
| HIV | Both ↓ production + immune destruction |

**Congenital causes (rare):**
- Mutation of **c-MPL** (TPO receptor)
- **RBM8A gene** mutation + absent radii
- **May-Hegglin anomaly** — large inclusions in granulocytes
- **Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome (WAS)** — WASP gene mutation; eczema + immune deficiency

Chemotherapy patients: platelet support given if count drops **below 20 × 10⁹/L**

### Altered Distribution

- Normal spleen holds **one third** of total platelet volume → rises to **90%** in massive splenomegaly
- Causes of splenomegaly: myeloproliferative disorders · extramedullary haematopoiesis · haemolytic anaemias · liver disease
- Platelet lifespan **normal** → thrombocytopenia of splenomegaly usually **not associated with bleeding**

Splenectomy → sudden release of sequestered platelets → risk of **thrombotic complications**

**Massive Transfusion Syndrome:**
- Replacement of **≥10 units** blood in 24 hours
- Platelets + coagulation factors **diluted** → transient thrombocytopenia + coagulopathy
- Correct with **platelet transfusions + FFP**

### Drug-Induced Immune Thrombocytopenia

**Two mechanisms:**
1. Drug → antidrug antibody → binds platelet **glycoprotein** → removed by **RES**
2. Drug + carrier protein → **hapten** → antibody formed → platelet destruction in spleen

**Key drugs:**
| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Analgesics/anti-inflammatory | Gold salts, NSAIDs |
| Antimicrobials | Penicillins, rifamycin, sulfonamides, trimethoprim |
| Anticonvulsants/sedatives | Diazepam, sodium valproate, carbamazepine |
| Diuretics | Furosemide, chlorothiazides, acetazolamide |
| Antidiabetics | Chlorpropamide, tolbutamide |
| Others | **Heparin**, quinine, quinidine, digitoxin, methyldopa |
| BM suppression (dose-related) | Cytotoxics, ionising radiation, ethanol |

- Platelet count often **<10 × 10⁹/L**; BM shows normal/↑ megakaryocytes
- **Rx:** Stop offending drug immediately; platelet transfusion if dangerous bleeding

### Post-Transfusion Purpura (PTP)

- Thrombocytopenia **~10 days** after transfusion of platelet-containing products
- Antibody directed against **HPA-1a (Pl1A)** antigen on donor platelets
- Recipient lacks HPA-1a → donor platelets destroyed → **recipient's own platelets also destroyed** (bystander effect)
- Results in **prolonged, dramatic thrombocytopenia**; treatment difficult
- Rare condition

### Feto-Maternal Alloimmune Thrombocytopenia (FNAIT)

- Maternal **anti-HPA-1a** antibody crosses placenta → destroys fetal platelets
- Baby born with petechiae, skin haemorrhages, **abnormally low platelet count**
- Count normalises within days as maternal antibodies clear
- Risk of **CNS haemorrhage** — treat only when risk is present
- Antibody can also affect baby **in utero**

### Consumption — ITP

### Chronic ITP

- **Most common cause** of isolated thrombocytopenia (no anaemia, no neutropenia)
- Highest incidence: **women aged 15–50 years**
- Associated with: SLE · HIV · Hepatitis C · H. pylori · CLL · Hodgkin lymphoma · autoimmune haemolytic anaemia

**Pathogenesis:**
- **IgG autoantibodies** (anti-GPIIb/IIIa or anti-GPIb) → platelet coated → phagocytosed by macrophages in **spleen** (RES)
- In 66% of cases antibody directed against **GPIIb/IIIa or GPIb-IX**
- Platelet lifespan reduced from 10 days → **few hours**
- Megakaryocyte mass ↑ **~5×** normal but **poorly functioning**
- Bleeding less severe than equivalent thrombocytopenia from BM failure — due to **young, functionally superior platelets** in circulation

**Clinical features:**
- Insidious onset — petechiae · easy bruising · menorrhagia
- Mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, gum bleeding) in severe cases
- Intracranial haemorrhage — **rare**
- Spleen **NOT palpable** unless associated disease
- Relapsing-remitting course; many asymptomatic cases found on routine FBC

**Diagnosis:**
- Platelets usually **10–100 × 10⁹/L**; Hb + WCC normal
- Blood film: ↓ platelets; **large platelets** present; other lines normal
- BM: **normal or ↑ megakaryocytes**
- Anti-GPIIb/IIIa or anti-GPIb antibodies detectable (not routinely used in practice)

**Treatment — stepwise:**
| Step | Treatment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Corticosteroids** (prednisolone 1mg/kg/day) | 80% remit; first-line |
| 2 | **IV immunoglobulin** (400mg/kg/day × 5 days OR 1g/kg/day × 2 days) | Rapid ↑ platelets; use in life-threatening bleeds, pregnancy, pre-surgery; blocks Fc receptors on macrophages |
| 3 | **Rituximab** (anti-CD20) | ~50% response; try before splenectomy |
| 4 | **Immunosuppressives** (vincristine, cyclophosphamide, azathioprine, mycophenolate, ciclosporin) | Refractory to steroids/rituximab |
| 5 | **Thrombomimetics** (Romiplostim SC · Eltrombopag oral) | Stimulate TPO receptor; refractory/steroid-contraindicated; risk of BM reticulin/fibrosis (reversible) |
| 6 | **Splenectomy** | Less common now; splenunculi must also be removed to prevent relapse |
| 7 | **Danazol** · anti-D immunoglobulin · treat H. pylori · treat Hep C | Adjuncts |
| 8 | **Platelet transfusion** | Life-threatening bleeding only; benefit lasts **few hours** |
| 9 | **Stem cell transplantation** | Severe refractory cases |

Treatment goal: maintain platelet count **above bleeding threshold** with minimum intervention. Count **>20 × 10⁹/L** without symptoms → no treatment needed

### Acute ITP

- Most common in **children aged 2–6 years**
- **75%** follow vaccination or infection (chickenpox, infectious mononucleosis)
- Mechanism: **non-specific immune complex** attachment to platelets
- Platelet count may drop to **<20 × 10⁹/L**
- Child shows bruising, epistaxis, petechiae — **rarely life-threatening haemorrhage**
- **Spontaneous remission** usual — resolves within **6 weeks** as child recovers from illness
- **5–10%** become chronic (>6 months)
- Treatment: steroids + IVIg if significant bleeding; platelet count **>30 × 10⁹/L** → no treatment needed

### Consumption — TTP

**Pathogenesis:**
- Deficiency of **ADAMTS13** metalloprotease (cleaves ultra-large VWF multimers at Tyr842-Met843 bond)
- **Familial TTP** — >50 ADAMTS13 mutations reported
- **Acquired TTP** — inhibitory **IgG autoantibody** against ADAMTS13 (triggered by infection, SLE, drugs, stem cell transplant, cardiac surgery)
- Ultra-large VWF multimers accumulate → anchored to endothelial cells → platelets adhere via GPIb → **massive platelet aggregation** → **occlusive platelet thrombi** in microvessels → organ ischaemia

**Clinical features — Classic Pentad:**
1. **Thrombocytopenia** — platelets **<20 × 10⁹/L**
2. **Microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia (MAHA)** — schistocytes on blood film
3. **Neurological abnormalities** — headache · confusion · seizures · coma · paresthesias
4. **Renal failure** — proteinuria, haematuria
5. **Fever**

PT and APTT are **NORMAL** in TTP — distinguishes from DIC

**Diagnosis:**
- Thrombocytopenia + **schistocytes** on blood film + **↑↑ LDH** = sufficient to diagnose
- ADAMTS13 absent or severely reduced
- LDH from both ischaemic tissue AND lysed RBCs

**Treatment:**
- **Plasma exchange** (FFP or cryosupernatant) — removes UL-VWF multimers + antibody + replenishes ADAMTS13; **performed over 3–5 days**
- **Rituximab** — used with plasma exchange; reduces relapse risk
- Corticosteroids · vincristine · IVIg · azathioprine · cyclophosphamide — refractory/relapsing cases
- Untreated mortality: **~90%** | With treatment: **>80% survive**

**Platelet transfusions CONTRAINDICATED** in TTP and HUS — worsen thrombosis

### Consumption — HUS

- Predominantly affects **children aged 6 months–4 years**
- Mimics TTP but **renal damage predominates**
- Caused by **E. coli O157:H7** (verotoxin) or **Shigella** toxin → endothelial cell death in renal microvasculature → platelet thrombi
- **ADAMTS13 levels NORMAL** — key distinction from TTP

**Clinical features:**
- Initially: **bloody diarrhoea + vomiting**
- Then: **haemolytic anaemia + thrombocytopenia + renal failure**
- Fever · abdominal pain · schistocytes on film (MAHA)
- Neurological complications possible

**Treatment:**
- **Supportive** — renal dialysis if acute renal failure
- Control hypertension
- **Atypical HUS** → eculizumab (complement inhibitor)
- Usually **self-limiting** in children; most make full recovery; some have residual kidney problems

### DIC (brief)

- Thrombocytopenia from **high rate of platelet consumption** in widespread microvascular clotting
- Unlike TTP: **PT and APTT are prolonged**; D-dimer elevated; fibrinogen low

### SECTION 3: THROMBOCYTOSIS

**Definition:** Platelet count **>450 × 10⁹/L**

| Type | Cause | Platelet Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** (Essential Thrombocythaemia) | Myeloproliferative disorder | **Impaired** — high numbers, poor function; may paradoxically bleed OR clot |
| **Secondary (Reactive)** | Acute/chronic blood loss · chronic inflammation · post-splenectomy · iron deficiency anaemia | **Normal** |

- Essential thrombocythaemia — highest platelet count of all MPDs; can exceed **1 million**
- Severe iron deficiency anaemia → count may reach **2 million** from marrow stimulation → normalises with iron therapy
- Secondary thrombocytosis: elevation lasts **days to weeks**

### SECTION 4: QUALITATIVE PLATELET DISORDERS

**Definition:** Normal platelet count but **impaired platelet function** → bleeding time prolonged; platelet count normal

### Inherited Disorders — Disorders of Adhesion

### Von Willebrand Disease (vWD)

- **Most prevalent inherited bleeding disorder worldwide** — affects **1–3%** of population
- **Autosomal dominant**
- Commonest cause of bleeding in children investigated for **epistaxis** and women investigated for **menorrhagia**
- Blood group **O** individuals have lower baseline vWF → ↑ susceptibility

**Pathophysiology:**
- Qualitative OR quantitative defect in **vWF**
- vWF sources: **endothelial cells** (stored in Weibel-Palade bodies) + **megakaryocytes** (stored in α granules)
- vWF coded on **chromosome 12**; carried in plasma by Factor VIII
- vWF serves as **intermediary** for platelet adhesion via GPIb receptor on subendothelium
- Without functioning vWF → **platelet adhesion impaired** → no stable plug

**Clinical features:**
- Easy bruising · epistaxis · menorrhagia · excessive bleeding post-dental procedures/surgery
- Women significantly **underdiagnosed** — menorrhagia key feature

**Treatment:**
- **DDAVP (desmopressin)** — releases endothelial vWF stores from Weibel-Palade bodies; given IV or intranasal spray
- Non-responders → **high-purity Factor VIII concentrates** containing sufficient vWF

### Bernard-Soulier Syndrome

- **Autosomal recessive** — mutation in **GPIb gene**
- Platelets **larger than normal**
- Defective binding to VWF → defective subendothelial adhesion
- Platelets **do NOT aggregate with ristocetin**
- Variable degree of **thrombocytopenia**

### Inherited Disorders — Disorders of Aggregation

### Glanzmann's Thrombasthenia

- **Autosomal recessive** — mutations in **GPIIb or IIIa genes**
- Failure of **primary platelet aggregation**
- Presents in **neonatal period**
- Platelets fail to aggregate with **any agonist EXCEPT ristocetin**
- Platelet count and morphology normal; bleeding time prolonged

### Inherited Disorders — Storage Pool Diseases

| Disease | Defect | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Grey platelet syndrome** | GFI1B mutation; **absence of α granules** | Large pale platelets on film |
| **Delta storage pool disease** | Deficiency of **dense granules** (↓ ADP, ATP, serotonin, Ca²⁺) | More common; abnormal secondary aggregation |

- Both: abnormal secondary phase aggregation · postoperative bleeding · menorrhagia · easy bruisability
- Bleeding time prolonged; platelet count **normal**

### Acquired Qualitative Disorders

### Antiplatelet Drugs

| Drug | Mechanism | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Aspirin** | Irreversibly inhibits **cyclo-oxygenase** → ↓ TXA2 synthesis | ↓ Release reaction + aggregation; defect lasts **10 days** (platelet lifespan) |
| **Dipyridamole** | Blocks **adenosine reuptake** | ↓ Aggregation; used as adjunct to aspirin |
| **Clopidogrel / Prasugrel** | Inhibit **ADP binding** to platelet receptor | ↓ ADP-induced aggregation; used post-stenting/angioplasty |
| **Abciximab / Eptifibatide / Tirofiban** | Inhibit **GPIIb/IIIa receptor** | Used in PCI/unstable angina; risk of transient thrombocytopenia |

### Other Acquired Causes

| Condition | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Uraemia** | Various platelet function abnormalities |
| **Hyperglobulinaemia** (myeloma, Waldenström's) | Interferes with platelet adherence, release, aggregation |
| **Myeloproliferative/myelodysplastic disorders** | Intrinsic platelet function abnormalities |
| **Liver disease** | Reduced VWF cleavage + platelet dysfunction |

### Vascular Disorders → Platelet Dysfunction

- **Telangiectasia** (HHT) — dilated microvascular swellings; defective haemostasis at lesion sites
- **Haemangiomas** — giant cavernous haemangiomas can cause chronic DIC + thrombocytopenia

### SECTION 5: LABORATORY DIAGNOSIS

### Screening Tests

| Test | Measures | Normal |
|---|---|---|
| **PT (Prothrombin Time)** | Extrinsic pathway: Factors VII, X, V, II (prothrombin), fibrinogen | 10–14 seconds |
| **APTT** | Intrinsic pathway: Factors VIII, IX, XI, XII + X, V, II, fibrinogen | 30–40 seconds |
| **TT (Thrombin Time)** | Fibrinogen deficiency or thrombin inhibition | 14–16 seconds |

- Prolonged PT/APTT corrected by 50:50 mix with normal plasma → **factor deficiency**
- No correction with 50:50 mix → **inhibitor present**

### PFA-100 Test

- Citrated blood aspirated through capillary → membrane coated with **collagen/ADP** or **collagen/adrenaline**
- Platelets adhere + aggregate via **VWF-GPIb** and **GPIIb/IIIa** → occlude aperture
- **Prolonged** in: VWD · platelet function defects · aspirin use
- Can give **false-negative** results → full aggregation studies + VWF assay still needed if clinical suspicion high

### Platelet Aggregometry

- Measures **fall in light absorbance** in platelet-rich plasma as platelets aggregate
- Primary aggregation = external agonist | Secondary aggregation = released granule contents

**Aggregation patterns:**
| Agonist | Glanzmann's | Bernard-Soulier | Aspirin | Clopidogrel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ADP | ✗ | ✓ | ↓ secondary | ✗ |
| Collagen | ✗ | ✓ | ↓ | Normal |
| Arachidonic acid | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | Normal |
| Ristocetin | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Adrenaline | ✗ | ✓ | ↓ secondary | Normal |

### D-Dimer and Fibrinolysis

- D-dimer = fibrin degradation product → **↑↑ in DIC**
- Also elevated in: infection · cancer · pregnancy
- Measured by: **thromboelastography (TEG)** or thromboelastometry
- **Tranexamic acid** → reduces hyperfibrinolysis → reduces bleeding

### Thrombomimetics — Summary

| Drug | Route | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Romiplostim** | Subcutaneous weekly | ITP refractory to steroids |
| **Eltrombopag** | Oral daily | ITP; also trials in MDS, aplastic anaemia, post-chemo |

- Mechanism: activate **TPO receptor (c-MPL)** on megakaryocytes → ↑ platelet production
- Side effects: **disturbed liver function** · long-term → ↑ BM reticulin/fibrosis (reversible on stopping)

### Platelet Transfusion — Indications

| Indication | Target Count |
|---|---|
| Before invasive procedure (e.g. liver biopsy, LP) | **>50 × 10⁹/L** |
| Prophylactic (no infection/coagulopathy) | **<5–10 × 10⁹/L** |
| Prophylactic (with infection/coagulopathy/bleeding risk) | **Keep >20 × 10⁹/L** |
| Active life-threatening bleeding | Transfuse regardless |

**Platelet transfusions CONTRAINDICATED in TTP and HUS** — fuel the thrombotic process

## Practice Questions
1. What is the most common inherited bleeding disorder worldwide? → Von Willebrand Disease (vWD)
2. What is the most common cause of isolated thrombocytopenia? → Chronic ITP
3. What are the hallmark laboratory findings in TTP? → Schistocytes, elevated LDH, normal PT/APTT, and absent or severely reduced ADAMTS13.
4. What is the key difference between HUS and TTP in terms of ADAMTS13 levels and dominant clinical feature? → In HUS, ADAMTS13 levels are normal and renal damage is the dominant feature, whereas in TTP, ADAMTS13 is deficient and neurological symptoms are more prominent.
5. What is the aggregation pattern for Glanzmann's thrombasthenia? → Platelets fail to aggregate with any agonist EXCEPT ristocetin.
6. What is the mechanism of action of aspirin in inhibiting platelet function? → Aspirin irreversibly inhibits cyclo-oxygenase, leading to decreased TXA2 synthesis, and this effect lasts for the lifespan of the platelet (10 days).
7. What is the mechanism by which DDAVP (desmopressin) helps treat von Willebrand Disease? → DDAVP releases endothelial von Willebrand Factor (vWF) stores from Weibel-Palade bodies.
8. In which two conditions are platelet transfusions generally contraindicated due to the risk of worsening thrombosis? → Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP) and Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS).
