**Cholinomimetic drugs** mimic the action of acetylcholine and can be direct-acting (bind receptors) or indirect-acting (inhibit acetylcholinesterase).
## Examples and Effects:
- **Pilocarpine (Direct-acting muscarinic agonist):**Increases salivation and sweating
- Induces miosis (pupil constriction)
- Lowers intraocular pressure in glaucoma

- **Neostigmine (Indirect-acting cholinesterase inhibitor):**Enhances neuromuscular transmission in myasthenia gravis
- Increases GI motility and bladder contraction
- Reverses non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockade
## Significance:
- **Mechanism:** Inhibit reuptake of serotonin (5-HT) at synaptic clefts, increasing serotonin availability.
- **Clinical Use:** First-line treatment for depression, anxiety disorders, OCD, PTSD.
- **Examples:** Fluoxetine, Sertraline, Paroxetine, Escitalopram.

**Definition: **The **GABA shunt** is a closed-loop metabolic pathway that converts glutamate into GABA and recycles GABA into metabolites for energy and neurotransmitter balance.
## Key Steps:
- **Glutamate → GABA:**Enzyme: **Glutamate decarboxylase (GAD)**
- Cofactor: Pyridoxal phosphate (Vitamin B6)

- **GABA → Succinic semialdehyde: **Enzyme: **GABA transaminase (GABA-T)**
- Transfers amino group to α-ketoglutarate

- **Succinic semialdehyde → Succinate: **Enzyme: **Succinic semialdehyde dehydrogenase**
- Enters the **TCA cycle**
## Significance:
- Maintains **GABA levels**, the chief inhibitory neurotransmitter in the CNS
- Regulates **neuronal excitability**
- Connects **neurotransmitter metabolism with energy production**

**Dopamine Degradation Pathways** (15 Marks)
**Overview:** Dopamine is a catecholamine neurotransmitter synthesized from L-DOPA and degraded primarily via **monoamine oxidase (MAO)** and **catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT)**. The pathways occur in the **presynaptic neuron**, **glial cells**, and **extracellular space**.

## I. Main Enzymes Involved:
- **MAO (Monoamine Oxidase)**Location: Mitochondria of neurons and glia
- Type: Two isoforms – MAO-A and MAO-B

- **COMT (Catechol-O-methyltransferase)**Location: Cytoplasm of glial cells, liver, kidney, postsynaptic neurons

## II. Degradation Pathways:
## Pathway 1 (Intraneuronal – MAO first):
- **Dopamine → DOPAC (3,4-dihydroxyphenylacetic acid)**Enzyme: **MAO-B**
- Intermediate: Dopamine aldehyde

- **DOPAC → HVA (homovanillic acid)**Enzyme: **COMT**
## Pathway 2 (Extraneuronal – COMT first):
- **Dopamine → 3-Methoxytyramine (3-MT)**Enzyme: **COMT**

- **3-MT → HVA**Enzyme: **MAO-B**

## Final Excretion Product:
- **Homovanillic Acid (HVA)**Excreted in urine; useful in clinical diagnostics (e.g., neuroblastoma, Parkinson’s disease monitoring)

- **Regulates GI Motility: **Coordinates peristalsis and segmental contractions.

- **Controls Secretion: **Modulates mucus, enzyme, and hormone secretion from glands.

- **Maintains Mucosal Integrity: **Involved in epithelial cell turnover and barrier protection.

- **Regulates Blood Flow: **Controls vasodilation/constriction in gut vasculature.

- **Sensory Integration: **Detects stretch, pH, and nutrient content, triggering reflexes independently of CNS.
## 1. Resting Membrane Potential (RMP):
- Maintained by Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase and K⁺ leak channels (~ -70 mV)

## 2. Depolarization:
- **Trigger:** Stimulus raises membrane potential above threshold (~ -55 mV)
- **Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open:**→ Na⁺ influx→ Membrane potential becomes more positive (~+30 mV)

## 3. Peak and Inactivation:
- Na⁺ channels **inactivate** after peak depolarization
- **Voltage-gated K⁺ channels open:**→ K⁺ efflux→ Initiates **repolarization**

## 4. Repolarization and Hyperpolarization:
- Outflow of K⁺ returns membrane potential toward RMP
- K⁺ channels remain open longer → **hyperpolarization** (~ -80 mV)

## 5. Refractory Periods:
- **Absolute Refractory:** Na⁺ channels inactivated – no new AP possible
- **Relative Refractory:** Stronger stimulus needed to generate new AP

**Overview:** Steroid hormones are metabolized primarily in the liver and excreted via urine and bile.
## 1. Liver Metabolism:
- **Phase I Reactions: **Involves oxidation, reduction, and hydroxylation reactions (mainly by cytochrome P450 enzymes).
- Increases water solubility and introduces functional groups for conjugation.

- **Phase II Reactions (Conjugation):**Glucuronidation (via UDP-glucuronosyltransferase).
- Sulfation (via sulfotransferase).
- These conjugates are hydrophilic and ready for excretion.
## 2. Excretion Pathways:
- **Urinary Excretion: **Majority of conjugated steroid metabolites (e.g., pregnanediol, tetrahydrocortisol) are excreted in urine.
- Measurement used for hormonal assessments (e.g., 17-ketosteroids).

- **Biliary Excretion: **Some conjugates are excreted in bile → enter intestine → partly deconjugated by gut bacteria → may undergo enterohepatic circulation.
## 3. Clinical Significance:
- Impaired liver function affects hormone clearance → hormonal imbalance.
- Enzyme deficiencies (e.g., in glucuronidation) can lead to altered excretion profiles.
## 1. Osmotic Opening of Tight Junctions:
- Hyperosmotic agents (e.g., mannitol) shrink endothelial cells → temporarily open tight junctions.
- Allows passive diffusion of drugs.
- Used in treating brain tumors.
## 2. Receptor-Mediated Transcytosis:
- Conjugate drugs to ligands (e.g., transferrin, insulin) that bind endothelial receptors.
- The complex undergoes endocytosis and is transported across the BBB.
- Allows selective and controlled delivery.
## 3. Nanocarrier and Liposome Systems:
- Drugs encapsulated in nanoparticles or liposomes with surface modifications.
- Surface ligands facilitate receptor-mediated uptake.
- Enhances drug stability and targeting.
## 4. Inhibition of Efflux Transporters (e.g., P-glycoprotein):
- Co-administer P-gp inhibitors (e.g., verapamil) to prevent drug efflux from brain back to blood.
- Enhances brain accumulation of therapeutic agents.
- Important in CNS cancer and epilepsy treatment.
