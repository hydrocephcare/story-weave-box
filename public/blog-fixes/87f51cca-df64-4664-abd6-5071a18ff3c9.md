# Disorders of Growth: Short Stature, Faltering Growth, Tall Stature and Obesity

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Growth and endocrinology**

> **In one line:** a growth disorder is any departure from the expected size or growth rate. Before calling a child abnormal, check the **growth velocity**, the **parents' heights** and the **bone age**; most short children are normal variants, while undernutrition and chronic disease are the commonest pathological causes in Kenya.

## What you must know

- Ask three questions: is the child short for the population (below -2 SD, 3rd centile), short for the family (midparental height), and is the **growth velocity** normal?
- Normal growth after 2 years is at least about **4 to 5 cm per year** until puberty.
- **Familial short stature:** normal bone age, short parents. **Constitutional delay:** delayed bone age, late-maturing parent, final height normal.
- **Undernutrition** is the commonest cause of pathological short stature; check HIV, TB and thyroid function.
- **Unexplained short girl: karyotype** for Turner syndrome (45,X).
- **Faltering growth** is usually inadequate energy intake; non-organic causes outnumber organic ones.
- Simple obesity makes a child **tall**; obesity with **short stature** or falling velocity needs a hormonal work-up.

## 1. First, decide whether the problem is real

A single measurement is not enough.

1. **Is the child short (or light) for the population?** Height or weight below -2 SD (below the 3rd centile).
2. **Is the child short for the family?** Compare with the **midparental height**.
3. **Is the growth rate normal?** Velocity is the most useful single measurement. A child who is short but growing at a normal rate is usually a normal variant; a child whose velocity is falling is abnormal, however tall they are.

## 2. Short stature

**Definition:** height below the 3rd centile (-2 SD) for age and sex, or well below the parental target.

### Normal variants (most short children)

| Feature | Familial short stature | Constitutional delay of growth and puberty |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | Short | Often a late-maturing parent ("late bloomer") |
| Growth velocity | Normal | Normal, but slows in the early teens |
| Bone age | Equal to chronological age | **Delayed** |
| Puberty | Normal timing | Delayed |
| Final height | Short, as expected from parents | Normal (catch-up growth) |

### Pathological causes

**Proportionate** (normal upper : lower segment ratio):

![Malnutrition · Image from the Wikipedia article, Wikimedia Commons](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/A_malnourished_child_in_an_MSF_treatment_tent_in_Dolo_Ado.jpg/960px-A_malnourished_child_in_an_MSF_treatment_tent_in_Dolo_Ado.jpg)

- **Undernutrition**, commonest worldwide, often with repeated infection.
- **Chronic systemic disease:** HIV, TB, chronic kidney disease, congenital heart disease, coeliac disease, inflammatory bowel disease, severe asthma, sickle cell disease, chronic anaemia, cystic fibrosis.
- **Endocrine:** growth hormone deficiency, hypothyroidism, Cushing syndrome (including iatrogenic steroids), hypopituitarism, poorly controlled diabetes (Mauriac syndrome).
- **IUGR and small for gestational age** without catch-up by 2 years.
- **Genetic syndromes:** Turner (45,X), Down, Prader-Willi, Silver-Russell, Noonan.
- **Psychosocial deprivation:** severe neglect can switch off growth.

**Disproportionate** (abnormal upper : lower segment ratio):

- **Skeletal dysplasias**, such as achondroplasia (short limbs, normal trunk, large head).
- **Rickets** (bowed legs, widened wrists) and other metabolic bone disease.
- Spinal problems and some storage diseases.

> **Exam tip:** a short girl with a webbed neck, wide chest and delayed puberty has **Turner syndrome** until proven otherwise. Send a karyotype for every unexplained short girl.

### Approach to a short child

**History:** birth weight and gestation, feeding and diet, systemic symptoms (cough, diarrhoea, polyuria, headaches, vomiting), medications (corticosteroids), development, parents' heights and age at puberty, family illness, social circumstances.

![Growth chart · Image from the Wikipedia article, Wikimedia Commons](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/CDC_growth_chart_boys_birth_to_36_mths_cj41c017.pdf/page1-960px-CDC_growth_chart_boys_birth_to_36_mths_cj41c017.pdf.jpg)

**Examination:**
- Plot height, weight, head circumference and **growth velocity** over time.
- Measure the **upper : lower segment ratio** and arm span.
- Look for **dysmorphic features**, chronic disease, nutritional state, pubertal (Tanner) stage and hypothyroidism (goitre, dry skin, delayed reflexes).
- Neurological examination with visual fields (pituitary or hypothalamic tumour can cause short stature).

**Investigations (stepwise):**
1. First line: full blood count, ESR or CRP, urea and electrolytes, liver function, bone profile, urinalysis, HIV test, **TSH and free T4**, coeliac serology, **bone age** X-ray.
2. **Karyotype** in all short girls without obvious cause.
3. **IGF-1 and IGFBP-3**; if low, **growth hormone stimulation tests** (endocrinologist).
4. **MRI of pituitary and hypothalamus** if GH deficiency or tumour suspected.
5. Skeletal survey if proportions are abnormal.

### Management
- **Treat the cause:** feed the child, treat infection, levothyroxine for hypothyroidism, treat chronic disease, reduce or stop steroids if possible.
- **Growth hormone** (daily injection) for GH deficiency, Turner syndrome, chronic kidney disease, Prader-Willi syndrome and SGA children who have not caught up. Needs specialist supervision; cost is a major barrier in Kenya.
- **Reassure** families of children with familial short stature or constitutional delay. In selected boys with constitutional delay, a short course of testosterone can be used.

## 3. Faltering growth (failure to thrive)

**Definition:** weight (or weight gain) persistently below expectation, usually weight below the 3rd centile or a fall across two major centile lines. The commonest underlying problem is **inadequate energy intake**.

| Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Inadequate intake** | Poverty and food insecurity, poor feeding practices, incorrect formula dilution, early weaning, restrictive diets, poor breastfeeding technique, anorexia of illness, behavioural feeding problems, neglect |
| **Malabsorption or loss** | Coeliac disease, cystic fibrosis, chronic diarrhoea, giardiasis and other infections, cow's milk protein allergy, persistent vomiting or reflux |
| **Increased requirements** | Congenital heart disease, chronic lung disease, chronic infection (HIV, TB), malignancy, hyperthyroidism, chronic kidney disease |
| **Impaired utilisation** | Inborn errors of metabolism, genetic syndromes, endocrine disease |

- **Non-organic** (psychosocial and dietary) causes outnumber organic disease. Organic disease is more likely with vomiting, diarrhoea, recurrent infections or abnormal signs.

### Assessment
- **Dietary history** (24 hours or 3 days), feeding practices, appetite, mealtime behaviour, who feeds the child.
- Pregnancy, birth and perinatal history; development.
- **Social history:** income, food security, housing, caregiver mental health, sibling health, safeguarding concerns.
- Look for **dysmorphism**, signs of chronic disease, hydration and nutritional state (oedema, hair, skin).
- Observe a **feeding session**.
- **Tests only as indicated**; typical first line: full blood count, urea and electrolytes, urinalysis and culture, HIV, TB screen, stool microscopy, coeliac serology.

### Management
- Treat any organic cause.
- **Feeding advice:** breastfeeding support, energy-dense meals, frequent small feeds, fortified porridge, group sessions.
- **Severe acute malnutrition** per national guidelines: stabilise complications, then therapeutic feeds (F-75 then F-100 or ready-to-use therapeutic food) with community follow-up.
- Correct micronutrient deficiencies (iron, zinc, vitamin A) and deworm.
- Social support; involve social workers or community health workers if the home is the problem.
- **Review weight regularly**; reassess if there is no response to adequate feeding.

## 4. Tall stature

Much less common than short stature as a reason for referral.

![Human height · Image from the Wikipedia article, Wikimedia Commons](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/US_Navy_110426-N-00332-114_Students_measure_the_height_and_body_weight_of_fourth_and_fifth_grade_students_from_Lindenwood_Elementary_School.jpg/960px-US_Navy_110426-N-00332-114_Students_measure_the_height_and_body_weight_of_fourth_and_fifth_grade_students_from_Lindenwood_Elementary_School.jpg)

- **Familial tall stature** and **constitutional advance** of growth are the usual causes.
- **Endocrine:** precocious puberty (tall now, short as an adult because epiphyses fuse early), growth hormone excess (gigantism), hyperthyroidism, congenital adrenal hyperplasia (virilised).
- **Genetic syndromes:** **Marfan** (long limbs, arachnodactyly, lens dislocation, aortic root dilatation), **Klinefelter** (47,XXY), homocystinuria, Sotos, Beckwith-Wiedemann.
- **Obesity** causes tall stature in childhood, but final height is usually normal.
- **Approach:** growth velocity, parental heights, bone age, pubertal stage, then targeted tests (karyotype, hormone assays, echocardiography if Marfan suspected). Treat the cause.

## 5. Disorders of puberty

| Problem | Definition | Common causes |
|---|---|---|
| **Precocious puberty** | Girls: breast development before 8 years. Boys: testicular enlargement before 9 years | Central (early activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis): idiopathic in most girls, CNS lesions in boys. Peripheral: congenital adrenal hyperplasia, adrenal or gonadal tumours, McCune-Albright syndrome |
| **Delayed puberty** | Girls: no breast development by 13 years. Boys: no testicular enlargement (4 mL) by 14 years | Constitutional delay (commonest), chronic illness and undernutrition, hypogonadism (Turner, Klinefelter), hypothalamic-pituitary disease |

- Investigate with bone age, LH and FSH, sex steroids, pelvic ultrasound and brain MRI where indicated.

## 6. Overweight and obesity

**WHO definitions:**

![Overweight · Image from the Wikipedia article, Wikimedia Commons](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Body_mass_index_chart.svg/960px-Body_mass_index_chart.svg.png)

| Age | Overweight | Obesity |
|---|---|---|
| Under 5 years | Weight for height above +2 SD | Above +3 SD |
| 5 to 19 years | BMI for age above +1 SD | Above +2 SD |

- Childhood obesity is rising in Kenyan cities.
- Usually **simple (exogenous)**: excess intake, low activity, high screen time, family history, and **tall or normal** height.
- Consider a secondary cause when obesity comes with **short stature** or **falling growth velocity** (hypothyroidism, Cushing syndrome, GH deficiency) or **developmental delay and dysmorphism** (Prader-Willi and other syndromes).

**Complications:** type 2 diabetes and insulin resistance (acanthosis nigricans), hypertension, dyslipidaemia, fatty liver disease, obstructive sleep apnoea, slipped upper femoral epiphysis and knee pain, early puberty, psychological problems.

**Management:** family-based approach to healthy eating, more physical activity, less screen time, sleep, regular follow-up. Drugs and surgery are rarely used in children. Screen blood pressure, glucose and lipids when obesity is severe.

> **Remember:** a fat child who is also short is hormonal until proven otherwise; a fat child who is tall is usually simple obesity.

## Key points to revise

1. Look at growth **velocity**, **parental heights** and **bone age** before labelling a child abnormal.
2. Familial short stature has a normal bone age; constitutional delay has a delayed bone age; both end with expected or normal height.
3. Undernutrition and chronic disease cause most pathological short stature in Kenya; check HIV, TB and thyroid function.
4. Unexplained short girl: karyotype for Turner syndrome.
5. Faltering growth is usually inadequate energy intake; observe a feed and look at the home.
6. Precocious puberty: breasts before 8 years in girls, testes before 9 years in boys.
7. Obesity with short stature or poor velocity needs a hormonal work-up; simple obesity makes a child tall.
