# Diarrhoea in Children

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Gastroenterology**

> **In one line:** diarrhoea is the passage of three or more loose or liquid stools a day, mostly affecting children. It is classified by **mechanism** (osmotic, secretory, exudative, motility-related) and by **duration** (acute, persistent, chronic), and the pattern of the stool tests points to the cause.

## What you must know

- **Definition:** at least **3 loose or liquid stools per day** (stool weight above 200 to 300 g/day); mostly a disease of children.
- Four mechanisms: **osmotic, secretory, exudative (inflammatory), motility-related.**
- **Osmotic** diarrhoea **stops with fasting**; stool osmotic gap above 125 mOsm/kg. **Secretory** diarrhoea **continues with fasting**; osmotic gap below 100 mOsm/kg.
- **Fever, blood and pus (faecal leucocytes)** point to an invasive (exudative) process.
- Duration: **acute** below 2 weeks; **persistent** 2 to 4 weeks; **chronic** above 4 weeks.
- **Viral gastroenteritis (rotavirus)** is the commonest cause of acute diarrhoea worldwide.
- Complications: dehydration, electrolyte imbalance, metabolic acidosis, malnutrition.

## 1. Definition

Passing at least three loose or liquid stools a day (stool weight above 200 to 300 g/day). It mostly affects children.

## 2. Classification by mechanism

### 2.1 Osmotic diarrhoea

Poorly absorbed substances in the lumen raise osmotic pressure and **draw water into the gut**.

- **Features:** stool volume usually not massive; **improves with fasting** (less food in the gut means less osmotic load).
- **Causes:** malabsorption (lactose intolerance, coeliac disease); osmotic laxatives; **hexitols** (poorly absorbed sugars such as sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol in sugar-free gum); **giardiasis** (loss of brush border reduces absorption); disaccharidase deficiency (sugar cannot be absorbed).
- **Tests:** faecal smear for leucocytes **negative**; **stool osmotic gap above 125 mOsm/kg**.

### 2.2 Secretory diarrhoea

Active **secretion of water and electrolytes** into the gut.

- **Features:** **high stool output**; loss of **isotonic** fluid; **no response to fasting** (not related to osmosis); no mucosal invasion, which separates it from exudative diarrhoea.
- **Causes:** the commonest are **bacterial toxins** (enterotoxigenic *E. coli*, **cholera**) which stimulate anion secretion; **enteropathogenic viruses** (rotavirus, Norwalk virus); **neuroendocrine tumours** (carcinoid, which secretes serotonin; gastrinoma, a gastrin-secreting tumour of duodenum or pancreas that raises acid and causes ulceration and diarrhoea); rectal villous adenoma.
- **Tests:** faecal leucocytes negative; **osmotic gap below 100 mOsm/kg**; measure urinary **5-HIAA** (serotonin metabolite) to exclude carcinoid.

### Stool osmotic gap

Faecal osmolality equals serum osmolality (about 290 mOsm/kg). Normally the main osmoles are Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻ and HCO₃⁻ (normal faecal Na⁺ about 30 mmol/L, K⁺ about 75 mmol/L).

**Osmotic gap = stool osmolality - 2 × (stool Na⁺ + stool K⁺)**

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Above 125 mOsm/kg | Osmotic diarrhoea |
| Below 100 mOsm/kg | Secretory diarrhoea |

### 2.3 Exudative (inflammatory) diarrhoea

Outpouring of blood, protein or mucus from inflamed or ulcerated mucosa, so the stool contains **blood and pus**. It **persists on fasting**.

- **Causes:** invasive bacteria (*Campylobacter*, which invades jejunum, ileum and colon; *Salmonella typhi* and paratyphi A, B, C; **Shigella**, mainly in young children; enteroinvasive and enterohaemorrhagic *E. coli*); *Entamoeba histolytica*; **inflammatory bowel disease**.
- **Tests:** faecal leucocytes **positive**; stool culture and examination for ova and parasites.

### 2.4 Motility-related diarrhoea

Rapid transit of food through the gut (hypermotility). Example: **irritable bowel syndrome**, a motor disorder with abdominal pain and altered bowel habit, diarrhoea predominating.

### Summary table

| | Osmotic | Secretory | Exudative | Motility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume | Not massive | High | Variable, small and frequent | Variable |
| Fasting | Improves | No change | Persists | Variable |
| Faecal leucocytes | Negative | Negative | **Positive** | Negative |
| Stool osmotic gap | Above 125 | Below 100 | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| Examples | Lactose intolerance, coeliac, giardia | Cholera, ETEC, rotavirus, carcinoid | Shigella, amoebiasis, IBD | IBS |

## 3. Classification by duration

| Type | Duration |
|---|---|
| **Acute** | Less than 2 weeks |
| **Persistent** | 2 to 4 weeks |
| **Chronic** | More than 4 weeks |

### 3.1 Acute diarrhoea

**Causes:** infections (bacteria, viruses, helminths, protozoa), food poisoning, drugs.

- **Viral gastroenteritis**: the commonest cause worldwide; **rotavirus** especially in children.
- **Antibiotic-associated diarrhoea:** occurs in about 20% of patients on broad-spectrum antibiotics; about 20% of these are due to ***Clostridium difficile*** (Gram-positive rod), causing **pseudomembranous colitis**. Antibiotics allow overgrowth, with ulceration and necrosis of the bowel wall and a pseudomembrane of neutrophils, dead epithelium and inflammatory debris.

**Faecal leucocytes help to separate two groups:**

| | Inflammatory (leucocytes present) | Non-inflammatory (absent) |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Colonic mucosal damage by invasion or toxin | Small bowel source, or colon without mucosal injury |
| Examples | Shigellosis, salmonellosis, *Campylobacter* or *Yersinia*, amoebiasis, *C. difficile* toxin, *E. coli* O157:H7, IBD | Viral gastroenteritis, toxin-mediated (cholera, ETEC) |

### 3.2 Chronic diarrhoea

**Causes:** infection (*Giardia lamblia*; chronic intestinal infections such as cryptosporidiosis in AIDS); post-infectious; malabsorption; **IBD**; endocrine disease (e.g. hyperthyroidism); colon cancer; IBS (often linked to anxiety). Parasitic and protozoal infections affect over half of the world's population chronically or recurrently.

> **Remember:** in a Kenyan child with persistent or chronic diarrhoea always think of **HIV** (cryptosporidium, isospora), **giardiasis**, **malnutrition** and **post-infectious lactose intolerance**. Persistent diarrhoea in malnourished children is a high-mortality state.

#### Stepwise investigation of chronic diarrhoea

1. **Stool for ova and parasites.** Positive means infection. Negative: go to step 2.
2. **Stool fat test.** Negative suggests secretory or non-infectious inflammatory diarrhoea. Positive suggests **malabsorption**: go to step 3.
3. **Screen for malabsorption:** serum **anti-tissue transglutaminase antibodies**, **anti-endomysial IgA antibodies**, antigliadin antibodies (coeliac disease), and **duodenal biopsy**.

**Quantitative stool fat:** the best screening test; 72-hour collection; positive when above 7 g of fat per 24 hours (normal fat excretion below about 20% of intake is quoted on the slide).

## 4. Complications

1. **Dehydration** (fluid loss).
2. **Electrolyte imbalance** (sodium, potassium loss).
3. **Metabolic acidosis** (bicarbonate loss in stool).
4. **Malnutrition** if diarrhoea persists.

## 5. Management principles

The slides do not cover treatment, but the standard approach (WHO/Kenya MoH) is:

- **Assess dehydration** (none, some, severe) and treat by plan: **ORS** for none and some dehydration; **IV Ringer's lactate** for severe dehydration, then ORS.
- **Zinc** 10 mg daily (under 6 months) or 20 mg daily (6 months and above) for 10 to 14 days.
- **Continue feeding** and breastfeeding.
- **Antibiotics only for dysentery** (e.g. shigellosis), cholera in severe cases, or specific pathogens; antibiotics are not indicated for watery diarrhoea.
- Prevention: **rotavirus vaccine**, handwashing, clean water, exclusive breastfeeding.

> **Exam tip:** a child with an osmotic diarrhoea that stops when feeds are stopped is lactose-intolerant until proven otherwise; a child with cholera-like watery stool that continues despite nil by mouth has a secretory diarrhoea.

## Key points to revise

1. Diarrhoea: at least 3 loose stools a day; mostly children.
2. Four mechanisms: osmotic, secretory, exudative, motility.
3. Osmotic gap above 125 = osmotic (fasting helps); below 100 = secretory (fasting does not help).
4. Exudative diarrhoea: blood, pus and leucocytes in stool; Shigella, Campylobacter, Salmonella, amoeba, IBD.
5. Acute below 2 weeks, persistent 2 to 4 weeks, chronic above 4 weeks.
6. Rotavirus is the commonest cause of acute diarrhoea; C. difficile causes pseudomembranous colitis after antibiotics.
7. Chronic diarrhoea work-up: ova and parasites, then stool fat, then coeliac serology and duodenal biopsy.
8. Complications: dehydration, electrolyte disturbance, metabolic acidosis, malnutrition.
