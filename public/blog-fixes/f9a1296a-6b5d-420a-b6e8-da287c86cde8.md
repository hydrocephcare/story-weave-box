# HIV and AIDS

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Infectious Diseases and HIV**

> **In one line:** HIV is an RNA lentivirus that uses reverse transcriptase to make DNA copies of itself, infects and destroys CD4 T cells, and so progressively removes cellular immunity, leading to opportunistic infections and tumours (AIDS). Diagnosis is by antibody/antigen testing, staging uses the WHO clinical stages and CD4 count, and treatment is lifelong combination antiretroviral therapy (ART), now started in everyone with HIV.

## What you must know

- HIV is a **retrovirus** (family Retroviridae, subfamily **lentivirus**). **HIV-1** (groups M, N, O, P) causes most disease; **HIV-2** is milder and found mainly in West Africa.
- The hallmark of replication is **reverse transcription** of RNA to DNA by **reverse transcriptase**; the DNA is then integrated into the host genome by **integrase**.
- The target is the **CD4 T cell** (via CD4 receptor plus CCR5 or CXCR4 co-receptor).
- Clinical stage is set by the **WHO staging system** (stages 1 to 4); opportunistic risk rises as **CD4 falls below 200, 100 and 50**.
- **IRIS** (immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome) can follow starting ART.
- ART classes: NRTIs, NNRTIs, PIs, integrase inhibitors (INSTIs), fusion inhibitors, CCR5 antagonists, entry (post-attachment) inhibitors.
- Kenya first line for adults: **tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir (TLD)**.

## 1. Introduction and history

- AIDS was first recognised in the USA in the summer of **1981**.
- **1983:** HIV isolated from a patient with lymphadenopathy; **1984:** shown to be the cause of AIDS.
- **1985:** ELISA test developed.
- A confirmed HIV case is classified into one of five infection stages (0, 1, 2, 3 or unknown) for surveillance (stage 0 early infection, stages 1 to 3 by CD4 count, 3 being AIDS).

## 2. Epidemiology

- Affects all ages and ethnic groups and all areas.
- In the original US data males exceeded females, and AIDS became the leading cause of death in men aged 25 to 44 years.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa** carries the greatest burden worldwide; in Africa heterosexual spread and mother-to-child transmission predominate, with women and young girls disproportionately affected.
- Routes of transmission: **sexual** (commonest), **blood** (transfusion, needle sharing, needle-stick), and **mother to child** (in utero, at delivery, breastfeeding).

## 3. The virus

- HIV is the cause of AIDS. Of the four human disease-causing retroviruses, **HTLV-1 and HTLV-2** are transforming retroviruses, while **HIV-1 and HIV-2** are cytopathic lentiviruses.
- **HIV-1** groups: M (major, includes subtypes A to K), N, O, P. **HIV-2** groups A to H.
- Genome: two copies of single-stranded RNA with the genes *gag* (core proteins, p24), *pol* (reverse transcriptase, protease, integrase) and *env* (gp120 and gp41), plus regulatory genes (*tat*, *rev*) and accessory genes (*nef*, *vif*, *vpr*, *vpu*).

### Replication cycle

1. **Attachment:** gp120 binds CD4, then a co-receptor (CCR5 or CXCR4).
2. **Fusion and entry:** gp41.
3. **Reverse transcription:** viral RNA to DNA by reverse transcriptase.
4. **Integration** into host DNA by integrase (provirus).
5. **Transcription, translation and assembly;** polyproteins cleaved by **protease**.
6. **Budding** of new virions.

> **Remember:** each step is a drug target: entry/fusion inhibitors, NRTI and NNRTI (reverse transcription), INSTI (integration), PI (maturation).

## 4. Pathogenesis and natural history

- Acute infection: high viral load, CD4 falls, then partial recovery after seroconversion (a viral set point).
- **Acute retroviral (seroconversion) illness:** fever, pharyngitis, rash, lymphadenopathy, mouth ulcers, myalgia, 2 to 4 weeks after exposure.
- Chronic asymptomatic phase (average about 8 to 10 years untreated), with continuing CD4 decline from direct infection, apoptosis, increased immune activation and impaired production.
- Symptomatic HIV, then AIDS (defined by an AIDS-defining illness or CD4 below 200).
- The gut lymphoid tissue is an early site of major CD4 loss.

## 5. WHO clinical staging (adults and adolescents)

| Stage | Features |
|---|---|
| **1** | Asymptomatic; persistent generalised lymphadenopathy |
| **2** | Moderate unexplained weight loss (under 10%); recurrent URTI; herpes zoster; angular cheilitis; recurrent oral ulcers; papular pruritic eruption; seborrhoeic dermatitis; fungal nail infections |
| **3** | Unexplained severe weight loss (over 10%); chronic diarrhoea over 1 month; persistent fever; oral candidiasis; oral hairy leukoplakia; **pulmonary TB**; severe bacterial infections; acute necrotising gingivitis; anaemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia |
| **4** (AIDS) | HIV wasting; PJP; recurrent severe bacterial pneumonia; chronic HSV; oesophageal candidiasis; **extrapulmonary TB**; Kaposi's sarcoma; CMV disease; CNS toxoplasmosis; HIV encephalopathy; cryptococcal meningitis; PML; chronic cryptosporidiosis; lymphoma; invasive cervical cancer; disseminated non-tuberculous mycobacteria |

## 6. Spectrum of disease

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Viruses** | Adenovirus, parvovirus B19, CMV, EBV, HAV/HBV/HDV/HCV, HSV, **HHV-8 (Kaposi's sarcoma)**, HPV, JC virus (PML), molluscum contagiosum |
| **Bacteria** | *Bartonella*, *Campylobacter* and *Shigella* enteritis, *Chlamydia trachomatis*, *Listeria*, *Mycoplasma*, *Nocardia*, non-typhoidal *Salmonella*, pneumococcal infection, *S. aureus*, syphilis |
| **Mycobacteria** | *M. tuberculosis*, *M. avium-intracellulare*, other non-tuberculous mycobacteria, leprosy, BCG infection |
| **Fungi** | *Aspergillus*, *Candida*, *Cryptococcus neoformans*, dermatophytes, *Histoplasma*, *Pneumocystis jirovecii* |
| **Parasites** | *Toxoplasma gondii*, *Leishmania*, microsporidia, scabies, malaria (worse in pregnancy), *Schistosoma*, *Trichomonas*, *Strongyloides stercoralis* |
| **Malignancy** | Kaposi's sarcoma, B-cell lymphoma, Hodgkin disease, T-cell lymphoma, **invasive cervical cancer**, anal cancer |

### Direct HIV-induced conditions

Acute seroconversion illness, bone marrow dyshaemopoiesis, HIV encephalitis, cerebral vasculitis, vacuolar myelopathy, HIV-associated myopathy, cardiomyopathy, inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy, HIV enteritis, papular pruritic dermatitis, **HIVAN** (HIV-associated nephropathy), persistent generalised lymphadenopathy (PGL), wasting syndrome, metabolic dysfunction.

> **Exam tip:** HIVAN is a collapsing focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, especially in people of African descent; presents with heavy proteinuria and rapid decline in kidney function.

## 7. Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS)

- A paradoxical worsening (or unmasking) of an infection soon after starting ART, as the recovering immune system reacts to pathogen antigens.
- Two types: **unmasking** (new presentation of a subclinical infection) and **paradoxical** (worsening of a known, treated infection).
- Usually within the first 3 months; classic triggers are TB, cryptococcus, CMV and herpes zoster.
- Management: continue ART in most cases, treat the underlying OI, and consider corticosteroids for severe TB-IRIS. Risk is highest with low starting CD4 and a recent OI.

## 8. Illustrations of AIDS disease

- **Kaposi's sarcoma:** violaceous skin and oral (gingival, palatal) lesions, e.g. on the legs; caused by HHV-8.
- **Primary CNS lymphoma** (EBV related).
- **Oral candidiasis.**

## 9. Antiretroviral therapy

### Classes of ART

| Class | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **NRTIs** | Nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors | Tenofovir (TDF), lamivudine (3TC), emtricitabine (FTC), abacavir (ABC), zidovudine (AZT) |
| **NNRTIs** | Non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors | Efavirenz, nevirapine |
| **PIs** | Protease inhibitors | Lopinavir/ritonavir, atazanavir/ritonavir, darunavir |
| **INSTIs** | Integrase strand transfer inhibitors | **Dolutegravir**, raltegravir |
| **Fusion inhibitors** | Block gp41-mediated fusion | Enfuvirtide |
| **Chemokine receptor antagonists** | Block CCR5 | Maraviroc |
| **Entry inhibitors** | CD4-directed post-attachment inhibitors | Ibalizumab |

### Practical points (Kenya Ministry of Health guidance)

- **Start ART in all people living with HIV regardless of CD4 count or clinical stage** ("treat all"), ideally within days to two weeks of diagnosis after readiness counselling.
- **Preferred first-line regimen** for adults and adolescents: **TDF + 3TC + DTG (TLD)**, a once-daily fixed-dose combination. Alternatives (e.g. efavirenz-based, abacavir-based) are used where TDF or DTG cannot be given.
- **Second line** after first-line failure: typically a boosted PI-based regimen with an optimised NRTI backbone; **third line** is for failure of both and needs specialist advice (often darunavir, raltegravir/dolutegravir and etravirine).
- Timing in TB, cryptococcal meningitis and other OIs needs special consideration (delay ART in cryptococcal meningitis for 4 to 6 weeks; start within 2 to 8 weeks in TB depending on CD4).
- Dose adjustment of **TDF and 3TC** is needed in renal impairment; ARVs, co-trimoxazole and fluconazole also need adjustment in hepatic and renal impairment.
- Main adverse effects: TDF (renal toxicity, bone loss), AZT (anaemia), abacavir (hypersensitivity, HLA-B*5701), efavirenz (neuropsychiatric), nevirapine (rash, hepatitis), PIs (metabolic, GI), dolutegravir (weight gain, insomnia, neural tube defect concern now considered small).

### Monitoring

- Viral load at 6 months, 12 months and then yearly; a viral load above the threshold (≥1,000 copies/mL on repeated testing after adherence support) means treatment failure.
- CD4 at baseline and where clinical status or advanced HIV disease needs it.

## 10. Initial clinical evaluation of a person living with HIV

- **History:** date and mode of acquisition, prior ART, TB history and contacts, OI symptoms, sexual and reproductive history, mental health, substance use, vaccination, adherence readiness.
- **Examination:** weight, nutrition, skin, mouth, lymph nodes, chest, abdomen, CNS, fundi where indicated, genital examination, cervical screening for women.
- **Baseline tests:** confirmatory HIV status, CD4, haemoglobin, creatinine, urinalysis, liver function, hepatitis B surface antigen, TB symptom screen (and Xpert if positive), cryptococcal antigen if CD4 is below 200, syphilis serology, pregnancy test.
- **Preventive care:** co-trimoxazole prophylaxis, TB preventive therapy (isoniazid or rifapentine-based) once active TB is excluded, vaccination, cervical screening, counselling and partner testing.

## Key points to revise

1. HIV is a lentivirus; reverse transcriptase makes DNA from RNA, which integrates into host DNA; CD4 T cells are the target.
2. Diagnosis in Kenya uses rapid tests in series, and staging uses WHO stage plus CD4 count.
3. WHO stage 3 and 4 include TB, oesophageal candidiasis, PJP, Kaposi's sarcoma, cryptococcal meningitis and wasting.
4. Opportunistic infections appear at predictable CD4 levels (below 200 PJP, below 100 toxoplasma and cryptococcus, below 50 CMV and MAC).
5. IRIS is a paradoxical worsening after starting ART; treat the OI and usually continue ART.
6. ART classes: NRTI, NNRTI, PI, INSTI, fusion, CCR5 and entry inhibitors; first line is TLD.
7. Treat all, lifelong; monitor viral load; adjust TDF and 3TC in renal impairment.
