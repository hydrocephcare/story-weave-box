## FOUNDATION: NORMAL CELL CYCLE
**Key players:** Cyclins | CDKs | CDK Inhibitors (CKDIs)
CDKs are constitutively inactive — activated only after binding their specific cyclin. Cyclins are synthesized at specific phases to activate CDKs. Over 15 cyclins identified. They appear sequentially: **Cyclin D → E → A → B**, each binding one or more CDKs.

### Cyclin D and RB Phosphorylation
The most important cell cycle concept to know.
- Cyclin D is the first cyclin to rise (mid-G1)
- Cyclin D binds CDK4 → **Cyclin D-CDK4 complex** → phosphorylates RB protein
- RB = the **molecular on/off switch** of the cell cycle
**Hypophosphorylated RB** (active state): binds E2F transcription factor → blocks progression → cell STAYS in G1
**Hyperphosphorylated RB** (inactive state): releases E2F → E2F target genes transcribed → cell enters **S phase**

### Cell Cycle Progression
**G1/S Restriction Point**
- Cyclin E-CDK2 forms → E2F increases cyclin E transcription → DNA replication begins
- S phase = **point of no return**
**G2/M Transition**
- E2F transcribes Cyclin A → Cyclin A-CDK2 (regulates mitotic prophase)
- Cyclin B-CDK1 (activated by protein phosphatase) → breaks down nuclear membrane → **mitosis begins**

### Cell Cycle Checkpoints
**G1/S checkpoint** — checks for DNA damage
- DNA damaged → repair + cell cycle arrest
- Irreparable damage → apoptosis
**G2/M checkpoint** — confirms DNA replication is complete before mitosis
- Critical after ionizing radiation exposure
- Defects → chromosomal abnormalities

### CDK Inhibitors (CKDIs) — tumour suppressors
**Cip/Kip family:** p21, p27, p57
- Bind and inactivate cyclin-CDK complexes
- p21 transcription controlled by **p53** (guardian of the genome)
- p53 = surveillance protein; mutated in many cancers
**INK4/ARF family:** p16INK4a and p14ARF
- p16INK4a competes with Cyclin D for CDK4 binding → prevents RB phosphorylation → **G1 arrest**
- Frequently mutated or inactivated by **hypermethylation** in cancers

## HALLMARK 1: SELF-SUFFICIENCY IN GROWTH SIGNALS
**Core concept:** Cancer cells grow autonomously without external mitogenic signals via oncogenes.
**Definitions**
- **Proto-oncogenes:** Normal physiological regulators of cell proliferation and differentiation
- **Oncogenes:** Mutated/activated proto-oncogenes that drive uncontrolled proliferation
- **Oncoproteins:** Resemble normal proto-oncogene products but lack regulatory elements → constitutively active
**Mechanisms — 6 levels**
**1. Growth factors**
Cancer cells synthesize the same growth factors they have receptors for → autocrine self-stimulation
**2. Growth factor receptors**
Mutant receptors constitutively dimerize and signal without ligand binding → continuous mitogenic signal
**3. Signal-transducing proteins (RAS oncogene)**
Located under cell membrane, relay signals from receptors to nucleus. RAS oncogene — mutant RAS is permanently active (cannot hydrolyse GTP)
**4. Non-receptor tyrosine kinases**
- c-ABL gene translocated **t(9;22)** in CML and some ALL
- Creates **BCR-ABL** fusion gene → BCR-ABL protein loses inhibitory region → constitutive kinase activity
- This translocation = **Philadelphia chromosome**
**5. Transcription factors**
- MYC, MYB, JUN, FOS oncogene products found in nuclei of transformed cells
- **MYC** = most commonly involved oncogene in human cancers; potent transcriptional activator
**6. Cyclins and CDKs**
- Abnormal expression in: breast, oesophagus, head and neck, liver, mantle cell lymphoma
- **CDK4 gene amplification** in sarcomas and glioblastoma

## HALLMARK 2: INSENSITIVITY TO GROWTH INHIBITORY SIGNALS
**Core concept:** Tumour suppressor genes apply brakes to proliferation. Unlike oncogenes, **both copies must be lost** for tumour development (Knudson's two-hit hypothesis).

### RB Gene — "Governor of the Cell Cycle"
- First tumour suppressor gene discovered | Locus **13q14**
- **Knudson's Two-Hit Hypothesis** — from studying retinoblastoma:
  - **Familial:** one germline mutation inherited + second somatic hit → tumour
  - **Sporadic:** both alleles lost by somatic mutation in a single retinoblast
- Homozygous RB loss seen in many cancers
- Heterozygosity at RB locus alone is NOT neoplastic
**Mechanism:** RB (hypophosphorylated, active) binds E2F → blocks cyclin E transcription → no S phase entry
**In cancer:**
- Almost all cancers have a disabled G1 checkpoint via: RB mutation OR mutations in cyclin D, CDK4, or CDKIs
- HPV and other oncogenic DNA viruses encode proteins that bind and inactivate RB

### TP53 Gene — "Guardian of the Genome"
**Activating stresses:** DNA damage | anoxia | inappropriate oncogene signalling
**Activation mechanism:** DNA damage → p53 phosphorylation → p53 activated
**Functions of activated p53:**
- Cell cycle arrest (G1/S block)
- DNA repair
- Cellular senescence
- Apoptosis
**Mechanism:** Activated p53 → transcribes **CDKN1A** → prevents RB phosphorylation → G1/S block → time for repair. If irreparable → senescence or apoptosis.
**In cancer:**
- **70% of tumours** show biallelic TP53 loss
- **Li Fraumeni Syndrome:** germline TP53 mutation (one allele) — second somatic hit later
- HPV also inactivates p53 by protein binding

### TGF-β (Transforming Growth Factor-β) Pathway
- Potent inhibitor of proliferation in normal epithelial, endothelial, and haematopoietic cells
- TGF-β binds TGF-β receptor I + II → receptor dimerisation → cascade → activates CDKIs + represses MYC, CDK2, CDK4, cyclin A, cyclin E
**In cancer:**
- Mutated in **100% of pancreatic cancers** and **83% of colon cancers**
- In **late-stage tumours:** TGF-β activates **EMT (Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition)** → promotes invasion and metastasis (paradoxical pro-tumour effect)

### Contact Inhibition, NF2 and APC
**Contact inhibition** — normal cells stop dividing on cell-to-cell contact; abolished in cancer cells
- **E-cadherin** maintains contact inhibition → lost in malignant cells
- **NF2 gene** → produces **neurofibromin-2 (merlin)** → facilitates E-cadherin-mediated contact inhibition
**APC gene** → regulates destruction of cytoplasmic **β-catenin**
- APC loss → β-catenin accumulates → translocates to nucleus → acts as growth-promoting transcription factor
- **Familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP):** germline APC mutation → hundreds of colonic polyps → inevitable malignant transformation
- Somatic loss of both APC alleles in **~70% of sporadic colon cancers**

## HALLMARK 3: EVASION OF CELL DEATH
### Apoptosis
Two pathways:
- **Extrinsic:** death receptor-mediated (e.g., FAS ligand)
- **Intrinsic (mitochondrial):** regulated by BCL2 family
Both converge → **caspase cascade** → cell destruction
**BCL2 family — the critical balance:**
| Pro-apoptotic | Anti-apoptotic | Regulators (BH3-only) |
|---|---|---|
| BAX, BAK | BCL2, BCL-XL | BAD, BID, PUMA |
BH3-only proteins tilt balance toward pro-apoptotic molecules → apoptosis proceeds
**Cancer evasion mechanisms:**
- Overexpression of BCL2 (e.g., follicular lymphoma — **t(14;18)**)
- Downregulation of BAX, BAK
- TP53 loss → reduced apoptosis signalling
- Viral proteins that mimic BCL2

### Autophagy
- Stress-induced process: cells digest their own components for survival
- Cancer cells either:
  - Accumulate mutations to **avoid** autophagy, OR
  - **Corrupt** it to supply biosynthetic building blocks for growth (dual role)

## HALLMARK 4: LIMITLESS REPLICATIVE POTENTIAL
**Normal cells:** Telomeres shorten with each division → checkpoints activated → **senescence** (Hayflick limit)
**In cells with disabled checkpoints:** Shortened telomeres inappropriately activate DNA repair → massive **chromosomal instability** → **mitotic crisis**
**Cancer solution:** Reactivation of **telomerase** → elongates telomeres → avoids mitotic catastrophe → **immortality**

## HALLMARK 5: DEVELOPMENT OF SUSTAINED ANGIOGENESIS
**Why tumours need angiogenesis:**
- Cannot enlarge beyond **1–2 mm** without vascular supply
- Maximal diffusion distance of O₂ and nutrients from vessels = ~1–2 mm
- Need: oxygen, nutrients, waste removal
**Types of new vessel formation:**
- **Neoangiogenesis:** sprouting from existing capillaries
- **Vasculogenesis:** endothelial recruitment from bone marrow
Tumour vasculature is **abnormal** — leaky, dilated, haphazard connections
**Dual effects of neovascularisation:**
1. Perfusion — delivers O₂ and nutrients
2. Endothelial cells secrete growth factors (IGF, PDGF, GM-CSF) → stimulate adjacent tumour cells

### The Angiogenic Switch
Balance between pro- and anti-angiogenic factors:
- **Prototypical inducer:** VEGF (Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor)
- **Prototypical inhibitor:** TSP-1 (Thrombospondin-1) — induced by **normal p53**
Early tumours remain small/dormant until the switch is activated.
Switch = increased angiogenic factors AND/OR loss of angiogenesis inhibitors. Sources include tumour cells, macrophages, and stromal cells.
**Proteases and angiogenesis:**
- MMPs release bFGF stored in ECM → pro-angiogenic
- Proteolytic cleavage produces inhibitors: **angiostatin** (from plasminogen), **endostatin** (from collagen), **vasculostatin** (from transthyretin)
- Stromal fibroblasts produce TSP-1 in response to tumour signals

### HIF-1α Pathway — Must Know
**In normoxia:**
VHL protein binds HIF-1α → ubiquitination → **degradation**
**In hypoxia (tumour > critical size):**
VHL cannot bind HIF-1α → HIF-1α translocates to nucleus → transcribes **VEGF** and other pro-angiogenic genes
VHL = tumour suppressor gene
**VHL syndrome (germline VHL mutation):**
Hereditary renal cell cancers | phaeochromocytomas | CNS haemangiomas | retinal angiomas | renal cysts
VEGF also activates **Notch signalling** → regulates branching and density of new vessels
**Clinical application:** Anti-VEGF antibody (**bevacizumab**) approved for treatment of several cancers

## HALLMARK 6: ABILITY TO INVADE AND METASTASIZE
### The Invasion-Metastasis Cascade (sequential steps)
1. Local invasion
2. Intravasation into blood/lymph vessels
3. Transit through vasculature
4. Extravasation from vessels
5. Formation of micrometastases
6. Growth into macroscopic tumours
**Two main phases:**
- Phase 1: Invasion of ECM
- Phase 2: Vascular dissemination and homing

### Phase 1 — Invasion of ECM (4 steps)
**Step 1 — Loosening of tumour cell-to-cell contacts**
E-cadherins = intercellular glue; cytoplasmic portions bind β-catenin. E-cadherin also transmits antigrowth signals by sequestering β-catenin.
E-cadherin function lost in almost all epithelial cancers via:
- Mutational inactivation of E-cadherin genes
- Activation of β-catenin genes
- Overexpression of **SNAIL and TWIST** transcription factors (suppress E-cadherin)
**Step 2 — Local degradation of basement membrane and connective tissue**
Tumour cells secrete proteases OR induce stromal cells (fibroblasts, inflammatory cells) to do so.
Key proteases: **MMPs, cathepsin D, urokinase plasminogen activator**
**MMP-9** (gelatinase): cleaves type IV collagen of basement membrane + stimulates VEGF release from ECM
Benign tumours = low collagenase activity | Malignant = high
Metalloproteinase inhibitors are simultaneously reduced → balance tips toward degradation
**Step 3 — Changes in attachment to ECM proteins**
Normal cells: integrins at basal surface bind laminin and collagen → maintain resting/differentiated state. Detachment → apoptosis.
Tumour cells: resist detachment-induced apoptosis. MMP-2 and MMP-9 cleave collagen IV and laminin → generate **new receptor-binding sites** → stimulate tumour cell migration
**Step 4 — Locomotion**
Final step: tumour cells propelled through degraded ECM via actin cytoskeleton.
Directed by: autocrine motility factors | ECM cleavage products (chemotactic) | IGF-I and IGF-II
**HGF/SCF (hepatocyte growth factor/scatter factor)** produced by stromal cells → binds tumour cell receptors → paracrine motility signal. Elevated at advancing edges of **glioblastoma multiforme**.

### Role of Stromal Cells
Stroma is not a passive barrier — it is an active, dynamic environment.
Stromal cells (fibroblasts, immune cells) engage in **reciprocal signalling** with tumour cells. Tumour-associated fibroblasts alter ECM molecules, proteases, inhibitors, and growth factors. The most successful tumours co-opt this environment to their advantage.

### Phase 2 — Vascular Dissemination and Homing
- Tumour cells form **emboli** by aggregating with leukocytes and platelets → protected from immune attack
- Most circulate as single cells
- Extravasation: adhesion to vascular endothelium → egress through basement membrane into organ parenchyma
**Site of metastasis** predicted by: anatomical location of primary tumour and its vascular/lymphatic drainage. Many tumours metastasize to the **first capillary bed** encountered after entering circulation.
**Organ tropism — mechanisms:**
- Adhesion molecules on tumour cells match endothelium of target organs
- **Chemokine receptor matching:** Breast cancer cells express **CXCR4** and **CCR7**; their ligands CXCL12 and CCL21 are highly expressed in target organs (lung, liver, bone, lymph nodes) → blockade of these receptors may limit metastasis
**Permissive stroma:** Target tissue must be receptive. Skeletal muscle is well vascularised but rarely a metastatic site. Tumour cells secrete cytokines, growth factors, and proteases to modify the stromal environment and make the metastatic niche habitable.
**Note:** Millions of tumour cells shed daily from small tumours but gross metastases do not always develop — colonisation is inefficient.

### Molecular Genetics of Metastasis
**Classical (stochastic) model:** Random accumulated mutations in subclones → rare subpopulation acquires metastatic phenotype
**Modern (gene signature) model:** Some tumours acquire a metastasis-permissive gene expression signature **early in carcinogenesis** — before clinical evidence of metastasis appears (shown by gene profiling studies of breast cancer)
**Current view:** Both mechanisms likely operate together — early permissive signature + additional random mutations to complete the metastatic phenotype.

## HALLMARK 7: REPROGRAMMING OF ENERGY METABOLISM
### The Warburg Effect (Aerobic Glycolysis)
Cancer cells use glycolysis even in the **presence of adequate oxygen** instead of the more efficient mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation.
| Pathway | ATP yield | Cancer preference |
|---|---|---|
| Oxidative phosphorylation | 36 ATP/glucose | No |
| Aerobic glycolysis (Warburg) | 2 ATP/glucose | Yes |
Pyruvate is shunted toward **biosynthetic pathways** rather than oxidative phosphorylation — produces building blocks for rapidly dividing cells.
**Why is this advantageous despite being inefficient?**
- Provides rapid biosynthetic precursors for cell growth
- Tumours adopting this (e.g., **Burkitt lymphoma**) are the fastest-growing human cancers
- Also seen in rapidly dividing normal cells (embryo) — favoured whenever rapid growth is needed
- Darwinian advantage: cells that adopt this outpace competitors
**Clinical application — PET scanning:**
- Patient injected with **18F-fluorodeoxyglucose** (non-metabolisable glucose derivative)
- Glucose-hungry tumours take it up → PET-positive
- Rapidly growing tumours are markedly PET-positive
**Molecular basis:**
- TP53, PTEN, and Akt (RAS signalling intermediary) regulate glucose transporter proteins and promote aerobic glycolysis
- Drugs targeting the Warburg pathway are under development

## HALLMARK 8: EVASION OF THE IMMUNE SYSTEM
- Recognised as a core hallmark (alongside energy reprogramming)
- Most tumours arise in immunocompetent hosts
- Strategy: trick immune system so tumour is not recognised or eliminated
- Mechanisms involve: downregulation of MHC-I, expression of immune checkpoint ligands (e.g., PD-L1), secretion of immunosuppressive cytokines (e.g., TGF-β, IL-10), recruitment of regulatory T cells
- Detailed mechanisms in tumour immunology lectures

## HALLMARK 9: GENOMIC INSTABILITY *(Enabler)*
**Core concept:** Increased mutation rate enables accumulation of cancer-driving mutations. Inherited DNA repair defects confer very high cancer risk.
| Syndrome | Defective Pathway | Consequence | Cancer Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| HNPCC (Lynch syndrome) | Mismatch repair (MMR) | Microsatellite instability (MSI) | Colorectal carcinoma |
| Xeroderma pigmentosum | Nucleotide excision repair (NER) | Cannot repair UV-induced pyrimidine dimers | Skin cancers |
| Bloom syndrome, Ataxia-telangiectasia, Fanconi anaemia | Homologous recombination | Hypersensitivity to ionizing radiation | Various cancers |
| BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations | Homologous recombination | Failed repair of double-strand breaks | Familial breast and ovarian cancers |
RAG1, RAG2, AID — gene products in lymphoid cells that induce genomic instability → important causes of **lymphoid neoplasms**

## HALLMARK 10: TUMOUR-PROMOTING INFLAMMATION *(Enabler)*
### Setting 1 — Chronic pre-existing inflammation → tumour initiation
Examples: Barrett oesophagus | Ulcerative colitis | H. pylori gastritis | Hepatitis B and C | Chronic pancreatitis
Mechanism: Persistent tissue injury → compensatory proliferation + growth factors/cytokines from immune cells → cells accumulate mutations → carcinogenesis. Neutrophils secrete **reactive oxygen species (ROS)** → additional DNA damage in rapidly dividing cells.
### Setting 2 — Inflammation in response to established tumour → promotes progression
- Virtually all tumours contain infiltrating leukocytes (adaptive + innate)
- While initially a protective host response, inflammatory cells can promote tumour growth by secreting growth factors and inflicting additional DNA damage
### Clinical implications
**COX-2 (cyclooxygenase-2):** converts arachidonic acid to prostaglandins; induced by inflammatory stimuli; **overexpressed in colon cancers** and other tumours. COX-2 inhibitors under active investigation for cancer prevention and treatment.

## MULTISTEP CARCINOGENESIS — COLON CANCER MODEL
Best-characterised stepwise model of cancer development:
Normal epithelium → Hyperplasia → Small adenoma → Large adenoma → **Carcinoma**
**Molecular sequence (in order):**
1. **APC loss** — initiating event
2. **RAS activation**
3. Loss of tumour suppressor on **18q**
4. **TP53 loss** — final step, malignant transformation
Note: precise temporal sequence may differ between individual tumours. Each cancer results from accumulation of multiple mutations — no single hit is sufficient.
