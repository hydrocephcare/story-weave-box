# Long-Term Complications of Diabetes in Children

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Endocrinology**

> **In one line:** chronic hyperglycaemia damages small and large vessels and nerves through **glycation of tissue proteins and the polyol pathway**, producing retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy and, less commonly, bone disease and growth problems; **good glycaemic control and regular screening** delay or prevent most of them.

## What you must know

- **Microvascular:** retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy. **Macrovascular:** coronary, cerebrovascular and peripheral vascular disease. Also **diabetic osteopathy** (osteoporosis and fractures).
- Retinopathy risk after 15 years is **98% in T1DM** and 78% in T2DM; screen from **3 to 5 years after onset in T1DM (not before age 10)**, then every 1 to 2 years.
- Nephropathy: first sign is **microalbuminuria (30 to 300 mg/24 h)**; screen **yearly from 5 years after onset in T1DM**, confirm on 2 of 3 samples over 3 to 6 months.
- Nephropathy is treated with tight glucose control, **BP control, ACE inhibitors** and protein restriction.
- **Mauriac syndrome** = growth failure, hepatomegaly and moon face from chronic under-insulinisation.
- T1DM bone: low bone density and 2 to 7 times higher hip fracture risk in adults.

## 1. Overview and relation to glycaemic control

| Category | Complications |
|---|---|
| **Microvascular** | Retinopathy, nephropathy |
| **Macrovascular** | Coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, peripheral vascular disease |
| **Neuropathies** | Peripheral and autonomic |
| **Bone** | Diabetic osteopathy: osteoporosis and fracture |

Risk rises with **duration of diabetes, HbA1c, blood pressure and cholesterol**, and is modified by genetic factors.

## 2. Diabetic retinopathy

- Risk after **15 years' duration**: **98% in T1DM**, 78% in T2DM. Independent of duration, prevalence is higher in T1DM.
- Genetic factors matter: only about **50%** develop proliferative disease.
- **Lens opacities** (glycation of tissue proteins and activation of the polyol pathway) occur in at least 5% under age 19.

> **Check:** the slide quotes rates "from close to 15% to up to 30%" for retinopathy in childhood without context; in practice prevalence is low before puberty and rises steeply with duration and poor control.

### Stages

| Stage | Features | Effect on vision |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-proliferative (background)** | Microaneurysms, dot and blot haemorrhages, hard and soft exudates, venous dilatation and beading, intraretinal microvascular abnormalities | None |
| **Proliferative** | **Neovascularisation**, fibrous proliferation, pre-retinal and vitreous haemorrhage; relentlessly progressive | Impaired, leading to blindness |
| **Maculopathy** | Severe macular oedema | Loss of central vision |
| **Involutional** | Disease becomes quiescent | |

### Treatment

- **Panretinal laser photocoagulation** is the mainstay for proliferative disease.
- **Focal laser** for maculopathy.
- **Vitrectomy** for advanced disease (severe vitreous haemorrhage or fibrosis, often with retinal detachment).

### Screening

Dilated, comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist: **shortly after diagnosis in T2DM; within 3 to 5 years of onset in T1DM (but not before age 10)**; then **every 1 to 2 years**. Refer any visual symptoms at once.

## 3. Diabetic nephropathy

- Most ESRD from diabetic nephropathy is **preventable**.
- Affects **20 to 30% of T1DM** and 15 to 20% of T2DM patients 20 years after onset. Only 30 to 40% of T1DM patients eventually reach ESRD.
- Five-year survival with diabetes-related ESRD is under 20%; nephropathy may account for about 50% of deaths in long-term T1DM.
- Risk rises with duration (up to 25 to 30 years, after which it rarely starts), poor control and a genetic tendency to hypertension.
- **Mechanism:** glycation of tissue proteins thickens the glomerular basement membrane; hyperfiltration raises intraglomerular pressure.

### Stages (slow course)

| Stage | Findings |
|---|---|
| Early (incipient) | **Microalbuminuria**: albumin excretion 30 to 300 mg/24 h (20 to 200 µg/min); hyperfiltration and rising BP |
| Early overt | **Proteinuria**: above 300 mg/24 h (above 200 µg/min), with hypertension |
| Advanced | Falling GFR, raised urea and creatinine, progressive proteinuria, hypertension |
| ESRD | Uraemia, nephrotic/nephritic features, need for dialysis or transplant |

### Screening

- **Yearly** for T2DM; yearly for T1DM **after 5 years' duration**, using a **random spot urine albumin:creatinine ratio**.
- Confirm abnormal results on **2 further samples** on separate days (high variability). At least **2 of 3 samples over 3 to 6 months** must be abnormal before diagnosing microalbuminuria.
- False elevations: short-term hyperglycaemia, strenuous exercise, UTI, marked hypertension, heart failure, acute febrile illness.

### Treatment (reducing hyperfiltration)

1. **Meticulous glycaemic control.**
2. **Aggressive BP control.**
3. **ACE inhibitors** (dilate the efferent arteriole, lowering glomerular capillary pressure).
4. **Dietary protein restriction** (high protein raises renal perfusion).

Previous intensive therapy gives a lasting benefit for 7 to 8 years ("metabolic memory").

## 4. Diabetic neuropathy

- Can affect peripheral and autonomic nerves, even in children and adolescents.
- Cause incompletely understood. Proposed: **polyol pathway**, **non-enzymatic glycation**, disturbed **myo-inositol** metabolism, reduced neurotrophic effects of insulin and IGFs, nitric oxide and stress proteins.
- Recent studies in childhood found only a weak link with glycaemic control or disease duration, although older (pre-intensive insulin) studies reported higher rates.
- **Subclinical findings:** abnormal thermal perception on quantitative sensory testing in asymptomatic young patients (heat pain threshold correlates with duration, not with control); reduced sensory nerve conduction velocity and amplitude in **10 to 58%** of children.
- **Autonomic:** early sign is reduced **heart rate variability**, seen in adolescents with long disease and poor control.

### Treatment options (results variable)

| Approach | Examples |
|---|---|
| Better metabolic control | |
| Aldose reductase inhibitors | Reduce polyol pathway products |
| Antioxidant | **α-lipoic acid** |
| Anticonvulsants for neuropathic pain | Gabapentin, pregabalin, carbamazepine, valproate, phenytoin, topiramate, tiagabine |
| Antidepressants | Amitriptyline, imipramine, SSRIs |
| Others | Lidocaine (antiarrhythmic), topical analgesics, NSAIDs |

## 5. Skeletal effects of type 1 diabetes

- T1DM carries a higher skeletal risk than T2DM; increased fracture risk starts in childhood and persists for life.
- **Hip fracture risk 2 to 7 times higher** in adults. Most studies show **low bone mineral density** in T1DM; in T2DM density is normal or high because of obesity and mechanical loading.
- Density deficit does not fully explain fracture risk, so **bone quality** is probably impaired.
- Mechanisms (multifactorial): poor control/insulin deficiency, accumulation of **advanced glycation end products** in bone, chronic inflammation, abnormal **GH-IGF-1 axis**, and abnormal mineral metabolism (urinary calcium loss).
- No standard paediatric screening guideline. **DXA** and bone markers in adults with fracture or osteoporosis risk. Advise adequate **calcium and vitamin D** (RDA).

## 6. Other complications

- **Mauriac syndrome:** rare, from **chronic under-insulinisation**. Features: **growth failure, hepatomegaly** (glycogen and fat), moon face, protuberant abdomen, proximal muscle wasting. Now uncommon because of longer-acting insulins.
- **Limited joint mobility** (cheiroarthropathy): associated with early retinopathy and nephropathy (may appear before age 18); prevalence has fallen with better control.

> **Exam tip:** the earliest marker of diabetic kidney disease is **microalbuminuria**; the earliest retinal sign is **microaneurysms**; and screening for each starts at different times in T1DM (eyes at 3 to 5 years and age 10 or more; urine at 5 years).

## 7. Prevention in practice

Target HbA1c control, treat BP and lipids, annual eye, kidney, foot and neuropathy checks, and address smoking and obesity in adolescents.

## Key points to revise

1. Micro- and macrovascular, neuropathic and bone complications; risk follows duration and HbA1c.
2. Retinopathy: background (no visual loss) to proliferative (neovascularisation); treat with panretinal laser.
3. Eye screening: 3 to 5 years after T1DM onset (not before 10), then every 1 to 2 years.
4. Nephropathy begins with microalbuminuria (30 to 300 mg/24 h); screen yearly after 5 years in T1DM; confirm 2 of 3 samples.
5. Slow progression of nephropathy with glucose, BP, ACE inhibitor and protein control.
6. Neuropathy: polyol and glycation theories; treat pain with anticonvulsants or antidepressants.
7. T1DM lowers bone density and raises hip fracture risk 2 to 7 times.
8. Mauriac syndrome: poor growth, hepatomegaly, moon face from chronic under-insulinisation.
