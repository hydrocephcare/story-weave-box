# Evaluation of the Child with Special Needs: Assessment, Team Care and Support

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Developmental and community paediatrics**

> **In one line:** a child with special needs has a chronic physical, developmental, behavioural or emotional condition requiring services beyond those most children need. Evaluation aims to establish what the child can do, why the problem exists, what else is affected, and what support the child and family need, and not simply to attach a label.

## What you must know

- Children with special needs include cerebral palsy, intellectual disability, autism, Down syndrome, hearing or vision impairment, epilepsy, chronic illness (sickle cell disease, HIV) and technology-dependent children.
- Care is **child and family-centred, strengths-based, early, holistic, multidisciplinary and rights-based**.
- History must include a **family tree**, pregnancy, birth, milestones and **any regression**.
- Examine growth, **head circumference**, dysmorphism, skin, systems and neurology; **test hearing and vision in every child**.
- Use standard tools (Denver II, ASQ, Bayley, Griffiths, Vineland, GMFCS, M-CHAT-R/F) and repeat them to monitor change.
- Break bad news in private, with both parents, using **SPIKES**.
- Look for treatable problems (feeding, epilepsy, hips, pain, constipation) and for **abuse** and **caregiver stress**.
- Kenyan resources: **EARCs**, **NCPWD**, Persons with Disabilities Act, community-based rehabilitation.

## 1. Principles

- **Child and family-centred:** the family is part of the team and knows the child best.
- **Strengths first:** describe what the child can do, not only the deficits.
- **Early:** early identification and intervention give the best outcomes.
- **Holistic:** health, function, education, participation and family well-being.
- **Multidisciplinary** and coordinated, ideally with one lead clinician and a care plan.
- **Rights-based:** the same rights as other children, including health, education and protection from abuse.

## 2. The history

| Area | What to ask |
|---|---|
| **Concerns and goals** | What worries the parents? What do they want the child to be able to do? |
| **Pregnancy, birth, neonatal** | Infections, drugs and alcohol, gestation, birth asphyxia, jaundice, neonatal intensive care |
| **Development** | When each milestone was reached; **any regression**; current abilities |
| **Medical** | Seizures, meningitis, head injury, recurrent chest infection, feeding and swallowing problems, drooling, constipation, sleep, pain |
| **Hearing and vision** | Responses to sound and sight; previous tests |
| **Behaviour** | Social interaction, communication, repetitive behaviour, aggression, self-injury, attention |
| **Family** | **Family tree**, consanguinity, similar illness, early deaths, miscarriages |
| **Social and educational** | School or nursery, services received, carers, caregiver stress, finances, community attitudes and stigma, safeguarding |

## 3. The examination

1. **Growth and nutrition:** weight, height or length, **head circumference**, BMI. Poor growth is common and often treatable.
2. **Dysmorphology:** systematic (head, face, ears, eyes, nose, mouth, hands, feet, genitalia); record findings and measurements.
3. **Skin:** café-au-lait spots, ash-leaf macules (Wood's lamp), neurofibromas, haemangiomas (neurocutaneous syndromes).
4. **Systemic:** heart (murmur), liver and spleen (storage disease), spine, hips, joints.
5. **Neurological:** tone, power, reflexes, primitive reflexes, posture, gait, coordination, cranial nerves, eye movements, fundi.
6. **Vision:** fixation and following, squint, red reflex, acuity.
7. **Hearing:** response to sound; refer for formal audiology.
8. **Behaviour and interaction:** observe play and communication during the visit.

## 4. Assessing function and development

| Tool or approach | Use |
|---|---|
| **Developmental assessment** (Denver II, Ages and Stages Questionnaires, **Bayley**, **Griffiths**) | Developmental level in each domain, developmental quotient |
| **Cognitive tests** (Wechsler, Stanford-Binet) | IQ in older children |
| **Adaptive behaviour scales** (Vineland) | Everyday skills |
| **GMFCS I to V** | Mobility in cerebral palsy |
| **Autism tools** (M-CHAT-R/F, ADOS) | Screening and diagnosis |
| **Feeding and swallowing assessment** | Risk of aspiration and malnutrition |
| **Functional and participation measures** | What the child does at home, school and community |

> **Remember:** use the same tools to **monitor change**, not just to diagnose.

## 5. Investigations

Tailor to the findings.

![Thyroid · Image from the Wikipedia article, Wikimedia Commons](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e1/Anterior_thyroid.jpg)

- **Audiology and ophthalmology** for all children with delay or disability.
- **Thyroid function**, basic metabolic screen, **HIV test**.
- **Genetic tests:** karyotype and chromosomal microarray; **fragile X** in boys; targeted tests when a syndrome is suspected (Down syndrome confirmed by karyotype).
- **MRI brain** for microcephaly, focal signs, seizures or regression; **EEG** for seizures.
- **Metabolic tests** with regression, vomiting episodes or coarse features.
- **Creatine kinase** in boys with delayed walking or a Gowers sign.

## 6. Making and communicating the diagnosis

- Give the news in **private**, with **both parents** (and a support person) where possible, in simple language and unhurried. **SPIKES**: Setting, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge, Emotions, Strategy.
- Start with the child's **strengths**, name the condition clearly, avoid jargon, give **honest information** about what is known and unknown, answer questions.
- Expect **grief, guilt and denial**. Avoid blame; "witchcraft" or "punishment" beliefs are common, so explain the medical cause and counter stigma.
- Offer **early follow-up**, written information, and links to other families and support groups.

## 7. The multidisciplinary team

| Member | Role |
|---|---|
| **Paediatrician or developmental paediatrician** | Coordinates care, diagnoses, manages medical problems |
| **Physiotherapist** | Movement, posture, tone, walking, contracture prevention |
| **Occupational therapist** | Hand function, daily living skills, seating, adaptive equipment |
| **Speech and language therapist** | Communication, feeding, swallowing |
| **Audiologist, ophthalmologist or optometrist** | Hearing aids, glasses, visual problems |
| **Nutritionist** | Feeding plan, gastrostomy decisions, growth |
| **Orthopaedic surgeon and orthotist** | Deformity, splints, walking aids |
| **Psychologist or psychiatrist** | Behaviour, mental health, family support |
| **Social worker and community health worker** | Home visits, entitlements, linkage to services |
| **Special education teacher** | Individual education plan, inclusion |
| **Geneticist and genetic counsellor** | Diagnosis, recurrence risk |

## 8. Common medical problems to look for and treat

- **Feeding and nutrition:** swallowing difficulty, aspiration, reflux, constipation, undernutrition or obesity. Thickened feeds, positioning, gastrostomy when oral feeding is unsafe.
- **Epilepsy:** antiepileptic treatment, rescue medication, seizure plan for home and school.
- **Spasticity, contractures, hip displacement, scoliosis:** hip surveillance, physiotherapy, splints, surgery when needed.
- **Respiratory:** recurrent chest infections and aspiration.
- **Sleep, behaviour and pain:** often missed in children who cannot speak.
- **Vision, hearing and dental problems.**
- **Condition-specific monitoring:** **Down syndrome**: echocardiography at birth, yearly thyroid tests, hearing, vision, coeliac disease, atlantoaxial instability; **cerebral palsy**: hips and scoliosis.
- **Immunisation:** all routine vaccines, plus influenza and pneumococcal where recommended.
- **Puberty, sexuality and transition** to adult services.

> **Exam tip:** in a child who cannot speak, always consider **pain or constipation** as the cause of new distress or behaviour change.

## 9. Education, rights and support in Kenya

- **Early intervention**, stimulation programmes and **community-based rehabilitation**.
- **Educational Assessment and Resource Centres (EARCs)** assess children and recommend placement (inclusive mainstream class, special unit or special school) with an individual education plan.
- **Legal framework:** the Constitution of Kenya, the **Persons with Disabilities Act** and the Children Act. Families can register with the **National Council for Persons with Disabilities (NCPWD)** for assistive devices and other support.
- **Assistive devices:** wheelchairs, walking frames, orthoses, hearing aids, glasses.
- **Parent groups** and peer support reduce isolation.

## 10. Safeguarding and caregiver care

- Children with disabilities have a **higher risk of neglect, violence and abuse**: always ask, and know the local child protection pathway.
- Caregivers (usually mothers) often have **stress, depression, poverty and isolation**: ask about well-being, offer respite and counselling, involve the whole family.
- Plan ahead: transition to adulthood, guardianship, independence.
- In life-limiting conditions, offer **palliative care** alongside active treatment.

## 11. A one-page approach (exam)

1. **Identify** the problem: concerns, milestones, regression.
2. **Find the cause:** history (pregnancy, birth, postnatal), family history, examination (growth, head circumference, dysmorphism, neurology), targeted tests.
3. **Define the profile:** developmental level per domain, function, hearing and vision, behaviour, associated medical problems.
4. **Treat and support:** multidisciplinary therapy, education, medical care, family support.
5. **Communicate** the diagnosis sensitively and plan follow-up.
6. **Protect, include and plan ahead:** safeguarding, rights, schooling, transition.

## Key points to revise

1. The aim is to understand the child's function and needs, not just to name a diagnosis.
2. Take a careful history with a family tree; examine growth, head circumference, dysmorphism, skin and nervous system; test hearing and vision in everyone.
3. Use standard developmental and functional tools (Denver II, Bayley, GMFCS) and repeat them to monitor change.
4. Investigate in a targeted way (thyroid function, genetics, MRI, metabolic tests, CK); a cause is found in only about half.
5. Give the news with empathy (SPIKES); coordinate a multidisciplinary team and involve the family.
6. Look for treatable associated problems (feeding, epilepsy, hips, pain, constipation) and for abuse and caregiver stress.
7. Use local resources: EARCs, NCPWD, community-based rehabilitation and parent groups.
