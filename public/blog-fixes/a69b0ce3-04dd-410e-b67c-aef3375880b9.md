## CNS History Taking & Physical Examination
## 1. INTRODUCTION — NEUROLOGICAL TERMS
**Anatomical hierarchy** (cortex → basal ganglia → cerebellum → brain stem → spinal cord → nerve root → plexus → cauda equina → nerve → neuromuscular junction → muscle)
**Suffix conventions**
- **-opathy** = abnormality at the level of the nervous system named in the prefix
- **-itis** = inflammation at that level
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Encephalopathy | Abnormality of the brain — focal/diffuse, metabolic/toxic |
| Encephalitis | Inflammation of the brain (focal or diffuse) |
| Meningoencephalitis | Inflammation of brain + meninges |
| Meningitis | Inflammation of the meninges |
| Myelopathy | Abnormality of spinal cord (e.g., radiation, compressive) |
| Myelitis | Inflammation of the spinal cord |
| Radiculopathy | Abnormality of a nerve root |
| Plexopathy | Abnormality of a nerve plexus (brachial/lumbar) |
| Peripheral neuropathy | Abnormality of peripheral nerves |
| Polyradiculopathy | Abnormality of many nerve roots |
| Polyneuropathy | Similar to peripheral neuropathy; contrasts with polyradiculopathy |
| Mononeuropathy | Abnormality of a single nerve |
| Myopathy | Abnormality of muscle |
| Myositis | Inflammatory disorder of muscle |
| Functional | (1) Non-structural abnormality of function, e.g. migraine; (2) psychiatrically-induced neurological abnormality, e.g. hysterical conversion |

## 2. HISTORY
**Structure** — most important part of the neurological evaluation
1. Age, sex, handedness, occupation
2. History of present complaint
3. Neurological screening questions
4. Past medical history
5. Drug history
6. Family history
7. Social history
**Key principle** — for attacks of loss of consciousness or memory impairment, always take history from **both patient and bystanders/witnesses** if possible. Most common causes of reduced consciousness/awareness attacks: **neurocardiogenic (vasovagal) syncope, epilepsy**.
### Neurological symptoms (screening list)
Cognitive symptoms (esp. memory impairment) · headache · loss of awareness/consciousness · altered perception (incl. déjà vu) · dizziness/vertigo · loss of balance · falls · loss of smell/taste/vision · positive visual symptoms (migraine aura components, hallucinations) · double vision · polyopia/palinopsia · oscillopsia · deafness · tinnitus · difficulty with speech/swallowing · weakness · abnormal muscle movements (cramp, fasciculations) · abnormal body movements (tremor, dystonia, myoclonus) · clumsiness · impaired limb control · altered/loss of sensation · pain · postural hypotension symptoms · sexual dysfunction · bladder/bowel impairment
### Presenting complaint — approach
- Let the patient tell their story with minimal interruption
- Clarify vague terms precisely: "dizziness" — vertigo (spinning) or lightheadedness? "Numbness" — weakness or loss of sensation? "Blurred vision" — double vision or dim/hazy vision?
**Time course → pathological process** (key exam concept)
| Onset pattern | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Sudden/acute | Vascular |
| Subacute | Inflammation, infection, or neoplasia |
| Progressive | Neoplasia or degenerative |
| Stepwise/stuttering | Vascular or inflammatory |
| Relapsing–remitting | Inflammatory |
**Tempo of pathological processes** (onset timescale by category)
- **Seconds–minutes**: vascular, epileptic
- **Minutes–hours**: migrainous
- **Days–weeks**: inflammatory, infective
- **Weeks–months**: neoplastic
- **Months–years**: degenerative, genetic
- **Present since birth**: congenital
- *(Note: late vascular problems, e.g. chronic subdural haematoma, can present at the weeks mark — exception to typical vascular tempo)*
**Worked example — 50-year-old man, complete right eye visual loss**
| Time course | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Sudden, lasted 1 min | Optic nerve compression (e.g. meningioma) |
| Over 10 min, lasted 20 min | Optic neuritis (inflammatory) |
| Over 4 days, improved over 6 weeks | Amaurosis fugax (vascular — impaired retinal blood flow) |
| Progressed over 3 months | Migrainous |
**Worked example — 65-year-old woman, left face/arm/leg weakness**
| Time course | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Since childhood | Congenital |
| Sudden, lasted 10 min | TIA (vascular) |
| Over 10 min, persists days | Stroke (vascular) |
| Over 4 weeks | Likely tumour |
| Over 4 months | Consider subdural haematoma |
### History of Present Illness (HPI) — components
- Precipitating/relieving factors
- **Spontaneously reported symptoms carry more diagnostic weight** than those elicited by direct questioning
- Previous treatments/investigations — benefit or adverse effects
- Current neurological state — what can the patient do now, relative to normal daily activities
- **Hypothesis generation and testing** while listening
- Screening for other neurological symptoms: headaches, fits, faints, blackouts, numbness/tingling/weakness, sphincter disturbance (urinary/faecal incontinence, retention, constipation), visual symptoms
- Always close with an open question: "Is there anything else you wanted to tell me about?"
**Diagnostic reasoning flow**: Interpret symptoms → determine time course → generate hypothesis/differential → test hypothesis + ask about associated features/risk factors → neurological screening history → assess impact on life/work/family → conventional background history (PMHx, drug Hx, social Hx, family Hx) → synthesise differential diagnosis to guide examination

## 3. CNS EXAMINATION — OVERVIEW
**7 components**: 1) Mental status 2) Higher function 3) Cranial nerves 4) Motor exam 5) Sensory exam 6) Cerebellar 7) Gait
### Key cortical areas (for localizing signs)
- Primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus) / primary sensory cortex (postcentral gyrus)
- Prefrontal cortex, premotor cortex (somatic motor association area)
- Broca's area — speech production; Wernicke's area — speech comprehension
- Visual cortex/association area, auditory cortex/association area, somatic sensory association area
**Aphasias**
| Type | Comprehension | Speech | Repetition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wernicke's** | Poor | Fluent but often meaningless (can't self-monitor) | Absent |
| **Broca's** | Preserved | Non-fluent | Absent |

## 4. MENTAL STATE
Reflects mood and thoughts; abnormalities may indicate: neurological disease (frontal lobe disease, dementia), primary psychiatric illness, or psychiatric illness secondary to neurological disease (e.g., depression post-stroke)
**5 domains**: appearance/behaviour, mood, vegetative symptoms, delusions, hallucinations/illusions
**Appearance and behaviour**
- Unkempt/self-neglect → depression, dementia, alcoholism, drug abuse
- Furrowed brow, immobile/downcast facies, slow monotonous speech → depression
- Fidgety, restless, poor concentration → anxiety
- Overfamiliar, disinhibited, or aggressive → frontal lobe disease, bipolar disorder
- Crying/laughing easily → emotional lability
- Lack of concern despite significant disability → loss of insight; frontal lobe disease or conversion disorder
**Mood**
- Euphoric → mania
- Incongruous/blunted affect → schizophrenia
- Depressed mood → depression
**Hallucinations vs illusions**
- **Hallucination** — perception without an external stimulus
- **Illusion** — misinterpretation of a real external stimulus (common with altered consciousness)
**Delusions** — firmly held belief, not altered by rational argument, not a conventional cultural/societal belief; revealed in history, not elicited by direct questioning; seen in acute confusional states and psychotic illness

## 5. HIGHER FUNCTIONS — 7 TESTS
1. **Orientation and attention** — time, person, place; digit recall (normal: 7 forward, 5 backward)
2. **Memory** (immediate, short-term, long-term) — give a name + address, ask for immediate repetition, then recall after 5 minutes
3. **Calculation** — serial 7s, doubling 3 repeatedly
4. **Abstract thought** — proverb interpretation; estimation tasks (e.g., weight of an elephant); useful for frontal lobe lesions, dementia, psychiatric illness
5. **Spatial perception** (parietal/occipital) — draw a clock face with numbers; copy a 5-pointed star
   - Accurate → normal
   - Half the clock missing → visual inattention (right parietal lesion)
   - Cannot draw/copy → constructional apraxia
6. **Visual and body perception** (parietal/occipital) — **agnosia** = abnormal perception despite intact sensory pathways
   - Prosopagnosia — doesn't recognize faces
   - Hemi-neglect — ignores one side (usually left)
   - Anosognosia — unaware of weakness on affected (usually left) side
   - Astereognosis — can't identify objects placed in palm
   - Agraphaesthesia — can't identify numbers/letters traced on palm
7. **Apraxia** — inability to perform a task despite no weakness/incoordination/movement disorder preventing it; test with imaginary tasks ("show me how you'd comb your hair, drink tea, strike a match"); localizes to **dominant (usually left) hemisphere**
### Patterns of focal cognitive loss
- Impaired attention/orientation — diffuse cerebral dysfunction; chronic → dementia; also seen in anxiety/depression
- Memory loss: short-term loss in an alert patient → diffuse encephalopathy, bilateral temporal lesions; prominent in **Korsakoff's psychosis**
- Long-term loss with preserved short-term → functional (non-organic) memory loss
- Impaired calculation → diffuse encephalopathy
- Concrete proverb interpretation → diffuse encephalopathy; delusional interpretation → psychiatric illness with frontal involvement; poor estimates → frontal/diffuse encephalopathy or psychiatric illness
- Loss of spatial appreciation → parietal lobe lesions
- Prosopagnosia → bilateral temporoparietal lesions
- Neglect, sensory agnosia, astereognosis, agraphaesthesia → parietal lobe lesions
- Apraxia → parietal lobe disease

## 6. GAIT
Gait is a coordinated action integrating sensory and motor function.
**Assessment**: observe symmetry, arm swing, step size
| Gait pattern | Cause |
|---|---|
| Stooped, reduced arm-swing, festinant (difficulty starting/stopping) | **Parkinson's disease** |
| High-stepping, knees lifted high | Sensory ataxia / peripheral neuropathy pattern |
| Leg swings out to the side (circumduction) | **Hemiplegic gait** |
| One knee lifts higher than the other | **Foot drop** |
| Falls consistently to one side on tandem (tightrope) walking | **Cerebellar disease** — on the side they fall toward |
| Unable to walk on heels | Foot drop |
| Unable to walk on toes | Weakness of calf muscles |
| Variable, inconsistent with rest of exam, worse when watched | Functional gait |
**Romberg's test** (tests joint position sense; performed after gait exam)
- Feet together, eyes open then closed
- Stands with eyes open, falls with eyes closed = **positive Romberg's** → loss of joint position sense (causes: peripheral neuropathy, B12 deficiency, cord compression, tabes dorsalis)
- Unable to stand even with eyes open = severe unsteadiness → typically **cerebellar syndrome**
- Stands with eyes open, rocks back and forth with eyes closed → suggests **cerebellar syndrome**

## 7. CRANIAL NERVES
**Localization principle**: cranial nerve nuclei in the brainstem mark the lesion level. If a cranial nerve deficit is on the **opposite side** to a hemiparesis, the lesion is at the level of that nerve's nucleus (brainstem).
**4 possible lesion sites**
- (a) CNS pathways to/from cortex, diencephalon, cerebellum, or other brainstem parts
- (b) The nucleus itself
- (c) The nerve itself
- (d) Generalized nerve/neuromuscular junction/muscle problem
**Brainstem level of nuclei**: Midbrain — III, IV · Pons — VI, VII · Medulla — XII (nuclei lie near the 4th ventricle/medial longitudinal fasciculus)
### Examination summary table
| Nerve | Name | Tests |
|---|---|---|
| I | Olfactory | Sense of smell (only if change reported) |
| II | Optic | Visual acuity, colour vision, visual fields, pupillary responses, ophthalmoscopy |
| III | Oculomotor | Eyelids (ptosis), pupil size/symmetry/reactions, eye movements |
| IV | Trochlear | Eye movements (superior oblique) |
| V | Trigeminal | Facial sensation, corneal reflex, muscles of mastication |
| VI | Abducens | Eye movements (lateral rectus) |
| VII | Facial | Facial symmetry and movements |
| VIII | Vestibulocochlear | Otoscopy, hearing, tuning fork tests (Rinne, Weber) |
| IX | Glossopharyngeal | Swallowing |
| X | Vagus | Palatal elevation (uvula deviates away from lesion side), swallowing, cough (bovine if abnormal), speech |
| XI | Accessory | Trapezius/SCM wasting, shoulder elevation, head turning |
| XII | Hypoglossal | Tongue wasting/fasciculation, tongue protrusion (deviates toward lesion side) |
### CN I — Olfactory
- Rarely tested clinically; ask about noticed change; test each nostril separately with fruit/soap/juice
- **Bilateral anosmia** → COVID, common cold, trauma (also reduced in Parkinson's disease, normal ageing)
- **Unilateral** → blocked nostril, or unilateral frontal lesion (e.g. meningioma)
### CN II — Optic
**General eye inspection**
- **Ptosis** — eyelid lower than normal (complete/partial)
- **Lid retraction** — eyelid higher than normal
- **Exophthalmos** — eye protrusion
- **Enophthalmos** — sunken eyes
Key syndromes:
- **Horner's syndrome** — ptosis + miosis + enophthalmos ± anhidrosis (usually unilateral)
- **Dysthyroid eye disease** — exophthalmos with lid retraction
- **Oculomotor (CN III) nerve palsy** — complete ptosis, mydriasis (dilated pupil), eye deviated **down and out** (unopposed superior oblique + lateral rectus)
**Visual acuity** — glasses on; test each eye separately; Snellen chart → newspaper → counting fingers → hand movements → light perception (increasing severity of impairment, in that order)
**Visual fields (confrontation testing)**
- Patient looks at examiner's eyes with both eyes open
- Examiner's hands ~50 cm apart, ~30 cm above eye level, index fingers extended in patient's upper temporal fields
- Ask which finger moves (right/left/both); repeat ~30 cm below eye level
- **Visual inattention** — one side ignored when both fingers move together, but detected when moved alone
- Then test each eye individually
**Visual field defect localization**
- Defect in one eye only → ocular, retinal, or optic nerve pathology
- Constricted field → chronic papilloedema, chronic glaucoma (progresses to tunnel vision)
- **Scotoma** (patchy visual field loss) → MS, toxic optic neuropathy, ischaemic optic neuropathy, retinal haemorrhage/infarct
- Defect in both eyes → lesion at or behind the optic chiasm, or bilateral prechiasmal lesions
- **Bitemporal hemianopia** → lesion at the optic chiasm
- **Homonymous quadrantanopia** — upper = temporal lobe lesion; lower = parietal lobe lesion
- **Homonymous hemianopia**:
  - Incongruous → optic tract lesion
  - Congruous → lesion behind the lateral geniculate body
  - Macula-sparing → occipital cortex lesion (or partial optic tract/radiation lesion)
### CN III, IV, VI — Eye movements
**Muscle actions**: Superior rectus (elevation, III) · Inferior oblique (elevation, III) · Inferior rectus (depression, III) · Superior oblique (depression, IV) · Medial rectus (adduction, III) · Lateral rectus (abduction, VI)
**Palsy patterns** (deficit affecting the right eye, as example)
- **CN III palsy** — eye deviates down and out; ptosis present
- **CN IV palsy** — eye deviates upward (affected in looking down/inward)
- **CN VI palsy** — eye deviates medially (can't abduct)
**Lateral gaze palsy** — causes:
- Large **frontal or parietal lobe lesion** — patient looks away from the paralysed side
- **Pontine lesion** — patient cannot look toward the non-paralysed side; may have other pontine signs (e.g., facial weakness)
### CN V — Trigeminal
- Sensory testing — 3 divisions: ophthalmic (V1), maxillary (V2), mandibular (V3) — test light touch in each territory
- **Corneal reflex** — touch cornea → involuntary bilateral blink; **afferent = trigeminal (V), efferent = facial (VII)**
- Motor — feel for masseter wasting; ask patient to clench teeth (masseters) and open mouth against resistance on the chin (pterygoids)
- **Jaw deviation toward the lesion side** with temporal/masseter hollowing = muscle atrophy/motor weakness (V3 motor lesion)
### CN VII — Facial
- Test at rest, smiling, whistling, closing eyes, frowning/raising eyebrows
- **Facial nerve palsy pattern** distinguishes UMN vs LMN:
  - **UMN lesion** (e.g., stroke) — forehead spared (bilateral cortical innervation to frontalis), lower face weak
  - **LMN lesion** (e.g., Bell's palsy) — entire side of face affected including forehead
### CN VIII — Vestibulocochlear
- Gross hearing — rub fingers ~3–4 inches from each ear, ask patient to acknowledge hearing it
- **Weber test** — tuning fork on vertex (forehead midline): normal = heard equally; lateralizes to the **same ear** in conductive loss, to the **opposite ear** in sensorineural loss
- **Rinne test** — compare air conduction (AC) vs bone conduction (BC) at the mastoid: **AC > BC = normal**; **BC > AC = conductive loss**
### CN IX, X, XII — Mouth
- **IX** — sensory: posterior 1/3 tongue, pharynx, middle ear
- **X** — motor: palate, pharynx, larynx (via recurrent laryngeal)
- **XII** — intrinsic tongue muscles
- Assess: tongue fasciculations, uvula position, tongue deviation on protrusion, gag reflex (afferent IX, efferent X)
**Findings and interpretation**
- Saliva pooling in mouth → swallowing difficulty
- Small tongue with fasciculations → bilateral LMN lesion (e.g., motor neurone disease/progressive bulbar palsy, basal meningitis, syringobulbia)
- Tongue deviates to one side → weakness on the side it deviates **toward**
- Uvula moves to one side → UMN or LMN vagus lesion on the **opposite** side
- Uvula doesn't move on "ahh" or gag → bilateral palatal muscle paresis
- Uvula moves on "ahh" but not gag, with reduced pharyngeal sensation → **IX (glossopharyngeal) palsy**
### CN XI — Accessory
- Ipsilateral cerebral hemisphere supplies contralateral trapezius but ipsilateral sternocleidomastoid — a single UMN lesion can therefore produce signs on **both** sides
- Look for wasting/fasciculation of SCM, head position; test shoulder shrug

## 8. MOTOR SYSTEM
### Patterns of weakness by lesion level
| Lesion level | Features |
|---|---|
| **Upper motor neurone (UMN)** | ↑ tone, ↑ reflexes, pyramidal pattern (weak extensors in arm, weak flexors in leg) |
| **Lower motor neurone (LMN)** | Wasting, fasciculation, ↓ tone, reduced/absent reflexes |
| **Muscle disease** | Wasting, ↓ tone, impaired/absent reflexes |
| **Neuromuscular junction** | Fatigable weakness, normal/↓ tone, normal reflexes |
| **Functional weakness** | Normal tone, normal reflexes, no wasting, erratic power |
The level affected is determined by the distribution/pattern of weakness plus associated findings.
### Examination sequence
1. **Inspection** — patient position (hemiplegic posture, foot/wrist drop), wasting, fasciculation
2. **Tone**
3. **Power**
4. **Reflexes**
### Tone testing
- **Arm** — take the hand as if to shake it, hold the forearm; pronate/supinate, flex/extend elbow
- **Hip** — patient supine, legs straight; roll the knee side to side
- **Knee** — hold knee and ankle; flex/extend the knee
- **Ankle** — hold ankle; flex and dorsiflex the foot
**Findings on tone**
- **Flaccidity/reduced tone** — common: LMN or cerebellar lesion; rare: myopathies, spinal shock (e.g. acute post-stroke), chorea
- **Spasticity** — UMN lesion; takes time to develop
- **Rigidity / cogwheel rigidity** — extrapyramidal syndromes; common causes: Parkinson's disease, phenothiazines
### Power testing (key muscle actions)
- Shoulder abduction, elbow flexion, wrist extension (upper limb)
- Hip flexion, hip extension, knee extension, knee flexion (lower limb)
**MRC (Medical Research Council) grading scale**
| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Normal power |
| 4+ | Submaximal movement against resistance |
| 4 | Moderate movement against resistance |
| 4− | Slight movement against resistance |
| 3 | Moves against gravity but not resistance |
| 2 | Moves with gravity eliminated |
| 1 | Flicker of movement |
| 0 | No movement |
Grade according to the **maximum power attained**, however briefly maintained.
### Reflexes and their spinal root levels
| Reflex | Root level |
|---|---|
| Biceps | C5, C6 |
| Supinator (brachioradialis) | C5, C6 |
| Triceps | C7, C8 |
| Finger jerk | C8 |
| Knee (patellar) | L3, L4 |
| Ankle (Achilles) | S1, S2 |
