## Pathology Practical Revision Guide — Stains Reference
## General Histopathology Stain
**Haematoxylin and Eosin (H&E)**
The routine stain used for all tissue sections.
- Haematoxylin stains nuclei blue/purple (basophilic structures)
- Eosin stains cytoplasm and extracellular matrix pink (acidophilic structures)
## Special Stains and What They Detect
1. **Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) stain** — acid-fast bacilli (*Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, *M. leprae*)
2. **Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS)** — glycogen, fungal cell walls, basement membranes, mucin
3. **Masson's Trichrome** — collagen and fibrosis (collagen stains blue/green, muscle stains red)
4. **Congo Red** — amyloid deposits (shows apple-green birefringence under polarized light)
5. **Reticulin stain** — reticulin fibers; used to assess liver architecture and bone marrow fibrosis
6. **Prussian Blue (Perls' stain)** — iron deposits/haemosiderin; used in haemochromatosis and iron overload
7. **Mucicarmine** — mucin; confirms mucin-producing adenocarcinomas and stains *Cryptococcus* capsule
8. **Gram stain** — bacterial classification (gram-positive vs gram-negative)
9. **Grocott's Methenamine Silver (GMS)** — fungal organisms (*Pneumocystis*, *Aspergillus*, *Candida*)
10. **Oil Red O** — neutral lipids/fat; used on frozen sections since fat dissolves during routine paraffin processing
11. **Von Kossa stain** — calcium deposits
12. **Papanicolaou (Pap) stain** — routine cytology stain (cervical smears, sputum, body fluids)
13. **Giemsa / Diff-Quik** — rapid on-site cytology staining; also used for malaria parasites and blood films
## Cytology-Specific Fixatives
- **95% ethanol** — wet fixation for Pap-stained cytology preparations
- **Air-drying** — used when Giemsa/Diff-Quik staining is intended
- **10% neutral buffered formalin** — standard fixative for histopathology tissue biopsies
## Immunohistochemistry (IHC) Stains by Tumor Type
**Carcinomas (general)**
- Cytokeratin (CK) markers — confirms epithelial origin
**Lymphomas**
- CD45 (leukocyte common antigen)
- CD20 (B-cells)
- CD3 (T-cells)
- CD30 (Hodgkin/Reed-Sternberg cells)
- CD15
**Breast cancer**
- Estrogen receptor (ER)
- Progesterone receptor (PR)
- HER2/neu
- Ki-67 (proliferation index)
**Neuroendocrine tumors**
- Chromogranin A
- Synaptophysin
- CD56
**Sarcomas / mesenchymal tumors**
- Vimentin
**Melanoma**
- S-100
- HMB-45
- Melan-A
**Prostate cancer**
- PSA (prostate-specific antigen)
- PSAP
**Germ cell tumors**
- AFP — yolk sac tumor
- β-hCG — choriocarcinoma
- PLAP and OCT4 — seminoma/dysgerminoma

## Topic-Wise Predictions by Organ System
This section predicts likely question chains — the way examiners typically pair a diagnosis question with a follow-up on complications, risk factors, or confirmatory tests for that same condition.
## Respiratory System
**Bronchiectasis**
- Likely question chain: identify the condition → list predisposing/causative factors → list complications
- Predisposing factors: cystic fibrosis, recurrent childhood respiratory infections, bronchial obstruction (tumor, foreign body), ciliary dyskinesia, tuberculosis
- Complications: recurrent infections, haemoptysis, lung abscess, cor pulmonale, amyloidosis (secondary, AA type)
**Lung carcinoma**
- Likely question chain: identify histological type from gross/microscopic appearance → risk factors → paraneoplastic syndromes
- Risk factors: smoking, asbestos exposure, radon exposure, occupational carcinogens
- Histological types: squamous cell carcinoma (central, cavitating), adenocarcinoma (peripheral, most common in non-smokers), small cell carcinoma (central, aggressive, paraneoplastic association), large cell carcinoma
**Tuberculosis**
- Likely question chain: identify granuloma on histology → describe morphology → confirmatory stain/test
- Morphology: caseating granuloma with central necrosis, epithelioid histiocytes, Langhans giant cells, lymphocytic cuffing
- Confirmatory stain: Ziehl-Neelsen stain for acid-fast bacilli
## Cardiovascular System
**Myocardial infarction**
- Likely question chain: identify gross/microscopic finding by time interval → complications → laboratory markers
- Complications: arrhythmia, cardiac rupture, papillary muscle rupture, mural thrombus, aneurysm, heart failure
- Laboratory markers: troponin I/T (most specific), CK-MB, myoglobin (earliest but non-specific)
**Atherosclerosis**
- Likely question chain: identify the lesion → risk factors → complications
- Risk factors: hypertension, diabetes, smoking, hyperlipidaemia, obesity, family history
- Complications: thrombosis, embolism, aneurysm formation, ischaemia/infarction of downstream organs
## Gastrointestinal System
**Peptic ulcer disease**
- Likely question chain: identify ulcer on specimen → causes → complications
- Causes: *Helicobacter pylori* infection, NSAID use, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome
- Complications: haemorrhage, perforation, obstruction, malignant transformation (gastric ulcers)
**Colorectal carcinoma**
- Likely question chain: identify gross appearance → risk factors → tumor markers
- Risk factors: familial adenomatous polyposis, hereditary non-polyposis colorectal cancer (Lynch syndrome), inflammatory bowel disease, low-fibre diet, adenomatous polyps
- Tumor marker: carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) — used for monitoring, not screening
**Liver cirrhosis**
- Likely question chain: identify gross morphology → causes → complications
- Causes: chronic alcohol use, chronic viral hepatitis (B and C), non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, autoimmune hepatitis
- Complications: portal hypertension, ascites, oesophageal varices, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatocellular carcinoma
## Genitourinary System
**Benign prostatic hyperplasia**
- Likely question chain: identify condition from clinical vignette → clinical features → complications
- Clinical features: frequency, hesitancy, weak stream, dribbling, nocturia
- Complications: acute urinary retention, recurrent UTIs, bladder diverticula, hydronephrosis
**Renal cell carcinoma**
- Likely question chain: identify gross specimen → classic clinical triad → paraneoplastic syndromes
- Classic triad: haematuria, flank pain, palpable abdominal mass
- Paraneoplastic syndromes: polycythaemia (erythropoietin secretion), hypercalcaemia (PTHrP secretion), hypertension
**Ovarian tumors**
- Likely question chain: classify by origin → name specific tumor type → tumor marker
- Classification by origin: surface epithelial, germ cell, sex cord-stromal, metastatic
- Associated tumor markers: CA-125 (epithelial), AFP and β-hCG (germ cell)
## Haematology
**Iron deficiency anaemia**
- Likely question chain: describe red cell morphology → confirmatory laboratory tests → causes
- Morphology: microcytic, hypochromic red cells
- Confirmatory tests: serum ferritin (low), TIBC (high), serum iron (low), transferrin saturation (low)
- Causes: chronic blood loss, poor dietary intake, malabsorption, increased demand (pregnancy)
**Sickle cell disease**
- Likely question chain: identify red cell morphology → confirmatory test → complications
- Morphology: sickle-shaped erythrocytes, target cells, Howell-Jolly bodies (post-splenectomy/hyposplenism)
- Confirmatory test: haemoglobin electrophoresis
- Complications: vaso-occlusive crisis, splenic sequestration, stroke, avascular necrosis, autosplenectomy
**Leukaemia**
- Likely question chain: describe abnormal cell morphology → confirmatory tests → classification
- Confirmatory tests: bone marrow aspirate/biopsy, flow cytometry, cytogenetics
- Key distinguishing morphology: Auer rods (acute myeloid leukaemia), smudge cells (chronic lymphocytic leukaemia)
## Endocrine System
**Thyroid swelling (anterior neck mass)**
- Likely question chain: identify condition from clinical vignette → causes → confirmatory investigation
- Causes: multinodular goitre, Graves' disease, Hashimoto's thyroiditis, thyroid neoplasm
- Confirmatory investigation: fine needle aspiration cytology, thyroid function tests, thyroid ultrasound

**Question**
The materials provided will be used to obtain specimen from a breast lump.
a) What is the procedure?
b) Which fixative will be used?
**Answer**
a) Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology (FNAC) — a needle and syringe are used to aspirate cells directly from the lump for cytological examination.
b) 95% ethanol for wet fixation if Papanicolaou staining is intended, or air-drying if Giemsa/Diff-Quik staining is intended. If a core biopsy rather than FNAC is taken, 10% neutral buffered formalin is used instead.
**Question**
A 26-year-old female with elevated β-hCG and grape-like vesicles obtained from evacuation of uterine contents. What is the diagnosis?
**Answer**
Hydatidiform mole (molar pregnancy) — most likely a complete hydatidiform mole, given the classic grape-like vesicle description and markedly elevated β-hCG.
**Question**
A 76-year-old male with urinary frequency, hesitancy, weak stream and dribbling. What is the diagnosis?
**Answer**
Benign prostatic hyperplasia — a classic presentation of lower urinary tract symptoms in an elderly male.
**Question**
The fluid was tapped from the abdominal cavity of a 48-year-old male with alcohol addiction.
a) What is the fluid called?
b) What is the most likely diagnosis?
**Answer**
a) Ascitic fluid (ascites)
b) Liver cirrhosis, most likely alcoholic in origin, causing portal hypertension and subsequent ascites formation.

**Question**
Sputum specimen to be processed for cytology.
a) Name three cytologic methods of collection of this specimen.
b) Name the common cytologic stain used.

**Answer**
a) Spontaneous expectoration using an early morning deep cough sample, induced sputum using nebulized hypertonic saline, and bronchoalveolar lavage or bronchial washing obtained via bronchoscopy.

c) Ziehl-Neelsen stain, used when tuberculosis is suspected.
**Question**
List the reporting format in body fluid cytology.
**Answer**
Inadequate or unsatisfactory for evaluation, negative for malignancy, atypical cells of undetermined significance, suspicious for malignancy, and positive for malignancy.
**Question**
Surgical resection specimen from a 69-year-old presenting with persistent hoarseness and dysphagia.
a) State the diagnosis shown.
b) List two predisposing factors.
c) Give the expected microscopic findings.
**Answer**
a) Laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma, based strongly on the presentation of persistent hoarseness and dysphagia in an older patient. Confirm against the actual specimen shown on your paper.
b) Predisposing factors include smoking, chronic alcohol use, HPV infection (types 16 and 18), gastroesophageal reflux disease, and occupational exposure to asbestos, wood dust, or chemical irritants.
c) Expected microscopic findings include keratin pearl formation, intercellular bridges, nuclear pleomorphism and hyperchromasia, increased mitotic activity, and invasion through the basement membrane.
**Question**
Results for a 47-year-old female with pallor, fever, and oedema.
a) Explain the condition.
b) Classify the causes of this condition.
**Answer**
a) The combination of pallor, fever, and oedema is consistent with an anaemic process accompanied by a systemic inflammatory or infective trigger, and possible fluid overload or hypoproteinaemia, such as haemolytic anaemia, anaemia of chronic disease with an acute infective component, or anaemia associated with renal or nephrotic disease.
b) Causes of anaemia are classified as decreased red cell production, including iron deficiency, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, aplastic anaemia, and chronic disease; increased red cell destruction or haemolysis, including hereditary spherocytosis, G6PD deficiency, autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, and sickle cell disease; blood loss, whether acute or chronic; and bone marrow infiltration by leukaemia, myelofibrosis, or metastatic disease.
**Question**
Laboratory results from a 32-year-old female on routine health check.
a) State the qualities of an ideal tumor marker.
**Answer**
High sensitivity, high specificity, organ or tumor specificity, correlation with tumor burden and stage, usefulness for monitoring treatment response and detecting recurrence, and ease and reproducibility of measurement with a short half-life reflecting current disease status.
**Question**

**Answer**
Incisional biopsy, involving partial removal of a lesion; excisional biopsy, involving complete removal of a lesion; core needle biopsy; endoscopic biopsy obtained via gastroscopy or colonoscopy forceps; and punch biopsy, used for skin lesions.
**Question**
A labelled liquid chemical.
b) State four criteria desired when selecting this group of substances.
**Answer**
This question most likely refers to tissue fixatives. Desired criteria include rapid and even penetration of tissue, preservation of cellular and tissue architecture without excessive shrinkage or distortion, prevention of autolysis and bacterial decomposition, compatibility with subsequent staining and immunohistochemistry, non-toxicity and cost-effectiveness, and stability with a reasonable shelf life.
**Question**
State the patient with the following haemoglobins: Hb SS, Hb AS, Hb A
A.
**Answer**
Hb SS indicates sickle cell disease, a homozygous state.
Hb AS indicates sickle cell trait, a heterozygous carrier state that is typically asymptomatic.
Hb AA indicates a normal patient.
**Question**
State four likely observations the doctor would have seen to make the verdict that a blood bag was unsuitable for transfusion.
**Answer**
Visible haemolysis shown as pink or red discoloration of the plasma, clots or clumping within the bag, discoloration such as a purple, brown, or black tinge suggesting bacterial contamination, turbidity or cloudiness of the plasma, a damaged, leaking, or punctured bag, and an expired date on the unit label.
**Question**
A 45-year-old sawmill worker presented with a severed hand following an accident, due for emergency surgery. Hb 12g/dl, Hct 30%, BP 60/90mmHg and low urine output.
a) State the likely diagnosis suggested by the above measurements.
b) Suggest the most suitable immediate infusion fluid to use before going to theatre.
c) Estimate the type and amount of transfusion component that will be used.
**Answer**
a) Hypovolaemic shock secondary to acute traumatic blood loss. The low blood pressure and low urine output indicate poor perfusion, even though the haemoglobin and haematocrit have not yet fully reflected the extent of blood loss, since haemodilution has not yet fully occurred.
b) A crystalloid solution such as normal saline or Ringer's lactate, given rapidly to restore intravascular volume while blood products are cross-matched.
c) Packed red blood cells, typically starting with two units, cross-matched if time allows or O-negative blood in an extreme emergency, with further units adjusted based on ongoing blood loss and the patient's response to resuscitation.
**Question**
Name two other clinical emergencies of the male genital tract.
**Answer**
Testicular torsion, paraphimosis, priapism, Fournier's gangrene, and penile fracture.
