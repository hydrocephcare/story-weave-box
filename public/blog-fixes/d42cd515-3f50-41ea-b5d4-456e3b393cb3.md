# Multiple Pregnancy (Twin Gestation)

**Year 4 · Obstetrics & Gynaecology · Complications in Pregnancy**

> **In one line:** a multiple pregnancy is more than one fetus in the uterus at once. Almost every complication of it comes from two things: a bigger uterus and placenta, and *shared* placental circulation when the twins are monochorionic. Work out chorionicity early; it decides the risk and the follow-up.

## What you must know

- Twins are the commonest multiple pregnancy; West Africa has the highest twinning rate in the world.
- **Dizygotic (DZ)** twins are about 80%, **monozygotic (MZ)** about 20%.
- **Chorionicity**, not zygosity, determines the obstetric outcome. Best determined on first-trimester ultrasound.
- Mothers get more of everything: anaemia, pre-eclampsia, hyperemesis, polyhydramnios, antepartum haemorrhage, preterm labour and postpartum haemorrhage.
- Babies get preterm birth, growth restriction, congenital anomalies and, in monochorionic twins, twin-to-twin transfusion.

## 1. Definition and epidemiology

A multiple pregnancy is two or more fetuses developing in the uterus at the same time: twins, triplets (3), quadruplets (4), quintuplets (5), sextuplets (6). Most are twins.

| Population | Twin rate | DZ : MZ ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Caucasian | 1 in 80 pregnancies | about 3 : 1 |
| West African (highest in the world) | 1 in 44 pregnancies | 4 to 6 : 1 |
| Asian | lowest rates | – |

**Hellin's rule** gives the frequency of higher-order births: twins 1 in 80, triplets 1 in 80², quadruplets 1 in 80³, and so on.

> **Why the ratio differs between populations:** MZ twinning happens at a roughly constant rate everywhere (about 4 per 1000 births). The variation is almost entirely in DZ twinning, which depends on how often a woman double-ovulates.

## 2. Aetiology and risk factors

- **Monozygotic:** one fertilised ovum splits (about 4 per 1000 births). Not influenced by family history or age.
- **Dizygotic:** two ova are released and both fertilised, naturally or after ovulation induction.

**Risk factors (for DZ twinning)**

- Ovulation induction, e.g. clomiphene (5 to 10% multiple rate) and gonadotrophins; assisted reproduction (IVF with more than one embryo transferred)
- Increasing maternal age
- Increasing parity
- High BMI and tall stature
- African ancestry
- Family history of twinning, **on the mother's side**

## 3. Types of twins

| | Dizygotic ("fraternal") | Monozygotic ("identical") |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Two ova, two sperm | One ovum, one sperm, then splits |
| Frequency | about 80% | about 20% |
| Genetic make-up | As different as ordinary siblings | Identical |
| Placenta and membranes | Always two (dichorionic-diamniotic) | Depends on when the split occurs |

**Two terms that are often confused**

- **Superfecundation:** two ova from the *same cycle* fertilised by separate acts of intercourse in a short period.
- **Superfetation:** two ova from *different cycles* fertilised (a second conception while already pregnant; theoretically possible up to about 12 weeks).

### Timing of the split in monozygotic twins

The later the egg splits, the more the twins share.

| Split occurs | Result | Share of MZ twins |
|---|---|---|
| Within 72 hours (before the morula) | Dichorionic-diamniotic (DCDA), two placentas | about 30% |
| Day 4 to 8 (inner cell mass formed, chorion present) | Monochorionic-diamniotic (MCDA) | 70 to 75% |
| After day 8 (amniotic cavity formed) | Monochorionic-monoamniotic (MCMA) | 1 to 2% |
| After 2 weeks (embryonic disc formed) | Conjoined ("Siamese") twins | below 1% |

Conjoined twins are named by the site of fusion: **thoracopagus** (chest, the commonest), **pyopagus** (back of the pelvis), **craniopagus** (head), **ischiopagus** (lower end).

> **Exam tip:** MCDA is the commonest type of monozygotic twinning, and monochorionic twins are the "high-risk" twins because the two babies share one placenta.

## 4. Zygosity versus chorionicity

- **Zygosity** is the genetic make-up (identical or not).
- **Chorionicity** is the placental and membrane arrangement, set by the timing of the split.

Chorionicity matters most because the obstetric and perinatal outcome depends on it. It is decided by **first-trimester ultrasound** by counting the gestational sacs and examining the dividing membrane:

- **Lambda (twin-peak) sign:** a triangular wedge of placental tissue in the membrane, so the pregnancy is **dichorionic**.
- **T-sign:** thin membrane meeting the placenta at a right angle, so the pregnancy is **monochorionic**.

Zygosity can be inferred when the fetuses are of **different sex** (always dizygotic), when the placenta is **monochorionic** (always monozygotic), or by DNA testing.

## 5. Lie and presentation

About 90% of twins lie longitudinally, but malpresentation is common.

| Presentation of twin 1 / twin 2 | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Both vertex | about 50% |
| Vertex / breech | about 30% |
| Breech / vertex | about 10% |
| Both breech | about 10% |

If both lie transverse, think of conjoined twins.

## 6. Unique twin phenomena

- **Vanishing twin:** one fetus dies early (usually before 14 weeks) and is reabsorbed while the other continues. Seen in as many as 40% of detected twin pregnancies.
- **Fetus papyraceus (compressus):** one twin dies in the second trimester and is flattened and mummified between the membranes and the uterine wall. More common in monozygotic twins; found at delivery or on scan.
- **Fetus acardiacus (TRAP sequence):** only in monozygotic twins. One twin is a parasitic, amorphous mass with no heart, perfused by the "pump" twin through placental anastomoses; the pump twin is at risk of heart failure.

## 7. Maternal physiological changes

Changes are exaggerated compared with a singleton pregnancy:

- Greater weight gain and higher cardiac output
- Plasma volume rises by an extra 500 mL, but red-cell mass does not keep pace, so haemodilution and **anaemia** are more marked
- Higher tidal volume and glomerular filtration rate
- Higher maternal serum AFP, hCG and unconjugated oestriol (roughly double those of a singleton pregnancy)

## 8. Diagnosis

### History and symptoms

- Use of ovulation-inducing drugs or assisted reproduction
- Family history of twins (maternal side)
- The usual discomforts of pregnancy start earlier and are worse: nausea and vomiting, pelvic pressure, backache, varicose veins, constipation, haemorrhoids, abdominal distension, breathlessness
- Mother says the baby feels very active or the abdomen is "too big"

### Signs

- **Uterus larger than dates** (more than 4 cm)
- Excessive weight gain not explained by oedema or obesity
- Polyhydramnios (about 10 times more common)
- More than one fetus palpable; **two fetal heart rates** at least 8 beats/minute apart and not in time with the mother's pulse
- A fetus still palpable in the uterus after the first baby is delivered

### Investigations

**Ultrasound** is the key test. It gives:

- Confirmation of the diagnosis as early as **10 weeks**
- Viability, and detection of a vanishing twin
- **Chorionicity** (lambda sign) and accurate dating
- Anomaly screening
- **Growth scans every 3 to 4 weeks** to detect IUGR and discordant growth
- Lie and presentation
- Doppler studies for twin-to-twin transfusion
- Placental site and liquor volume

**Biochemistry:** hCG, AFP and unconjugated oestriol are about twice the singleton values. Used only as supporting evidence.

### Differential diagnosis

Polyhydramnios, a big baby, wrong dates, a fibroid or ovarian tumour with pregnancy, ascites with pregnancy.

## 9. Complications

### Maternal

- **Hyperemesis gravidarum** (higher hCG)
- **Anaemia:** two fetuses need more iron and folate
- **Pre-eclampsia (about 25%):** about three times commoner than in a singleton pregnancy, from the bigger placental mass
- **Polyhydramnios (about 10%):** commoner in MZ twins
- **Antepartum haemorrhage:** placenta praevia is commoner because the placenta is larger
- **Malpresentation** in about 70% of cases (see above)
- **Preterm labour:** about 50% of twin pregnancies deliver preterm
- **Postpartum haemorrhage:** over-distended uterus gives uterine atony
- Operative delivery is more likely

### Fetal and neonatal

- Prematurity (the main cause of perinatal death)
- Intrauterine growth restriction and **discordant growth**
- Congenital anomalies (about twice as common)
- **Twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome** (monochorionic twins): unequal shunting of blood across placental vessels
- Cord accidents, especially in monoamniotic twins; **locked twins** during delivery
- Perinatal mortality several times that of singletons

> **Twin-to-twin transfusion (TTTS) at a glance:** the *donor* twin is small, anaemic, and has oligohydramnios ("stuck twin"). The *recipient* is plethoric, has polyhydramnios and may develop heart failure and hydrops. Treated by laser coagulation of the connecting vessels or by serial amnioreduction.

## 10. Management

### Antenatal care

- Confirm **chorionicity** at the first scan
- Counselling about risks; early booking, more frequent visits
- **Iron and folic acid** supplementation, a balanced diet with extra calories and protein
- Regular ultrasound: every 2 weeks for monochorionic twins, every 3 to 4 weeks for dichorionic
- Screen for pre-eclampsia, anaemia and preterm labour; rest and avoid heavy work late in pregnancy
- Corticosteroids for fetal lung maturity if preterm delivery is threatened

### Timing of delivery

| Type | Recommended delivery |
|---|---|
| Uncomplicated DCDA | 37 to 38 weeks |
| Uncomplicated MCDA | 36 to 37 weeks |
| MCMA | 32 to 34 weeks, by caesarean section |
| Any complications | Individualised |

### Mode of delivery

- **Twin 1 vertex:** vaginal delivery is possible if there is skilled attendance, continuous fetal monitoring and a theatre ready.
- **Twin 1 not cephalic**, monoamniotic, conjoined, or any other obstetric indication: **caesarean section**.
- After the first twin is delivered, the second twin's lie is checked and corrected (external cephalic version, or internal podalic version and breech extraction if needed). Contractions are augmented with oxytocin; aim to deliver the second twin within about 30 minutes.

### Third stage

- Give oxytocin or other uterotrophic drug and be ready for **postpartum haemorrhage**.
- Examine the placenta(s) and membranes to confirm chorionicity.

## Key points to revise

1. DZ = two ova = about 80%; MZ = one ovum = about 20%.
2. **Chorionicity decides the risk**; monochorionic twins share a placenta.
3. Lambda sign = dichorionic; T-sign = monochorionic.
4. Uterus larger than dates is the commonest presenting sign; ultrasound confirms.
5. Anaemia, pre-eclampsia, polyhydramnios, APH, preterm labour and PPH are the maternal complications to name.
6. TTTS only occurs in monochorionic twins.
7. Deliver DCDA at 37 to 38 weeks and MCDA at 36 to 37; MCMA by caesarean at 32 to 34 weeks.
