# Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Paediatric Endocrinology**

> **In one line:** congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) is a group of autosomal recessive enzyme defects of cortisol synthesis; low cortisol raises ACTH, which drives adrenal hyperplasia and a pile-up of precursors that are shunted into androgens (virilisation) and, depending on the enzyme, cause salt loss or hypertension. Over 90% is **21-hydroxylase deficiency**, the commonest cause of ambiguous genitalia in a female newborn and of adrenal crisis at 1 to 2 weeks.

## What you must know

- CAH = **autosomal recessive** defects of cortisol biosynthesis; **ACTH is high** and the adrenals are hyperplastic.
- **21-hydroxylase deficiency** causes **more than 90%** of cases: salt-wasting (about 70%), simple virilising (about 30%) and non-classic forms.
- **Salt-wasting crisis** appears at about **10 to 14 days**: vomiting, weight loss, dehydration, shock, **hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia, hypoglycaemia, acidosis**.
- Affected **girls** are born with ambiguous genitalia but **normal internal organs** (uterus and ovaries). **Boys** look normal and may present only in crisis.
- Diagnosis: markedly raised **17-hydroxyprogesterone**; ACTH (cosyntropin) stimulation test confirms.
- Treatment: **hydrocortisone 15 to 20 mg/m²/day in 3 divided doses**, **fludrocortisone** for salt-wasters, **stress dosing**, surgery for virilised girls at 2 to 6 months.
- **11β-hydroxylase deficiency** and **17-hydroxylase deficiency** cause **hypertension and hypokalaemia**; **3β-HSD deficiency** causes salt loss with incomplete virilisation in boys.

## 1. Revision of adrenal anatomy and physiology

- Adrenal glands sit at the **upper pole of each kidney**; each weighs **8 to 10 g**.
- **Medulla** (inner): chromaffin cells that make **catecholamines** (adrenaline, noradrenaline; dopamine). It is an extension of the sympathetic nervous system, a "peripheral amplifier" activated by exercise, cold, stress and haemorrhage.
- **Cortex** (outer) makes steroids from **cholesterol** supplied by the circulation. Its three zones are specialised by their enzymes:

| Zone | Share of cortex | Product | Key enzyme |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zona glomerulosa** (just under the capsule) | about 15% | **Mineralocorticoids** (aldosterone) | Aldosterone synthase |
| **Zona fasciculata** (largest) | about 75% | **Glucocorticoids** (cortisol), some androgens | |
| **Zona reticularis** | about 10% | **Adrenal androgens** | 17,20-lyase |

- **Regulation:** hypothalamic CRH stimulates pituitary **ACTH**, which stimulates cortisol; cortisol gives **negative feedback** on CRH and ACTH. Aldosterone is mainly controlled by the renin-angiotensin system and potassium.

> **Remember:** in CAH the feedback loop is broken because cortisol is low, so ACTH stays high and drives hyperplasia ("hyper-plasia" is the response to ACTH).

## 2. Definition and overview

CAH is a family of **autosomal recessive** disorders of cortisol biosynthesis. Cortisol deficiency increases **ACTH**, leading to adrenocortical hyperplasia and overproduction of intermediate metabolites. Depending on the enzyme step, there may be mineralocorticoid deficiency or excess, and androgen deficiency or excess:

- **Males:** incomplete virilisation or precocious puberty.
- **Females:** virilisation or sexual infantilism.

| Enzyme defect | Share of CAH | Salt | BP | Sex hormones |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **21-hydroxylase** | > 90% | Wasting (classic) | Low | Excess androgens |
| **11β-hydroxylase** | about 5% | Retention | **High** | Excess androgens |
| **3β-HSD** | < 2% | Wasting | Low | Deficient androgens (males under-virilised) |
| **17-hydroxylase** | < 1% | Retention | **High** | Deficient sex steroids |

## 3. 21-Hydroxylase deficiency

### 3.1 Pathophysiology

- 21-hydroxylase converts **progesterone** to 11-deoxycorticosterone and **17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP)** to 11-deoxycortisol; these steps are needed for **aldosterone and cortisol**.
- Both hormones are low in the severe **salt-wasting** form.
- High ACTH causes hyperplasia, and precursors (17-OHP, progesterone) rise to **hundreds of times normal**.
- Progesterone and other metabolites **antagonise the mineralocorticoid receptor**, worsening salt loss.
- 17-OHP is **shunted into androgen synthesis**: high **androstenedione**, converted outside the adrenal to testosterone.

### 3.2 Forms

| Form | Cortisol | Aldosterone | Androgens | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Salt-wasting (classic)** | Low | Low | High | Most severe; about 70% of classic cases |
| **Simple virilising (classic)** | Low (or inappropriately low) | Adequate | High | About 30% of classic cases; boys diagnosed late |
| **Non-classic** | Normal | Normal | Mildly high | May be asymptomatic, or present any time after birth |

### 3.3 Epidemiology

- Classic form: about **1 in 15,000 to 20,000** births in most populations.
- Less common in African Americans than white children (1 in 42,000 vs 1 in 15,500, US data).
- Non-classic form: about **1 in 1,000** in the general population; commoner in Ashkenazi Jews and Hispanics.

### 3.4 Clinical features

**Cortisol and aldosterone deficiency (salt-wasting crisis)**

- Typically from **10 to 14 days of age**.
- Progressive weight loss, anorexia, vomiting, dehydration, weakness, hypotension, hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia.
- Untreated: shock, arrhythmias and death within days or weeks.

**Prenatal androgen excess (affected females)**

- Starts at **8 to 10 weeks' gestation**.
- Masculinised external genitalia: **clitoral enlargement**, partial or complete **labial fusion**, vagina opening into a **urogenital sinus** with the urethra.
- The clitoris may look like a penis; with the urethra below it the baby may be mistaken for a **boy with hypospadias and cryptorchidism**.
- Virilisation is usually worst in the salt-losing form.
- **Internal organs are normal**: ovaries (not testes) so no anti-Müllerian hormone, and the uterus and tubes form.
- Brain exposure to androgens may alter behaviour: rough play, preference for "masculine" toys, less interest in dolls and maternal roles; more homosexuality, but most function heterosexually and **gender dysphoria is uncommon** (mainly in the most virilised salt-wasters).

**Affected males**

- Look normal at birth. Diagnosis may be missed until adrenal crisis, so **male infants are more likely to die** than females.

**Postnatal androgen excess (untreated or under-treated, both sexes)**

- Rapid growth and **advanced bone age**; tall in childhood but **short adults** because of early epiphyseal closure.
- Muscular build, pubic and axillary hair, acne, deep voice.
- Boys: enlarged penis, scrotum and prostate but **small, prepubertal testes**; testicular adrenal rest tumours.
- Girls: further clitoral enlargement; breast development and menstruation may not occur unless androgens are suppressed.
- Simple virilising boys are often diagnosed at 3 to 7 years, with bone age 5 or more years ahead.

**Non-classic disease**

- Normal cortisol and aldosterone; girls have normal genitalia at birth.
- **Precocious pubarche**; later hirsutism, acne, menstrual disorders, infertility. Many are asymptomatic.

**Adrenomedullary dysfunction**

- The medulla needs the very high local cortisol to develop. Classic CAH has blunted adrenaline responses, lower glucose and lower heart rate on exercise (exercise ability is normal). This may worsen the cardiovascular effects of cortisol deficiency.

### 3.5 Laboratory findings

- Salt-wasters: **hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia, metabolic acidosis, hypoglycaemia** (may take 10 to 14 days or more to appear).
- **17-OHP markedly elevated.**
- Cortisol low in salt-wasters; in simple virilisers often normal but inappropriately low for the ACTH and 17-OHP.
- **Androstenedione and testosterone raised in affected females** (testosterone is not raised in affected males).
- ACTH raised (no more useful than 17-OHP).
- **Plasma renin high**, aldosterone inappropriately low for the renin.

### 3.6 Diagnosis

- Most reliable: **17-OHP before and 30 or 60 minutes after IV cosyntropin (ACTH 1-24) 0.125 to 0.25 mg**; affected patients have a high stimulated 17-OHP.
- In an infant with electrolyte derangement or shock, **do not delay treatment**: a random sample shows high precursors.
- **Genotyping** is available, but expensive and takes weeks.

> **Exam tip:** a baby with ambiguous genitalia and vomiting at 2 weeks, with Na low and K high, is CAH until proven otherwise. Give hydrocortisone and fluids first; do not wait for tests.

### 3.7 Differential diagnosis (infant with ambiguous genitalia)

- Examine: genital anatomy, position of the urethral meatus, palpate the labioscrotal folds and inguinal regions for **gonads**, other anomalies.
- **Pelvic ultrasound** for uterus and gonads.
- **Rapid karyotype** for genetic sex.
- **Genitogram** (contrast into the urogenital sinus) can show a vagina and uterus.
- Other causes: 46,XY disorders (androgen insensitivity, 5α-reductase deficiency, testosterone synthesis defects), mixed gonadal dysgenesis, ovotesticular disorder, maternal androgens.
- Differential of salt-wasting crisis: sepsis, pyloric stenosis (hypochloraemic alkalosis, not hyperkalaemia), inborn errors of metabolism.

### 3.8 Prenatal diagnosis and screening

- **Prenatal diagnosis:** chorionic villus sampling (late first trimester) or amniocentesis (second trimester); usually when a previous child is affected.
- **Newborn screening** (17-OHP on a dried blood spot) is used in many countries because boys are often missed until adrenal crisis. It prevents many crises. It does not reliably detect non-classic CAH, which is of little significance as these patients do not develop adrenal insufficiency. (Not part of routine screening in Kenya.)

### 3.9 Treatment

**Glucocorticoid replacement**

- Replaces cortisol and **suppresses adrenal androgen overproduction** (limits growth, bone maturation and virilisation).
- **Hydrocortisone 15 to 20 mg/m²/24 h orally in 3 divided doses.**
- **Double or triple the dose** in stress (infection, surgery); give parenteral hydrocortisone if vomiting.
- **Lifelong** for all classic cases; non-classic only if signs of androgen excess.
- Individualise: crossing **up** height centiles suggests **under-treatment**; crossing **down** (and excess weight gain) suggests **over-treatment**.
- Monitor puberty, **bone age** (serial hand and wrist X-rays), and **17-OHP and androstenedione** measured early morning before the morning dose (or at a constant time relative to dosing).
- Menarche is normal if control is good, delayed if poor.
- **Central precocious puberty** may be unmasked once treatment starts in children with bone age 12 years or more (androgen suppression releases gonadotropins); treat with a **GnRH analogue (leuprolide)**.
- **Testicular adrenal rest tumours** usually regress with more steroid; testis-sparing surgery if steroid-unresponsive.

**Mineralocorticoid replacement (salt-wasters)**

- **Fludrocortisone**: infants 0.1 to 0.3 mg daily in 2 divided doses (sometimes up to 0.4 mg); older children **0.05 to 0.1 mg daily**.
- Infants often need **sodium chloride 8 mmol/kg** supplement.
- Simple virilisers may be easier to control with low-dose fludrocortisone too.
- Monitor: vital signs (**tachycardia and hypertension = over-treatment**), frequent electrolytes in early infancy, and **plasma renin activity** (keep in or near normal, not suppressed).

**Acute adrenal crisis**

- IV fluid bolus (0.9% saline 20 mL/kg), IV dextrose for hypoglycaemia, **IV hydrocortisone** (e.g. 50 to 100 mg/m² bolus), correct hyperkalaemia, then maintenance.

**Surgery for ambiguous genitalia**

- Significantly virilised girls: surgery at **2 to 6 months**.
- Severe clitoromegaly: reduce the clitoris with partial excision of corporal bodies, **preserving the neurovascular bundle**. Moderate clitoromegaly may regress with growth.
- Vaginoplasty and correction of the urogenital sinus at the same time; revision in adolescence is often needed.
- Discuss risks and benefits fully with parents.
- Female sexual dysfunction is commoner with greater virilisation and enzyme impairment.
- **Sex assignment** is based on expected adult sexual function and fertility; genital surgery is done to conform. Gender dysphoria is uncommon.

**Prenatal treatment**

- Mothers at risk may be given **dexamethasone 20 µg/kg of pre-pregnancy weight daily** in 2 or 3 doses, to suppress the fetal adrenal.
- Starting **by 6 weeks' gestation** reduces virilisation of affected girls.
- Chorionic villus biopsy determines sex and genotype; **continue only if the fetus is an affected female**.
- Effects: slightly lower birthweight. Maternal effects: oedema, weight gain, hypertension, glucose intolerance, cushingoid face, severe striae.

## 4. 11β-Hydroxylase deficiency

- Mutation of **CYP11B1** (chromosome **8q24**); the enzyme converts 11-deoxycortisol to cortisol. The neighbouring **CYP11B2** (aldosterone synthase) is unaffected.
- ACTH high; **11-deoxycortisol and deoxycorticosterone (DOC)** accumulate and are shunted to androgens.
- **Epidemiology:** about 5% of CAH; 1 in 100,000 to 250,000; commoner in Israeli Jews of North African origin (1 in 15,000 to 17,000). Mostly classic; non-classic very rare.
- **Clinical:** adrenal insufficiency is unusual. About **65% become hypertensive** (over years) from **DOC** (mineralocorticoid activity). All the androgen excess features of 21-hydroxylase deficiency occur. After starting hydrocortisone, infants may briefly show salt loss because DOC drops suddenly and the zona glomerulosa is atrophic.
- **Labs:** high 11-deoxycortisol and DOC; **renin suppressed**; aldosterone low despite intact capacity; occasional **hypokalaemic alkalosis**.
- **Treatment:** hydrocortisone as in 21-hydroxylase deficiency; mineralocorticoid sometimes briefly in infancy; hypertension often settles, long-standing cases may need extra drugs (e.g. **calcium channel blockers**).

## 5. 3β-Hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase deficiency

- Fewer than **2%** of CAH. Gene **HSD3B2** on chromosome **1p13.1**.
- The enzyme converts **Δ5 steroids** (pregnenolone, 17-hydroxypregnenolone, DHEA) to **Δ4 steroids** (progesterone, 17-OHP, androstenedione).
- Result: **low cortisol, aldosterone and androstenedione; high DHEA**.
- **Clinical:** salt-wasting crises. **Males under-virilised** (hypospadias, bifid scrotum, cryptorchidism). **Females mildly virilised** (DHEA is a weak androgen). Later: precocious adrenarche; in females hirsutism, irregular menses, polycystic ovarian disease; males variable hypogonadism.
- **Labs:** raised **Δ5 steroids**; 17-OHP may also be raised (peripheral 3β-HSD activity), mimicking 21-hydroxylase deficiency. The **17-hydroxypregnenolone : 17-OHP ratio is high** (low in 21-hydroxylase deficiency). Renin high in salt-wasters.
- **Treatment:** hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone. Under-virilised genetic males reared male may get depot **testosterone 25 mg every 4 weeks** in infancy to enlarge the phallus, and testosterone at puberty.

## 6. 17-Hydroxylase deficiency

- Under 1% of CAH; commoner in Brazil and China. Single enzyme **CYP17** (chromosome **10q24.3**) does **17-hydroxylation** and **17,20-lyase** activity; present in adrenal and gonads. Most mutations affect both.
- **Cortisol cannot be made, but corticosterone** (an active glucocorticoid) **is**, so there is **no adrenal insufficiency**.
- **DOC excess** causes **hypertension, hypokalaemia, suppressed renin and aldosterone**.
- Unlike 11β-hydroxylase deficiency, **sex hormones cannot be made**:
  - Genetic males: incomplete virilisation; female phenotype (gonads palpable in groin or labia) or ambiguity.
  - Females: failure of sexual development at puberty; consider in **primary hypogonadism**.
- Cortisol and sex steroids do not respond to ACTH and hCG stimulation. **Isolated 17,20-lyase deficiency** gives low androgens with normal cortisol and no hypertension.
- **Treatment:** hydrocortisone (suppresses DOC, controls BP), extra antihypertensives if needed; **oestrogen** at puberty for females; oestrogen or androgen in genetic males depending on sex of rearing; **gonadectomy at or before adolescence** in genetic males reared female (risk of malignancy in abdominal testes, as in androgen insensitivity).

> **Exam tip:** hypertension plus virilisation = 11β-hydroxylase; hypertension plus lack of puberty = 17-hydroxylase; salt loss plus virilisation = 21-hydroxylase.

## Key points to revise

1. CAH is autosomal recessive; low cortisol leads to high ACTH, adrenal hyperplasia and androgen excess.
2. 21-hydroxylase deficiency is over 90%; salt-wasting presents at 10 to 14 days with hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia and hypoglycaemia.
3. Girls have ambiguous genitalia with normal uterus and ovaries; boys look normal and present in crisis.
4. Diagnose with high 17-hydroxyprogesterone (cosyntropin test); do not delay treatment in a sick infant.
5. Treat with hydrocortisone 15 to 20 mg/m²/day in 3 doses, fludrocortisone for salt-wasters, and double or triple doses in stress.
6. Monitor growth, bone age, 17-OHP, androstenedione and renin; crossing centiles up means under-treatment.
7. 11β-hydroxylase and 17-hydroxylase deficiency cause hypertension from DOC; 3β-HSD deficiency causes salt loss with under-virilised boys.
8. Surgery for virilised girls at 2 to 6 months; prenatal dexamethasone only from 6 weeks and only if the fetus is an affected female.
