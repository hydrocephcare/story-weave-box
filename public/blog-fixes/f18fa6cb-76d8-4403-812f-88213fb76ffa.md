## SECTION 1: Clinical Chemistry — Fundamentals, Laboratory Tests & Analytical Techniques (Pages 1–83)

## 1. Introduction to Clinical Chemistry
**Definition:** The study of chemical processes and substances in the body — analysing blood, urine, and other fluids to assess health, diagnose disease, and monitor treatment.
**Key analytical techniques:** Spectrophotometry, chromatography, immunoassays, electrophoresis, molecular diagnostics.
**Key areas of analysis:** Blood glucose, cholesterol, kidney function (creatinine, urea), liver function (AST, ALT), electrolytes (Na⁺, K⁺), hormones (thyroid, cortisol), tumour markers.

## 2. Routine Chemistry Panels
| Panel | Key Components | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **BMP** (Basic Metabolic Panel) | Glucose, electrolytes, creatinine, BUN, calcium | Electrolyte balance, kidney function, acid-base status |
| **CMP** (Comprehensive Metabolic Panel) | BMP + AST, ALT, ALP, bilirubin, total protein, albumin | Liver + kidney function, protein status |
| **LFTs** | AST, ALT, ALP, bilirubin (total/direct/indirect), albumin, GGT | Liver function, hepatobiliary disease, jaundice |
| **Lipid Profile** | Total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, triglycerides | Cardiovascular risk, lipid disorders |
| **Thyroid Panel** | TSH, T4, T3 ± thyroid antibodies | Thyroid function, hypo/hyperthyroidism |
| **Renal Panel** | Creatinine, BUN, eGFR ± electrolytes | Kidney function, CKD, AKI |
| **Electrolyte Panel** | Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻ ± Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, PO₄³⁻ | Electrolyte balance, acid-base disorders |

## 3. Specialised Chemistry Tests
### Hormone Assays
- **Thyroid:** TSH, T4, T3
- **Reproductive:** Oestrogen, progesterone, testosterone
- **Adrenal:** Cortisol, aldosterone
- **Pituitary:** GH, prolactin
- **Purpose:** Diagnose endocrine disorders, hormonal imbalances, reproductive issues, monitor HRT
### Tumour Markers
| Marker | Cancer |
|---|---|
| PSA | Prostate |
| CA-125 | Ovarian |
| CA 15-3 | Breast |
| AFP | Liver/testicular |
| CEA | Colorectal |
| CA 19-9 | Pancreatic |
### Cardiac Markers
- **Troponin (cTnI, cTnT):** Gold standard for myocardial infarction
- **CK-MB:** Creatine kinase-MB, cardiac muscle damage
- **Myoglobin:** Early but non-specific marker
- **BNP:** B-type natriuretic peptide — heart failure severity
### Other Specialised Markers
- **Autoimmune:** ANA, anti-dsDNA, RF, anti-CCP, ANCA — diagnose lupus, RA, vasculitis
- **Infectious disease:** HIV antibodies, HBsAg, anti-HCV, VDRL/RPR
- **Allergy/Immunology:** Total IgE, specific IgE, allergen panels
- **Genetic/Molecular:** PCR, FISH, gene sequencing — diagnose genetic disorders, personalise treatment

## 4. Clinical Utility of Biochemical Tests
1. **Diagnosis** — Blood glucose → diabetes; creatinine/BUN → kidney disease; ALT/AST → liver damage; lipid panel → hyperlipidaemia
2. **Monitoring disease progression** — HbA1c (long-term glucose), serial eGFR (CKD), PSA (prostate cancer), BNP (heart failure)
3. **Screening** — Lipid panels (cardiovascular risk), liver enzymes (asymptomatic patients), metabolic syndrome
4. **Risk assessment** — Cholesterol for CVD, coagulation tests pre-surgery
5. **Preoperative/postoperative** — Baseline organ function before surgery
6. **Emergency** — Troponin for MI, blood gases for respiratory failure, lactate for sepsis

## 5. Specimen Collection & Handling
### Types of Specimens
- **Blood:** Serum (clotted), plasma (anticoagulated), whole blood
- **Urine:** Random, first-void, 24-hour collection
- **Other:** CSF, sputum, stool, synovial fluid
### Key Principles of Handling
- **Collection:** Correct tube type (e.g., SST, EDTA, heparin), proper order of draw, minimize haemolysis
- **Transport:** Timely delivery, maintain temperature (refrigerate if needed, freeze for long storage)
- **Storage:** Stability varies — some analytes degrade quickly (e.g., ammonia, lactate)
- **Pre-analytical errors** are the most common source of lab errors — lipemia, haemolysis, improper labelling

## 6. Analytical Techniques
| Technique | Principle | Applications |
|---|---|---|
| **Spectrophotometry** | Measures light absorption | Glucose, bilirubin, haemoglobin |
| **Chromatography** | Separates components by physical/chemical properties | Drug levels, amino acids, hormones |
| **Electrophoresis** | Separates by charge/size in electric field | Protein fractions, haemoglobin variants |
| **Mass Spectrometry** | Measures mass-to-charge ratio | Toxicology, newborn screening |
| **Immunoassays** | Antigen-antibody reaction | Hormones, drugs, tumour markers |
| **Electrochemical** | Measures electrical properties | Electrolytes (ISE), blood gases |

## 7. Automation in the Lab
Key principles: speed, precision/accuracy, integration (with LIS), quality control, cost efficiency, scalability.
- Autoanalysers process many samples simultaneously with built-in QC checks

## 8. Basic Statistics in Clinical Chemistry
- **Accuracy:** How close to the true value
- **Precision:** How reproducible the result is (low CV%)
- **Sensitivity:** Ability to correctly identify disease (true positives)
- **Specificity:** Ability to correctly identify absence of disease (true negatives)
- **Disease prevalence** affects positive predictive value (PPV) — high prevalence → higher PPV for same test

## SECTION 2: Histopathology, Cytopathology, Cytology Techniques & Nutrition (Pages 28–245)

## 1. Key Terminologies
- **Histology:** Microscopic study of normal tissue structure
- **Pathology:** Study of disease — causes, development, effects
- **Histopathology:** Microscopic examination of diseased tissue (biopsy/autopsy) for diagnosis
- **Cytology:** Study of individual cells
- **Cytopathology:** Diagnosis of disease using cellular analysis

## 2. Fields Within Pathology
| Field | Focus |
|---|---|
| Oncological | Tumour typing, grading, staging |
| Dermatopathology | Skin disorders |
| Neuropathology | Brain, spinal cord diseases |
| Paediatric | Childhood diseases, congenital conditions |
| Forensic | Cause of death determination |
| GI Pathology | Digestive tract diseases |
| Gynaecologic | Female reproductive system |
| Renal | Kidney diseases |
| Pulmonary | Lung diseases |
| Haematopathology | Blood/bone marrow diseases |

## 3. The Four Primary Tissue Types
| Tissue | Subtypes | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Epithelial** | Simple, stratified, squamous, cuboidal, columnar, pseudostratified | Protection, absorption, secretion |
| **Connective** | Loose, dense, bone, cartilage, blood, adipose | Support, binding, transport |
| **Muscle** | Skeletal (voluntary), cardiac (involuntary, heart), smooth (involuntary, organs) | Movement/contraction |
| **Nervous** | Neurons + glial cells (CNS/PNS/ENS) | Electrical signal transmission |
### Cell Organelle Summary
- **Nucleus** — gene expression, DNA replication
- **Mitochondria** — ATP production (cellular respiration)
- **Rough ER** — protein synthesis; Smooth ER — lipid synthesis, detox
- **Golgi apparatus** — modifies, sorts, packages proteins/lipids
- **Lysosomes** — digest waste (digestive enzymes)
- **Peroxisomes** — fatty acid breakdown, detoxification
- **Cytoskeleton** — structural support (microfilaments, microtubules)

## 4. Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
**Definition:** Technique using antibodies to detect specific antigens (proteins) in tissue sections — allows visualisation of where a protein is expressed.
### IHC Process (Steps)
1. Tissue fixation (formalin)
2. Embedding (paraffin wax)
3. Sectioning (microtome)
4. Deparaffinisation
5. **Antigen retrieval** — heat (HIER) or enzyme methods to unmask antigens
6. Blocking (non-specific binding)
7. Primary antibody application
8. Secondary antibody (conjugated with marker) application
9. Detection (chromogen — DAB gives brown; hematoxylin counterstain)
10. Interpretation under microscope
### Types of IHC
- Direct vs indirect (most common is indirect — signal amplification)
- By antigen type: nuclear, cytoplasmic, membranous
- By cell type: carcinoma markers (cytokeratin), lymphoid (CD markers), neuroendocrine
### Quality Control in IHC
- Positive and negative controls on every run
- Antigen retrieval standardisation
- Antibody concentration titration
- Microscopy interpretation consistency

## 5. Vitamins, Trace Elements & Malnutrition
### Vitamin Deficiencies
- **Vitamin A** — night blindness, xerophthalmia
- **Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)** — Beriberi, Wernicke's encephalopathy
- **Vitamin B12/Folate** — megaloblastic anaemia
- **Vitamin C** — Scurvy (collagen defect)
- **Vitamin D** — Rickets (children), Osteomalacia (adults)
- **Vitamin K** — clotting deficiency
- **Lab evaluation:** Serum levels, functional tests (e.g., prothrombin time for Vit K)
### Trace Elements
- **Iron** — anaemia; measured via serum iron, ferritin, TIBC
- **Zinc** — poor wound healing, immune dysfunction
- **Copper** — Wilson's disease (excess), Menkes disease (deficiency)
- **Iodine** — hypothyroidism, goitre
### Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM)
| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **Kwashiorkor** | Protein deficiency — oedema, ascites, skin changes, fatty liver |
| **Marasmus** | Calorie deficiency — severe wasting, no oedema, "old man face" |
| **Marasmic Kwashiorkor** | Mixed — wasting + oedema |

## 6. Cytology Techniques
### Exfoliative Cytology (Topic VII)
Study of naturally shed or scraped cells from body surfaces.
- **Pap smear** — cervical cancer screening (Papanicolaou stain)
- **Sputum cytology** — lung cancer, TB
- **Urine cytology** — transitional cell carcinoma (bladder)
- **Bronchial washings/BAL** — lung lesions
### Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA) (Topic IX)
- **Definition:** Minimally invasive procedure using a fine needle (22–25 gauge) to aspirate cells from a mass/lesion
- **Technique:** Needle inserted, negative pressure applied, cells aspirated onto slide
- **Indications:** Thyroid nodules, lymph nodes, breast lumps, soft tissue masses
- **Advantages:** Rapid, inexpensive, minimal trauma, outpatient procedure
- **Limitations:** Small sample, no tissue architecture (unlike core biopsy), operator-dependent
### Squash Cytology
- Tissue fragment crushed between two slides, then stained
- Used mainly in **neurosurgery** (intraoperative brain tumour diagnosis)
- Limitations: Tissue destroyed; poor for fibrotic/hard tissue
### Touch Cytology (Touch Imprint)
- Fresh tissue touched onto a slide; cells transfer and adhere
- Used for intraoperative assessment, bone marrow touch preps
- Applications: Lymph nodes, spleen, liver biopsies
### Special Techniques in Cytology (Topic X)
- **Immunocytochemistry (ICC):** Same principle as IHC but on cytology preparations
- **Papanicolaou (Pap) Stain:** Multi-chromatic stain — differentiates cell types; nuclear detail
- **Giemsa Stain:** Used in FNA; stains cytoplasm well
- **Electron Microscopy (EM):** Ultra-structural analysis; identifies organelles, viral particles
- **Flow Cytometry:** Measures cell characteristics (size, granularity, surface markers) as cells pass laser; used in leukaemia/lymphoma immunophenotyping
- **FISH (Fluorescence In Situ Hybridisation):** Detects chromosomal abnormalities using fluorescent probes; used in cancer genetics (e.g., HER2 amplification)

## 7. Body Fluid Cytology (Topic VIII)
### Types of Body Fluids Analysed
- **Pleural fluid** (chest cavity)
- **Peritoneal/ascitic fluid** (abdominal cavity)
- **Pericardial fluid** (around heart)
- **CSF** (cerebrospinal fluid — via lumbar puncture)
- **Synovial fluid** (joint spaces)
- **Bile**
- **Urine, sputum, BAL**
### Transudate vs Exudate
| Feature | Transudate | Exudate |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | <30 g/L | >30 g/L |
| LDH | Low | High |
| Cause | Hydrostatic/oncotic pressure (CCF, hypoalbuminaemia) | Inflammation, infection, malignancy |
| Appearance | Clear, pale yellow | Turbid, may be bloody |
### Processing of Body Fluids
1. Centrifugation → concentrate cells
2. Slide preparation (cytocentrifuge, smear)
3. Staining (H&E, Pap, Giemsa)
4. Drying and coverslipping
5. Microscopy + interpretation
- Look for: malignant cells, inflammatory cells, organisms, crystals

## SECTION 3: Plasma Proteins & Enzymes, Acid-Base Disorders, CVD, Lipid Disorders, Tissue Processing & Quality Control (Pages 224–405)

## 1. Plasma Proteins
### Major Types & Clinical Significance
| Protein | Function | ↑ = | ↓ = |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Albumin** | Oncotic pressure; transports hormones, fatty acids, drugs | Dehydration | Liver disease, nephrotic syndrome, malnutrition |
| **α1-Antitrypsin** | Inhibits neutrophil elastase | Inflammation | Emphysema, cirrhosis |
| **α2-Macroglobulin** | Inhibits proteases | Nephrotic syndrome, inflammation | — |
| **Haptoglobin** | Binds free haemoglobin | — | Haemolytic anaemia, liver disease |
| **Transferrin** | Transports iron (β-globulin) | Iron deficiency | Chronic disease, liver disease |
| **Caeruloplasmin** | Copper transport, ferroxidase | Pregnancy, inflammation | Wilson's disease |
| **Fibrinogen** | Blood clotting; forms fibrin | Inflammation, pregnancy | Bleeding disorders, DIC |
| **Immunoglobulins (IgG/A/M/E/D)** | Immune defence (antibodies) | Chronic infection, myeloma, autoimmune | Immunodeficiency |
**Electrophoresis:** Separates proteins into albumin, α1, α2, β, γ fractions → diagnoses myeloma (M-spike), liver disease, immunodeficiency.

## 2. Key Diagnostic Enzymes
| Enzyme | Location | ↑ In |
|---|---|---|
| **ALT** (Alanine transaminase) | Mainly liver | Hepatitis, liver damage — liver-specific |
| **AST** (Aspartate transaminase) | Liver, heart, muscle | Liver disease, MI, muscle damage |
| **ALP** (Alkaline phosphatase) | Liver, bone, placenta, intestine | Cholestatic liver disease, Paget's, osteomalacia |
| **GGT** (γ-Glutamyl transferase) | Liver, kidney, pancreas | Biliary obstruction, alcohol abuse — sensitive hepatobiliary marker |
| **LDH** (Lactate dehydrogenase) | Widespread; 5 isoenzymes | MI, haemolytic anaemia, lymphoma, liver damage. LD1 → heart/RBCs; LD5 → liver/muscle |
| **Amylase** | Pancreas, salivary glands | Acute pancreatitis, parotitis |
| **Lipase** | Pancreas | Acute pancreatitis (more specific than amylase) |
| **CK-MB** | Cardiac muscle | Myocardial infarction |
| **Troponin (cTnI/cTnT)** | Cardiac muscle | Myocardial infarction — gold standard |

## 3. Acid-Base Homeostasis
### Normal Blood pH: **7.35 – 7.45**
### Regulation Mechanisms
- **Respiratory:** CO₂ + H₂O ↔ H₂CO₃ ↔ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. Hyperventilation → ↓CO₂ → ↑pH. Hypoventilation → ↑CO₂ → ↓pH
- **Renal:** Kidneys excrete H⁺ and reabsorb/generate HCO₃⁻ (slow but powerful)
- **Buffer systems:** Bicarbonate (main), protein, phosphate buffers
- **Hormonal:** Aldosterone → Na⁺ reabsorption + H⁺ excretion → ↑pH
### Four Primary Acid-Base Disorders
| Disorder | pH | Primary Defect | Compensation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Metabolic Acidosis** | ↓ | ↓HCO₃⁻ | ↑Ventilation (↓PCO₂) |
| **Metabolic Alkalosis** | ↑ | ↑HCO₃⁻ | ↓Ventilation (↑PCO₂) |
| **Respiratory Acidosis** | ↓ | ↑PCO₂ | Kidneys retain HCO₃⁻ |
| **Respiratory Alkalosis** | ↑ | ↓PCO₂ | Kidneys excrete HCO₃⁻ |
### Key Lab Tests
- **ABG (Arterial Blood Gas):** pH, PCO₂, HCO₃⁻, PO₂, O₂ saturation
- **Serum electrolytes:** Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻ — calculate anion gap
- **Anion gap = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)** — normal 8–12 mEq/L; elevated in DKA, lactic acidosis, renal failure, toxins

## 4. Cardiovascular Disease (CVD)
### Clinical Features
- **Coronary Artery Disease (CAD):** Chest pain (angina), MI
- **Heart Failure:** Dyspnoea, oedema, fatigue
- **Arrhythmias:** Palpitations, syncope
- **Stroke:** Neurological deficits
- **Hypertension:** Often asymptomatic; leads to target organ damage
### Diagnosis
- **ECG**, **echocardiogram**, **coronary angiography**
- **Cardiac biomarkers:** Troponin (most specific for MI), CK-MB, BNP (heart failure), hs-CRP (inflammatory risk)
### Cardiac Biomarkers Summary
| Marker | Rise After MI | Peak | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Troponin (cTnI/cTnT)** | 3–6 hrs | 12–24 hrs | Remains elevated 7–10 days; gold standard |
| **CK-MB** | 3–6 hrs | 12–24 hrs | Returns to normal in 48–72 hrs |
| **Myoglobin** | 1–3 hrs | 6–9 hrs | First to rise; not cardiac-specific |
| **BNP** | In heart failure | — | Reflects ventricular wall stress; monitors HF |
| **hs-CRP** | — | — | Cardiovascular risk marker, not acute damage |

## 5. Lipid & Lipoprotein Disorders
### Lipid Metabolism
- **Absorption:** Dietary fats → chylomicrons → lymphatics → circulation
- **Synthesis:** Liver synthesises VLDL → IDL → LDL
- **HDL:** Reverse cholesterol transport — takes cholesterol from periphery to liver
- **Degradation (Lipolysis):** Lipases break down triglycerides
### Classification of Lipid Disorders
1. **Hypercholesterolaemia** — elevated total/LDL cholesterol → atherosclerosis risk
2. **Hypertriglyceridaemia** — elevated triglycerides → pancreatitis risk
3. **Dyslipidaemia** — mixed abnormality
4. **Familial disorders:** Familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH) — defective LDL receptor; Familial dysbetalipoproteinaemia — elevated IDL
5. **Secondary causes:** Hypothyroidism, diabetes, obesity, alcohol, renal disease, liver disease
### Lipid Panel Interpretation
- **Total cholesterol:** <5.0 mmol/L desirable
- **LDL:** Lower = better; target <2.0 for high-risk patients
- **HDL:** Higher = protective; <1.0 mmol/L = risk
- **Triglycerides:** <1.7 mmol/L normal
### Management
- **Lifestyle:** Diet (↓saturated fat), exercise, weight loss, stop smoking
- **Pharmacologic:** Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors), fibrates (triglycerides), ezetimibe (cholesterol absorption)
- **Manage underlying causes** (thyroid, diabetes)

## 6. Tissue Fixation & Processing
### Tissue Fixation
**Definition:** A process that preserves tissue morphology and prevents autolysis and putrefaction.
**Purpose:**
- Stop enzyme degradation (autolysis)
- Prevent bacterial decomposition (putrefaction)
- Preserve cellular architecture for microscopy
**Autolysis:** Self-digestion of cells by their own enzymes after death — begins within minutes; lysosomes release enzymes that destroy cell components.
### Criteria of a Good Fixative
- Penetrates tissue rapidly
- Preserves cellular morphology accurately
- Compatible with downstream staining and processing
- Safe to use and chemically stable
- Does not cause excessive hardening or shrinkage
### Classification of Fixatives
| Category | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Aldehyde-based** | Formalin (10% neutral buffered formalin — NBF), glutaraldehyde | Most common; formalin for light microscopy, glutaraldehyde for EM |
| **Alcohol-based** | Ethanol, methanol | Precipitating fixatives; denature proteins |
| **Oxidising agents** | Osmium tetroxide, chromic acid | Used for EM, lipid preservation |
| **Combination** | Bouin's fluid, Zenker's | Specific applications |
### Decalcification
- Removes calcium from bone/calcified tissue before sectioning
- Methods: **Acid decalcification** (fast, harsher — nitric/HCl acid), **EDTA chelation** (slow, gentler, better antigen preservation for IHC), **electrophoretic** methods
- Important for bone biopsies, teeth, calcified tumours
### Tissue Processing Steps (Paraffin Embedding)
1. **Fixation** (formalin)
2. **Dehydration** (ascending alcohol series — 70%, 80%, 95%, 100%)
3. **Clearing** (xylene — removes alcohol, makes tissue transparent)
4. **Infiltration** (molten paraffin wax penetrates tissue)
5. **Embedding** (tissue placed in mould with wax, then solidified)
6. **Sectioning** (microtome — typically 3–5 µm sections)
7. **Mounting** (sections placed on glass slides)
8. **Staining** (H&E is routine)
### Quality Control in Tissue Processing
- Check fixation time (over-fixation hinders antigen retrieval)
- Monitor dehydration and clearing adequacy
- Use proper temperature for wax embedding
- Microtome blade condition (poor sections = knife artefact)
- Documentation of all steps

## 7. Quality Control & Quality Assurance in the Laboratory
### Definitions
- **Quality Control (QC):** Monitoring lab processes to detect errors during testing (analytical phase)
- **Quality Assurance (QA):** Broader system covering all phases to ensure overall reliability of results
### Three Phases of QC
| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Pre-analytical** | Patient ID, sample collection, labelling, transport, storage |
| **Analytical** | Instrument calibration, control samples, reagent QC |
| **Post-analytical** | Result reporting, reference ranges, clinician communication |
### Internal QC
- **Control samples** (known concentrations) run with each batch
- Results plotted on **Levey-Jennings charts**
- **Westgard Rules** flag systematic or random errors
- Action taken if control falls outside ±2SD (warning) or ±3SD (rejection)
### External QC
- **External Quality Assessment (EQA)/Proficiency Testing:** Lab tests samples from external body (blind); results compared to peer labs
- Identifies systematic bias in a lab's performance
### QC Materials
- Calibration standards (known concentration to set instrument)
- Control materials (normal + abnormal levels)
- Reference materials (certified values)
### Quality Assurance Elements
- Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
- Staff training and competency assessment
- Equipment maintenance and calibration logs
- Reagent management (expiry, storage)
- Regulatory compliance (accreditation)
- Patient safety and confidentiality

> **Tip for exam:** Focus on understanding the *why* behind tests — which enzyme is specific for which organ, what happens in each acid-base disorder, and how fixation/processing links to the final IHC or histology result. These connections are commonly tested!
