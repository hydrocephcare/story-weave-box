## SECTION 1: SKIN PATHOLOGY (DERMATOPATHOLOGY)
**Intro:** This section covers the major disease categories of the skin — infections, chronic inflammatory dermatoses, blistering disorders, disorders of epidermal appendages, and disorders of pigmentation/melanocytes (including melanoma). Focus areas for exams: pathogenesis mechanisms, key morphological buzzwords, and distinguishing features between similar-looking conditions (e.g., pemphigus vs. pemphigoid).

## 1. INFECTIOUS DERMATOSES
### A. Bacterial Infections (Impetigo)
- Range: superficial (impetigo) → deep dermal abscesses (e.g., Pseudomonas, puncture wounds)
- **Organism:** *S. aureus* (most common) or *S. pyogenes*
- **Morphology:** neutrophils beneath stratum corneum → **subcorneal pustule**; Gram stain shows cocci
- **Clinical:** mainly children; starts as small macule (face/extremities) → **honey-colored crust**
- Bullous variant of childhood impetigo can mimic autoimmune blistering disease
### B. Fungal Infections
- Spectrum: superficial (Tinea, Candida) → deep/systemic (Aspergillus in immunosuppressed)
- **Morphology:** superficial → neutrophilic epidermal infiltrate; Candida → psoriasiform hyperplasia; deep → granulomatous response; Aspergillus = angioinvasive
- **Stains:** PAS and Gomori methenamine silver (GMS)
- **Clinical:** superficial = pruritic erythematous scaly macules (can mimic psoriasis — always exclude fungal cause before diagnosing new psoriasis)
### C. Viral – Verrucae (Warts)
- Caused by **HPV**; self-limited (resolve in 6 mo–2 yrs)
- Low-risk HPV → warts (no transforming potential); high-risk HPV → anogenital cancers (via E6/E7 oncoproteins)
- **Types:** Verruca vulgaris (hands, most common), Verruca plana (flat, face), Verruca plantaris/palmaris (soles/palms), Condyloma acuminatum (genital)
- **Morphology:** verrucous epidermal hyperplasia + **koilocytosis** (perinuclear vacuolization/halo)

## 2. CHRONIC INFLAMMATORY DERMATOSES
### A. Psoriasis
- Affects 1–2%; linked to ↑ CV risk, arthritis (10%)
- **Pathogenesis:** multifactorial immune disease — CD4+ TH17/TH1 and CD8+ T cells → keratinocyte hyperproliferation; **Koebner phenomenon** (trauma-induced lesions); HLA association
- **Morphology:** well-demarcated salmon plaque with silvery scale; **acanthosis** with "test-tube in rack" rete ridges; loss of stratum granulosum; **parakeratosis**; thin suprapapillary plates with dilated vessels → **Auspitz sign** (pinpoint bleeding); neutrophil microabscesses (Munro)
- **Clinical:** elbows, knees, scalp, lumbosacral, glans penis; nail pitting (30%)
- **Treatment:** NSAIDs, cyclosporine, TNF antagonists
### B. Lichen Planus
- Mnemonic: **"Pruritic, Purple, Polygonal, Planar Papules and Plaques"**
- **Pathogenesis:** CD8+ T cell cytotoxic response vs. basal layer antigens (post-viral/drug)
- **Morphology:** **Wickham striae** (white lines on papules); interface dermatitis; **sawtooth** rete ridges; **Civatte/colloid bodies** (necrotic basal cells); squamatization
- **Clinical:** middle-aged adults; wrists, elbows, glans penis; 70% oral mucosa (reticulate white pattern); skin lesions resolve in 1–2 yrs, oral lesions persist
### C. Lichen Simplex Chronicus
- Response to chronic rubbing/scratching; nodular form = **prurigo nodularis**
- **Morphology:** acanthosis, hyperkeratosis, hypergranulosis, dermal fibrosis (resembles thickened volar skin)
- **Clinical:** can mask underlying pruritic dermatosis — rule out cause

## 3. BLISTERING (BULLOUS) DISORDERS
### A. Inflammatory — Pemphigus Group
- **Common mechanism:** IgG autoantibodies vs. **desmogleins** → **acantholysis** (loss of cell-cell adhesion)
| Type | Level of blister | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| Pemphigus vulgaris (>80%) | Suprabasal | "Row of tombstones"; oral ulcers first |
| Pemphigus vegetans | Suprabasal | Verrucous plaques, no true blisters |
| Pemphigus foliaceus | Subcorneal (superficial) | Fogo selvagem (Brazil); mucosa spared |
| Pemphigus erythematosus | Superficial | Localized, malar/lupus-like |
| Paraneoplastic pemphigus | — | Associated with NHL |
- IF: net-like intercellular IgG deposits
### B. Bullous Pemphigoid
- Elderly patients; thighs, forearms, axillae, groin
- **Pathogenesis:** autoantibodies vs. **BPAG2** (hemidesmosome component) → complement activation → eosinophil/neutrophil recruitment
- **Morphology:** **tense bullae** (don't rupture easily, unlike pemphigus); **subepidermal, nonacantholytic** blister; eosinophils beneath basal layer
### C. Dermatitis Herpetiformis
- Associated with **celiac disease**; males, 3rd–4th decade; intensely pruritic
- **Pathogenesis:** IgA anti-gliadin cross-reacts with reticulin → subepidermal blister
- **Morphology:** **microabscesses at dermal papillary tips** (fibrin + neutrophils); IF = **granular IgA** at papillary tips
- Responds to gluten-free diet
### D. Noninflammatory Blistering Disorders
- **Epidermolysis Bullosa** (defects in structural proteins; blisters at trauma sites from birth):
  - Simplex — keratin 5/14 mutation (AD, most common)
  - Junctional — laminin or BPAG2 mutation (AR), lamina lucida split
  - Dystrophic — COL7A1 mutation, beneath lamina densa
  - Mixed — multiple levels
- **Porphyria:** disturbed porphyrin metabolism; 5 types (congenital erythropoietic, erythrohepatic protoporphyria, acute intermittent, porphyria cutanea tarda, mixed); photosensitive subepidermal vesicles with vessel wall thickening

## 4. DISORDERS OF EPIDERMAL APPENDAGES
### A. Acne Vulgaris
- Universal in teens; worse in males; exacerbated by androgens, drugs, occlusive cosmetics
- **4 pathogenic factors:** (1) follicular keratin plug, (2) sebaceous hypertrophy (androgen-driven), (3) *Propionibacterium acnes* colonization → proinflammatory fatty acids, (4) secondary inflammation
- **Morphology:** open comedones (black, oxidized melanin) vs. closed comedones (trapped plug); severe form = acne conglobata (scarring)
- **Treatment:** antibiotics (anti-*P. acnes*), isotretinoin (antisebaceous)
### B. Rosacea
- Middle-aged females
- **4 stages:** flushing → persistent erythema/telangiectasia → papules/pustules → **rhinophyma**
- **Pathogenesis:** abnormal cathelicidin processing (via kallikrein 5, TLR2 activation)
- **Morphology:** perifollicular lymphocytic infiltrate, telangiectasia; rhinophyma = sebaceous hypertrophy + keratotic plugging

## 5. DISORDERS OF PIGMENTATION & MELANOCYTES
### A. Freckle (Ephelis)
- Childhood; ↑ melanin in basal keratinocytes (not ↑ melanocyte number); darkens with sun, fades in winter
### B. Lentigo
- Benign melanocyte hyperplasia (linear, non-nested) just above basement membrane; does **not** darken with sun (unlike freckle)
### C. Melanocytic Nevus (Mole)
- Acquired **RAS/BRAF** activating mutations → limited by **oncogene-induced senescence** (p16/INK4a)
- **Progression:** Junctional nevus (DEJ) → Compound nevus (epidermis + dermis) → Intradermal nevus (dermis only)
- Deep cells show **neurotization** (loss of pigment, neural-like morphology) — helps distinguish from melanoma
### D. Dysplastic Nevi
- Precursor/marker of melanoma risk; **dysplastic nevus syndrome** (AD, germline **CDKN2A**/p16 or CDK4 mutation) → >50% lifetime melanoma risk
- **Morphology:** >5mm, pebbly/target lesions; **lentiginous hyperplasia**, architectural + cytologic atypia, melanin incontinence, lamellar fibrosis around rete ridges
### E. Melanoma
- Most lethal skin cancer; UV-induced (~85–90% sporadic), 10–15% familial (CDKN2A, telomerase genes)
- **Growth phases:**
  - **Radial** — horizontal spread, no metastatic potential (subtypes: lentigo maligna, superficial spreading [most common], acral/mucosal lentiginous [UV-independent])
  - **Vertical** — downward invasion, metastatic potential, heralded by a nodule
- **Morphology:** irregular borders, color variation, large nuclei with peripheral clumped chromatin, prominent nucleoli; no neurotization (unlike nevi)
- **Clinical — ABCDEs:** **A**symmetry, **B**order irregularity, **C**olor variegation, **D**iameter increase, **E**volution/change

## 6. PANNICULITIS
- Inflammation of subcutaneous fat (lobular and/or septal); often lower legs
| Feature | Erythema Nodosum | Erythema Induratum |
|---|---|---|
| Population | Any age | Adolescents/menopausal women |
| Cause | Hypersensitivity (strep, TB, sarcoid, IBD, drugs, malignancy) | Primary vasculitis of deep dermal vessels (TB-related historically) |
| Lesion | Tender plaques/nodules, no ulceration | Nodule that **ulcerates** |
| Histology | **Septal** panniculitis, **no vasculitis** | **Granulomatous + caseous necrosis**, **vasculitis present** |
- **Other types:** Weber-Christian disease (relapsing febrile nodular panniculitis), factitial panniculitis (self-inflicted), lymphoma-related, lupus panniculitis

## SECTION 2: BREAST PATHOLOGY
**Intro:** This section covers benign and malignant breast disease — benign epithelial lesions (and their cancer-risk stratification), inflammatory/infective breast disorders, stromal tumors, and breast carcinoma (the most clinically important topic — focus heavily here for exams).

## 1. BENIGN EPITHELIAL LESIONS OF THE BREAST
Classified into **3 risk groups**:
### A. Nonproliferative Changes ("Fibrocystic Changes") — *no ↑ cancer risk*
- **Cysts** — dilated lobules; lined by flattened or apocrine metaplastic epithelium; calcifications common; resolves on FNA
- **Fibrosis** — from cyst rupture → chronic inflammation
- **Adenosis** — ↑ acini/lobule; normal in pregnancy
- **Lactational adenoma** — palpable mass in pregnancy/lactation; regresses after weaning
- **Flat epithelial atypia** — earliest clonal lesion (16q deletion); mild atypia; risk only when combined with atypical hyperplasia
### B. Proliferative Disease WITHOUT Atypia — *small ↑ risk (1.5–2×)*
- **Epithelial hyperplasia** — filled ducts/lobules, both luminal + myoepithelial cells, irregular lumens
- **Sclerosing adenosis** — compressed acini in fibrotic stroma; can mimic invasive carcinoma
- **Complex sclerosing lesion / Radial scar** — central hyalinized nidus + radiating projections; mimics carcinoma radiologically/grossly/histologically (NOT from trauma, despite name)
- **Papilloma** — branching fibrovascular cores in dilated duct; large duct type (solitary, nipple discharge) vs small duct type (multiple, deeper)
### C. Proliferative Disease WITH Atypia — *moderate ↑ risk (4–5×)*
- **Atypical Ductal Hyperplasia (ADH)** — resembles DCIS but only partially fills duct
- **Atypical Lobular Hyperplasia (ALH)** — identical to LCIS cells, but fills <50% of acini in a lobule
- Both: ER-positive, low proliferation; ALH shows **loss of E-cadherin** (shared with LCIS)
### D. Gynecomastia
- Only common benign male breast lesion; subareolar button-like enlargement
- **Cause:** estrogen/androgen imbalance — cirrhosis (↓ estrogen metabolism), aging (↓ androgens), drugs (alcohol, marijuana, anabolic steroids), Klinefelter syndrome, Leydig/Sertoli cell tumors
- Histology: ↑ collagenous stroma + ductal epithelial hyperplasia; **no lobule formation**
### Clinical Significance
- Risk reduction: prophylactic mastectomy, tamoxifen (ER antagonist)
- <20% of atypical hyperplasia patients ever develop cancer → many opt for surveillance over intervention

## 2. INFLAMMATORY DISORDERS OF THE BREAST
| Condition | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Acute Mastitis** | 1st month of breastfeeding; *S. aureus* (abscess) or strep (cellulitis); via nipple cracks; treat with antibiotics |
| **Squamous Metaplasia of Lactiferous Ducts** (Periductal mastitis / Zuska disease / recurrent subareolar abscess) | **>90% smokers**; keratin plug → duct dilation/rupture → granulomatous inflammation; nipple inversion; treat = duct excision |
| **Duct Ectasia** | 5th–6th decade, multiparous women; thick nipple discharge; **not** smoking-related; dilated ducts + periductal chronic inflammation + lipid-laden macrophages → fibrosis/retraction; mimics carcinoma |
| **Fat Necrosis** | History of trauma/surgery (~50%); mimics cancer; liquefactive necrosis → giant cells/calcification → scarring |
| **Lymphocytic Mastopathy** (Sclerosing Lymphocytic Lobulitis) | Type 1 DM or autoimmune thyroid disease; dense collagenized stroma + lymphocytic infiltrate around atrophic ducts |
| **Granulomatous Mastitis** | Systemic granulomatous disease (TB, sarcoid, GPA) or localized; granulomas around lobules with lipid vacuoles; cystic neutrophilic granulomatous mastitis (Corynebacteria) |

## 3. STROMAL TUMORS
- Breast stroma = **intralobular** (gives biphasic tumors) vs **interlobular** (gives connective-tissue tumors as elsewhere in the body)
- Fibroadenoma & phyllodes tumor are both driven by **MED12 mutations**
### A. Fibroadenoma
- **Most common benign tumor of the female breast**; women in 20s–30s; hormonally responsive (grows in pregnancy, regresses post-menopause)
- Well-circumscribed, rubbery; pericanalicular or intracanalicular epithelial pattern
- Cyclosporin A (post-transplant) → multiple bilateral fibroadenomas (reversible)
### B. Phyllodes Tumor
- Less common; **6th decade** (later than fibroadenoma); leaf-like ("phyllodes") protrusions
- Distinguished from fibroadenoma by: ↑ cellularity, ↑ mitoses, stromal overgrowth, infiltrative border
- Malignant variants have additional **TERT** mutations; only stromal component metastasizes; axillary node dissection NOT indicated
### C. Interlobular Stroma Lesions
- **Myofibroblastoma** — equally common in males
- **Lipoma**
- **Fibromatosis** — locally aggressive, no metastasis; associated with FAP/Gardner syndrome
- **Angiosarcoma** (malignant) — rare; sporadic (young women) or post-radiation (older women, 5–10 yrs latency)

## 4. CARCINOMA OF THE BREAST
*(High-yield — most heavily tested topic)*
### Epidemiology & Risk Factors
- Most common cancer-related cause of death in women globally
- **Risk ↑:** age, female sex (99%), lifetime estrogen exposure, genetic mutations, obesity, alcohol, delayed childbearing/fewer pregnancies/reduced breastfeeding
- **Risk ↓:** early pregnancy (<20 yrs), prolonged breastfeeding, prophylactic mastectomy (~90% ↓), ER-antagonist chemoprevention
### Molecular Classification (by ER/HER2)
| Group | ER | HER2 |
|---|---|---|
| Luminal | + | − |
| HER2 | +/− | + |
| Triple-negative (TNBC) | − | − (also PR−) |
- 3 main pathogenic pathways: Luminal (precursor = flat epithelial atypia/atypical hyperplasia) → TNBC (precursor = p53-overexpressing normal cells) → HER2 (no defined precursor)
- **Hereditary genes** (germline): **BRCA1, BRCA2** (most important), TP53, PTEN, CDH1, STK11, ATM, CHEK2, PALB2
### Carcinoma In Situ (confined by basement membrane — cannot metastasize)
**DCIS:**
- Patterns: comedo, cribriform, micropapillary, papillary
- Untreated low-grade DCIS → invasive cancer ~1%/year; mastectomy curative >95%
**LCIS:**
- Dyscohesive cells, **loss of E-cadherin (CDH1)**; signet-ring cells; **pagetoid spread** within ducts (≠ Paget disease of nipple)
- Bilateral in 20–40% (vs DCIS 10–20%); always ER/PR+, HER2−
**Paget Disease of the Nipple:**
- Malignant (Paget) cells from DCIS spread via lactiferous sinuses into nipple skin (without crossing BM) → eczema-like eruption
- Palpable mass present → usually invasive (ER−, HER2+ overexpressing); no mass → usually just DCIS
### Invasive Carcinoma
- **Invasive Carcinoma of No Special Type (NST)** = majority; hard, gritty, desmoplastic mass → skin/nipple retraction
- **Grading (Nottingham):** tubule formation + nuclear pleomorphism + mitotic rate → Grade 1–3
- **Special types:**
  - **Lobular carcinoma** — biallelic CDH1 loss; single-file infiltration; metastasizes to peritoneum, leptomeninges, GI tract, ovary/uterus; germline CDH1 → also ↑ gastric signet-ring risk
  - **Medullary pattern** — associated with BRCA1 (germline or promoter hypermethylation); paradoxically better prognosis; heavy lymphocytic infiltrate
### Prognostic & Predictive Factors
- **Most important:** axillary lymph node status
- Others: distant metastases, tumor size, locally advanced disease (skin/muscle invasion), lymphovascular invasion, **inflammatory carcinoma** (dermal lymphatic invasion, 3-yr survival only 3–10%), gene expression profiling, response to neoadjuvant chemo
### Male Breast Cancer
- Rare (1% of female incidence); strongest hereditary factor = **germline BRCA2** (~6% of carriers affected)
- >90% luminal type; presents as subareolar mass/nipple discharge; similar prognosis to women when matched for stage

## SECTION 3: RENAL, URINARY TRACT & MALE REPRODUCTIVE PATHOLOGY

**Intro:** This is the largest section — kidney parenchymal disease (glomerular, tubulointerstitial, vascular), urinary tract obstruction/stones, renal tumors, lower urinary tract disease (ureter, bladder, urethra), and male reproductive pathology (penis, testis, prostate). Glomerular diseases and prostate/testis tumors are the highest-yield exam topics here.

## 1. CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS OF RENAL DISEASE

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Azotemia** | ↑BUN/creatinine from ↓GFR. **Prerenal** = hypoperfusion (shock, CHF, cirrhosis); **Postrenal** = obstruction distal to kidney (reversible on relief) |
| **Uremia** | Azotemia + clinical/biochemical syndrome (GI, neuro, cardiac — e.g., **fibrinous pericarditis**) |
| **Nephritic syndrome** | Hematuria (dysmorphic RBCs/RBC casts), ↓GFR, mild-mod proteinuria, HTN. Classic = post-strep GN |
| **Nephrotic syndrome** | Proteinuria >3.5g/day, hypoalbuminemia, edema, hyperlipidemia, lipiduria |
| **AKI** | Rapid ↓GFR (hrs-days); glomerular/interstitial/vascular/tubular causes |
| **CKD** | GFR persistently <60 mL/min/1.73m² for ≥3 months and/or albuminuria; **most common causes = diabetes & hypertension** |
| **ESRD** | GFR <5% of normal |
| **Renal tubular defects** | Polyuria, nocturia, electrolyte disorders (e.g., metabolic acidosis) |
| **UTI** | Bacteriuria + pyuria; pyelonephritis (kidney) or cystitis (bladder) |
| **Nephrolithiasis** | Renal colic + hematuria |

## 2. GLOMERULAR DISEASES
*(Highest-yield topic — know mechanisms + IF/EM patterns cold)*

### Classification terms
- **Diffuse** (all glomeruli) vs **Focal** (some glomeruli); **Global** (whole glomerulus) vs **Segmental** (part of glomerulus)

### Basic Tissue Reactions to Injury
1. **Hypercellularity** — mesangial/endothelial proliferation, leukocyte infiltration, **crescents** (epithelial cell proliferation + leukocytes, from fibrin deposition after capillary wall injury)
2. **GBM thickening** — immune complex deposition / ↑ matrix synthesis (diabetes) / new BM layers (MPGN)
3. **Hyalinosis** — insudated plasma proteins, occludes capillary lumens
4. **Sclerosis** — collagenous matrix deposition

### Pathogenesis (Immune Mechanisms)
- **In situ immune complex formation** → **granular** IF pattern (e.g., membranous nephropathy)
- **Anti-GBM antibody disease** → **linear** IF pattern (Goodpasture-type; <5% of GN but causes severe crescentic RPGN)
- **Circulating immune complex deposition** → granular IF; antigens may be endogenous (SLE, IgA nephropathy) or exogenous (post-infectious — strep, Hep B/C, malaria)
- **Alternative complement pathway activation** → Dense Deposit Disease (MPGN type II) / C3 glomerulopathies
- Mediators: neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, T cells, platelets, mesangial cells

### A. NEPHRITIC SYNDROME DISEASES
| Disease | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Acute Postinfectious (Post-strep) GN** | Subepithelial immune complexes, neutrophils, ↑glomerular cellularity; children mostly recover, worse in adults |
| **Crescentic (Rapidly Progressive) GN — RPGN** | Severe injury + GBM breaks + crescents; antibody-mediated (anti-GBM) or immune-complex or **ANCA-associated** (pauci-immune) |

### B. NEPHROTIC SYNDROME DISEASES
| Disease | Mechanism | EM/IF | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Membranous Nephropathy** | Autoimmune anti-**PLA2R** (or THSD7A) on podocytes | Subepithelial granular IgG4 deposits, GBM "spikes" | 75% primary; secondary = drugs, malignancy, SLE, Hep B/C; 1/3 remit, 1/3 stable, 1/3 →ESRD |
| **Minimal Change Disease (MCD)** | Podocyte foot process effacement (EM only); normal LM | No deposits | **Most common pediatric nephrotic syndrome** (peak 2–6yrs); dramatic steroid response; adults → think Hodgkin lymphoma |
| **Focal Segmental Glomerulosclerosis (FSGS)** | Podocytopathy | Focal/segmental sclerosis + hyalinosis | **Most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in adults**; genetic forms (podocin, α-actinin-4, TRPC6); collapsing variant = worst prognosis, linked to **HIV** |
| **HIV-Associated Nephropathy** | Collapsing FSGS variant; APOL1 risk alleles | Tubuloreticular inclusions (also in SLE) | Microcystic tubular dilation |
| **Membranoproliferative GN (MPGN)** | Type I = immune complex (IgG+complement); Type II = **Dense Deposit Disease** (alternative complement activation, ↓C3, normal C1/C4) | — | A "pattern" not single disease |
| **Fibrillary GN** | Fibrillary deposits, Congo red **negative** (unlike amyloid) | IgG4, DNAJB9 marker | Recurs in transplant |

### C. OTHER PRIMARY GLOMERULOPATHIES
- **IgA Nephropathy** — **most common GN worldwide**; mesangial IgA deposits; children/young adults; recurrent hematuria
- **Alport Syndrome** — hereditary; **COL4A** (type IV collagen) mutation; hematuria + progressive renal failure + (deafness, ocular defects — classic triad)
- **Thin Basement Membrane Nephropathy** — also COL4 mutation; benign course

### D. SYSTEMIC DISEASE-ASSOCIATED GLOMERULAR LESIONS
- **Lupus Nephritis** — wide spectrum (hematuria → nephrotic → RPGN)
- **Henoch-Schönlein Purpura** — children 3–8yrs; purpura (extensor surfaces) + abdominal pain + arthralgia + hematuria; **mesangial IgA** (same spectrum as IgA nephropathy)
- **Diabetic Nephropathy** — leading cause of CKD; up to 40% of diabetics affected
- **Goodpasture / Microscopic Polyangiitis / GPA (Wegener)** — necrotizing + crescentic GN, often pauci-immune (ANCA)
- **Essential Mixed Cryoglobulinemia** — IgG-IgM complexes; associated with **Hepatitis C**; → MPGN pattern

## 3. TUBULAR AND INTERSTITIAL DISEASES

### A. Acute Tubular Injury (ATI) — *most common cause of AKI (~50% hospitalized)*
- **Causes:** Ischemic (shock, hypovolemia) vs **Nephrotoxic** (aminoglycosides, contrast, heavy metals)
- **Pathogenesis:** loss of cell polarity → ↑Na delivery distally → tubuloglomerular feedback vasoconstriction → cell detachment → luminal obstruction → backleak of filtrate
- **Morphology:** patchy necrosis with skip areas; straight proximal tubule + thick ascending limb most vulnerable; **tubulorrhexis**
- **3 Clinical Phases:** Initiation (~36h, ↓UOP) → Maintenance (oliguria, hyperkalemia, acidosis) → Recovery (polyuria, hypokalemia, infection risk)
- Prognosis: ~95% recover with supportive care (toxic); sepsis/multiorgan failure → >50% mortality

### B. Tubulointerstitial Nephritis (TIN)

- **Distinguishing feature vs glomerular disease:** NO nephritic/nephrotic syndrome; tubular dysfunction instead (polyuria, salt wasting, acidosis)

**Pyelonephritis** (most common cause of TIN):
| | Acute Pyelonephritis | Chronic Pyelonephritis |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Ascending infection (E. coli most common) ± hematogenous | Recurrent infection + VUR or obstruction |
| Morphology | Patchy suppuration, neutrophils in tubules, **glomeruli spared early** | **Coarse, asymmetric corticomedullary scars** over blunted calyces; "thyroidization" of tubules |
| Complications | Papillary necrosis (diabetics, sickle cell, obstruction), pyonephrosis, perinephric abscess | Xanthogranulomatous variant (Proteus, foamy macrophages — mimics RCC); secondary FSGS → proteinuria (poor prognosis) |
| Clinical | Costovertebral pain, fever, dysuria, **WBC casts** | Often silent until HTN/renal insufficiency found |

**Drug/Toxin-Induced TIN:**
- **Acute drug-induced interstitial nephritis** — hypersensitivity (Type I/IV); methicillin classic cause; fever + eosinophilia + rash + ↑creatinine; interstitial edema + eosinophils + lymphocytes; reversible on drug withdrawal
- **NSAID nephropathy** — AKI (↓prostaglandin → ischemia), acute interstitial nephritis, MCD + nephrotic syndrome, membranous nephropathy
- Other: Analgesic nephropathy (phenacetin, historical), Urate nephropathy, Light-chain cast nephropathy ("myeloma kidney")

## 4. RENAL VASCULAR DISEASES

| Disease | Key Points |
|---|---|
| **Nephrosclerosis** | Associated with HTN/aging/diabetes; hyaline arteriolosclerosis + fibroelastic hyperplasia → ischemic glomerulosclerosis; grossly "granular, leather-like" cortex; ↑risk in Black patients, severe HTN, diabetics; **malignant nephrosclerosis** = with malignant HTN (BP >200/120, retinal hemorrhage, papilledema) |
| **Renal Artery Stenosis** | 2–5% of HTN, surgically curable; **70% atheroma** (older men) vs **fibromuscular dysplasia** (younger women); ↑renin → angiotensin II → HTN |
| **Thrombotic Microangiopathies (TMA)** | **Typical HUS** = Shiga toxin (E. coli O157:H7); **Atypical HUS** = complement dysregulation; **TTP** = ADAMTS13 deficiency → large vWF multimers. 2 triggers: endothelial injury (HUS) vs platelet activation (TTP) |

## 5. URINARY TRACT OBSTRUCTION & UROLITHIASIS

### Obstructive Uropathy
- Causes: congenital anomalies, calculi, BPH, tumors, inflammation, pregnancy, neurogenic
- **Hydronephrosis** = pelvicalyceal dilation + progressive renal atrophy; initial defect = tubular (↓concentrating ability) → later ↓GFR
- Post-relief of complete obstruction → **post-obstructive diuresis**

### Urolithiasis (Renal Stones)
- 5–10% lifetime prevalence; men > women; peak 20–30yrs
- **4 types:** Calcium oxalate/phosphate (~70%) > Struvite/Mg-ammonium-phosphate (15%, infection stones) > Uric acid (5–10%) > Cystine (1–2%)
- Key mechanism: **supersaturation** of urine + deficiency of stone inhibitors (citrate, pyrophosphate, nephrocalcin)

## 6. NEOPLASMS OF THE KIDNEY

**Benign:** Papillary adenoma; **Angiomyolipoma** (associated with **tuberous sclerosis**, TSC1/TSC2); **Oncocytoma** (eosinophilic cells from collecting duct intercalated cells)

**Malignant — Renal Cell Carcinoma (85% of adult renal cancers):**
- Risk factors: **smoking** (most significant), obesity, HTN, ESRD/acquired cystic disease, tuberous sclerosis
- Hereditary syndromes (4%): **Von Hippel-Lindau**, hereditary leiomyomatosis, hereditary papillary carcinoma, Birt-Hogg-Dubé
- **Subtypes:** Clear cell (70–80%) > Papillary (10–15%) > Chromophobe (5%) > Xp11 translocation > Collecting duct/Bellini (1%)

**Urothelial Carcinoma of Renal Pelvis:** 5–10% of primary renal tumors; poor prognosis

## 7. DISORDERS OF THE LOWER URINARY TRACT

### URETERS
- **UPJ obstruction** — most common cause of hydronephrosis in infants/children
- **Obstruction causes:** Intrinsic (calculi, strictures, tumors, clots) vs Extrinsic (pregnancy, periureteral inflammation, endometriosis, tumors)
- **Sclerosing Retroperitoneal Fibrosis (Ormond disease)** — idiopathic or **IgG4-related disease**; encases ureters → hydronephrosis; treat with steroids ± stents

### URINARY BLADDER
- **Congenital:** Vesicoureteral reflux (most important — predisposes to pyelonephritis); diverticula; **exstrophy** (↑adenocarcinoma risk); **urachal anomalies** (urachal cysts → 20–40% of bladder adenocarcinomas despite being rare overall)

**Cystitis:**
- Most common organism: **E. coli**; women > men (short urethra)
- **Schistosoma haematobium** → squamous metaplasia → **squamous cell carcinoma** (endemic regions)
- Special forms: **Interstitial cystitis** (chronic pelvic pain, Hunner ulcers, mast cells); **Malakoplakia** (defective phagocytosis, **Michaelis-Gutmann bodies**); Polypoid cystitis (catheter-related, mimics carcinoma)
- **Metaplastic lesions:** Cystitis glandularis/cystica (von Brunn nests) — intestinal metaplasia → adenocarcinoma risk; Squamous metaplasia → SCC risk (esp. schistosomiasis); **Nephrogenic adenoma** (implanted renal tubular cells, mimics cancer)

**Bladder Neoplasms** (~90% urothelial):
- Two precursor pathways: **Papillary tumors** (FGFR3, RAS, PI3K mutations; recur but only ~20% progress to muscle-invasive) vs **Flat CIS** (p53/RB mutations; 50–75% progress if untreated)
- **Key prognostic factor:** invasion of **muscularis propria (detrusor)** — 30% 5-yr mortality once invaded
- Risk factors: smoking (3–7× risk), aryl amines (2-naphthylamine), Schistosoma (→ SCC), cyclophosphamide, analgesics
- Clinical: **painless hematuria** (most common symptom)
- Other types: Squamous cell carcinoma (schistosomiasis), Adenocarcinoma (urachal/intestinal metaplasia), Small-cell carcinoma (TP53/RB mutations), Leiomyoma (most common benign mesenchymal), Rhabdomyosarcoma (children, "sarcoma botryoides"), Leiomyosarcoma (adults)

**Bladder Outlet Obstruction:** Males → BPH (most common); Females → cystocele; morphology = trabeculation → diverticula → massive dilation

### URETHRA
- **Urethritis:** Gonococcal vs Nongonococcal (Chlamydia, Mycoplasma); reactive arthritis (Reiter) triad = arthritis + conjunctivitis + urethritis
- **Urethral caruncle** — small painful mass at meatus, older females
- Proximal urethral cancer → urothelial type; Distal → squamous (HPV-related)

## 8. MALE REPRODUCTIVE SYSTEM

### PENIS
- **Hypospadias** (ventral urethral opening, more common) vs **Epispadias** (dorsal)
- **Balanitis/Balanoposthitis** — poor hygiene, smegma irritant; **Phimosis** — prepuce can't retract
- **Squamous Cell Carcinoma** — HPV 16/18, smegma, smoking; **Bowen disease** = SCC in situ (→invasive in ~10%); **Verrucous carcinoma** = locally invasive, doesn't metastasize

### TESTIS
- **Cryptorchidism** — undescended testis; 3–5× ↑ testicular cancer risk (via intratubular germ cell neoplasia); orchiopexy reduces risk
- **Torsion** — venous obstruction first (arteries patent longer); Neonatal (no anatomic defect) vs Adult ("**bell-clapper**" deformity); surgical emergency, viable if untwisted within ~6h

**Testicular Germ Cell Tumors** (95% of postpubertal testis tumors; all malignant; isochromosome **i(12p)** in virtually all):
| Tumor | Peak Age | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| Seminoma | 40–50 | ~10% ↑hCG |
| Embryonal carcinoma | 20–30 | Negative (pure) |
| Yolk sac tumor | 3 (children) | 90% ↑**AFP** |
| Choriocarcinoma | 20–30 | 100% ↑**hCG** |
| Teratoma | All ages | Negative (pure) |
| Mixed tumor | 15–30 | ↑hCG + AFP |
- Seminoma → lymphatic spread (iliac/para-aortic nodes) late hematogenous; Nonseminomatous → early lymphatic + hematogenous (liver, lung)

### PROSTATE
- **Prostatitis:** Acute bacterial (2–5%), Chronic bacterial (2–5%), Chronic pelvic pain/nonbacterial (**90–95%**, most common), Asymptomatic inflammatory
- **BPH:** Androgen-dependent (DHT via **5α-reductase type 2**); inner **transitional zone**; nodules compress urethra; symptoms: hesitancy, weak stream, urgency/frequency/nocturia; Tx = finasteride (5α-reductase inhibitor) or alpha-blockers (tamsulosin)
- **Prostate Carcinoma:** Most common male cancer; outer **peripheral zone**; **TMPRSS2-ETS fusion**, PI3K/AKT mutations; graded by **Gleason system**; bone metastases typically **osteoblastic**; screened by **PSA** (imperfect — false +/−)
