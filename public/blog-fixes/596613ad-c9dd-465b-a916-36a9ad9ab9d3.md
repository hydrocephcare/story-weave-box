## SECTION 1: FOUNDATIONS, ANTIVIRALS, VACCINES & DNA VIRUSES
## A. INTRODUCTION TO VIRUSES
- A virus is an **obligate intracellular parasite** — has no ribosomes, no independent metabolic machinery, cannot generate its own ATP, and therefore cannot replicate outside a living host cell
- Composed minimally of: **genome** (DNA or RNA, never both) + **capsid** (protein coat protecting the genome) ± **envelope** (lipid bilayer stolen from host membrane, studded with viral glycoproteins)
- The unit of a fully assembled, infectious virus particle is called a **virion**
- Viruses are acellular — this is the core distinction from bacteria/fungi/parasites, which are cellular
## B. CLASSIFICATION OF VIRUSES
*1. ICTV system (structural/taxonomic)*
- Hierarchy: Order → Family → Subfamily → Genus → Species
- Formed and maintained by the **International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses** — standardizes naming so research globally uses the same nomenclature
*2. Baltimore Classification (by genome type + replication strategy) — the exam favorite*
- **Class I — dsDNA:** Herpesviruses, Adenovirus, Poxvirus, Papillomavirus
- **Class II — ssDNA:** Parvovirus
- **Class III — dsRNA:** Rotavirus
- **Class IV — (+)ssRNA:** Picornaviruses (polio, coxsackie, rhino), Togavirus (rubella), Flavivirus (yellow fever, dengue, Zika), Coronavirus
- **Class V — (−)ssRNA:** Orthomyxovirus (influenza), Paramyxovirus (measles, mumps, RSV), Rhabdovirus (rabies), Filovirus (Ebola, Marburg)
- **Class VI — ssRNA-RT (Retrovirus):** HIV
- **Class VII — dsDNA-RT:** Hepatitis B
- Why examiners love this: genome type predicts replication site and enzymes needed. (+)ssRNA genome = acts directly as mRNA, fastest to replicate. (−)ssRNA must first be transcribed to (+)RNA by a virus-carried RNA-dependent RNA polymerase before translation can happen. Retroviruses carry **reverse transcriptase** to convert RNA→DNA before integration.
## C. VIRUS STRUCTURE
- **Capsid symmetry:** icosahedral (most viruses — efficient packing), helical (rabies, influenza, paramyxoviruses), complex (poxvirus — brick-shaped, no clear symmetry)
- **Enveloped viruses:** fragile, destroyed by heat/drying/detergents/bile/gastric acid → transmitted via blood, sexual contact, respiratory droplets, organ transplant — need close contact, don't survive well outside host
- **Non-enveloped (naked) viruses:** tough, resistant to drying, detergents, and gut pH → transmitted via faecal-oral route, fomites, survive long on surfaces and in water
## D. ATYPICAL INFECTIOUS AGENTS
- **Prions** — no nucleic acid at all; infectious misfolded protein (PrP^Sc) that converts normal PrP^C into the misfolded form; cause spongiform encephalopathies (CJD, kuru); resistant to standard sterilization (autoclaving, formalin)
- **Viroids** — small circular ssRNA, no capsid, infect plants only
- **Satellite viruses** — defective, require a helper virus to replicate. Classic exam example: **Hepatitis D requires Hepatitis B** as helper (HDV uses HBV's surface antigen as its envelope)
- **Slow viruses** — long incubation, slowly progressive disease: HIV, prions, and **SSPE** (subacute sclerosing panencephalitis — a late, fatal complication of measles years after infection)
## E. VIRAL REPLICATION CYCLE
1. **Attachment** — viral surface protein binds specific host receptor (explains tissue/species tropism — e.g. HIV needs CD4 + CCR5/CXCR4)
2. **Penetration** — membrane fusion (enveloped) or receptor-mediated endocytosis
3. **Uncoating** — capsid removed, genome exposed
4. **Biosynthesis** — transcription + genome replication + protein synthesis (mechanism is class-specific — this is the "mechanism of replication of the seven virus families" your lecturer flagged)
5. **Assembly** — capsid proteins package new genomes
6. **Release** — **budding** (enveloped, cell can survive and keep shedding virus — chronic infection) vs **lysis** (non-enveloped, cell dies — acute infection)
## F. LYTIC, LYSOGENY, LATENCY, PERSISTENCE
- **Lytic cycle:** immediate replication → cell lysis → progeny released
- **Lysogenic cycle:** genome integrates into host genome (prophage in bacteria; provirus in eukaryotic cells e.g. HIV); dormant, replicates passively with host cell division; can be induced to switch to lytic
- **Latent infection:** virus genome persists silently (often in neurons/lymphocytes) with no virion production, can reactivate — HSV, VZV, EBV, CMV
- **Persistent/chronic infection:** continuous low-level virion production without killing the cell — Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C
- **Effect of lysogeny on host cell:** can confer new properties (lysogenic conversion — e.g. toxin genes in bacteriophage-infected bacteria), or in eukaryotic latency, can predispose to malignant transformation later (EBV, HHV8)
## G. PATHOGENESIS & HOST DEFENSE
- Mechanisms of cell damage: direct **cytopathic effect** (CPE), immune-mediated damage (your own CTLs/inflammation cause the pathology — e.g. Hep B liver damage is mostly immune-mediated, not direct viral killing), **cell transformation** (oncogenic viruses push cells toward malignancy instead of killing them)
- **Innate defenses:** interferons (α/β — block replication in neighbouring uninfected cells by activating antiviral enzymes), NK cells (kill virus-infected cells lacking MHC-I), complement
- **Adaptive defenses:** humoral immunity (neutralizing antibodies block attachment/entry — basis of vaccine protection), cell-mediated immunity (CD8+ CTLs kill infected cells via MHC-I recognition; CD4+ T-helper cells coordinate the response)
## H. LABORATORY DIAGNOSIS
- **Cell culture:** virus grown in susceptible cell lines, identified by characteristic **cytopathic effect** (e.g. syncytia formation, inclusion bodies, cell rounding)
- **Microscopy:** electron microscopy (direct visualization — fast for outbreak ID), light microscopy for inclusion bodies (Tzanck smear for herpesviruses, owl's eye cells for CMV)
- **Serology:** detects host antibody response — **IgM = acute/recent infection**, **IgG = past infection or immunity**; ELISA most common platform
- **Antigen detection:** rapid tests (e.g. rapid flu antigen test)
- **Molecular (PCR):** detects viral nucleic acid directly — **gold standard** for most viral diagnoses today (most sensitive and specific, especially for CSF in encephalitis cases)
## I. ANTIVIRAL AGENTS
*Principle of selective toxicity:* drug must inhibit a viral-specific process/enzyme while sparing host cell function — harder to achieve than with antibacterials since viruses use host machinery extensively.
*Classified by stage of replication they block:*
- **Entry/attachment inhibitors:** Enfuvirtide, Maraviroc, Docosanol, Palivizumab (anti-RSV monoclonal antibody)
- **Uncoating inhibitors:** Amantadine, Rimantadine (block influenza M2 ion channel)
- **Replication inhibitors — nucleoside/nucleotide analogues** (mimic natural nucleotides, get incorporated into viral DNA/RNA by viral polymerase, terminate chain elongation since they lack a functional 3'-OH): Acyclovir (HSV/VZV), Ganciclovir (CMV), Zidovudine/AZT (HIV), Lamivudine (HIV/HepB), Tenofovir (HIV/HepB)
- **Protease inhibitors** (block cleavage of viral polyprotein into functional pieces — used heavily in HIV): Ritonavir, Lopinavir, Indinavir, Saquinavir
- **Integrase inhibitors** (block insertion of viral DNA into host genome — HIV): Raltegravir, Dolutegravir
- **Release inhibitors — neuraminidase inhibitors** (block influenza virion budding/release from cell surface): Oseltamivir, Zanamivir
- **Interferons** — boost the host's own innate antiviral state, used in chronic Hep B/C therapy historically
## J. VACCINES
*Classes:*
- **Live attenuated** — weakened but replicating virus; strongest, most durable immunity (mimics natural infection); risk: can revert/cause disease in immunocompromised. E.g. MMR, OPV, varicella, yellow fever vaccine
- **Killed/inactivated** — whole virus chemically/physically inactivated; safer but weaker, usually needs boosters. E.g. IPV (polio), Hepatitis A
- **Toxoid** — inactivated bacterial toxin (used for toxin-mediated disease, included here for completeness as your course covers it alongside vaccines broadly)
- **Subunit** — only specific antigenic viral protein used (e.g. HPV vaccine uses capsid protein L1 — viral protein, not whole virus)
- **Conjugate** — polysaccharide antigen linked to a protein carrier to improve immune response in young children
- **DNA vaccines / modern trends (mRNA)** — genetic material encoding the antigen is delivered; host cells produce the antigen themselves, triggering immune response without using any live virus
*General mechanism:* induce immunological memory (memory B and T cells) so that on real exposure, a fast secondary antibody response (mostly IgG, high affinity) clears the pathogen before disease develops.
## K. DNA ENVELOPED VIRUSES
*Herpesviridae — family overview*
- dsDNA, icosahedral, enveloped, replicate in the **nucleus**
- Defining trait: **all establish lifelong latency**
- 3 subfamilies: **Alpha** (HSV-1, HSV-2, VZV — fast replication, latent in sensory neurons), **Beta** (CMV — slow replication, latent in monocytes/glandular tissue, causes cell enlargement/cytomegaly), **Gamma** (EBV, HHV8 — latent in lymphocytes, both oncogenic)
*HSV-1*
- Transmission: saliva/direct contact, usually childhood acquisition
- Latency site: **trigeminal ganglion**
- Primary disease: gingivostomatitis (children — painful oral ulcers, fever, refusal to eat)
- Recurrent disease: herpes labialis/cold sores (prodromal tingling before vesicles erupt)
- Other syndromes: herpetic whitlow (finger — occupational risk in dental/nursing staff), keratoconjunctivitis (dendritic corneal ulcers — leading infectious cause of blindness in developed countries), eczema herpeticum (disseminated cutaneous spread in atopic dermatitis patients)
- **HSV encephalitis** — most common cause of sporadic fatal encephalitis in adults; targets **temporal and frontal lobes**; presents with fever, altered consciousness, focal seizures
- Diagnosis: PCR (gold standard, especially CSF for encephalitis); Tzanck smear shows multinucleated giant cells (not specific to HSV-1 vs HSV-2 vs VZV)
- Treatment: acyclovir, valacyclovir, famciclovir
*HSV-2*
- Transmission: sexual contact, vertical (perinatal)
- Latency site: **sacral ganglia**
- Primary genital herpes: painful genital vesicles/ulcers ± fever, inguinal lymphadenopathy
- **Neonatal herpes** — acquired during vaginal delivery if mother has active lesions; highest risk when maternal primary infection occurs near term (no protective maternal IgG transferred yet); presents as skin-eye-mouth disease, CNS disease, or disseminated disease (most fatal form)
- Prevention: caesarean section if active genital lesions at term; antiviral suppression in late pregnancy
- Also causes recurrent aseptic (Mollaret) meningitis
*VZV*
- Highly contagious — airborne spread + direct contact with vesicle fluid
- **Primary infection: Varicella (chickenpox)** — rash spreads centrifugally (trunk→face→limbs); lesions appear in **different stages simultaneously** ("dew drops on a rose petal" — macule, papule, vesicle, pustule, crust all present at once); this asynchronous pattern is the key exam differentiator from smallpox, where lesions are synchronous
- Complications: bacterial superinfection, varicella pneumonia (worse in adults, pregnant women, immunocompromised), cerebellar ataxia, encephalitis, **Reye syndrome if given aspirin during infection**, congenital varicella syndrome (infection before 20 weeks gestation → limb hypoplasia, skin scarring, eye defects), severe neonatal varicella (if maternal rash occurs 5 days before to 2 days after delivery — no time for protective antibody transfer)
- Latency site: **dorsal root ganglia**
- Reactivation: **Herpes zoster (shingles)** — painful vesicular rash strictly in a single dermatome; major complication is **post-herpetic neuralgia** (persistent nerve pain after rash resolves); if it involves the ophthalmic branch of the trigeminal nerve → **herpes zoster ophthalmicus**, vision-threatening
- Diagnosis: PCR or Tzanck smear; Treatment: acyclovir/valacyclovir; varicella vaccine and zoster vaccine both exist (live attenuated)
*EBV*
- Transmission: saliva ("kissing disease")
- Primary disease: **infectious mononucleosis** — fever, exudative pharyngitis, posterior cervical lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly (avoid contact sports — splenic rupture risk), **atypical lymphocytes** on peripheral smear
- Latency site: **B lymphocytes** (EBV is unique among herpesviruses for infecting B cells via CD21 receptor)
- **Oncogenic associations (very high-yield):** Burkitt lymphoma (classic association t(8;14) translocation involving c-myc), nasopharyngeal carcinoma, Hodgkin lymphoma, and in immunosuppressed/transplant patients — post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease
- Diagnostic clue: **heterophile antibodies** detected by the Monospot test; specific serology = anti-VCA IgM (acute), anti-EBNA (past infection)
- No specific antiviral treatment routinely used; management is supportive
*CMV*
- Transmission: saliva, sexual contact, blood transfusion, organ transplant, transplacental (congenital), breast milk
- In immunocompetent hosts: usually asymptomatic or a mono-like illness (but heterophile-antibody negative, unlike EBV)
- **Congenital CMV** — most common congenital viral infection; part of the **TORCH** group; can cause sensorineural deafness, microcephaly, periventricular calcifications, chorioretinitis
- In immunocompromised (HIV/AIDS, transplant patients): retinitis (vision-threatening), pneumonitis, colitis, esophagitis
- Diagnostic clue: **"owl's eye" intranuclear inclusion bodies** in infected cells
- Treatment: ganciclovir/valganciclovir
*HHV8*
- Associated with **Kaposi sarcoma** — vascular tumour of skin/mucosa/viscera, classically seen in HIV/AIDS patients (AIDS-defining illness) due to immunosuppression allowing viral reactivation/proliferation
- Also linked to: primary effusion lymphoma, multicentric Castleman disease
- Transmission: saliva mainly, also sexual contact
*Poxvirus — Smallpox (Variola)*
- Largest, most complex virus structure — brick-shaped, dsDNA, **only DNA virus family that replicates entirely in the cytoplasm** (carries its own complete transcription machinery, doesn't need the host nucleus — common exam "exception" question)
- Rash distribution: centrifugal, but unlike VZV, lesions are **synchronous** (all at the same stage at the same time) — key differentiator
- Historically eradicated (1980, WHO) via global vaccination using live vaccinia virus (cross-protective, related orthopoxvirus) — classic example of successful eradication through vaccination
## L. DNA NON-ENVELOPED VIRUSES
*Adenovirus*
- dsDNA, icosahedral, non-enveloped (so resistant to drying — spreads well via fomites, swimming pools)
- Disease: pharyngoconjunctival fever, acute respiratory disease (common in military recruits/crowded settings), epidemic keratoconjunctivitis, gastroenteritis (in children), hemorrhagic cystitis
- No specific widely-used antiviral; supportive treatment
*Papillomavirus (HPV)*
- dsDNA, non-enveloped
- Low-risk types (6, 11): genital warts (condyloma acuminata), laryngeal papillomas
- **High-risk oncogenic types (16, 18):** cervical cancer (and also implicated in anal, oropharyngeal, penile cancers) — mechanism: viral oncoproteins **E6 inactivates p53** and **E7 inactivates Rb (retinoblastoma protein)**, the two major tumour suppressor genes — disabling cell cycle checkpoints → uncontrolled proliferation
- Prevention: HPV vaccine (subunit, uses L1 capsid protein) — given before sexual debut for maximum effectiveness
*Parvovirus B19*
- ssDNA (Baltimore Class II) — **smallest DNA virus**, non-enveloped
- Disease: **Erythema infectiosum ("Fifth disease")** in children — "slapped cheek" rash
- In adults: can cause arthropathy
- In pregnancy: crosses placenta, infects erythroid progenitor cells → **fetal hydrops/severe fetal anemia** (virus has tropism for rapidly dividing erythroid precursors)
- In sickle cell/chronic hemolytic anemia patients: causes **transient aplastic crisis** (virus temporarily shuts down erythropoiesis, devastating in someone already dependent on high RBC turnover)

**SECTION 2: RNA VIRUSES, HEPATITIS, ARBOVIRUSES, ONCOGENIC VIRUSES, ENCEPHALITIS/HEMORRHAGIC FEVERS & HIV**
## A. RNA ENVELOPED VIRUSES
*Orthomyxoviridae — Influenza*
- (−)ssRNA, segmented genome (8 segments) — segmentation is the key feature enabling **reassortment** (genetic shift)
- Surface glycoproteins: **Hemagglutinin (H)** — attachment to sialic acid receptors on respiratory epithelium; **Neuraminidase (N)** — cleaves sialic acid to release budding virions
- **Antigenic drift** (minor point mutations in H/N, gradual, causes seasonal epidemics) vs **Antigenic shift** (major reassortment of whole gene segments between strains, e.g. in pigs as "mixing vessel," causes pandemics — classic exam distinction)
- Types: Influenza A (causes pandemics, has animal reservoirs, found in birds/pigs/humans), B (humans only, milder), C (mild, rarely tested)
- Disease: fever, myalgia, cough, headache; complications — secondary bacterial pneumonia (S. pneumoniae, S. aureus), worse outcomes in elderly/pregnant/chronic disease
- Diagnosis: rapid antigen test, PCR
- Treatment/prevention: oseltamivir (neuraminidase inhibitor), annual inactivated vaccine (reformulated yearly due to drift)
*Paramyxoviridae — Measles, Mumps, RSV, Parainfluenza*
- (−)ssRNA, helical capsid, enveloped, **no neuraminidase needed for most members** (except parainfluenza)
- Characteristic CPE in culture: **syncytia formation** (cell fusion via viral F protein)
## Measles
- Highly contagious, airborne
- Clinical course: prodrome (fever, cough, coryza, conjunctivitis — the "3 C's"), **Koplik spots** (pathognomonic — white spots on buccal mucosa, appear before rash), then maculopapular rash spreading **head to feet (cephalocaudal)**
- Complications: otitis media (most common), pneumonia (most common cause of death), encephalitis (acute), and **SSPE** (subacute sclerosing panencephalitis — fatal, occurs years after infection, due to persistent defective virus in CNS — a "slow virus" presentation)
- Prevention: live attenuated vaccine (MMR)
## Mumps
- Transmission: respiratory droplets
- Disease: **parotitis** (bilateral painful parotid gland swelling) — classic presentation
- Complications: orchitis (can cause infertility, more severe if post-pubertal), oophoritis, aseptic meningitis, pancreatitis, sensorineural deafness (usually unilateral)
- Prevention: live attenuated vaccine (MMR)
## RSV (Respiratory Syncytial Virus)
- **Leading cause of bronchiolitis and pneumonia in infants <1 year**
- Disease: wheezing, respiratory distress in infants; in older children/adults — mild URI
- High risk groups: premature infants, congenital heart disease, immunocompromised
- Prevention: **Palivizumab** (monoclonal antibody, passive prophylaxis in high-risk infants — not a vaccine), newer maternal/infant vaccines now exist
- Diagnosis: rapid antigen test, PCR
## Parainfluenza
- Major cause of **croup (laryngotracheobronchitis)** in young children — barking cough, stridor, hoarseness
- Also causes bronchiolitis, pneumonia (less commonly than RSV)
*Togaviridae — Rubella*
- (+)ssRNA, enveloped
- Postnatal disease: mild — low fever, **fine pink maculopapular rash**, **posterior auricular/suboccipital lymphadenopathy** (distinguishing feature from measles)
- **Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS)** — high-yield: maternal infection in **first trimester** carries highest risk; classic triad = **sensorineural deafness, cataracts, congenital heart defects (PDA)**; also causes "blueberry muffin" rash (extramedullary hematopoiesis)
- Prevention: live attenuated vaccine (MMR) — never given to pregnant women due to theoretical risk
*Rhabdoviridae — Rabies*
- (−)ssRNA, bullet-shaped, helical, enveloped
- Transmission: bite from infected animal (dogs are the major reservoir in most of Africa/Asia; bats in the Americas) — virus present in saliva
- Pathogenesis: replicates locally at bite site → travels via **retrograde axonal transport** along peripheral nerves to CNS → incubation period highly variable (depends on bite location — shorter if bite is closer to head/face due to shorter nerve distance to travel)
- Clinical course: prodrome (fever, paresthesia at bite site) → **furious rabies** (hyperactivity, hydrophobia, aerophobia, hypersalivation) or **paralytic rabies** (ascending paralysis) → coma → death (almost universally fatal once symptomatic)
- Diagnostic clue: **Negri bodies** (cytoplasmic inclusion bodies in neurons, seen post-mortem)
- Management: **post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)** — wound washing + rabies immunoglobulin (passive) + rabies vaccine series (active) — must be given before symptom onset, as no treatment exists once clinical disease starts
## B. RNA NON-ENVELOPED VIRUSES
*Picornaviridae — smallest RNA viruses, (+)ssRNA, non-enveloped, icosahedral*
## Poliovirus (Enterovirus)
- Transmission: fecal-oral
- Most infections asymptomatic or cause mild febrile illness
- **<1% progress to paralytic poliomyelitis** — virus has tropism for **anterior horn motor neurons** of spinal cord → asymmetric **flaccid paralysis** (lower motor neuron signs: no sensory loss, this distinguishes it from other causes of paralysis — high-yield exam point)
- Prevention: **OPV** (oral, live attenuated — induces mucosal/gut immunity, used in eradication campaigns, but carries small risk of vaccine-associated paralytic polio) vs **IPV** (injectable, killed — safer, used in many high-income countries, no mucosal immunity)
## Coxsackievirus (Enterovirus)
- Group A: **hand-foot-mouth disease**, herpangina (painful vesicles on soft palate)
- Group B: **myocarditis, pericarditis**, pleurodynia (Bornholm disease)
## Rhinovirus
- Most common cause of the **common cold**
- Replicates best at cooler temperatures (~33°C) — explains why it favors the nasal mucosa over the lower respiratory tract
*Reoviridae — Rotavirus*
- dsRNA (Baltimore Class III), non-enveloped, segmented genome, wheel-shaped capsid ("rota" = wheel)
- **Leading cause of severe gastroenteritis/dehydration in infants and young children worldwide**
- Mechanism: infects enterocytes of small intestine villi → destroys absorptive cells → osmotic + secretory diarrhea (viral enterotoxin NSP4 contributes)
- Prevention: live attenuated oral vaccine (Rotarix, RotaTeq)
## C. HEPATITIS VIRUSES
| Virus | Genome | Transmission | Chronicity | Key notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HAV** | (+)ssRNA, non-enveloped | Fecal-oral | Never chronic | Self-limiting, no carrier state, vaccine = killed |
| **HBV** | dsDNA-RT (Baltimore VII) | Blood, sexual, vertical | Can become chronic (esp. if acquired perinatally) | Only DNA hepatitis virus; vaccine = subunit (HBsAg) |
| **HCV** | (+)ssRNA, enveloped | Blood (IV drug use, transfusion) | **Most likely to become chronic** (~75-85%) | High risk of cirrhosis/hepatocellular carcinoma; no vaccine yet; direct-acting antivirals now curative |
| **HDV** | ssRNA, defective/satellite | Blood, sexual | Chronic if co/superinfects with HBV | **Requires HBsAg as its envelope** — cannot infect alone |
| **HEV** | (+)ssRNA, non-enveloped | Fecal-oral (contaminated water) | Usually self-limiting | **High mortality in pregnant women** (up to 20% in 3rd trimester) — key exam fact |
**HBV serology** (classic MCQ territory):
- **HBsAg+** = currently infected (acute or chronic)
- **Anti-HBs+** = immune (either from vaccination or resolved infection)
- **Anti-HBc IgM** = acute infection; **Anti-HBc IgG** = past/chronic infection
- **HBeAg+** = active viral replication, high infectivity
- "Window period": HBsAg negative, anti-HBs not yet positive, but **anti-HBc IgM positive** — only marker detectable during this gap
## D. ARBOVIRUSES AND YELLOW FEVER
- "Arbovirus" = **Ar**thropod-**bo**rne virus — transmitted by mosquitoes/ticks, not a taxonomic family itself (spans Flaviviridae, Togaviridae, Bunyaviridae)
- Major arboviruses: Yellow fever, Dengue, Zika, West Nile, Chikungunya, Eastern/Western/St. Louis equine encephalitis
## Yellow Fever
- Flavivirus, (+)ssRNA, enveloped
- Transmission: **Aedes aegypti** mosquito (also has a sylvatic/jungle cycle with monkeys as reservoir, and urban human-to-human cycle)
- Disease course: initial phase (fever, myalgia, headache) → brief remission → **toxic phase** in ~15% — jaundice, hemorrhage, renal failure (hence "yellow" fever from jaundice)
- Diagnostic clue: **Faget's sign** (relative bradycardia despite high fever)
- Prevention: **live attenuated vaccine (17D strain)** — single dose gives long-lasting immunity, required for travel to/from endemic zones (yellow card)
**Dengue** (commonly examined alongside, even if grouped generally under arboviruses)
- Transmission: Aedes aegypti
- Disease: "breakbone fever" — high fever, severe myalgia/arthralgia, retro-orbital pain, rash
- **Dengue hemorrhagic fever/shock syndrome** — most dangerous on **second infection with a different serotype** due to antibody-dependent enhancement (non-neutralizing antibodies from first infection enhance entry of second serotype into cells)
## E. ONCOGENIC VIRUSES — MECHANISMS OF VIRAL CARCINOGENESIS
- **Proto-oncogenes** — normal genes promoting cell growth/division; mutation/dysregulation → **oncogenes** → uncontrolled proliferation
- **Tumor suppressor genes** (p53, Rb) — normally halt cell cycle/induce apoptosis when DNA damage detected; viral inactivation of these removes the "brakes" on proliferation
- **Apoptosis** — programmed cell death; cancer cells (and oncogenic viruses) evade this
- Two broad mechanisms viruses use:
 1. **Direct transformation** — viral oncoproteins directly inactivate tumor suppressors (e.g. HPV E6 inactivates p53, E7 inactivates Rb)
 2. **Indirect/chronic inflammation route** — chronic infection causes ongoing tissue damage/regeneration cycles, increasing mutation accumulation over time (e.g. HBV/HCV → hepatocellular carcinoma via chronic liver inflammation, not direct transformation alone)
**DNA tumor viruses:** HPV (cervical cancer), EBV (Burkitt lymphoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma), HBV (hepatocellular carcinoma), HHV8 (Kaposi sarcoma)
**RNA tumor viruses:** HCV (hepatocellular carcinoma), HTLV-1 (adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma — retrovirus, carries the **Tax protein** which activates host genes promoting proliferation)
## F. VIRAL ENCEPHALITIS AND HEMORRHAGIC FEVERS
*Viral encephalitis — general pathogenesis*
- Virus reaches CNS via **hematogenous spread** (most arboviruses, enteroviruses) or **retrograde neuronal/axonal spread** (HSV, rabies)
- Common causes: HSV-1 (temporal lobe, most common sporadic cause), arboviruses (West Nile, Japanese encephalitis, Eastern equine encephalitis), enteroviruses, rabies
- Presentation: fever, headache, altered consciousness, seizures, focal neurological deficits
*Viral hemorrhagic fevers — Filoviridae (Ebola, Marburg)*
- (−)ssRNA, enveloped, filamentous (thread-like) shape
- Transmission: direct contact with blood/body fluids of infected person or animal (bats are the suspected natural reservoir); highly contagious in healthcare/burial settings
- Pathogenesis: virus infects monocytes/macrophages/dendritic cells → massive cytokine release ("**cytokine storm**") → vascular endothelial damage → increased permeability → **hemorrhage, hypovolemic shock**; also direct hepatocyte damage
- Clinical course: sudden fever, severe malaise, myalgia → progresses to vomiting, diarrhea, rash, then hemorrhagic manifestations (bleeding from gums, GI tract, injection sites) in severe cases
- Very high case fatality rate (varies by outbreak/strain, historically 25-90% for Ebola)
- Diagnosis: PCR (handled under strict biosafety containment — BSL-4)
- Management: supportive care (fluid/electrolyte management is critical), monoclonal antibody therapies now exist for Ebola specifically; strict isolation and contact tracing for outbreak control
*Coronavirus (brief, since not explicitly named on your outline but likely covered in class)*
- (+)ssRNA, enveloped, largest RNA genome among RNA viruses, named for crown-like spike proteins
- Spike protein binds **ACE2 receptor** for cell entry (relevant to SARS-CoV-2 specifically)
- Disease spectrum: common cold (seasonal human coronaviruses) to severe respiratory illness (SARS-CoV, MERS-CoV, SARS-CoV-2) — severe forms can cause **ARDS** via excessive cytokine release, similar conceptually to the cytokine storm mechanism above
## G. HIV
*Classification*
- Retrovirus (Baltimore Class VI), subfamily **Lentivirus** — "lenti" = slow, reflects the long, slowly progressive course
- Two types: **HIV-1** (global pandemic strain, more virulent) and **HIV-2** (mainly West Africa, less virulent, slower progression)
*Structure*
- ssRNA genome (two copies — diploid, unusual among viruses), enveloped, carries its own **reverse transcriptase, integrase, and protease** enzymes packaged in the virion
- Surface glycoproteins: **gp120** (binds CD4 receptor + co-receptor) and **gp41** (mediates membrane fusion)
*Life cycle (special aspects — high-yield)*
1. **Attachment** — gp120 binds CD4 receptor on T-helper cells, plus a co-receptor: **CCR5** (used early, on macrophages/M-tropic strains) or **CXCR4** (used later, on T-cells/T-tropic strains) — co-receptor switching correlates with disease progression
2. **Fusion/entry** — gp41 mediates membrane fusion
3. **Reverse transcription** — viral RNA → dsDNA via reverse transcriptase (error-prone — major source of HIV's high mutation rate and drug resistance)
4. **Integration** — viral DNA integrates into host genome via integrase → becomes a permanent **provirus** (basis of lifelong infection, latent reservoir, and why HIV cannot currently be cured by drugs alone)
5. **Transcription/translation** — produces a long polyprotein, cleaved by **protease** into functional viral proteins
6. **Assembly and budding** — new virions bud from cell surface
*Natural history*
- **Acute phase** — flu-like illness 2-4 weeks post-infection, high viral load, often missed clinically
- **Clinical latency** — years of low-level viral replication while CD4 count gradually declines (virus is NOT truly dormant — ongoing replication, just clinically silent)
- **AIDS** — defined by CD4 count <200 cells/µL or presence of an AIDS-defining illness (e.g. Kaposi sarcoma, PCP pneumonia, CMV retinitis, candidiasis esophagitis)
*Antiretroviral therapy (ART) — drug classes mapped to life cycle stages*
- **NRTIs** (nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors) — Zidovudine, Lamivudine, Tenofovir
- **NNRTIs** (non-nucleoside RT inhibitors) — Efavirenz, Nevirapine
- **Protease inhibitors** — Ritonavir, Lopinavir
- **Integrase inhibitors** — Dolutegravir, Raltegravir
- **Entry/fusion inhibitors** — Enfuvirtide (fusion), Maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist)
- Standard modern regimen = combination therapy (**HAART**) — typically 2 NRTIs + 1 integrase inhibitor, to prevent resistance from HIV's high mutation rate
*Challenges for HIV vaccine production (your outline specifically flags this — likely essay territory)*
- Extremely high mutation rate (error-prone reverse transcriptase) → rapid antigenic variation, hard for antibodies to keep up
- Virus integrates into host genome and establishes **latent reservoirs** in resting CD4 T-cells — immune system/vaccines can't target cells not actively expressing viral antigen
- Destroys the very CD4 T-helper cells needed to mount an effective adaptive immune response
- No known case of natural sterilizing immunity to model a vaccine on (unlike e.g. measles, where natural infection gives lifelong immunity)
- Heavy glycosylation ("glycan shield") on gp120 hides conserved vulnerable regions from antibodies

## 1. MASTER CLASSIFICATION (Baltimore, all 7 classes, full roster)
- **I (dsDNA):** Herpesviruses, Adenovirus, Poxvirus, Papillomavirus
- **II (ssDNA):** Parvovirus
- **III (dsRNA):** Rotavirus
- **IV ((+)ssRNA):** Picornaviruses (polio, coxsackie, rhino), Togavirus (rubella), Flavivirus (yellow fever, dengue), Coronavirus, HAV, HCV, HEV
- **V ((−)ssRNA):** Orthomyxovirus (influenza), Paramyxovirus (measles, mumps, RSV, parainfluenza), Rhabdovirus (rabies), Filovirus (Ebola, Marburg)
- **VI (ssRNA-RT):** HIV
- **VII (dsDNA-RT):** HBV
- **Exception to memorize:** Poxvirus is dsDNA but replicates entirely in the **cytoplasm** (only DNA virus that does) — everyone else with dsDNA replicates in the nucleus
## 2. LATENCY — who hides, and where
- HSV-1 → trigeminal ganglion | HSV-2 → sacral ganglion | VZV → dorsal root ganglia
- EBV → B lymphocytes | CMV → monocytes/glands
- HIV → resting CD4 T-cells (as integrated provirus)
- HBV/HCV → don't classically "latent," but persist chronically in hepatocytes
## 3. ONCOGENIC VIRUSES — mechanism comparison
- **Direct oncoprotein transformation:** HPV (E6→p53, E7→Rb), EBV, HHV8
- **Indirect, via chronic inflammation/regeneration:** HBV, HCV → hepatocellular carcinoma
- **Retroviral (Tax protein activates host proliferation genes):** HTLV-1 → adult T-cell leukemia
- Quick recall list: HPV–cervical, EBV–Burkitt/nasopharyngeal/Hodgkin, HBV/HCV–HCC, HHV8–Kaposi sarcoma, HTLV-1–T-cell leukemia
## 4. CONGENITAL/VERTICAL TRANSMISSION — cross-virus pregnancy risk
- **Rubella** — 1st trimester worst → deafness, cataracts, PDA
- **CMV** — most common congenital viral infection → deafness, microcephaly, chorioretinitis
- **Parvovirus B19** — fetal hydrops/severe anemia
- **VZV** — <20wks → limb hypoplasia, skin scarring; peripartum → severe neonatal varicella
- **HSV-2** — peripartum (vaginal delivery, active lesions) → neonatal herpes
- **HIV** — vertical transmission preventable with maternal ART
- **HEV** — not congenital, but flagged because pregnancy = highest maternal mortality (up to 20% in 3rd trimester)
## 5. RASH DIFFERENTIATORS (classic "compare X vs Y" essay)
- **Measles** — cephalocaudal spread, preceded by Koplik spots
- **Rubella** — fine pink rash + posterior auricular/suboccipital lymphadenopathy (no Koplik spots)
- **Varicella** — lesions in different stages simultaneously ("dew drops on rose petal")
- **Smallpox** — lesions synchronous (all same stage) — this is THE differentiator from varicella
- **Parvovirus B19** — "slapped cheek" facial rash
## 6. DIAGNOSTIC CLUE → VIRUS (rapid-fire recall table)
- Negri bodies → Rabies
- Owl's eye inclusions → CMV
- Koplik spots → Measles
- Heterophile antibody (Monospot) → EBV
- Tzanck smear (multinucleated giant cells) → HSV/VZV (not specific between them)
- Faget's sign (relative bradycardia + fever) → Yellow fever
- "Wheel-shaped" capsid → Rotavirus
## 7. VACCINE TYPE → DISEASE cross-reference
- **Live attenuated:** MMR (measles, mumps, rubella), Varicella, Zoster, OPV, Yellow fever (17D), Rotavirus
- **Killed/inactivated:** IPV, HAV, Influenza (most formulations)
- **Subunit:** HPV (L1 protein), HBV (HBsAg)
- **Monoclonal antibody (passive, not a vaccine):** Palivizumab (RSV)
## 8. ANTIVIRAL DRUG CLASS → LIFE CYCLE STAGE (cuts across all viruses)
- Entry blockers: Maraviroc, Enfuvirtide, Docosanol
- Uncoating blockers: Amantadine/Rimantadine (flu)
- Nucleoside analogues (block replication): Acyclovir, Ganciclovir, Zidovudine, Tenofovir
- Protease inhibitors: Ritonavir, Lopinavir
- Integrase inhibitors: Dolutegravir, Raltegravir
- Release inhibitors: Oseltamivir, Zanamivir (flu neuraminidase)
## 9. "SLOW VIRUS" / DELAYED PRESENTATION LINK
- Measles → SSPE (years later)
- HIV → AIDS (years later)
- Prions → CJD (years later)
- All three share: long incubation, progressive, fatal
**10. BACTERIOPHAGES (your learning outcomes mention this — likely a short-answer item)**
- Phages are viruses that infect **bacteria specifically**, not human cells
- Same lytic/lysogenic cycle concept you already know, just applied to a bacterial host
- **Phage therapy** = using lytic phages to kill specific antibiotic-resistant bacteria — proposed as an alternative to antibiotics, highly specific (won't harm normal flora the way broad-spectrum antibiotics do)
- Relevant modern application your lecturer likely wants you to know: phages used experimentally against multidrug-resistant infections (e.g. resistant *Pseudomonas*, *Staphylococcus*) when antibiotics fail
- Also historically important in molecular biology (phages were used to first prove DNA, not protein, is genetic material — Hershey-Chase experiment)
