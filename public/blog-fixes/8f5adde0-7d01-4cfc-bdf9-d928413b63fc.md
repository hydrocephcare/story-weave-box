# Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Infectious Diseases and HIV**

> **In one line:** HIV is a retrovirus (genus *Lentivirus*) that binds CD4 and a co-receptor (CCR5 or CXCR4), reverse-transcribes its RNA into DNA, integrates into the host genome and progressively destroys CD4+ T cells, leading to AIDS and opportunistic infections. It is diagnosed by antibody and nucleic acid tests, and controlled but not cured by combination antiretroviral therapy (ART) that blocks entry, reverse transcription, integration or protease activity.

## What you must know

- Two types: **HIV-1** (more virulent and infective, causes most infections worldwide) and **HIV-2** (less infective, largely confined to West Africa, **intrinsically resistant to NNRTIs**).
- Transmission: **sexual (70 to 80%)**, **parenteral** (transfusion, needle-stick, shared needles) and **vertical** (in utero, labour and delivery, breastfeeding).
- Target cells: **CD4+ T cells, macrophages, dendritic cells, microglia**. Entry is gp120 to CD4, then CCR5 (R5, M-tropic) or CXCR4 (X4, T-tropic) co-receptor, then gp41-mediated fusion.
- Disease progression is measured by **CD4 count** (falling = worse immunity) and **viral load** (higher = more immune suppression).
- Untreated, average survival is **9 to 11 years** after infection; with ART, a person with a consistently **undetectable viral load (<50 copies/mL)** does not transmit HIV sexually (U=U).
- Diagnosis: ELISA/rapid antibody tests, confirmed by a second test or western blot/PCR. In **children under 18 months, antibody tests can be falsely positive** (maternal antibody); use **HIV DNA PCR**.
- ART: **two NRTIs (backbone) plus a third agent** (INSTI, NNRTI or PI). Kenya first line is **dolutegravir-based**.

## 1. Introduction and history

- HIV comprises two species of *Lentivirus* (a subgroup of retroviruses). Over years they cause **AIDS**, in which progressive immune failure allows life-threatening opportunistic infections (OIs) and cancers to thrive.
- AIDS was first recognised clinically in **1981** in the USA: clusters of injecting drug users and gay men with *Pneumocystis* pneumonia (PCP, now PJP because the organism is *Pneumocystis jirovecii*) and **Kaposi's sarcoma** (KS), with no known cause of immune failure. The US CDC formed a task force to monitor the outbreak.
- Both HIV-1 and HIV-2 arose from **zoonotic transfer** from non-human primates in West-central Africa in the early 20th century.
  - **HIV-1** from SIVcpz of chimpanzees (southern Cameroon).
  - **HIV-2** from SIVsmm of the sooty mangabey (West Africa).
- HIV-1 was first called lymphadenopathy-associated virus (LAV) and HTLV-III.

## 2. Epidemiology (UNAIDS 2022 figures)

- **39.0 million** people living with HIV (37.5 million adults, 1.5 million children aged 0 to 14); 53% women and girls.
- **1.3 million** new infections and **630 000** AIDS-related deaths in 2022; **29.8 million** on ART.
- **85.6 million** infected and **40.4 million** dead from AIDS-related illness since the start of the epidemic.
- **86%** knew their status; about **5.5 million** did not know they were infected.
- In the past, testing coverage was very low in Africa (in 2001 under 1% of sexually active urban adults tested, and only 0.5% of pregnant women in urban facilities were counselled, tested and got results). Donor blood is therefore routinely screened.

> **Remember:** eastern and southern Africa, including Kenya, carries the largest burden; most paediatric HIV is mother-to-child.

## 3. Virology

### Structure

- Roughly spherical, about **120 nm**, around 100 000 times smaller in volume than a red cell.
- **Two copies of positive-sense single-stranded RNA** encoding nine genes, inside a **conical capsid of about 2000 copies of p24**.
- RNA is bound to nucleocapsid protein **p7** and the enzymes **reverse transcriptase, protease, ribonuclease and integrase**.
- A **matrix of p17** surrounds the capsid.
- The envelope carries the **gp160 spike**, made of **gp120** (binds CD4 and co-receptor) and **gp41** (fusion).

### Tropism (which cells and which co-receptor)

| Strain | Old name | Co-receptor | Cells infected |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R5** | M-tropic, non-syncytium-inducing (NSI) | **CCR5** (a beta-chemokine receptor) | Macrophages and CD4+ T cells |
| **X4** | T-tropic, syncytium-inducing (SI) | **CXCR4** (an alpha-chemokine receptor) | CD4+ T cells (and macrophages) |
| **Dual-tropic** | Transitional | Both | Both |

- CCR5 is used by almost all primary HIV-1 isolates, whatever the subtype. Macrophages appear to be the **first cells infected**, and may keep producing virus when CD4+ cells are depleted.
- **Macrophages and microglia** are the cells infected in the CNS. In tonsils and adenoids, infected macrophages fuse into multinucleated giant cells that produce large amounts of virus.

### Life cycle and entry

1. **Attachment:** gp120 binds CD4 with high affinity.
2. gp120 changes shape, exposing its chemokine-receptor binding site, so it binds **CCR5 or CXCR4** (a stable two-pronged attachment). gp120 also binds integrin alpha4beta7, activating LFA-1, which forms virological synapses for cell-to-cell spread.
3. The N-terminal fusion peptide of **gp41** inserts into the host membrane. The HR1 and HR2 repeat sequences interact and the gp41 folds into a hairpin, pulling viral and cell membranes together to **fuse**.
4. The capsid releases RNA and enzymes into the cell.
5. **Reverse transcriptase** copies RNA to complementary DNA, degrades the RNA (RNase activity) and makes double-stranded DNA (DNA-dependent DNA polymerase activity). This process is **error-prone**, which causes drug resistance and immune escape.
6. Viral DNA enters the nucleus and **integrase** inserts it into a host chromosome (provirus).
7. Transcription and translation produce gag and gag-pol polyproteins; **protease** cleaves them so the budding virion matures and becomes infectious.

> **Exam tip:** each step of the life cycle is a drug target. Learn the step and the class together (entry, reverse transcription, integration, protease cleavage).

## 4. Pathogenesis

- HIV infects helper T cells (CD4+), macrophages and dendritic cells.
- CD4+ T cells fall through several mechanisms: **pyroptosis** of abortively infected cells, **apoptosis** of uninfected bystander cells, direct viral killing, and killing of infected cells by **CD8+ cytotoxic T cells**.
- Below a critical CD4 level, **cell-mediated immunity is lost** and the body becomes susceptible to OIs and cancers, which defines AIDS.

## 5. Transmission

| Route | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Sexual** | Main mode, 70 to 80% of infections. Blood, pre-ejaculate, semen and vaginal fluids. |
| **Parenteral** | Transfusion (risk from one infected unit of whole blood **>90%**), contaminated needle pricks, needle sharing among drug users. |
| **Vertical (perinatal)** | In utero, during labour and delivery (exposure to blood and vaginal fluid), and via **breast milk**. |

- HIV is present in body fluids as free virus and within infected cells.
- **Undetectable = untransmittable:** no sexual transmission when the HIV-positive partner has a consistently undetectable viral load (**<50 copies/mL**) on ART.

### Factors affecting transmission and vulnerability

| Viral | Host |
|---|---|
| HIV strain | Presence of other **STIs** |
| **Viral load** | Stage of infection |
| | **Circumcision** (protective) |
| | Hereditary resistance (for example CCR5 delta-32 mutation) |

## 6. Natural history and clinical stages

### Phases of untreated infection

| Phase | Features |
|---|---|
| **Acute (seroconversion)** | HIV replicates rapidly, very high viral load; antibodies develop; flu- or mononucleosis-like illness may occur; energy needs rise |
| **Asymptomatic** | No signs; immune system holds virus production in check; CD4 falls slowly |
| **Symptomatic** | Signs of immune suppression and onset of OIs; nutritional requirements rise |
| **Late symptomatic (AIDS)** | Very high viral load, intense weight loss and wasting, OIs take control, end-stage disease |

- **Virus can be transmitted at every stage**, most easily in acute infection and late disease when viral load is highest.
- Over the untreated course, viral load peaks early, falls to a set point, then rises again as CD4 count declines.

### WHO clinical staging (children and adults, summary)

| Stage | Examples |
|---|---|
| **1** | Asymptomatic; persistent generalised lymphadenopathy |
| **2** | Hepatosplenomegaly, papular pruritic eruptions, recurrent mouth ulcers, recurrent upper respiratory infections, extensive warts or molluscum, parotid enlargement, herpes zoster |
| **3** | Moderate unexplained malnutrition, persistent diarrhoea, persistent fever, oral candidiasis (after first 6 weeks), oral hairy leukoplakia, pulmonary TB, severe bacterial pneumonia, acute necrotising gingivitis, lymph-node TB, unexplained anaemia, neutropenia or thrombocytopenia |
| **4** | Severe wasting or severe malnutrition, PJP, recurrent severe bacterial infections, oesophageal candidiasis, extrapulmonary TB, Kaposi's sarcoma, CMV disease, cryptococcal meningitis, toxoplasmosis of the brain, HIV encephalopathy, lymphoma, cryptosporidiosis |

> **Remember:** in Kenya every child with HIV starts ART regardless of stage or CD4 ("test and treat"). Stage 3 or 4 and low CD4 define advanced disease, which needs closer monitoring and OI screening.

## 7. Opportunistic infections to know

| Organ | Common OIs |
|---|---|
| Lung | **PJP**, TB, bacterial pneumonia, lymphoid interstitial pneumonitis (children) |
| CNS | Cryptococcal meningitis, toxoplasmosis, TB meningitis, HIV encephalopathy |
| GI | Oesophageal and oral candidiasis, cryptosporidiosis, chronic diarrhoea |
| Skin and mucosa | Herpes zoster, severe warts, molluscum |
| Malignancy | Kaposi's sarcoma, non-Hodgkin lymphoma |

- **Co-trimoxazole prophylaxis** prevents PJP, toxoplasmosis and some bacterial infections, and is given to all HIV-exposed infants from 6 weeks and to all children with HIV.
- TB preventive therapy is given to children with HIV who are exposed to TB or are older than 12 months and TB-free on screening.

## 8. Diagnosis

### Antibody testing algorithm

1. Initial **ELISA** (or rapid test) for HIV-1 antibodies. A non-reactive result is negative unless there was recent exposure.
2. Reactive specimens are **retested in duplicate**. If either repeat is reactive, the specimen is "repeatedly reactive".
3. **Confirmatory test:** PCR, **western blot** (the gold standard confirmatory antibody test, more specific than ELISA) or, less commonly, immunofluorescence assay (IFA).
4. Positive only if repeatedly reactive by ELISA **and** positive by IFA/PCR or reactive on western blot. Repeatedly reactive ELISA with an **indeterminate** western blot may be an incomplete antibody response (early infection) or non-specific reaction.

| HIV-1 | HIV-2 | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| + | - | HIV-1 antibodies |
| - | + | HIV-2 antibodies |
| + | + | Both |
| - or indeterminate | - | Do a **nucleic acid test** to detect acute HIV-1 infection or exclude it |

### Test types (summary)

- **ELISA/EIA:** most common screening test.
- **Western blot:** gold standard confirmation.
- **Radio-immunobinding assay:** expensive, used when antibody is low.
- **Dot-blot immunobinding:** cheap rapid screen.
- **PCR:** measures HIV genetic material (RNA or DNA).

### Key practice points

- **Window period:** the test may be negative for up to 3 months after infection; repeat at 3 months.
- Two tests are required, plus a third if they conflict.
- **False positives are very possible in children under 18 months** because maternal IgG crosses the placenta. HIV-exposed infants are tested by **HIV DNA PCR** (first test at 6 weeks, or earlier if high risk), with antibody confirmation at 18 months (rapid test at 9 months).
- Monitoring: **CD4 count** (immune status) and **viral load** (treatment response).

## 9. Management

There is **no cure**. Treatment slows progression to AIDS, reduces OIs and limits malnutrition. ART is the most effective treatment.

### Classes of ART

Six classes are used in combination, usually **two NRTIs (backbone) plus one of an NNRTI, PI or INSTI (base)**.

| Class | Mechanism | Examples | Key adverse effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Entry / fusion inhibitors** | Block binding, fusion or entry. Maraviroc blocks CCR5 (risk of tropism shift to CXCR4, so check tropism first). Ibalizumab works against CCR5- and CXCR4-tropic virus. | Maraviroc, enfuvirtide, ibalizumab | Hepatotoxicity (maraviroc); injection-site reactions (enfuvirtide) |
| **NRTIs / NtRTIs** | Nucleoside/nucleotide analogues; **competitive substrate inhibitors and chain terminators** (no 3' OH group) | Zidovudine, abacavir, lamivudine, emtricitabine; tenofovir, adefovir | Mitochondrial toxicity: **lactic acidosis**, liver steatosis, peripheral neuropathy, myopathy, lipoatrophy. First-line NRTIs (lamivudine/emtricitabine, tenofovir, abacavir) are less likely to do this. |
| **NNRTIs** | Bind an **allosteric site** of reverse transcriptase (non-competitive). Not active against HIV-2. | 1st generation: nevirapine, efavirenz. 2nd generation: etravirine, rilpivirine | Generally safe. **Efavirenz:** neuropsychiatric effects, suicidal ideation (main reason to stop). **Nevirapine:** severe hepatotoxicity and rash, especially in women with high CD4 counts. |
| **INSTIs** | Inhibit integrase (two metal-binding groups compete with the two Mg2+ ions) | Raltegravir (first, FDA 2007), elvitegravir, **dolutegravir**, bictegravir, cabotegravir | Among the best tolerated; raised creatine kinase, rare myopathy; less long-term data |
| **Protease inhibitors** | Block cleavage of gag and gag-pol, producing defective, non-infectious virions | Lopinavir, indinavir, nelfinavir, amprenavir, ritonavir; darunavir and atazanavir as first-line PI choices | Usually boosted with **ritonavir** (strong CYP450 inhibitor, so many drug interactions); lipodystrophy, raised triglycerides, raised heart attack risk |

- Maturation inhibitors (bevirimat and vivecon) bind gag but development stopped in 2010.
- **Mitochondrial haplogroups** may influence CD4 recovery and idiosyncratic toxicity on ART.

### Kenyan guidelines (summary; follow the current MoH guideline)

- **Test and treat:** all people with HIV start ART, whatever the CD4 count or stage.
- **Adults and adolescents:** first line is **tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir (TLD)**.
- **Children:** dolutegravir-based regimens with an **abacavir + lamivudine** backbone are preferred, using weight-band dosing (dispersible formulations for small children). Infants under 4 weeks follow a special regimen in the guideline.
- **PMTCT:** lifelong ART for all pregnant and breastfeeding women; infant nevirapine prophylaxis for 6 weeks (extended for high-risk infants); early infant diagnosis.
- Monitor viral load at 6 months, 12 months and then yearly; a repeatedly unsuppressed viral load triggers adherence work and regimen review.

### Co-infection: TB and HIV

- Treat the TB first, then start ART **as soon as TB treatment is tolerated (within 2 to 8 weeks)**, earlier when CD4 is very low.
- Watch for **drug interactions**, especially **rifampicin**, which lowers dolutegravir levels (dolutegravir dose is increased to twice daily) and interacts with PIs and nevirapine.
- **Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis** and **TB preventive therapy** apply.

### IRIS (immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome)

- A paradoxical worsening of a known infection (**paradoxical IRIS**) or unmasking of a hidden infection (**unmasking IRIS**) after starting ART, as immune recovery produces an exaggerated inflammatory response.
- Common with TB, cryptococcus, CMV and mycobacterium avium complex, usually within the first weeks to 3 months of ART.
- **Do not stop ART** unless life-threatening; treat the infection, and consider steroids for severe IRIS.

## 10. Comprehensive HIV/AIDS programme

| Component | Elements |
|---|---|
| **Prevention** | Health and sex education; behaviour change and harm reduction; wider access to **VCT** (voluntary counselling and testing); **PMTCT** |
| **Treatment** | Secure ARVs for people living with HIV; treatment of OIs; proper nutrition |
| **Care and support** | Links to prevention; health services; psychosocial support; management of ARV side effects; mitigating social and economic impact; care of **orphans and vulnerable children (OVC)**; access to basic needs and rights |
| **Reducing vulnerability** | Services for women, girls, people living with HIV and people in conflict; economic empowerment and life-skills training |
| **Cross-cutting** | Participation and acceptance of people living with HIV; strong leadership from community to national level |

## Key points to revise

1. HIV is an RNA retrovirus (*Lentivirus*); HIV-1 causes most infections, HIV-2 is West African and NNRTI-resistant.
2. Entry: gp120 to CD4, then CCR5 (R5) or CXCR4 (X4), then gp41 fusion; then reverse transcriptase and integrase.
3. CD4 depletion (pyroptosis, apoptosis, direct killing, CD8 killing) leads to AIDS.
4. Sexual transmission is 70 to 80%; vertical transmission occurs in utero, at delivery and by breastfeeding; U=U at <50 copies/mL.
5. Diagnose with ELISA then confirmatory test; window period up to 3 months; use PCR under 18 months.
6. Six ART classes; regimen is two NRTIs plus an INSTI, NNRTI or PI; know each class's toxicity.
7. Kenya: test and treat, dolutegravir-based first line; start ART 2 to 8 weeks after TB treatment starts and watch for IRIS.
8. Progress is tracked by CD4 count and viral load.
