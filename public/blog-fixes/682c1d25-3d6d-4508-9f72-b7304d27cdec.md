## COMPLETE NUTRITION REVISION NOTES
*(Compiled: Macronutrients + Micronutrients + BND 3104 Notes —)*

## PART 1: FOUNDATIONS
## 1.1 Key Definitions
| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Nutrition** | Science of food & its relationship to health; ingestion→digestion→absorption→assimilation for growth/repair/maintenance |
| **Health (WHO)** | Complete physical, mental, social wellbeing — not just absence of disease |
| **Food** | Any nutritious substance ingested to sustain life/growth |
| **Nutrients** | Chemical substances in food needed for growth, maintenance, repair (~50 known) |
| **Diet** | What a person habitually eats/drinks |
| **Digestion** | Mechanical + chemical breakdown of food |
| **Malnutrition** | Imbalance from deficiency/excess/faulty utilization of nutrients |
| **Nutritional status** | Current body state re: nutrient consumption/utilization |
| **Functional foods** | Foods with added health benefits beyond normal nutrition |
| **Disease** | Abnormal condition — Signs (observable e.g. swelling) vs Symptoms (felt e.g. nausea) |
## 1.2 Disease Prevention Levels
1. **Primary** — averting disease occurrence
2. **Secondary** — early screening (e.g. diabetes screening)
3. **Tertiary** — treatment post-diagnosis to prevent complications
## 1.3 Core Principles of Nutrition Science
- Food = basic human need (Maslow's hierarchy)
- Health problems originate at **cellular level**
- Poor nutrition = **both** inadequate AND excessive intake
- Body has adaptive mechanisms (storage/elimination) — but limited protection
- **Nutrigenomics** = study of nutrient-gene interactions
- Vulnerable groups need higher nutrient intake (pregnant/lactating, infants, ill, elderly)
- Healthy diet = **Adequacy + Variety + Balance**
- No food is inherently "good" or "bad"
- **70% of disease is preventable through good nutrition**
## 1.4 Classification of Nutrients
| Basis | Categories |
|---|---|
| Chemical nature | Carbs, Proteins, Fats, Minerals, Vitamins, Fibre, Water |
| Function | Energy-giving (CHO/fat/protein) / Body-building (protein/fat/CHO) / Protective-regulators (vitamins/minerals) |
| Chemical property | Organic (carbon-containing) vs Inorganic |
| Essentiality | Essential (diet-required) vs Non-essential (body-synthesized) |
| Mass | Macronutrients (large amounts) vs Micronutrients (small amounts) |
| Origin | Animal-source vs Plant-source |

## PART 2: MACRONUTRIENTS
## 2.1 Carbohydrates
- **Definition:** Organic compounds (C,H,O) — main energy source; **4 kcal/g**
- **Types:**
  - Simple: Monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose) + Disaccharides (maltose, lactose, sucrose)
  - Complex: Starch, Glycogen (body's storage form — liver & muscle), Fibre
- **Fibre:** Soluble (oats, beans, citrus — ↓cholesterol, regulates blood sugar) vs Insoluble (wheat bran, fruit/veg skins — bowel regularity, ↓cancer risk)
- **Functions:** Energy/fuel, spares protein, prevents ketosis, aids Ca absorption (lactose), faecal bulk
- **RDA:** 45–65% total calories; minimum 130g/day
- **Deficiency:** Hypoglycemia, ketosis→ketoacidosis, impaired cognition, constipation, muscle breakdown
- **Excess:** Obesity, insulin resistance/T2DM, cardiovascular disease
## 2.2 Proteins
- **Definition:** Amino acid chains (C,H,O,**N**) — tissue building blocks; **4 kcal/g**
- **Amino acids:**
  - **Essential (9):** Histidine, Isoleucine, Leucine, Lysine, Methionine, Phenylalanine, Threonine, Tryptophan, Valine
  - **Non-essential:** Body-synthesized (Alanine, Glutamic acid, Serine, etc.)
  - **Conditionally essential** (illness/stress): Arginine, Cysteine, Glutamine, Tyrosine, Glycine
- **Types:** Complete (all essential AAs — meat, dairy, eggs, soy) | Incomplete (missing AAs — beans, nuts; **limiting amino acid** = cereals→lysine, legumes→methionine/cysteine)
- **Functions:** Repair/growth, enzymes (trypsin, pepsin), hormones (insulin), antibodies, transport (haemoglobin), backup energy
- **RDA:** 10–35% total calories; Men 56g/d, Women 46g/d, Pregnant 71g/d
- **Deficiency:** Muscle wasting, oedema, weak immunity, growth retardation
- **Excess:** Kidney strain, ↑CVD risk
## 2.3 Fats/Lipids
- **Definition:** Fatty acids + glycerol; **9 kcal/g** (most energy-dense)
- **3 main types:**
  - **Triacylglycerols** (>95% dietary fat) — fried foods, oils, butter
  - **Phospholipids** (~2%) — build cell membranes, water-soluble
  - **Sterols** (e.g. cholesterol) — cell membranes, hormone/vitD/bile salt precursor
- **Fatty acid types:** Saturated (no double bonds, solid) | Monounsaturated (1 double bond — olive oil) | Polyunsaturated (2+ double bonds — soybean oil) | Cis (natural) vs Trans (hydrogenated, harmful)
- **Essential fatty acids:** Omega-3 (alpha-linolenic — flaxseed, fish) & Omega-6 (linoleic — corn, sunflower oil)
- **Functions:** Energy, cell structure, absorbs vitamins A/D/E/K, hormone precursor, insulation, organ padding, satiety
- **RDA:** 20–35% total calories; saturated fat <10%, trans fat <1%
- **Deficiency:** Dry skin, hormonal imbalance, impaired brain function
- **Excess:** Obesity, ↑cholesterol/CVD, insulin resistance
## 2.4 Metabolism
- **Catabolism** = breakdown → releases energy (ATP) | **Anabolism** = synthesis → uses energy
- **Importance:** Energy production, growth/repair, homeostasis, waste elimination
- **Factors affecting rate:** Age(↓), muscle mass(↑rate), genetics, diet, hormones (thyroid), cold temp(↑rate)
**Pathways:**
- **Carbs:** Glycolysis (glucose→2 pyruvate, 2 ATP) → Krebs cycle (Acetyl-CoA, 2 ATP) → ETC (~34 ATP) → Gluconeogenesis/Glycogenesis/Glycogenolysis
- **Protein:** Proteolysis → Deamination (liver: AA→ammonia→urea) → Transamination → Urea cycle → Anabolism (new protein)
- **Fat:** Lipolysis (lipase) → Beta-oxidation (→Acetyl-Co
A) → Krebs/ETC → Ketogenesis (fasting) → Fat storage (adipose)

## PART 3: WATER
- Largest body constituent; man 60–65%, woman 50–54% body weight
- Muscle 70% water, fat tissue 30%, bone 10%
- **Compartments:** ECF (~20% body weight: plasma, interstitial, lymph) | ICF (~40–45%, largest)
- **Functions:** Solvent/transport medium, temperature regulation (sweat), lubricant (joints/mucus), body shape/structure, structural component of protein/glycogen
- **Regulation:** Hypothalamus (thirst), kidneys (main regulator via ADH), skin, lungs, GI tract
- **RDA:** ~2.5L/day adults (1ml/kcal); infants 1.5ml/kcal
- **Dehydration:** >10% loss→renal failure; >20%→death
- **Water intoxication:** Cell swelling (esp. brain)→headache, convulsions, death
- **Best sources:** Tap/bottled water, juice, milk, soups (food provides ~19% of total water intake)

## PART 4: MICRONUTRIENTS — MINERALS
## 4.1 General
- **4 functions:** Body building, control body processes, part of fluids/cells, part of enzymes
- **Major minerals** (>5g body/≥100mg RDA): Ca, P, Mg, S, Na, K, Cl
- **Trace minerals** (<5g body/<100mg RDA): Fe, Zn, I, Se, Cu, Mn, F, Cr, Mo
- **Ultratrace:** <1mg/day requirement
## 4.2 Macro Minerals
| Mineral | Function | Source | RDA | Deficiency | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Calcium** | Bone/tooth formation, clotting, nerve/muscle function | Milk, sardines, broccoli, legumes | 1000mg | Osteoporosis, osteomalacia, rickets | Calcium rigour, deposits in organs |
| **Phosphorus** | ATP energy release, DNA/RNA, bone calcification, acid-base balance | Meat, dairy, cereals | 700mg | Rare (antacid users) | Loss of Ca from bones |
| **Magnesium** | Enzyme catalyst, nerve conduction, muscle relaxation, bone formation | Dark greens, legumes, nuts, plant foods | 310-400mg | Weakness, tetany, growth failure | Coma, heart failure |
| **Sulphur** | Metabolism, clotting, part of B1/B5/biotin, detox, collagen | Sulphur amino acids | — | — | — |
| **Fluorine** | Strengthens enamel/bone | Water, tea, fish | 3-4mg | Tooth decay | Dental fluorosis, mottled teeth |
| **Sodium** | Fluid balance, acid-base, nerve/muscle activation | Salt, processed foods | 1500mg | Weakness, cramps, headache | Oedema, hypertension |
| **Potassium** | Fluid balance, neuromuscular activity, protein synthesis | Bananas, potatoes, plant foods | 4700mg | Weakness, irregular heartbeat | Irregular heartbeat, heart attack |
| **Chloride** | HCl for digestion, acid-base, water balance | Table salt | 2300mg | Long-term mental retardation (infants) | Same as sodium |
## 4.3 Trace Minerals
| Mineral | Function | Source | RDA | Deficiency | Toxicity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Iron** | O₂ transport (Hb/myoglobin), RBC formation | Liver, red meat, dark greens | M 8-10mg, F 15-18mg, Pregnant 27-30mg | **Iron-deficiency anaemia** | Hemosiderosis, hemochromatosis |
| **Iodine** | Thyroxin synthesis, metabolism, brain development | Seafood, iodized salt | 150mg | **Goitre, Cretinism, Myxedema** | Hyperthyroidism, goitre |
| **Zinc** | 300+ enzymes, immunity, wound healing, insulin | Red meat, shellfish, eggs | M 11-15mg, F 8-12mg | Growth retardation, delayed sexual maturation, poor wound healing | Nausea, copper deficiency, ↑blood lipids |
| **Selenium** | Antioxidant (glutathione peroxidase) | Seafood, legumes, meat | 55mcg | Muscle wasting, Keshan/Kashin-Beck disease | Selenosis — hair/nail loss, garlic breath |
| **Copper** | Iron/oxygen utilization enzymes, brain development | Bread, grains, seafood | 900mcg | Anaemia, growth retardation | Wilson's disease |
| **Manganese** | Body fat/bone formation | Whole grains, tea, nuts | — | Weight loss, infertility (men) | Neurological/psychotic symptoms |
| **Chromium** | Glucose/fat utilization | Whole grains, wheat germ | — | Weight loss | Kidney/skin damage |
| **Molybdenum** | Oxygen transfer enzymes | Legumes, grains, liver | 45mcg | Extremely rare | Joint pain, anaemia, gout |

## PART 5: MICRONUTRIENTS — VITAMINS
## 5.1 General
- Organic, no energy value themselves — facilitate energy-yielding reactions
- **Water-soluble** (B-complex + C): Not stored (except B12), absorbed directly into blood, excess excreted in urine, generally non-toxic, needed frequently
- **Fat-soluble** (A,D,E,K "ADEK"): Absorbed via lymph→blood, need protein carrier, stored in fat/liver, less readily excreted, TOXIC risk (esp. A & D), needed only periodically
## 5.2 Water-Soluble Vitamins
| Vitamin | Function | Deficiency | Toxicity | Source | RDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B1 Thiamin** | Carb metabolism (TPP coenzyme) | **Beriberi** (wet=oedema/heart failure; dry=nerve wasting); Wernicke-Korsakoff | Rare — rapidly excreted | Pork, legumes, whole grains | 1.1-1.5mg |
| **B2 Riboflavin** | Energy coenzyme (FAD/FMN), vision | Cheilosis, glossitis, angular stomatitis | None known | Dairy, liver, leafy veg | 1.1-1.7mg |
| **B3 Niacin** | Energy metabolism (NAD/NADP) | **Pellagra — 4 D's:** Dermatitis, Diarrhoea, Dementia, Death | Flushing, liver damage (>0.5g/d) | Protein foods, grains (maize deficient) | 14-19mg (NE) |
| **B5 Pantothenic acid** | Coenzyme A — energy release | Rare (fatigue, numbness) | None known | Mushrooms, eggs, liver | 4-7mg |
| **Folate (B9)** | DNA synthesis, RBC maturation (with B12) | Megaloblastic anaemia; **neural tube defects** | May mask B12 deficiency | Leafy greens, liver, legumes | 180-400mcg (pregnancy) |
| **B6 Pyridoxine** | Amino acid metabolism, neurotransmitters | Irritability, convulsions (infants) | Nerve damage (loss of balance) | Meat, fish, bananas, avocado | 1.3mg |
| **B12 Cobalamin** | DNA synthesis, myelin sheath (needs intrinsic factor) | **Pernicious anaemia** | None — excreted by kidneys | Animal foods ONLY | 2.4mcg |
| **Biotin (B7)** | Fat/protein/glycogen metabolism | Rare (raw egg avidin binds it) — seizures, hair loss | None known | Liver, egg yolk | 30mcg |
| **Vitamin C** | Collagen formation, iron absorption, antioxidant | **Scurvy** — bleeding gums, poor healing | Nausea, diarrhoea, kidney stones (>1g/d) | Citrus, peppers, broccoli | 75-90mg |
| **Choline** | Cell membrane structure, memory development | Fatty liver, infertility | Fishy body odour, liver damage | Eggs, beef, fish | 425-550mg |
## 5.3 Fat-Soluble Vitamins
| Vitamin | Function | Deficiency | Toxicity | Source | RDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Vision (rhodopsin), epithelial integrity, growth, reproduction | **Night blindness→xerophthalmia**, ↑infections | Hypervitaminosis A — headache, liver damage, birth defects | Liver, carrots, spinach (beta-carotene) | 700-900mcg RE |
| **D** | Ca/P absorption, bone mineralization | **Rickets** (children); **Osteomalacia** (adults) | Hypercalcemia — calcium deposits in organs | Sunlight, fatty fish, fortified milk | 5-10mcg |
| **E** | Antioxidant — protects cell membranes | Hemolytic anaemia (preterm infants) | Impairs clotting (>800IU/d) | Plant oils, nuts, wheat germ | 15mg |
| **K** | Blood clotting (prothrombin), bone proteins | Hemorrhage (newborns at risk — given injection at birth) | Rare — liver disease (synthetic forms only) | Liver, green leafy veg | 90-120mcg |
**Key micronutrient deficiency diseases (quick recall):**
Iron→anaemia | Vit C→scurvy | Vit A→xerophthalmia | Niacin(B3)→pellagra | Iodine→goitre/cretinism | Thiamin(B1)→beriberi | Riboflavin(B2)→ariboflavinosis | Vit D→rickets
**Public Health priority micronutrients:** Vitamin A, Iron, Iodine, Zinc (highest deficiency burden globally = "hidden hunger")

## PART 6: MALNUTRITION
## 6.1 Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM)
- Affects children 1-5yrs mainly; caused by lack of protein/CHO or excess CHO relative to protein
| Feature | Kwashiorkor | Marasmus | Marasmic-Kwashiorkor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cause | Protein deficiency (weaning to low-protein family food) | Energy deficiency (starvation) | Both protein + energy |
| Age | 1–3 years | 6–18 months (infants) | Mixed |
| Signs | **Oedema**, protruding belly, dark/scaly skin, enlarged liver (hepatomegaly), reddish hair, anaemia, diarrhoea, "moon face" | Severe wasting ("skin & bones"), sunken eyes, "old man" face, alert, retarded growth, prominent ribs (pigeon chest), NO oedema | Oedema + wasting, flaky dermatitis, hepatomegaly |
| Cure | Adequate protein + carbohydrate-rich food | Same | Same |
**Acute malnutrition classification:** SAM (severe wasting/oedema) vs MAM (moderate wasting); GAM = SAM+MAM combined
## 6.2 Micronutrient Deficiencies — Public Health Priority
- Iron deficiency anaemia (IDA), Vitamin A deficiency (VAD), Iodine deficiency disorder (IDD), Zinc deficiency
- Iodine deficiency → BMR/growth/reproduction issues; common in highlands (soil erosion); **law mandates iodized salt**
## 6.3 Overweight & Obesity
**Predisposing factors:**
- Genetics: both lean parents 9-14% child obesity risk; both obese parents 60-80%
- Physiological: poor hunger/satiety response, ↓thyroxine→↓BMR
- Behavioural: inactivity, emotional eating
**Complications:** Type 2 diabetes, CVD, hypertension, gallbladder disease, arthritis, psychological disturbance

## PART 7: DIETARY PLANNING TOOLS
## 7.1 Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs)
- **RDA** — meets needs of ~97% of population group
- **AI** — used when insufficient data for RDA
- **EER** — Estimated Energy Requirement (age, sex, weight, activity-adjusted)
- **UL** — highest safe long-term intake
## 7.2 Food Guides
- **MyPyramid** (USDA 2005, now replaced) — 6 components: Activity, Moderation, Personalization, Proportionality, Variety, Gradual improvement
- **MyPlate** — current USDA guide, plate divided into food group proportions
## 7.3 Kenya Dietary Guidelines (2017) — Key Messages
- Variety from food groups daily; whole/unprocessed starches
- Plenty veg/fruit daily
- Legumes/nuts ≥4x/week
- Lean meat/fish/eggs ≥2x/week
- Fresh milk daily
- Oil/fat in moderation; sugar sparingly
- **Iodised salt**, used sparingly
- Plenty safe water
## 7.4 Principles of Dietary Planning
1. **Adequacy** — sufficient for growth/maintenance/repair
2. **Balance** — sufficient from all nutrient classes
3. **Calorie control** — intake = output
4. **Density** — most nutrients per calorie
5. **Moderation** — avoid excess
6. **Variety** — wide food selection

## PART 8: NUTRITION-DISEASE RELATIONSHIP
## 8.1 Illness ↔ Malnutrition Vicious Cycle
**Illness→poor nutrition:** poor appetite, fever(↑BMR+sweat loss), diarrhoea/vomiting(nutrient loss), nutrients diverted to immune response
**Poor nutrition→illness:** weight loss, ↓immunity, promotes pathogen growth
## 8.2 Diet-Related Chronic Diseases
| Disease | Key Facts | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Diabetes | Type 1=no insulin; Type 2=insulin resistance (more common) | Healthy weight, exercise, fibre |
| Cardiovascular disease | Leading cause of death (developed countries); CHD most common | Fruits/veg/fish, limit alcohol |
| Cancer | Diet = ~1/3 of cancers (2nd to tobacco) | Healthy BMI, activity, limit alcohol/salt/preserved meat |
| Dental disease | Sugar + bacteria required; frequency of sugar = biggest risk | Fluoride, limit sugar |

## PART 9: VULNERABLE GROUPS
- Children <5 (high needs, disease-prone)
- Pregnant/lactating women (high needs, low food-generating capacity)
- Elderly/chronically ill (dependency, medical cost diversion)
- Marginalized populations (low food access)
- Displaced populations (cut from food sources)

## PART 10: NUTRITION ACROSS LIFE CYCLE
## 10.1 Pregnancy
- 3 trimesters (wk1-12, 13-27, 28-birth)
- Weight gain depends on pre-pregnancy BMI (underweight gains most)
- **Energy:** +300kcal/day average (+340 2nd tri, +452 3rd tri)
- **Carbs:** 45-65%, min 175g/d | **Protein:** 71g/d (+25g) | **Fat:** 20-35%, omega-3 for fetal brain
- **Key nutrients:** Folate (neural tube defects), Vit A (excess=birth defects), Vit D, Iron (↑demand), Calcium (1000mg/d), Iodine, Zinc
**Common problems:** Pica (anaemia/lead risk), morning sickness, leg cramps (low Ca/Mg), constipation, heartburn
**Complications:**
- **Hyperemesis gravidarum** — severe vomiting past wk14 → dehydration
- **Hypertensive disorders** — Chronic HTN, Gestational HTN, **Pre-eclampsia** (HTN+proteinuria after wk20), **Eclampsia** (seizures — emergency)
- **Gestational diabetes** — develops wk24-28
## 10.2 Infancy (0-1yr)
- **WHO:** Exclusive breastfeeding 6mo → continued breastfeeding + complementary feeding to 2yrs+
- **Colostrum** = first milk, protective, high nutrients
- **Benefits:** Infant (disease protection, digestion, bonding) | Mother (natural contraception/LAM, ↓bleeding, ↓cancer risk) | Family/community (economic, environmental)
- **Complementary feeding** (from 6mo) must be: Timely, Adequate, Safe, Properly fed
  - Early introduction risk: displaces breastmilk, diarrhoea, allergies
  - Late introduction risk: growth deficits, Fe/Zn deficiency
- Feeding problems: choking, allergies, early childhood caries, reflux, colic, failure to thrive
## 10.3 Life Stage Quick Reference
| Stage | Energy (kcal/d) | Key Concerns |
|---|---|---|
| Toddler (2-3yr) | 1,000-1,400 | Picky eating, choking risk, obesity |
| Childhood (4-8yr) | 1,200-2,000 | Malnutrition, food allergies |
| Puberty (9-13yr) | 1,400-2,600 | ↑Ca/Fe needs (menstruation) |
| Older adolescence (14-18yr) | 1,800-3,200 | **Eating disorders** (anorexia, bulimia, binge-eating) |
| Young adulthood (19-30yr) | 1,800-3,000 | Obesity (BMI≥30) |
| Middle age (31-50yr) | 1,800-3,000 | ↑Homocysteine/CVD risk (need folate, B6, B12), menopause |
| Old age (51+yr) | 1,600-2,800 | ↓taste/smell, dysphagia, anorexia of aging, dementia |

## PART 11: NUTRITIONAL ASSESSMENT ("ABCD")
| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| **A**nthropometric | Height, weight, %body fat, circumferences |
| **B**iochemical | Blood nutrient/enzyme levels, DNA markers — detects deficiency BEFORE clinical signs |
| **C**linical/Physical | Visual inspection — pallor, Bitot's spots, pitting oedema, goitre, wasting |
| **D**ietary | 24-hr recall, dietary history, food records, Food Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ) |
