# Bleeding Disorders

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Haematology**

> **In one line:** haemostasis needs an intact vessel wall, enough working platelets, enough clotting factors and a balanced fibrinolytic system; a defect in any one gives a bleeding disorder. Platelet and vessel disorders bleed from skin and mucous membranes (petechiae, epistaxis) immediately after injury, while clotting-factor disorders bleed deep (joints, muscles) and later.

## What you must know

- Haemostasis has four phases: **vascular, platelet, coagulation and fibrinolytic**.
- Screening tests: **platelet count, bleeding time, PT (extrinsic), aPTT (intrinsic), thrombin time**.
- **Platelet disorders:** petechiae, purpura, mucosal bleeding, immediate bleeding after cuts. **Coagulation disorders:** deep haematomas and **haemarthrosis**, delayed bleeding after surgery.
- **Haemophilia A** (factor VIII) and **B** (factor IX) are X-linked recessive, with a **prolonged aPTT**, normal PT and normal bleeding time. Haemophilia A is 80 to 85% of cases.
- **von Willebrand disease** is autosomal dominant with mucocutaneous bleeding, **prolonged bleeding time and aPTT**; treat with DDAVP, cryoprecipitate or factor VIII concentrate containing VWF.
- **Vitamin K** is needed for factors **II, VII, IX, X** and proteins C and S; deficiency is treated with vitamin K and fresh frozen plasma.
- Inhibitors (antibodies against infused factor) develop in a proportion of patients with severe haemophilia and are treated with high-dose factor VIIa.

## 1. Normal haemostasis

Response to vessel injury:

1. **Vascular phase:** vasoconstriction reduces blood flow. It is caused by **thromboxane A2** from activated platelets and injured endothelial cells, pain-induced nervous reflexes, and direct injury to vascular smooth muscle.
2. **Platelet phase:** von Willebrand factor (VWF) binds the damaged vessel and platelets adhere and aggregate, forming a temporary **platelet plug**.
3. **Coagulation phase:** the clotting cascade (like a row of dominoes; if one factor is missing the chain stops) converts fibrinogen to fibrin through two pathways:
   - **Intrinsic:** collagen, XII, XI, IX, VIII.
   - **Extrinsic:** tissue thromboplastin (factor III), VII.
   - Both converge on X and V, then **prothrombin (II) to thrombin, then fibrinogen (I) to fibrin**.
4. **Fibrinolytic phase:** anti-clotting mechanisms are activated so the clot breaks down and the vessel is repaired.

Haemostasis depends on: vessel wall integrity, adequate platelet numbers, proper platelet function, adequate clotting factors, and a functioning fibrinolytic pathway.

## 2. Laboratory evaluation

| Test | What it tests | Normal | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Platelet count** | Platelet number | 100,000 to 400,000/mm³ (slide; usual lower limit is 150,000) | Below 100,000 thrombocytopenia; 50,000 to 100,000 mild; below 50,000 severe |
| **Bleeding time (BT)** | Platelet number and function (and vessel constriction) | 2 to 8 minutes | Time for vessel constriction and platelets to seal the hole |
| **Prothrombin time (PT)** | **Extrinsic** pathway (factor VII) and common pathway | 10 to 15 seconds | Also monitors warfarin (INR); prolonged in liver disease and vitamin K deficiency |
| **aPTT / PTT** | **Intrinsic** pathway (VIII, IX, XI, XII) and common pathway | 25 to 40 seconds | Monitors **heparin** |
| **Thrombin time (TT)** | Conversion of fibrinogen to fibrin by excess thrombin | 9 to 13 seconds | Prolonged by heparin, low or abnormal fibrinogen, fibrin degradation products |

> **Check:** the slide says aPTT measures factors 1, 2, 5, 7, 10, 11, 12. Correctly, aPTT measures the intrinsic factors (**XII, XI, IX, VIII**) plus the common pathway (X, V, II, I). **Factor VII is measured by PT, not aPTT.** Thrombin time assesses fibrinogen conversion; it is not a direct test of fibrinolysis.

Platelet studies: morphology (blood film), function tests (aggregation), antibodies.

## 3. Causes of bleeding disorders

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Vessel defects** | Vitamin C deficiency (scurvy); bacterial and viral infections; acquired and hereditary conditions; infectious and hypersensitivity vasculitides: rickettsial and meningococcal infections, **Henoch-Schönlein purpura** (immune); senile purpura |
| **Platelet disorders** | **Thrombocytopenia** (too few): drug-induced, bone marrow failure, hypersplenism, lymphoma, HIV, **ITP**. **Thrombocytopathy** (normal number, abnormal function): uraemia, inherited (von Willebrand disease, Glanzmann's thrombasthenia, Bernard-Soulier syndrome), myeloproliferative disorders, drugs (aspirin, NSAIDs) |
| **Clotting factor deficiency (congenital)** | Haemophilia A, haemophilia B, von Willebrand disease |
| **Acquired** | Anticoagulants (coumarins, heparin), **liver disease**, malabsorption, broad-spectrum antibiotics (kill vitamin K-producing gut flora), vitamin K deficiency, **DIC** |

## 4. Clinical pattern: platelet versus coagulation disorder

| Feature | Platelet / vessel disorder | Coagulation factor disorder |
|---|---|---|
| Site of bleeding | **Skin and mucous membranes** (epistaxis, gum, vaginal, GI) | **Deep** in soft tissues (joints, muscles) |
| Petechiae | **Yes** | No |
| Ecchymoses | Small, superficial | Large, deep |
| Haemarthrosis / muscle bleeds | Extremely rare | **Common** |
| Bleeding after cuts and scratches | Yes | No |
| Bleeding after surgery or trauma | **Immediate**, usually mild | **Delayed (1 to 2 days)**, often severe |

- **Petechiae** do not blanch with pressure (unlike angiomas) and are not palpable (unlike vasculitis); they are typical of platelet disorders. **Palpable purpura** suggests vasculitis (Henoch-Schönlein purpura). Petechiae also occur in rickettsial infection (Rocky Mountain spotted fever) and meningococcaemia.
- **Large ecchymoses and haematomas** are typical of coagulation factor disorders.

> **Exam tip:** "bleeds from mucosa and skin, bleeding time prolonged" is a platelet or von Willebrand problem; "bleeds into joints, prolonged aPTT, normal bleeding time" is haemophilia.

## 5. Haemophilia A and B

| | Haemophilia A (classic) | Haemophilia B (Christmas disease) |
|---|---|---|
| Deficient factor | **VIII** | **IX** |
| Share of haemophilia | 80 to 85% | 10 to 15% |
| Inheritance | X-linked recessive | X-linked recessive |
| Incidence | 1 in 10,000 males | 1 in 50,000 males |
| Lab | **Prolonged aPTT**, normal PT, normal BT | Prolonged aPTT, normal PT, normal BT |

Males are affected; carrier females are usually asymptomatic. The two types are clinically indistinguishable.

### Severity (factor level)

| Level | Severity | Bleeding |
|---|---|---|
| Below 1% | **Severe** | Spontaneous bleeding |
| 1 to 5% | Moderate | Bleeding with mild injury |
| 5 to 25% | Mild | Bleeding with surgery or trauma |

### Clinical manifestations

- **Haemarthrosis (commonest)**, leading to chronic arthropathy and fixed joints (knees, elbows, ankles).
- Soft tissue haematomas (muscle), with muscle atrophy and shortened tendons.
- Other sites: urinary tract (haematuria), **CNS bleeding and neck bleeding (may be life-threatening)**.
- Prolonged bleeding after surgery or dental extraction.

### Treatment of haemophilia A

Principles: management of haemostasis and bleeding episodes; treatment and rehabilitation of haemophilic synovitis; treatment of patients with inhibitors; factor replacement with adjuvant drugs.

| Product | Features |
|---|---|
| Intermediate-purity plasma products | Virucidally treated; may contain VWF |
| High-purity (monoclonal) plasma products | Virucidally treated; no functional VWF |
| **Recombinant factor VIII** | Virus-free, no apparent risk; no functional VWF |

Add-on treatments: desmopressin (for mild haemophilia A), tranexamic acid, rest, ice, compression, elevation, physiotherapy. Avoid IM injections and aspirin.

### Treatment of haemophilia B

- **High-purity factor IX** or **recombinant human factor IX**.
- Initial dose **100 U/kg**; then **50 U/kg every 24 hours**.

### Complications of therapy

- **Inhibitors (antibodies):** 10 to 15% of severe haemophilia A patients and 1 to 2% of severe haemophilia B patients (the lecture also states that up to 30% of severe haemophilia A patients may develop them). Bleeds or surgery are treated with **high-dose recombinant factor VIIa**, which bypasses the FVIII or FIX defect. Long-term, inhibitors are eradicated by **immune tolerance induction** with high-dose FVIII (or FIX).
- **Viral infections** from plasma products: hepatitis B, hepatitis C, HIV, parvovirus, hepatitis A.

## 6. Von Willebrand disease

- **VWF** is made in endothelium and megakaryocytes, forms large multimers, **carries factor VIII**, anchors platelets to the subendothelium and bridges platelets.
- Inheritance **autosomal dominant**; incidence about 1 in 10,000. It is the commonest inherited bleeding disorder.
- Deficiency of VWF and reduced factor VIII: **prolonged bleeding time and aPTT**.
- Clinical features: **mucocutaneous bleeding** (epistaxis, menorrhagia, gum bleeding, bruising, bleeding after dental work).

### Treatment

| Agent | Notes |
|---|---|
| **DDAVP** (desmopressin) | Raises plasma VWF by stimulating release from endothelium; response duration variable; **not generally used in type 2** disease; **0.3 µg/kg IV every 12 hours** |
| **Cryoprecipitate** | Source of fibrinogen, factor VIII and VWF; the only plasma fraction that consistently contains VWF multimers |
| **Factor VIII concentrate** (intermediate purity) | Virally inactivated; contains VWF |

## 7. Acquired coagulation disorders

- **Liver disease** (reduced synthesis of factors and vitamin K absorption problems).
- **Vitamin K deficiency or warfarin overdose.**
- **DIC.**

### Vitamin K deficiency

- Sources: green vegetables (sukuma wiki, spinach, parsley) and synthesis by **intestinal flora**.
- Required for synthesis of **factors II, VII, IX, X** and **proteins C and S** (carboxylation).
- Causes: malnutrition, **biliary obstruction** (no bile salts so fat-soluble vitamin is not absorbed), malabsorption, **antibiotic therapy**; also newborns (haemorrhagic disease of the newborn).
- Treatment: **vitamin K** and **fresh frozen plasma** (or prothrombin complex concentrate in life-threatening bleeding).
- PT is prolonged first because factor VII has the shortest half-life.

## 8. Approach to the patient

1. Take a history: site and pattern of bleeding, family history (X-linked or autosomal), drugs (aspirin, anticoagulants), alcohol, liver disease, previous surgery and dental extractions.
2. Examine for petechiae, ecchymoses, haemarthrosis, splenomegaly, jaundice.
3. Send FBC with platelet count and film, PT/INR, aPTT, thrombin time, fibrinogen; then factor assays and VWF studies as indicated.
4. Interpret:

| Platelets | PT | aPTT | Likely cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | Normal | Normal | Thrombocytopenia (ITP, drug, marrow failure) |
| Normal | Normal | Prolonged | Haemophilia A/B, heparin, von Willebrand disease (BT also prolonged) |
| Normal | Prolonged | Normal | Factor VII deficiency, early warfarin or vitamin K deficiency |
| Normal | Prolonged | Prolonged | Liver disease, vitamin K deficiency, warfarin, DIC (platelets often low in DIC) |

## Key points to revise

1. Haemostasis: vascular, platelet, coagulation, fibrinolytic phases.
2. PT = extrinsic/warfarin; aPTT = intrinsic/heparin; bleeding time = platelets.
3. Platelet disorders: petechiae, mucosal bleeding, immediate bleeding. Factor disorders: deep haematomas, haemarthrosis, delayed bleeding.
4. Haemophilia A (VIII) and B (IX): X-linked recessive, prolonged aPTT; severity by factor level (below 1% severe).
5. Treat haemophilia with factor replacement; inhibitors need factor VIIa and immune tolerance.
6. von Willebrand disease: autosomal dominant, prolonged BT and aPTT; DDAVP, cryoprecipitate, VWF-containing FVIII.
7. Vitamin K is needed for II, VII, IX, X (and proteins C and S); treat with vitamin K and FFP.
