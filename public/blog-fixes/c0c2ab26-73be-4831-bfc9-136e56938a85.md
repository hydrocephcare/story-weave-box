# Chronic Kidney Disease in Children

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Paediatric Nephrology**

> **In one line:** chronic kidney disease (CKD) is kidney damage, or a GFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m², lasting 3 months or more. Loss of nephrons causes hyperfiltration of the survivors, which then fail in turn, so CKD progresses even after the original insult is removed; management is supportive and aims to treat the complications (bone disease, anaemia, acidosis, hypertension, poor growth) and slow progression.

## What you must know

- **Definition:** kidney damage for **3 months or more**, or **GFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²** for 3 months or more, with or without other signs of damage.
- **Staging** is by GFR: stage 1 (≥ 90) to stage 5 (< 15 or on dialysis).
- **Cause depends on age:** below 5 years, **congenital anomalies of the kidney and urinary tract (CAKUT)** predominate; above 5 years, **glomerulonephritis** (acquired or inherited).
- **Hyperfiltration**, proteinuria, hypertension, hyperphosphataemia and hyperlipidaemia drive progression; **tubulointerstitial fibrosis** is the final determinant.
- **Complications to treat:** CKD-mineral and bone disorder, anaemia (low erythropoietin), metabolic acidosis, hyperkalaemia, hypertension, growth failure.
- **Do not restrict protein** in children; give 100% of the energy requirement for age.
- **ACE inhibitors or ARBs** are the antihypertensives of choice; target BP below the 50th centile.
- Childhood-onset ESRD carries a **30-fold higher mortality** than in healthy peers, mainly from cardiovascular disease and infection.

## 1. Definition and staging

A patient has CKD if **either** is present:

1. **Kidney damage for ≥ 3 months**, structural or functional, with or without reduced GFR, shown by one or more of: abnormal blood or urine composition, abnormal imaging, abnormal kidney biopsy.
2. **GFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m² for ≥ 3 months**, with or without other signs of damage.

| Stage | Description | GFR (mL/min/1.73 m²) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kidney damage with normal or increased GFR | ≥ 90 |
| 2 | Kidney damage with mild decrease in GFR | 60 to 89 |
| 3 | Moderate decrease in GFR | 30 to 59 |
| 4 | Severe decrease in GFR | 15 to 29 |
| 5 | Kidney failure | < 15 or on dialysis |

**End-stage renal disease (ESRD)** is an administrative term (from the USA) for all patients treated by dialysis or transplantation. It is a subset of stage 5 CKD.

> **Remember:** the 3-month rule separates CKD from acute kidney injury. Stage 5 is defined by GFR < 15 **or** being on dialysis, whatever the GFR.

## 2. Epidemiology

- Paediatric CKD prevalence is about **18 per million children**.
- Survival has improved greatly since the 1970s because of better medical care, dialysis and transplantation.
- Even so, childhood-onset ESRD has **30 times the mortality** of healthy peers. The leading causes of death are **cardiovascular disease and infection**.

## 3. Aetiology

Causes may be congenital, acquired, inherited or metabolic, and are divided into **non-glomerular** and **glomerular**. The cause correlates with age at diagnosis:

| Age | Commonest causes |
|---|---|
| **Below 5 years** | **CAKUT**: renal hypoplasia, dysplasia, obstructive uropathy (often found on antenatal ultrasound) |
| **Above 5 years** | **Acquired or inherited glomerulonephritis** |

Other examples: reflux nephropathy with recurrent UTI, nephronophthisis, cystic kidney diseases, haemolytic uraemic syndrome, steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (FSGS), Alport syndrome, and in Kenya, HIV-associated nephropathy and sickle cell nephropathy.

## 4. Pathophysiology of progression

Renal injury can progress **even after the original insult has gone**.

### Hyperfiltration (final common pathway)

1. Nephrons are lost.
2. Surviving nephrons **hypertrophy** and glomerular blood flow rises.
3. The driving force for filtration increases, which preserves total GFR for a time.
4. The raised hydrostatic pressure and increased protein traffic across the capillary wall **damage the surviving glomeruli**.
5. The remaining nephrons carry a bigger excretory load, and the cycle repeats.

### Other drivers of progression

| Factor | How it harms |
|---|---|
| **Proteinuria** | Toxic to tubular cells; recruits monocytes and macrophages, promoting glomerulosclerosis and tubulointerstitial fibrosis |
| **Hypertension** | Arteriolar nephrosclerosis and added hyperfiltration injury |
| **Hyperphosphataemia** | Calcium phosphate deposition in renal interstitium and vessels |
| **Hyperlipidaemia** | Oxidant-mediated glomerular injury |

CKD is a **continuum**: manifestations increase as function falls. Whatever the cause, **progression of tubulointerstitial fibrosis** is the main determinant of CKD progression.

## 5. Clinical features

Presentation depends on the cause and the stage.

| Group | Typical features |
|---|---|
| **CAKUT and genetic tubular disease** (e.g. familial nephronophthisis) | Growth failure, vomiting, **polyuria with polydipsia**; UTIs are common with urological anomalies |
| **Glomerular CKD** | **Oedema, hypertension, haematuria, proteinuria**; malnutrition in severe glomerulonephritis |
| **Advanced CKD (any cause)** | Uraemic symptoms: fatigue, weakness, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, poor sleep; oedema, hypertension and fluid overload |

Also look for pallor (anaemia), bone pain and deformity (renal osteodystrophy), and short stature.

## 6. Investigations

### Laboratory findings

- Raised **urea (BUN) and creatinine**.
- **Hyperkalaemia**, **acidosis**, **hypocalcaemia**, **hyperphosphataemia**, raised uric acid.
- **Sodium** may be low (salt wasting or volume overload) or high (free water loss).
- **Hypoalbuminaemia** with heavy proteinuria.
- FBC: **normochromic, normocytic anaemia**.
- **Dyslipidaemia** is common.
- **Urinalysis:** glomerulonephritis gives haematuria and proteinuria; congenital lesions such as dysplasia give **low specific gravity** with little else.

### Measuring renal function

| Method | Comment |
|---|---|
| **Inulin clearance** | Gold standard, but no longer readily available |
| Iohexol, 99mTc-DTPA, 51Cr-EDTA, 125I-iothalamate | Measured GFR in clinical practice |
| **Creatinine and/or cystatin C** | Estimated GFR; the most used method |
| **Bedside Schwartz equation** | eGFR = **0.43 × height (cm) / serum creatinine (mg/dL)**; validated in children aged 1 to 16 years with GFR 15 to 90 |

Also: imaging (ultrasound for size, scarring, obstruction, cysts), and biopsy when the cause is unclear. Protein loss is quantified by **spot urine protein:creatinine ratio** or 24-hour collection.

> **Exam tip:** the bedside Schwartz equation uses height in cm and creatinine in mg/dL (divide micromol/L by 88.4 to convert).

## 7. Management

CKD care is **supportive**: screen for and treat the metabolic complications to improve quality of life and possibly slow progression. Children should be managed by a **multidisciplinary paediatric team** (medical, nursing, social, nutrition, psychology). Monitor bloods, urine (quantitative proteinuria) and symptoms closely.

### 7.1 Blood pressure monitoring

- **24-hour ambulatory BP monitoring (ABPM)** is the gold standard and is recommended in renal disease.
- **Masked hypertension** (normal office BP but abnormal ABPM) occurs in up to **35%** of pre-dialysis paediatric CKD and carries a **4-fold risk of left ventricular hypertrophy**.

### 7.2 Nutrition

- Dietitian experienced in paediatric renal disease (KDOQI recommendation).
- Give **100% of the estimated energy requirement for age**, adjusted for activity, BMI and weight trend.
- If oral supplements with extra calories or fluid fail, use **tube feeding** (nasogastric or gastrostomy).
- Balance calories between carbohydrate, unsaturated fat and protein.
- **Do not restrict protein** (risks poor growth); give about 100% of the DRI for ideal weight, more in dialysis.
- Give 100% of the DRI of vitamins and trace elements in stages 2 to 5; water-soluble vitamins are often needed on dialysis.

### 7.3 CKD-mineral and bone disorder (CKD-MBD)

CKD disturbs **calcium, phosphate, PTH and vitamin D**, causing bone disease (**renal osteodystrophy**) and vascular and soft tissue calcification.

**Sequence of events**

1. **FGF23** (with its cofactor **Klotho**) rises first, as early as **stage 2**, with normal calcium, phosphate, PTH and calcitriol.
2. FGF23 increases urinary phosphate excretion and **suppresses 1-α-hydroxylase**, so **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D falls** and PTH rises.
3. With further loss of function: **secondary hyperparathyroidism** (low 1,25(OH)₂D, hypocalcaemia, hyperphosphataemia, high PTH).

**Renal osteodystrophy**

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **High turnover (osteitis fibrosa cystica)** | Commonest in advanced paediatric CKD. Low calcium, high phosphate, **high ALP and PTH**; X-ray: **subperiosteal resorption, metaphyseal widening**. Bone pain, fractures from minor trauma, rachitic changes, varus/valgus deformities, **slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE)** |
| **Low turnover (adynamic)** | PTH oversuppression, **hypercalcaemia, low ALP**; more common in dialysis patients treated for secondary hyperparathyroidism |
| **Defective mineralisation** | In high turnover (mixed lesion) or low-to-normal turnover (osteomalacia) |

Bone volume is normal to high in most children unless they have had prolonged corticosteroids.

**Vascular calcification** occurs in the **media** of vessels (unlike atherosclerotic plaque in the intima). It is linked to hypercalcaemia, hyperphosphataemia and a high Ca × PO₄ product, but also occurs with normal levels. Vascular smooth muscle cells transform into osteoblast-like cells by triggers not yet known.

**Treatment** is guided by calcium, phosphate, 25-OH vitamin D and PTH. Goals: normal mineral metabolism, better growth, fewer deformities and fractures, less calcification.

| Measure | Details |
|---|---|
| **Low-phosphate diet** | All ages. Infants: low-phosphate formula (Similac PM 60/40) |
| **Phosphate binders** (with meals) | Start at onset of hyperphosphataemia. Calcium-based (**calcium carbonate, calcium acetate**) or non-calcium (**sevelamer, ferric citrate**). Keep within the recommended total daily calcium. **Avoid aluminium binders** (aluminium toxicity) |
| **Correct 25-OH vitamin D** | Sufficient is **≥ 30 ng/mL** (US guideline); give **ergocalciferol or cholecalciferol**. Delays secondary hyperparathyroidism and improves mineralisation |
| **Active vitamin D sterols** | Used when 1,25(OH)₂D is below target, PTH is above target after correcting 25-OH D, or PTH is high with hypocalcaemia. **Calcitriol** is best known; **paricalcitol and doxercalciferol** cause less gut calcium and phosphate absorption and suit children prone to hypercalcaemia |

> **Remember:** vitamin D sterols raise gut calcium and phosphate absorption, so monitor for hypercalcaemia. The ideal PTH target in pre-dialysis CKD is still debated.

### 7.4 Fluid and electrolytes

- **Renal dysplasia** with polyuria and loss of sodium and free water: high-volume, low-calorie-density feeds with **sodium supplements**.
- **Hypertension or oedema:** sodium restriction and diuretics.
- **Fluid restriction:** severe nephrotic syndrome, or when dialysis is needed.
- **Hyperkalaemia** (severe renal decline, high potassium intake, severe acidosis, or **hyporeninaemic hypoaldosteronism** from damage to the juxtaglomerular apparatus): restrict dietary potassium, give oral alkali and/or **Kayexalate** (sodium polystyrene sulfonate). Sodium zirconium and patiromer are used in adults.
- **Metabolic acidosis** (reduced net acid excretion): **Bicitra** (1 mEq sodium citrate/mL) or **sodium bicarbonate tablets** (650 mg = 8 mEq base) to keep serum bicarbonate **≥ 22 mEq/L**.

### 7.5 Linear growth

- **Short stature** is a major long-term problem.
- CKD is a **growth hormone-resistant state**: GH is high but **IGF-1 is low** and IGF-binding proteins are abnormal.
- First optimise calories, renal osteodystrophy, anaemia and acidosis.
- If height stays **below −2 SD**, give **recombinant human growth hormone (rHuGH)** by daily subcutaneous injection until the 50th centile for mid-parental height, final adult height, or transplantation.
- It improves final height and induces persistent catch-up growth; some reach normal height.

### 7.6 Anaemia

- Main cause: **inadequate erythropoietin** from the failing kidney; usually appears when GFR falls below **40 mL/min/1.73 m²**.
- Other causes: iron, folate or B12 deficiency, shortened red cell survival in uraemia.
- **Definition:** Hb below the 5th centile for age and sex, or Hb < **11 g/dL** (6 months to 5 years), < **11.5 g/dL** (5 to 12 years), < **12 g/dL** (females over 12 years; males 12 to 15 years), < **13 g/dL** (males over 15 years).
- Investigate for iron, B12 and folate deficiency.
- **Iron** (oral or IV) if **TSAT ≤ 20% and ferritin ≤ 100 ng/mL**.
- **Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESA):** erythropoietin and **darbepoetin alfa**; they reduce transfusion need, especially on haemodialysis. All patients on an ESA need iron.
- **ESA resistance:** look for iron deficiency, occult blood loss, chronic infection or inflammation, B12 or folate deficiency, and **marrow fibrosis from secondary hyperparathyroidism**.

> **Check:** the slide's "< 5% for age" is the 5th centile. Avoid transfusion where possible in potential transplant candidates, because of HLA sensitisation.

### 7.7 Hypertension and proteinuria

- Causes: **volume overload** and/or excess **renin** from glomerular disease.
- Hypertension and proteinuria each independently speed CKD progression.
- **ESCAPE trial:** 24-hour MAP **below the 50th centile** by ABPM gave a **35% lower risk** of the composite outcome (doubling of creatinine, eGFR < 10, dialysis or transplant) compared with the conventional target (MAP 50th to 95th centile); the effect was greater with significant proteinuria.
- **Non-drug:** sodium restriction **< 2 g/24 h**, healthy weight.
- **Drugs:** start when BP is **> 90th centile** for age, sex and height; titrate to **< 50th centile**, especially with proteinuria.

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **ACE inhibitors** (enalapril, lisinopril) and **ARBs** (losartan) | **First choice in all paediatric CKD** (slow progression, good BP control). Monitor renal function and electrolytes, especially in advanced CKD |
| **Thiazides** (hydrochlorothiazide, chlorothiazide), **loop diuretics** (furosemide) | For salt and fluid retention. **Thiazides fail when eGFR < 30** |
| **Amlodipine, β-blockers** (propranolol, atenolol), **clonidine** | Add-ons when BP is still uncontrolled |

### 7.8 Immunisation and drug dosing

- Give all **standard vaccines** on the usual schedule, plus **yearly influenza** vaccine.
- **Withhold live vaccines** (measles, mumps, rubella, varicella) in those on immunosuppression (transplant recipients, some with glomerulonephritis). Give live vaccines **before transplantation** if possible.
- Responses to vaccines may be suboptimal.
- **Adjust doses** of renally excreted drugs: lengthen the interval, lower the dose, or both.

### 7.9 Renal replacement therapy

When stage 5 is reached: **dialysis** (peritoneal dialysis is preferred in small children; haemodialysis) as a bridge, and **kidney transplantation** as the treatment of choice. In Kenya, access is limited, so early referral matters.

## 8. Progression and prognosis

- Median GFR loss (CKiD study): **1.5 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr** in non-glomerular CKD versus **4.3** in glomerular CKD.
- **Non-modifiable risk factors:** older age, glomerular cause, severity of CKD, onset of puberty.
- **Modifiable risk factors:** hypertension, **persistent nephrotic-range proteinuria**, anaemia, dyslipidaemia, and **not using ACE inhibitor/ARB**.
- Minimise further loss by promptly treating infections and dehydration, avoiding tobacco, preventing obesity, and **avoiding nephrotoxins** (NSAIDs, street drugs, herbal and homeopathic products).

> **Exam tip:** common exam question: "Why is protein not restricted in a child with CKD?" Because of the risk to growth and development.

## Key points to revise

1. CKD = damage or GFR < 60 for ≥ 3 months; five stages by GFR.
2. Under 5 years: CAKUT; over 5 years: glomerulonephritis.
3. Hyperfiltration, proteinuria, hypertension, high phosphate and lipids drive progression; tubulointerstitial fibrosis is the final path.
4. FGF23 rises first; then low calcitriol, high PTH and renal osteodystrophy; treat with low phosphate diet, binders, vitamin D.
5. Anaemia from low erythropoietin: iron if TSAT ≤ 20% and ferritin ≤ 100, then ESA.
6. Acidosis: keep bicarbonate ≥ 22; hyperkalaemia: restrict potassium, alkali, Kayexalate.
7. ACEi/ARB first line, target BP < 50th centile; no protein restriction; rHuGH if height < −2 SD.
8. Live vaccines before transplant; adjust renally cleared drugs; avoid nephrotoxins.
