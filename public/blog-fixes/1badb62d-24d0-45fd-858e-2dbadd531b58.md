## PART 1: CNS HISTORY & PHYSICAL EXAMINATION---
### Neurological Terminology
- **-opathy** = abnormality; **-itis** = inflammation (at the level named in prefix)
- **Encephalopathy** — brain abnormality; focal/diffuse, metabolic/toxic
- **Encephalitis** — brain inflammation; + meninges = **Meningoencephalitis**
- **Myelopathy/Myelitis** — spinal cord abnormality/inflammation
- **Radiculopathy** — nerve root; **Polyradiculopathy** — many roots
- **Plexopathy** — brachial or lumbar plexus
- **Mononeuropathy** — single nerve; **Polyneuropathy** — multiple
- **Myopathy/Myositis** — muscle abnormality/inflammation
- **Functional** — (1) non-structural e.g. migraine; (2) psychiatrically induced e.g. hysterical conversion

### Neurological
History Framework
- Age, sex, handedness, occupation
- History of present complaint
- Neurological screening questions
- Past medical, drug, family, social history
> Always get history from a **bystander** in loss of consciousness or memory problems. Commonest causes: **neurocardiogenic syncope** and **epilepsy.**

### Presenting Complaint —
Key Points
- Let patient tell their story with **minimal interruption**
- Clarify ambiguous symptoms:
 - "Dizziness" → vertigo (spinning) or light-headedness?
- "Numbness" → weakness or sensory loss?
- "Blurred vision" → diplopia or dim/hazy?
**Time course = pathology clue:**
| Onset | Pathology |
|---|---|
| Sudden/acute | Vascular |
| Subacute | Inflammation, infection, neoplasia |
| Progressive | Neoplasia or degenerative |
| Stepwise/stuttering | Vascular or inflammation |
| Relapsing-remitting | Inflammation (MS) |
**Visual loss — right eye (exam favourite):**
| Time Course | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Sudden, 1 min | Optic nerve compression (meningioma) |
| 10 min onset, lasted 20 min | Optic neuritis |
| Over 4 days, improved over 6 weeks | Amaurosis fugax |
| Progressive over 3 months | Migrainous |
**Left-sided weakness (face/arm/leg):**
| Time Course | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Sudden, lasted 10 min | TIA |
| Over 10 min, persists days | Tumour |
| Over 4 weeks | Subdural haematoma |
| Over 4 months | Stroke |
| Since childhood | Congenital |

### HPI —
Additional Components
- **Precipitating/relieving factors** — spontaneously reported > directly elicited
- **Previous treatments/investigations** — helped or caused side effects?
- **Current functional state** — what can the patient do now?
- **Screening questions** — headaches, fits, faints, blackouts, numbness/tingling/weakness, sphincter disturbance, visual symptoms
- Always end with: *"Is there anything else you wanted to tell me?"*

### CNS
Examination Sequence
- Mental status
- Higher functions
- Cranial nerves
- Motor examination
- Sensory examination
- Cerebellar examination
- Gait

### Mental
State ExaminationAbnormalities reflect: neurological disease (e.g. frontal lobe/dementia), primary psychiatric illness, or psychiatric illness secondary to neurological disease (e.g. depression post-stroke).
**Appearance & Behaviour:**
- Unkempt, self-neglect → depression, dementia, alcohol/drugs
- Slow monotonous speech, downcast facies → **depression**
- Fidgety, poor concentration → **anxiety**
- Disinhibited/aggressive → **frontal lobe disease, Bipolar I**
- Emotional lability → crying/laughing easily
- Lack of concern despite disability → **loss of insight, frontal lobe/conversion disorder**
**Mood:** Euphoric → mania | Blunted/incongruous → schizophrenia | Depressed → depression

**Hallucination** = perception without external stimulus | **Illusion** = misinterpretation of real stimulus (altered consciousness) | **Delusion** = fixed false belief; seen in confusional states and psychosis

### Higher Functions — 7 Tests
- 1. Orientation & Attention** — time, place, person; digit recall (normal = 7 forward, 5 backward)
**2. Memory** — give name + address; recall after 5 min (short-term). Short-term memory loss in alert patient → bilateral temporal lesion, Korsakoff's psychosis
**3. Calculation** — serial 7s; doubling 3s → impaired = diffuse encephalopathy
**4. Abstract Thought** — proverb interpretation; estimates (e.g. weight of elephant). Concrete = encephalopathy; delusional = psychiatric/frontal lobe
**5. Spatial Perception** — draw clock + copy star
- Half clock missing → **right parietal lobe** (visual inattention)
- Cannot draw → **constructional apraxia**
**6. Visual/Body Perception:**

- Cannot recognise faces → **prosopagnosia** (bilateral temporoparietal)
- Ignores one side → **hemi-neglect** (parietal)
- Unaware of own weakness → **anosognosia**
- Cannot identify object in palm → **astereognosis**
- Cannot identify writing on palm → **agraphaesthesia**
**7. Apraxia** — cannot perform purposeful task despite no weakness. Test: "Show me how you comb your hair / drink tea / strike a match." Lesion = **dominant hemisphere**

### Aphasia
- **Wernicke's** — poor comprehension, fluent but meaningless speech, no repetition
- **Broca's** — preserved comprehension, non-fluent speech, no repetition

### Cranial Nerve ExaminationCN nuclei in the brainstem = anatomical markers for lesion level. If CN is affected
- opposite** to a hemiparesis → lesion is at the **level of that CN's nucleus.**
**CN I — Olfactory**
- Test each nostril separately
- Bilateral anosmia → COVID, trauma, common cold (Parkinson's/aging reduce smell)
- Unilateral → blocked nostril or frontal lesion (meningioma)
**CN II — Optic**

- Inspect: ptosis, lid retraction, exophthalmos (dysthyroid), enophthalmos (Horner's)
- **Horner's syndrome** = ptosis + miosis + enophthalmos + anhidrosis
- Acuity: Snellen → newspaper → counting fingers → hand movements → light (increasing severity)
- Visual fields by confrontation; test each eye individually
**Visual field defects:**

- One eye only → optic nerve/retinal
- Constricted (tunnel vision) → glaucoma, chronic papilloedema
- Scotoma → MS, ischaemic/toxic optic neuropathy
- **Bitemporal hemianopia** → optic chiasm (pituitary)
- Homonymous upper quadrantanopia → **temporal lobe**
- Homonymous lower quadrantanopia → **parietal lobe**
- Incongruous homonymous hemianopia → **optic tract**
- Congruous homonymous hemianopia → behind lateral geniculate
- Macular sparing → **occipital cortex**
**CN III, IV, VI — Eye movements**

- CN III palsy: ptosis + mydriasis + eye **down and out**
- Lateral gaze palsy:
 - Frontal/parietal lobe → looks **away** from paralysed side
- Pontine lesion → cannot look toward non-paralysed side + facial weakness
**CN V — Trigeminal**
- Test sensation all 3 divisions; compare sides
- **Corneal reflex** — afferent CN V, efferent CN VII; absent = CN V or VII lesion
- Motor: clench teeth (masseters), open against resistance (pterygoids); jaw deviates **toward** weak side
- **Jaw jerk** — absent/normal = bulbar palsy (LMN); **exaggerated** = pseudobulbar palsy (UMN above pons)
**CN VII — Facial**

- Test: raise brows, close eyes tight, smile, whistle, frown
- **UMN lesion** — forehead spared, contralateral lower face weak (stroke)
- **LMN lesion** — entire ipsilateral face including forehead (Bell's palsy)
**CN VIII — Vestibulocochlear**

- Rub fingers 3–4 inches from each ear; test each separately
**CN IX, X, XII — Mouth exam**

- Open mouth and look for: tongue fasciculations, uvula position, tongue deviation on protrusion
- Gag reflex — afferent IX, efferent X
| Finding | Meaning |

|---|---|
| Saliva pooling | Swallowing difficulty |
| Small tongue + fasciculations | Bilateral LMN — MND, basal meningitis, syringobulbia |
| Tongue deviates to one side | Weakness on that side |
| Uvula moves to one side | Vagus lesion on opposite side |
| Uvula moves on "ahh" but not gag | CN IX palsy |
**CN XI — Accessory**
- Ipsilateral hemisphere → contralateral trapezius + ipsilateral SCM (single UMN = bilateral signs)
- Ask patient to shrug (trapezius) and turn head against resistance (SCM)

### Motor System
- Patterns of weakness:**
- **UMN** — increased tone/reflexes, no wasting, pyramidal pattern (weak extensors arm, weak flexors leg)
- **LMN** — flaccidity, wasting, fasciculations, absent reflexes
- **Myopathy** — wasting, decreased tone, impaired reflexes
- **Neuromuscular junction** — fatigable weakness, normal reflexes
- **Functional** — normal tone/reflexes, no wasting, erratic power
**Tone findings:**

- Flaccidity → LMN or cerebellar; also spinal shock, chorea
- Spasticity → UMN (takes time to develop)
- Cogwheel rigidity → extrapyramidal (Parkinson's, phenothiazines)
**MRC Power Scale:**

- 5 = normal | 4+/4/4− = movement against resistance (submaximal/moderate/slight) | 3 = against gravity only | 2 = gravity eliminated | 1 = flicker | 0 = nothing
**Key nerve roots:** Shoulder abduction C5 | Elbow flexion C5/6 | Wrist extension C6/7 | Hip flexion L2/3 | Knee extension L3/4 | Knee flexion L5/S1

### Reflexes| Reflex | Root |
|---|---|
| Biceps | C5/6 |
| Triceps | C7 |
| Supinator | C6 |
| Knee | L3/4 |
| Ankle | S1/2 |
- Graded 0 (absent) to ++++ (clonus)
- If absent → reinforce with **Jendrassik manoeuvre** (interlock fingers and pull) before striking
- Clonus = UMN sign

### Gait| Pattern | Cause |
|---|---|
| Stooped, festinant, small steps | Parkinson's |
| Wide-based | Cerebellar ataxia |
| High-stepping | Bilateral foot drop |
| Circumduction | Hemiplegic |
| Variable, worse when watched | Functional |
- Cannot walk on heels → foot drop
- Cannot walk on toes → calf weakness
**Romberg's test** — stands eyes open, falls eyes closed = **positive** → proprioceptive loss (peripheral neuropathy, B12 deficiency, cord compression, tabes dorsalis). Unsteady with eyes open AND closed = cerebellar.

## PART 2: ABDOMINAL EXAMINATION---
### Palpation — Technique
- Patient relaxed, breathing quietly; examine painful area **last**
- Wrist and forearm horizontal; gentle firm pressure; slight flexion at MCPJs; no sudden poking
- All upper abdominal organs move **down on inspiration** — keep hand still and let the organ come to you
**Three-step sequence:**

- Light palpation — anti-clockwise through all 9 regions
- Deep palpation — repeat through all 9 regions
- Focused — liver, gallbladder, spleen, kidneys, bladder, aorta, groins
**Key signs:**

- **Guarding** — involuntary muscle spasm over inflamed viscus = localised peritonitis
- **Rigidity** — board-like abdomen = generalised peritonitis
- **Rebound tenderness** — pain on sudden release = peritoneal inflammation (not always reliable)

### Individual Organs
- Liver** — right hand below right costal margin, feel against radial border of index finger
- Soft/smooth/tender → heart failure | Firm/regular → cirrhosis, obstructive jaundice | Hard/irregular/nodular → metastatic carcinoma | Pulsatile → tricuspid regurgitation
- Hepatomegaly measured from right costal margin in mid-clavicular line
**Gallbladder** — not normally palpable; when distended = globular, well-defined, moves with respiration, dull on percussion, not ballotable

- Painless enlarged gallbladder + jaundice → **carcinoma head of pancreas**
- Painless enlarged gallbladder, no jaundice → **mucocele**
**Spleen** — must enlarge 2–3× before palpable; left hand over posterior lower ribs (traction), right hand beneath costal margin pressing downward-medially

**Kidneys** — bimanually palpable and **ballottable** (bounces between hands)
- Right kidney lower pole palpable in thin patients — normal
- Left kidney not usually palpable unless enlarged or low-lying
**Urinary bladder** — only palpable in retention; smooth, oval, suprapubic, dull, cannot feel lower border (arises from pelvis), pressure causes urge to micturate

- In women differentiate from: gravid uterus (mobile side-to-side), fibroid uterus (bosselated), ovarian cyst (eccentric)
**Aorta** — deep palpation above and left of umbilicus; estimate width with both hands

**Para-aortic nodes** — palpable only when very enlarged; firm, fixed masses along left border of aorta in epigastrium/umbilical region

### Characterising an
Abdominal Mass
- **Site, size, shape**
- **Surface/consistency** — hard, irregular, nodular = malignant; smooth, tense, round = cystic; ill-defined, tender = inflammatory
- **Temperature/tenderness** — use dorsum of hand; hot + tender = inflammatory/infective
- **Respiratory movement** — liver, spleen, kidneys, gallbladder move down on inspiration and cannot be moved by hand
- **Mobility** — small bowel/transverse colon tumours and mesenteric cysts are mobile on palpation but unaffected by breathing
- **Fixed masses** → retroperitoneal (pancreas), advanced tumour with spread, severe chronic inflammation (diverticulitis, TB ileocaecal mass)
**Common diagnostic pitfalls:**

- Loaded sigmoid colon → firm, tubular, ~12 cm, left iliac fossa, parallel to inguinal ligament; disappears on repeat exam
- Loaded caecum → soft, rounded, indistinct borders, right iliac fossa
- Transverse colon → tubular, epigastrium, distinct upper and lower borders

### Percussion
- **Liver** — upper border at 5th intercostal space (below right nipple); normal vertical span **12–15 cm**; dullness reduced in emphysema, pneumothorax, post-laparotomy
- **Spleen** — dullness from left lower ribs into left hypochondrium
- **Ascites** — shifting dullness: percuss umbilicus to flank, mark dullness, roll patient to opposite side, dullness shifts with fluid

### Auscultation
- Stethoscope just below and right of umbilicus; wait for sounds
- Normal = intermittent low/medium gurgles with occasional tinkle
- **Exaggerated/tinkling** → early mechanical small bowel obstruction
- **Absent (silent abdomen)** → generalised peritonitis, paralytic ileus
**Vascular bruits** — turbulent flow = stenosis, aneurysm, or malignant circulation:

- Above/left of umbilicus → aorta
- Iliac fossae → iliac arteries
- Epigastrium → coeliac/superior mesenteric arteries
- Lateral mid-abdomen → renal arteries
- Over liver → primary liver cancer (classically)

### Groin Examination
- After abdominal exam; consent, gloves, good lighting
- Ask patient to turn head away and cough — look for expansile impulse in inguinal canals
- If none visible, place both hands over both inguinal canals and ask for loud cough — feel for impulse
- **Femoral pulse** — midpoint between ASIS and symphysis pubis; compare both sides

### Anorectal Examination
- Indications:** incontinence, constipation, GI bleeding, peri-anal symptoms
**Position:** left lateral (Sims'), buttocks over edge of couch, knees drawn up; gloves, lubricant, good light
**Inspection findings:**
- Anal fissure → usually **posterior midline**
- Fistula-in-ano → external opening with pus/granulation tissue near anus
- External haemorrhoids, anal warts (condylomata acuminata)
- Ask patient to bear down — rectal prolapse or perineal descent indicates **pelvic floor weakness** (denervation)

### GI
Investigations to KnowLFTs, ascitic fluid analysis, OGD, colonoscopy, ERCP, PTC, plain abdominal X-ray, abdominal ultrasound, CT abdomen, transient liver elastography (Fibroscan — assesses liver fibrosis)
