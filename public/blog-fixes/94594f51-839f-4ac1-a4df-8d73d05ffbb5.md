# Cold Chain and Immunisation Schedule

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Immunisation and Public Health**

> **In one line:** vaccines are fragile biological products that lose potency irreversibly with heat, freezing or light, so they must be kept at **+2 to +8 °C** from manufacturer to child (the **cold chain**), using the right equipment, monitored by thermometers and **vaccine vial monitors (VVM)**; immunisation is one of the most cost-effective health interventions and is given on a national schedule.

## What you must know

- The **cold chain** is the system of transporting and storing vaccines at the recommended temperature from the point of manufacture to the point of use.
- Vaccine storage temperature is **+2 °C to +8 °C**; a vaccine stored outside this range is no longer a licensed product.
- **Heat-sensitive:** BCG, varicella, MMR, MenC, hepatitis B, DT/aP/IPV/Hib. **Freeze-sensitive:** hepatitis B and combinations, DT/aP/IPV/Hib, influenza, MenC (and the freeze-dried ones are the least cold-sensitive).
- **Freeze-dried vaccines** (BCG, MMR, varicella) are least sensitive to cold but are the most sensitive to heat once reconstituted.
- **VVM** shows cumulative heat exposure; stage 3 or 4 means **discard**.
- Immunisation averts about **2 to 3 million deaths** a year.

## 1. The cold chain

The chain runs from **manufacturer** to **distributor** to **vaccine depots** to **provider (health facility)** to the **client**.

### Why it matters

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Vaccines are biological products** | They lose potency with time; the process is **irreversible** and accelerated by poor storage |
| **Assurance of a potent product** | Professional responsibility (confidence the vaccine will work); public health responsibility; public confidence in programmes |
| **Maximum benefit** | Avoid wasting scarce resources; reduce wastage from errors |
| **Compliance with licence** | A vaccine not stored at 2 to 8 °C as per its licensing conditions is no longer a licensed product |

## 2. Cold chain storage equipment

| Equipment | Level | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Walk-in cold rooms (WIC)** | Regional | Storage up to **3 months** |
| **Deep freezers** | District and primary health care (PHC) | **−15 °C to −25 °C**. At PHC used only to **make ice packs** |
| **Ice-lined refrigerators (ILR)** | District and PHC | **+2 °C to +8 °C**. **Top-opening**, so they hold cold air better than front-opening fridges |

## 3. Vaccine stability

| Sensitivity to **heat** (most to least) | Sensitivity to **cold** (most to least) |
|---|---|
| BCG*, varicella*, MMR*, MenC, hepatitis B, DT and/or aP/IPV/Hib | Hepatitis B and combination vaccines, DT and/or aP/IPV/Hib, influenza, MenC, MMR*, varicella*, BCG* |

\* Freeze-dried (lyophilised) vaccines are **least** sensitive to cold.

- **Record the temperature twice a day** using a dial thermometer.
- **Light-sensitive** vaccines: **BCG, MMR, varicella, meningococcal C conjugate, most DTaP-containing vaccines**. They react to strong light, sunlight, ultraviolet and fluorescent (neon) light. Keep them in their **original packaging** until use.

> **Exam tip:** a vaccine that has frozen (hepatitis B, DPT, tetanus toxoid) must be discarded; use the **shake test** to check suspected freezing. Heat damage cannot be reversed either.

## 4. Vaccine storage: do's and don'ts

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| Store food or medical specimens in the vaccine fridge | Use a **dedicated vaccine fridge** |
| Place the fridge in direct sunlight or near a heat source | **Safeguard the electricity supply** |
| Store vaccines for **more than 1 month at PHC** | Keep the fridge **no more than 50% full** |
| Store vaccines in the **fridge door** or in solid plastic trays/containers | Place vaccines in clearly labelled **plastic mesh baskets** |
| Place vaccines against fridge walls or cold air vents | **Group vaccines by type** (paediatric, adult, adolescent) |
| | **Defrost and calibrate** the fridge regularly |
| | Have **back-up facilities** in case the fridge fails |

## 5. Transport equipment

| Equipment | Use |
|---|---|
| **Cold boxes** | Bulk transport. **Fully frozen ice packs** at the bottom and sides. **DPT, TT and DT must not touch the ice packs directly** (they are freeze-sensitive) |
| **Vaccine carriers** | Small quantities (**16 to 20 vials**) for outreach (out-of-reach) sessions; **4 ice packs** |
| **Day carriers** | Very small quantities (**6 to 8 vials**) for a nearby session; **2 ice packs**; only for a **2-hour** period |

### Diluents

- Specially designed to reconstitute the vaccine for the right **volume, pH and chemistry**.
- Store at **+2 to +8 °C** in the ILR.
- **Use only the diluent supplied and packaged by the manufacturer** for that vaccine.

## 6. Vaccine vial monitor (VVM)

A VVM is a **heat-sensitive label** on the vial that registers cumulative heat exposure. Time and temperature together make the **inner square darken gradually and irreversibly**. It does not measure potency directly, but shows the main factor that affects it.

| Stage | Appearance | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Inner square lighter than outer circle | Use (use earlier stock first) |
| **2** | Inner square still lighter than outer circle | Use soon |
| **3** | Inner square matches the outer circle | **Discard** |
| **4** | Inner square darker than outer circle | **Discard** |

> **Remember:** stage 1 and 2 are usable; stage 3 and 4 are not. Also discard expired vials, vials with a missing label, and (for freeze-dried vaccines) reconstituted vaccine at the end of the session (BCG and measles within 6 hours).

## 7. Immunisation

- **Immunisation** makes a person immune to an infection, typically by giving a vaccine.
- It controls and eliminates life-threatening diseases and averts **2 to 3 million deaths a year**.
- It is among the **most cost-effective** health investments and reaches even the hard-to-reach and vulnerable.
- Active immunisation is one of the **"Ten Great Public Health Achievements of the 20th Century"**.

### WHO objectives in immunisation

1. Accelerate control of vaccine-preventable diseases.
2. Strengthen routine immunisation to meet coverage targets.
3. Introduce new and improved vaccines.
4. Spur research and development for the next generation of vaccines and technologies.

## 8. Kenya Expanded Programme on Immunisation (KEPI) schedule

The slide set ends with the schedule itself. The standard KEPI schedule is:

| Age | Vaccines |
|---|---|
| **Birth** | **BCG**, **oral polio (OPV 0)** |
| **6 weeks** | OPV 1, **Pentavalent 1** (DPT-HepB-Hib), **PCV10** 1, **Rotavirus** 1 |
| **10 weeks** | OPV 2, Pentavalent 2, PCV10 2, Rotavirus 2 |
| **14 weeks** | OPV 3, Pentavalent 3, PCV10 3, **IPV** |
| **6 months** | Vitamin A supplementation (repeated 6-monthly); malaria vaccine (RTS,S) series starts in selected areas |
| **9 months** | **Measles-rubella (MR)** 1; yellow fever in some counties |
| **18 months** | MR 2 |

Adolescent girls: **HPV** vaccine. Women of child-bearing age: **tetanus toxoid**. Check the current MoH schedule, as it is updated periodically.

> **Check:** confirm the exact current schedule against the latest MoH/KEPI booklet before the exam; doses and ages have been revised in recent years.

> **Remember:** live vaccines (BCG, OPV, measles-rubella, yellow fever, rotavirus) should not be given to severely immunocompromised children, but are given to HIV-exposed or HIV-infected children who are not severely immunosuppressed. BCG is given intradermally on the right upper arm.

## Key points to revise

1. Cold chain: manufacturer to depot to facility to child, always at **+2 to +8 °C**.
2. Walk-in cold room (regional, 3 months), deep freezer (−15 to −25 °C, ice packs), ice-lined fridge (+2 to +8 °C, top-opening).
3. Heat-sensitive: BCG, varicella, MMR; freeze-sensitive: hepatitis B, DPT-containing vaccines, influenza; check temperature twice daily.
4. Vaccine fridge: dedicated, half full, not in the door, no food, not over 1 month at PHC.
5. Vaccine carrier 16 to 20 vials (4 ice packs); day carrier 6 to 8 vials (2 ice packs, 2 hours).
6. VVM: stage 1 and 2 use; stage 3 and 4 discard.
7. Use only the manufacturer's diluent, stored at +2 to +8 °C.
8. Immunisation prevents 2 to 3 million deaths yearly and is highly cost-effective.
