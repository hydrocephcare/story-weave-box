# Pleural Effusion

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Respiratory Medicine**

> **In one line:** a pleural effusion is excess fluid in the pleural space, caused by an imbalance of Starling forces (pressure problem, giving a transudate) or by diseased, leaky pleura or blocked lymphatics (giving an exudate). Light's criteria separate the two, pleural fluid analysis points to the cause, and treatment is of the cause, with a chest drain for infected fluid and pleurodesis for recurrent malignant effusions.

## What you must know

- Normal pleural space holds **5 to 15 mL** of fluid, filtered from parietal capillaries and removed by parietal lymphatics.
- Clinical signs: **stony dull percussion**, reduced breath sounds and tactile fremitus, with the trachea pushed **away** in a massive effusion.
- A **diagnostic tap** is indicated for any new unexplained unilateral effusion **over 1 cm** on imaging.
- **Light's criteria:** exudate if **any one** is met (fluid/serum protein over 0.5; fluid/serum LDH over 0.6; fluid LDH over two thirds of the upper limit of normal serum LDH).
- Diuretic-treated heart failure can look falsely exudative: use the **serum-pleural albumin gradient over 1.2 g/dL** to prove a transudate.
- **pH below 7.20** in a parapneumonic effusion, or frank pus, means a **chest drain**.
- In Kenya always think of **TB** (especially with HIV): lymphocyte-rich exudate, **ADA over 40 U/L**.

## 1. Definition and normal physiology

- A pleural effusion is an **abnormal accumulation of fluid in the pleural space** between the visceral (lung) and parietal (chest wall) pleura.
- Normally **5 to 15 mL** of lubricating serous fluid lies between the layers. It is filtered from the **parietal pleural capillaries** and drained by the **parietal lymphatics**.
- An effusion **restricts lung expansion**, which causes the symptoms.

## 2. Mechanisms (Starling forces)

| Mechanism | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Raised **hydrostatic pressure** | Pushes fluid out | **Heart failure** |
| Reduced **oncotic pressure** | Fluid not held in | **Hypoalbuminaemia** |
| Raised **capillary permeability** | Leaky vessels | **Pneumonia** |
| Reduced **lymphatic drainage** | Blocked exit | **Malignancy** |
| Trans-diaphragmatic flow | Fluid from the abdomen | Hepatic hydrothorax, pancreatitis |

## 3. Clinical features

### Symptoms

- **Pleuritic chest pain:** sharp on inspiration (parietal pleural inflammation). Pain often eases as the effusion grows and separates the layers.
- **Dyspnoea** from lung compression.
- Orthopnoea; fever if infective.
- Features of the cause: weight loss (malignancy), night sweats (TB), leg oedema (heart failure).
- **Small effusions (under 300 mL)** may be completely asymptomatic.

### Signs

- **Asymmetric chest expansion** (lagging on the affected side)
- **Stony dull percussion** (highly characteristic)
- **Absent or reduced breath sounds**
- **Reduced tactile vocal fremitus** (fluid absorbs vibration)
- Bronchial breathing or aegophony above the fluid level
- **Massive effusion** pushes the trachea and mediastinum **away**

### Percussion differential

| Condition | Percussion | Fremitus | Breath sounds | Trachea |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pleural effusion** | **Stony dull** | Reduced | Reduced or absent | Away (massive) |
| **Consolidation** | Dull | Increased | **Bronchial** | Central |
| **Pneumothorax** | **Hyper-resonant** | Reduced | Reduced or absent | Away (tension) |
| **Collapse** | Dull | Reduced | Reduced | **Towards** |

## 4. Imaging

### Chest X-ray

![Pleural effusion on chest X-ray](https://cdn.ompathstudy.com/uploads/2026/10/y4-imed-pe-06-96aa24ec.jpg)

- **Blunting of the costophrenic angle**
- **Meniscus sign:** concave upper border, homogeneous opacity
- About **200 to 300 mL** is needed to show on an upright PA film
- **Supine films** may show only a layering haze over the hemithorax

### Ultrasound (bedside gold standard)

- Detects as little as **20 mL**
- Shows **septations** and **loculated** fluid
- **Guides thoracentesis** safely and marks the site

### CT chest

- Finds causes: malignancy, PE, lung abscess
- Shows pleural thickening or nodularity
- Distinguishes **empyema** (split-pleura sign) from lung abscess

## 5. Diagnostic thoracentesis

- **Indication:** any new, unexplained unilateral effusion **over 1 cm** on imaging.
- **Safety rule:** insert the needle **over the top of the lower rib**, to avoid the intercostal vein, artery and nerve that run under each rib.
- **Complications:** pneumothorax, bleeding, infection, re-expansion pulmonary oedema (avoid removing more than 1.5 L at once).
- **Send:** appearance, **protein and LDH** (with paired serum values), **glucose, pH**, cell count and differential, **Gram stain, culture, AFB, GeneXpert**, **cytology**. Optional: ADA, amylase, triglycerides, albumin, haematocrit.

## 6. Transudate versus exudate

| | **Transudate** (systemic problem) | **Exudate** (local problem) |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Clear, straw-coloured | Cloudy, opaque |
| Mechanism | Pressure (hydrostatic or oncotic); **capillaries intact** | **Inflamed, leaky capillaries** or blocked lymphatics |
| Protein | Low (under 3 g/dL) | High |
| LDH | Low | High |
| Cells | Few | Many |

### Light's criteria

An effusion is **exudative if ANY ONE** is met:

1. **Fluid protein / serum protein over 0.5**
2. **Fluid LDH / serum LDH over 0.6**
3. **Fluid LDH over two thirds of the upper limit of normal serum LDH**

> **Exam tip:** all three negative means transudate. Only **one** positive makes it an exudate.

> **Exam tip:** diuretics in heart failure remove water from the pleural space but leave protein behind. A true transudate becomes concentrated and **falsely meets Light's criteria**. If the clinical picture is heart failure, check the **serum minus pleural albumin gradient: over 1.2 g/dL proves a transudate**.

## 7. Causes

### Transudates

- **Congestive heart failure:** commonest cause of **bilateral** effusions (raised hydrostatic pressure)
- **Cirrhosis (hepatic hydrothorax):** ascites crosses diaphragmatic defects; **usually right-sided**
- **Nephrotic syndrome:** urinary protein loss lowers oncotic pressure
- **Severe hypoalbuminaemia**
- Others: hypothyroidism, peritoneal dialysis, **Meigs' syndrome** (ovarian fibroma, ascites and right pleural effusion)

### Exudates

- **Infection:** parapneumonic effusion and empyema; **tuberculosis** (common in Kenya, especially with HIV)
- **Malignancy:** lung, breast, lymphoma, mesothelioma (about **25%** of effusions)
- **Pulmonary embolism:** about **80% exudative**, 20% transudative
- **Autoimmune and inflammatory:** rheumatoid arthritis, SLE, pancreatitis, oesophageal rupture

## 8. Special fluid analysis

### Appearance

| Appearance | Think of | Confirm |
|---|---|---|
| **Bloody** | Malignancy, trauma, PE | **Haemothorax** if fluid haematocrit is **over 50%** of blood haematocrit |
| **Milky** | **Chylothorax** (thoracic duct disruption: lymphoma, trauma) | **Triglycerides over 110 mg/dL** |
| **Purulent** | **Empyema** | Needs **immediate chest drain** |

> **Remember:** **pseudochylothorax** (chronic TB or rheumatoid pleurisy) has high cholesterol and cholesterol crystals, not high triglycerides.

### The pH clue: parapneumonic effusions

| Type | Features | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | Sterile, free-flowing, **pH over 7.20** | Antibiotics |
| **Complicated** | Bacterial invasion, **pH under 7.20**, high LDH, often loculated | **Chest drain** |
| **Empyema** | Frank pus or positive Gram stain | **Chest drain** with or without surgery |

### Low glucose (under 60 mg/dL): mnemonic MEAT

- **M**alignancy
- **E**mpyema
- **A**rthritis (rheumatoid, often under 30 mg/dL)
- **T**uberculosis

### Targeted biomarkers

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ADA over 40 U/L** | **Tuberculosis** (with lymphocyte-rich fluid) |
| **High amylase** | **Pancreatitis** or **oesophageal rupture** (Boerhaave: food particles, low pH) |
| **Triglycerides over 110 mg/dL** | True **chylothorax** |
| Lymphocytes over 50% | TB, malignancy, lymphoma |
| Positive cytology | Malignancy (repeat once if negative, then pleural biopsy) |

## 9. Management: escalating steps

1. **Treat the cause:** diuretics and salt restriction for heart failure; antibiotics for pneumonia; anti-TB therapy for TB. Small transudates often reabsorb. Therapeutic aspiration relieves breathlessness in symptomatic effusions.
2. **Tube thoracostomy (chest drain):** **mandatory** for **empyema**, **complicated parapneumonic effusion (pH under 7.20)** and significant **haemothorax**. For loculated empyema use **intrapleural tPA plus DNase**, or **VATS**.
3. **Pleurodesis:** for **recurrent malignant effusions**, by inflaming the pleura (for example **talc**) so the layers fuse. If the lung is **trapped** (cannot re-expand), use an **indwelling pleural catheter** instead.

## 10. Key numbers

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Over 0.5 | Light's protein ratio: exudate |
| Over 0.6 | Light's LDH ratio: exudate |
| Over 1.2 g/dL | Albumin gradient: proves transudate |
| Under 7.20 | pH needing chest drain |
| Under 60 mg/dL | Low glucose: MEAT |
| Over 110 mg/dL | Triglycerides: chylothorax |
| Over 40 U/L | ADA: tuberculosis |
| Over 50% | Fluid-to-blood haematocrit ratio: haemothorax |

## Key points to revise

1. Normal pleural fluid is 5 to 15 mL; effusion arises from Starling force imbalance, leaky pleura or lymphatic block.
2. Stony dull percussion, absent breath sounds and reduced fremitus; trachea away in massive effusion.
3. Tap any unilateral effusion over 1 cm; go over the top of the rib.
4. Exudate if any one Light's criterion is positive; use the albumin gradient (over 1.2 g/dL) in diuresed heart failure.
5. Heart failure is the commonest transudate; infection, malignancy and TB lead the exudates.
6. Low glucose: MEAT. ADA over 40 means TB. Triglycerides over 110 mean chylothorax.
7. pH under 7.20 or pus means drain; recurrent malignant effusion means pleurodesis or indwelling catheter.
8. Always ask: is this a systemic pressure problem (transudate) or diseased pleura (exudate)?
