# Acute Kidney Injury (Acute Renal Failure)

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Nephrology**

> **In one line:** acute kidney injury (AKI) is a rapid fall in glomerular filtration rate over hours to weeks, causing retention of nitrogenous waste and disturbance of fluid, electrolyte and acid-base balance. Causes are grouped as **pre-renal** (poor perfusion), **intrinsic renal** (tubules, glomeruli, interstitium, vessels) and **post-renal** (obstruction); most cases are reversible if the cause is found and corrected early.

## What you must know

- AKI is a **syndrome**: rapid decline in GFR (hours to weeks), **retention of nitrogenous waste**, and disturbed extracellular fluid volume, electrolytes and acid-base balance.
- About **7% of hospitalised patients** and **20% of acutely ill patients** develop AKI. It is usually **multi-causal (about 60%)**.
- **KDIGO definition:** rise in serum creatinine of **at least 0.3 mg/dL (26.4 µmol/L) within 48 hours**, or **at least 1.5 times baseline** within the previous 7 days.
- Causes: **pre-renal (40 to 80%)**, intrinsic renal (commonly **acute tubular necrosis, ATN**), **post-renal (under 5%)**.
- **NSAIDs and ACE inhibitors/ARBs** are classic drug precipitants, especially when the patient is volume-depleted.
- Life-threatening problems to treat first: **pulmonary oedema, hyperkalaemia, acidosis**.
- Indications for **renal replacement therapy (RRT)**: uraemic encephalopathy, intractable fluid overload, hyperkalaemia/acidosis, urea above 36 mmol/L, significant bleeding, uraemic pericarditis.
- Mortality is **50 to 70%** when AKI complicates sepsis and multi-organ failure; even a minor acute fall in function worsens prognosis.

## 1. Definition and overview

AKI (formerly acute renal failure, ARF) is an abrupt loss of excretory kidney function.

- Rapid decline in GFR over **hours to weeks**.
- Retention of nitrogenous waste (urea, creatinine).
- Disturbance of **extracellular fluid volume, electrolytes and acid-base balance**.

Features of AKI worth remembering:

- **Potentially reversible**, and most AKI is reversible.
- **Multi-causal** (about 60%), and a major cause of in-hospital morbidity and mortality.
- Mortality is about **50% in clinically apparent AKI**.
- **Renal size is preserved on ultrasound** (kidneys are not small, unlike chronic kidney disease).
- The patient often **looks too ill for the level of creatinine and urea**.
- Even a **minor acute reduction in function carries an adverse prognosis**.

## 2. Epidemiology

- About **7% of all hospitalised patients** and **20% of acutely ill patients** show signs of AKI.
- In **uncomplicated AKI** (for example from haemorrhage or drugs) mortality is low, even when RRT is needed.
- In AKI with **serious infection and multiple organ failure**, mortality is **50 to 70%**.
- Drugs commonly linked to renal dysfunction: **ACE inhibitors and NSAIDs**.
- Causes differ by setting: **developing vs developed countries**, **community-acquired vs hospital-acquired**, and **paediatric vs adult** patients. In Kenya, think of diarrhoeal dehydration, sepsis, malaria (including falciparum and haemoglobinuria), rhabdomyolysis, herbal and traditional medicines, nephrotoxic drugs, obstetric haemorrhage and sepsis, and schistosomal bladder disease.

## 3. Definition and staging systems

### RIFLE (2004)

- Proposed in **2004 by the Acute Dialysis Quality Initiative** workgroup.
- A **multilevel classification** that gives a graded definition of AKI built into its staging criteria.
- Grades are **R**isk, **I**njury, **F**ailure, **L**oss and **E**nd-stage kidney disease. It uses serum creatinine/GFR change and urine output.

### AKIN and KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes)

- Provide **both diagnostic and staging criteria**.
- **KDIGO defines AKI** as any of:
  - A rise in serum creatinine of **at least 0.3 mg/dL (at least 26.4 µmol/L) within 48 hours**, or
  - A rise to **at least 1.5 times baseline**, known or presumed to have occurred within the prior **7 days**.
  - (KDIGO also includes urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6 hours.)

| System | Key features |
|---|---|
| **RIFLE** | Risk, Injury, Failure, Loss, End-stage; creatinine/GFR and urine output |
| **AKIN** | Refined RIFLE; stages 1 to 3; small creatinine rise within 48 hours |
| **KDIGO** | Unifies both; diagnosis and staging (stages 1 to 3) |

> **Exam tip:** the KDIGO creatinine criteria have two time windows: **0.3 mg/dL in 48 hours** and **1.5 times baseline in 7 days**. Know both.

## 4. Classification by site: causes of AKI

AKI is divided by where the problem lies:

1. **Pre-renal** (renal hypoperfusion): pre-renal azotaemia, which can progress to ATN.
2. **Intrinsic renal** (renal parenchymal disease).
3. **Post-renal** (urinary tract obstruction).

### 4.1 Pre-renal AKI

- Decreased renal perfusion accounts for **40 to 80% of cases**.
- Kidney tissue is intact at first; function recovers if perfusion is restored. Prolonged hypoperfusion leads to **ischaemic ATN**.

| Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Reduced intravascular volume** | Gastrointestinal loss (vomiting, diarrhoea), renal loss (diuretics, osmotic diuresis), skin loss (burns, sweating), sequestration |
| **Reduced cardiac output** | Heart failure, myocardial infarction, arrhythmia, tamponade |
| **Vasodilatation** | Sepsis, anaphylaxis, drugs |
| **Third spacing** | Pancreatitis, hypoalbuminaemia, nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis |

**Drug effects on glomerular haemodynamics:**

- **NSAIDs** block prostaglandin production, causing severe vasoconstriction of the **afferent arteriole** and AKI.
- **ACE inhibitors and ARBs** block angiotensin action, lowering **efferent arteriolar tone** and so reducing glomerular capillary perfusion pressure.
- Patients on NSAIDs and/or ACE inhibitors/ARBs are most susceptible to haemodynamically mediated AKI when blood volume is reduced for any reason.
- **Bilateral renal artery stenosis** (or stenosis in a solitary kidney) depends on efferent arteriolar vasoconstriction to maintain filtration pressure. These patients suffer a precipitous fall in GFR when given ACE inhibitors or ARBs.

> **Remember:** "Triple whammy" = NSAID + ACE inhibitor/ARB + diuretic in a volume-depleted patient. A rise in creatinine of more than 30% after starting an ACE inhibitor should make you think of renal artery stenosis.

### 4.2 Intrinsic renal AKI

Processes involve the:

| Structure | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Tubules** | **ATN**, ischaemic or nephrotoxic (aminoglycosides, contrast, amphotericin, cisplatin, myoglobin, haemoglobin) |
| **Glomeruli** | Glomerulonephritis, vasculitides |
| **Microvasculature** | Thrombotic microangiopathy, HUS/TTP, DIC, malignant hypertension |
| **Interstitium** | Acute interstitial nephritis (drugs, infection) |
| **Large renal vessels** | Renal artery thrombosis/embolism, renal vein thrombosis |
| **Intratubular deposition and obstruction** | Crystals, myeloma light chains, uric acid (tumour lysis) |
| **Allograft** | Renal allograft rejection |

### 4.3 Post-renal AKI

- Urinary tract obstruction accounts for **under 5%** of AKI.
- Obstruction is usually diagnosed by **ureteral and renal pelvic dilation (hydronephrosis) on renal ultrasound**.
- For AKI to result from obstruction, it must be **bilateral** (or in a solitary functioning kidney).

| Level | Causes |
|---|---|
| **Intra-renal / ureteric** | Intra-renal calcification, stones, retroperitoneal fibrosis, radiotherapy (DXT) effects |
| **Bladder** | **Bladder tumours, schistosomiasis**, outlet obstruction |
| **Prostate** | Benign prostatic hypertrophy, cancer |
| **Neurogenic bladder** | Diabetes, spinal cord disease |
| **Urethra** | Stricture, posterior urethral valves (PUV, in boys) |

## 5. Phases of AKI (especially ATN)

1. **Initiation**: the insult occurs; function begins to fall.
2. **Oligo-anuria (maintenance)**: low urine output, rising urea/creatinine and potassium.
3. **Polyuria (diuretic/recovery phase)**: recovering tubules cannot yet concentrate urine, so large volumes are passed; watch for dehydration, hypokalaemia and hyponatraemia.
4. **Restitution (recovery)**: tubular function returns over weeks to months.

## 6. Clinical features

Symptoms relate to the **primary condition**, **excretory failure** and **multisystem effects**.

| Retained | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Water** | Oedema |
| **Sodium** | Oedema, hypertension |
| **Potassium** | Hyperkalaemia (arrhythmia, weakness) |
| **Nitrogenous products** | Uraemic symptoms (nausea, vomiting, drowsiness, pericarditis, bleeding) |
| **Acids** | Hyperventilation (Kussmaul breathing) |

- **Oliguria:** 24-hour urine output below **400 mL**.
- **Anuria:** complete absence of urine formation (think of complete obstruction, bilateral cortical necrosis, or severe vascular catastrophe).
- A non-oliguric form also occurs and has a better prognosis.

## 7. Evaluation and investigations

| Group | Tests |
|---|---|
| **Blood** | Urea, electrolytes, creatinine (U/E/Cr), calcium, phosphate, uric acid; **cystatin C**; FBC; blood gas |
| **Urinalysis** | Dipstick and microscopy (casts, blood, protein), urine sodium/osmolality |
| **Imaging** | **Ultrasound of kidneys, ureters and bladder (KUB)**; chest X-ray |
| **Others** | As appropriate for the primary diagnosis and complications (cultures, creatine kinase, serology, biopsy if intrinsic disease is unclear) |

**Interpretation points:**

- Renal ultrasound shows **normal or enlarged kidneys** in AKI and **small kidneys** in chronic disease (exceptions: diabetic nephropathy, amyloid, polycystic kidneys).
- Hydronephrosis points to obstruction.
- **Muddy brown granular casts** suggest ATN; red cell casts suggest glomerulonephritis; white cell casts suggest interstitial nephritis or pyelonephritis.
- Pre-renal AKI: concentrated urine, low urine sodium (below 20 mmol/L), high urine osmolality. ATN: urine sodium high and urine isosmotic.

### Estimating kidney function

Formulas estimate function from plasma creatinine (PCr). They are **unreliable in AKI** because creatinine is not at steady state.

- **Cockcroft-Gault:** CrCl (mL/min) = [(140 − age in years) × weight (kg) × 0.85 if female] / (72 × PCr in mg/dL)
- **Four-variable MDRD:** eGFR (mL/min per 1.73 m²) = 186.3 × PCr^(−1.154) × age^(−0.203) × 0.742 (if female) × 1.21 (if black)
- **CKD-EPI:** eGFR = 141 × min(PCr/k, 1)^a × max(PCr/k, 1)^(−1.209) × 0.993^age × 1.018 (if female) × 1.159 (if black)

> **Check:** the Cockcroft-Gault bracket on the slide is mis-typed; the correct form is (140 − age) multiplied by weight, as above.

## 8. Management

### 8.1 Conservative management (principles)

1. **Withdraw or withhold nephrotoxins** and **adjust drug doses** to renal function.
2. **Deal with emergencies:** pulmonary oedema, hyperkalaemia, acidosis.
3. **Manage the primary condition** (sepsis, obstruction, haemorrhage).
4. **Establish euvolaemia.** A diuretic challenge may be tried once volume is corrected.
5. **Restrictions:**
   - **Fluid:** according to fluid balance charts, haemodynamic monitoring, and an estimate of insensible losses ("guess-estimate"): previous 24-hour output plus insensible loss.
   - **Sodium** and **potassium** restriction.
   - **Protein:** keto-acids or essential amino acids may be used.
6. **Identify and treat:** hyponatraemia, metabolic acidosis, hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia, hypermagnesaemia, hyperuricaemia.
7. **Dietary considerations:** patients with kidney failure are a **high nutritional-risk group**. AKI patients need **energy 30 to 40 kcal/kg** and **protein 0.8 to 1 g/kg ideal body weight**, increasing as GFR improves. Also vitamins and calcium.
8. **Care for infections:** prevention, early diagnosis, effective management.
9. **Maintain tissue oxygenation.**
10. **Prophylaxis:** GI bleeding (PPI or H2 antagonists); consider DVT prophylaxis.

> **Remember:** the emergency order in AKI is **fluid overload and pulmonary oedema, hyperkalaemia (ECG changes: give IV calcium gluconate, insulin-dextrose, salbutamol), then acidosis**.

### 8.2 Indications for renal replacement therapy (RRT)

- Symptoms of uraemia, especially **encephalopathy**.
- **Intractable volume overload**.
- **Hyperkalaemia / acidosis** not controlled medically.
- **Azotaemia: urea above 36 mmol/L**.
- **Significant bleeding** (uraemic platelet dysfunction).
- **Uraemic pericarditis**.

(Memory aid: **AEIOU**: Acidosis, Electrolytes (K+), Intoxication, Overload, Uraemia.)

### 8.3 Other issues

- **N-acetylcysteine (NAC):** reported to protect the kidney from contrast media, ischaemia and toxins; evidence is limited.
- **Dopamine:** "renoprotective" claims are **not supported**; it is not recommended.
- **Oliguric vs non-oliguric AKI:** non-oliguric AKI carries a better prognosis.
- **Sodium bicarbonate:** used for severe metabolic acidosis and sometimes for contrast prophylaxis.

## 9. Outcomes and prognosis

| Outcome | Rate |
|---|---|
| Mortality | 40 to 50% |
| **Full reversal** | 65 to 75% |
| **Partial recovery** (stable CKD) | 25 to 35% |
| **Dialysis-dependent** | 5 to 10% |

AKI is a **risk factor for CKD**.

**Poor reversal potential and worse outcome with:**

- **Oliguria** (non-recovery and need for dialysis)
- **Advancing age (over 70 years)**
- **Background CKD**
- Associated serious medical conditions
- **Multi-organ failure**

**Increased mortality with:** age over 70, previous chronic illness, infection, congestive heart failure, ICU/CCU admission, multi-organ failure, need for RRT.

**Mortality in one series:**

| Group | Mortality |
|---|---|
| No AKI | 4.6% |
| AKI (primary) | 15.2% |
| AKI (secondary) | 32.6% |
| Dialysis | 32.9% |
| 90 days post-admission: no AKI | 13.1% |
| 90 days post-admission: with AKI | 30 to 49% |

## Key points to revise

1. AKI = rapid fall in GFR with retention of nitrogenous waste and disturbed fluid, electrolyte and acid-base balance.
2. KDIGO: creatinine up 0.3 mg/dL in 48 hours or at least 1.5 times baseline in 7 days.
3. Pre-renal causes make up 40 to 80%; post-renal under 5%; intrinsic causes include ATN, GN, AIN and vascular disease.
4. NSAIDs and ACE inhibitors/ARBs precipitate AKI in volume depletion and in bilateral renal artery stenosis.
5. Phases: initiation, oligo-anuria, polyuria, restitution. Oliguria is urine below 400 mL/24 hours.
6. Treat pulmonary oedema, hyperkalaemia and acidosis first; stop nephrotoxins; restore euvolaemia; treat the cause.
7. RRT indications: encephalopathy, intractable overload, hyperkalaemia/acidosis, urea above 36 mmol/L, bleeding, pericarditis.
8. Outcome: 65 to 75% full recovery, 5 to 10% dialysis-dependent; mortality highest with sepsis and multi-organ failure.
