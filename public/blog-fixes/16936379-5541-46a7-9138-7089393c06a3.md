## SECTION A: SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS

### 1. Differentiate between essential and non-essential amino acids (4 Marks)

**Answer:**

**Essential Amino Acids** are amino acids that the body cannot synthesize on its own or produces in insufficient amounts. Therefore, they **must be obtained through diet**. Examples include:
*   **Leucine**
*   **Isoleucine**
*   **Valine**
*   **Lysine**

**Non-Essential Amino Acids** are amino acids that the body can synthesize in sufficient amounts, meaning they do not necessarily need to be supplied by the diet. Examples include:
*   **Alanine**
*   **Glutamine**
*   **Glycine**
*   **Tyrosine**

### 2. Explain the importance of dietary fiber (5 Marks)

**Answer:**

**Dietary fiber** is crucial for maintaining optimal health for several reasons:

*   **Digestive Health**: Fiber promotes **regular bowel movements** and prevents **constipation** by adding bulk to stool and facilitating its passage through the digestive tract.
*   **Weight Management**: High-fiber foods increase **satiety** and help control appetite, leading to reduced calorie intake and better weight management.
*   **Blood Sugar Control**: **Soluble fiber** slows down the absorption of sugar, helping to regulate **blood glucose levels** and reduce the risk of type 2 diabetes.
*   **Cardiovascular Health**: Fiber, particularly **soluble fiber**, helps lower **blood cholesterol levels** by binding to cholesterol particles and removing them from the body, reducing the risk of heart disease.
*   **Colon Health**: Fiber promotes healthy gut bacteria and may reduce the risk of **colorectal cancer** and other gastrointestinal disorders.

### 3. Differentiate between major minerals and trace minerals (6 Marks)

**Answer:**

**Major Minerals** (also called **macrominerals**):
*   Required by the body in amounts **greater than 100 mg per day**.
*   Present in the body in **larger quantities** (more than 5 grams).
*   Examples include: **Calcium**, **Phosphorus**, **Magnesium**, **Sodium**, **Potassium**, **Chloride**, and **Sulfur**.
*   Functions: Building bones and teeth, maintaining fluid balance, nerve transmission, and muscle contraction.

**Trace Minerals** (also called **microminerals**):
*   Required by the body in amounts **less than 100 mg per day**.
*   Present in the body in **very small quantities** (less than 5 grams).
*   Examples include: **Iron**, **Zinc**, **Iodine**, **Selenium**, **Copper**, **Manganese**, **Fluoride**, and **Chromium**.
*   Functions: Enzyme activation, hormone production, immune function, and antioxidant activities.

### 4. Suggest five benefits of breastfeeding to infants (5 Marks)

**Answer:**

*   **Optimal Nutrition**: **Breast milk** provides the perfect balance of nutrients specifically designed for infant growth and development, including proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals in ideal proportions.
*   **Immune Protection**: Breast milk contains **antibodies**, white blood cells, and antimicrobial factors that protect infants from infections, including respiratory infections, ear infections, and diarrheal diseases.
*   **Digestive Health**: Breast milk is easily digestible and promotes the development of **healthy gut bacteria**, reducing the risk of gastrointestinal problems like colic and constipation.
*   **Cognitive Development**: Breastfeeding has been associated with improved **cognitive development** and higher IQ scores due to the presence of essential fatty acids like **DHA**.
*   **Reduced Risk of Chronic Diseases**: Breastfed infants have lower rates of **obesity**, type 1 and 2 diabetes, asthma, allergies, and **sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS)**.

### 5. Distinguish between soluble and insoluble fiber with examples of sources (5 Marks)

**Answer:**

**Soluble Fiber:**
*   **Dissolves in water** to form a gel-like substance.
*   Helps lower **blood cholesterol** and **glucose levels**.
*   Slows digestion and promotes satiety.
*   Sources: **Oats**, **barley**, **beans**, **lentils**, **apples**, **citrus fruits**, **carrots**, **psyllium**.

**Insoluble Fiber:**
*   **Does not dissolve in water**.
*   Adds bulk to stool and helps food pass through the digestive system.
*   Prevents **constipation** and promotes regular bowel movements.
*   Sources: **Whole wheat products**, **wheat bran**, **brown rice**, vegetables (**cauliflower**, **green beans**), **nuts**, **seeds**, **potato skins**.

### 6. Explain the limitations of early introduction of complementary feeds to infants (5 Marks)

**Answer:**

*   **Immature Digestive System**: Infants under 6 months have an immature digestive system that cannot properly digest complex foods, leading to **gastrointestinal discomfort**, diarrhea, or malabsorption.
*   **Increased Risk of Infections**: Early introduction of complementary foods may expose infants to contaminated food and water, increasing the risk of **infections** and diarrheal diseases.
*   **Nutritional Displacement**: Complementary foods may replace breast milk, which is nutritionally superior, leading to **inadequate nutrient intake** and potential malnutrition.
*   **Allergic Reactions**: Early introduction increases the risk of developing **food allergies**, particularly to common allergens like eggs, peanuts, and wheat.
*   **Choking Hazard**: Infants may not have developed adequate oral motor skills for chewing and swallowing solid foods, increasing the risk of **choking**.

## SECTION B: ESSAY QUESTIONS

### 7a. Examine five principles of diet planning (10 Marks)

**Answer:**

1.  **Adequacy**: An **adequate diet** provides sufficient energy and all essential nutrients in amounts necessary to prevent deficiencies and maintain health. This is achieved by consuming a variety of nutrient-rich foods from all food groups. An adequate diet meets the **Recommended Dietary Allowances (RDAs)** or **Adequate Intakes (AIs)** for all nutrients.
2.  **Balance**: A **balanced diet** supplies appropriate amounts of nutrients from each food group in the correct proportions. Balance is achieved by consuming foods from all food groups (grains, proteins, fruits, vegetables, dairy) without overemphasizing one group at the expense of others. This ensures that no single nutrient is consumed in excess while others are lacking.
3.  **Calorie Control**: This principle ensures that **energy intake matches energy expenditure** to maintain a healthy body weight. Calorie intake should be appropriate for age, sex, body size, and physical activity level. Consuming more calories than needed leads to weight gain, while consuming too few results in weight loss and potential nutrient deficiencies.
4.  **Moderation**: **Moderation** involves avoiding excessive consumption of any food or nutrient, particularly those that may be harmful in large amounts. This principle is especially important for foods high in saturated fats, added sugars, sodium, and alcohol. It also applies to supplements of vitamins and minerals to prevent toxicity.
5.  **Nutrient Density**: This principle emphasizes choosing foods that provide substantial amounts of nutrients relative to their calorie content. **Nutrient-dense foods** deliver maximum nutrition with minimal calories. For example, choosing fresh fruit over sugary soda, or choosing whole grains over refined grains provides more nutrients per calorie consumed.

### 7b. Explain the ABCD of Nutrition assessment (10 Marks)

**Answer:**

The **ABCD approach** is a comprehensive method for assessing nutritional status:

**A - Anthropometric Measurements**
This involves measuring physical dimensions and body composition:
*   **Weight**: Measured using bathroom scales or specialized scales; indicates overall nutritional status.
*   **Height/Length**: Measured using a stadiometer (adults/children who can stand) or infantometer (infants); used to calculate growth and BMI.
*   **Head Circumference**: Measured in infants to assess brain development, taken from the supraorbital ridge to the occipital bone.
*   **Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC)**: Measured using specialized tape with color indicators (red: severely malnourished, yellow: moderately malnourished, green: normal); used for children and pregnant women.
*   **Waist-Hip Ratio**: Indicates body fat distribution and risk of chronic diseases.
*   **BMI (Body Mass Index)**: Calculated as weight(kg)/height(m)² to classify nutritional status.

**B - Biochemical Tests**
This involves laboratory analysis of body fluids and tissues:
*   **Blood Tests**: Measure levels of nutrients (vitamins, minerals), metabolites (glucose, cholesterol), and proteins (albumin, hemoglobin).
*   **Urine Tests**: Detect nutrient deficiencies or excesses, and metabolic disorders.
*   Examples include: serum calcium, potassium, sodium levels, blood sugar levels, vitamin A levels.
*   These tests provide objective data on internal nutritional status.

**C - Clinical Examination**
This involves a physical examination for signs and symptoms of malnutrition:
*   **Skin**: Dryness, scaling, pallor, edema.
*   **Hair**: Thinness, brittleness, discoloration, easy pluckability.
*   **Eyes**: Pale conjunctiva (anemia), night blindness, Bitot's spots (vitamin A deficiency).
*   **Mouth**: Angular stomatitis, glossitis, dental caries.
*   **Nails**: Spooning (**koilonychia**), pallor.
*   General appearance: Muscle wasting, subcutaneous fat loss, edema.

**D - Dietary Data/History**
This involves the assessment of food intake patterns:
*   **24-Hour Dietary Recall**: The patient recalls all foods and beverages consumed in the previous 24 hours.
*   **Food Frequency Questionnaire**: Assesses how often specific foods are consumed over a period.
*   **Food Diary**: The patient records all food intake for 3-7 days.
*   **Dietary History**: A comprehensive assessment of usual eating patterns, food preferences, restrictions, and cultural practices. This information helps determine nutrient intake adequacy and identify dietary problems.

### 8a. Explain the vicious cycle of illness and malnutrition (10 Marks)

**Answer:**

The **vicious cycle of illness and malnutrition** describes the bidirectional relationship where illness leads to malnutrition, and malnutrition predisposes to illness, creating a continuous, self-reinforcing cycle.

**How Illness Causes Malnutrition:**
Illness can significantly contribute to malnutrition through several mechanisms. It often leads to **reduced food intake** due to loss of appetite, nausea, and difficulty eating or swallowing. Diseases like diarrhea and vomiting cause **increased nutrient losses** and fluids. Furthermore, illness, particularly gastrointestinal diseases, can impair the body's ability to absorb nutrients even when food is consumed, leading to **malabsorption** and nutrient deficiencies. Finally, during illness, the body experiences **increased metabolic demands** for immune response, tissue repair, and fighting infection, yet nutrient intake is often reduced.

**How Malnutrition Causes Illness:**
Conversely, malnutrition severely compromises the body's defenses, making it more susceptible to illness. Malnutrition **weakens the immune system**, reducing the body's ability to fight infections, thus increasing vulnerability to bacterial, viral, and parasitic infections. Inadequate protein and micronutrients also lead to **impaired tissue repair**, slowing wound healing and tissue regeneration, which prolongs recovery from illness or injury. Malnutrition causes muscle wasting and weakness, resulting in **reduced physical capacity**. Overall, malnourished individuals are more prone to developing infections and chronic diseases, with specific nutrient deficiencies leading to specific illnesses (e.g., **vitamin A deficiency** increases the risk of measles severity).

**The Perpetual Cycle:**
Once established, this cycle is self-reinforcing. Malnourished individuals who become ill experience more severe and prolonged illness, leading to further nutritional deterioration. This weakened state makes them vulnerable to additional infections before fully recovering, creating a downward spiral. Breaking this cycle requires simultaneous treatment of both the illness and nutritional rehabilitation, along with prevention strategies like vaccination, improved sanitation, and nutrition education.

### 8b. Describe the feeding problems in infancy (10 Marks)

**Answer:**

Infancy is a critical period for growth and development, and various feeding problems can arise, impacting an infant's health:

1.  **Breastfeeding Problems**:
    *   **Poor Latch**: The infant fails to properly attach to the breast, leading to inadequate milk transfer and nipple pain for the mother.
    *   **Insufficient Milk Supply**: The mother may produce inadequate milk due to stress, poor nutrition, or insufficient breast stimulation.
    *   **Breast Engorgement**: Painful swelling of the breasts makes it difficult for the infant to latch.
    *   **Mastitis**: A breast infection causing pain and reduced milk production.
    *   **Inverted or Flat Nipples**: Can make it challenging for the infant to latch properly.
2.  **Bottle Feeding Problems**:
    *   **Improper Formula Preparation**: Over-dilution leads to malnutrition, while under-dilution causes dehydration and digestive problems.
    *   **Contamination**: Improper sterilization of bottles and nipples can lead to infections.
    *   **Nipple Confusion**: An infant may refuse the breast after being introduced to a bottle.
    *   **Overfeeding**: It is easier to overfeed with bottles, potentially leading to obesity.
3.  **Colic**:
    *   Characterized by excessive crying (more than 3 hours per day) in otherwise healthy infants.
    *   Causes can include an immature digestive system, gas, food allergies, or overfeeding, leading to feeding difficulties and parental stress.
4.  **Reflux (Gastroesophageal Reflux)**:
    *   Frequent spitting up or vomiting after feeding.
    *   Causes discomfort and may lead to inadequate weight gain, with the infant potentially refusing feeds due to pain.
5.  **Food Allergies and Intolerances**:
    *   **Lactose Intolerance**: Inability to digest lactose, causing diarrhea, gas, and abdominal pain.
    *   **Cow's Milk Protein Allergy**: Causes vomiting, diarrhea, skin rashes, and respiratory problems, often requiring specialized formulas or elimination diets.
6.  **Oral-Motor Problems**:
    *   Includes a poor sucking reflex in premature infants or weak muscle tone affecting the ability to suck and swallow.
    *   A persistent **tongue thrust reflex** can interfere with feeding.
7.  **Underfeeding/Overfeeding**:
    *   **Underfeeding** leads to **failure to thrive**, poor growth, and developmental delays.
    *   **Overfeeding** may cause excessive weight gain, vomiting, and digestive discomfort. Both result from misreading infant hunger cues or improper feeding schedules.
8.  **Introduction of Solids**:
    *   Starting too early can cause digestive problems and allergies.
    *   Starting too late may lead to nutrient deficiencies and feeding difficulties.
    *   Improper texture or inappropriate foods pose **choking hazards**.
9.  **Dehydration**:
    *   Can occur rapidly in infants due to their small body size.
    *   Caused by inadequate fluid intake, diarrhea, vomiting, or fever, requiring immediate medical attention.
10. **Parental Factors**:
    *   Lack of knowledge about proper feeding techniques.
    *   Anxiety and stress affecting the feeding relationship.
    *   **Postpartum depression** affecting the mother's ability to respond to infant cues.
    *   Conflicting advice from multiple sources causing confusion.

## Additional Topics

### Functions of Proteins in the Body (5 Marks)

**Answer:**

*   **Structural Component**: Proteins serve as **building blocks** for body tissues, including muscles, skin, hair, nails, and organs. They are essential for growth, maintenance, and repair of body tissues.
*   **Enzymatic Function**: Proteins act as **enzymes** that catalyze various biochemical reactions in the body, including digestion, metabolism, and energy production.
*   **Immune Function**: Proteins form **antibodies** that protect the body from foreign invaders such as bacteria, viruses, and other pathogens, playing a crucial role in immune defense.
*   **Hormonal Function**: Some proteins function as **hormones** that regulate various body processes. For example, **insulin** controls blood glucose levels, and **growth hormone** regulates growth and development.
*   **Energy Source**: When carbohydrates and fats are insufficient, proteins can be broken down and used as an **energy source**, providing 4 calories per gram.

### Importance of Pre-conception Nutrition (6 Marks)

**Answer:**

*   **Prevention of Congenital Abnormalities**: Adequate nutrition, particularly **folic acid**, before conception reduces the risk of **neural tube defects** and other birth defects. Folic acid deficiency can cause serious developmental problems.
*   **Reduced Risk of Infertility**: Proper nutrition improves reproductive health and increases fertility in both men and women, ensuring healthy gametes (sperm and egg) are produced.
*   **Maternal Health**: Good nutritional status before pregnancy ensures the mother enters pregnancy in optimal health, reducing risks of complications such as **anemia**, **gestational diabetes**, and **hypertension**.
*   **Healthy Pregnancy Outcomes**: Pre-conception nutrition reduces the risk of pregnancy complications such as **miscarriage**, **preterm birth**, **low birth weight**, and premature delivery.
*   **Fetal Development**: Adequate nutrient stores before conception ensure the developing fetus has access to essential nutrients during critical early developmental stages, particularly in the first trimester.
*   **Preparation for Pregnancy Demands**: Pre-conception nutrition helps build adequate nutrient reserves to meet the increased demands of pregnancy, ensuring a smooth pregnancy journey.

### Complications During Pregnancy (6 Marks)

**Answer:**

1.  **Hyperemesis Gravidarum**:
    *   Severe nausea and vomiting occurring around 4 weeks of pregnancy, more severe than normal morning sickness.
    *   Complications include: Severe dehydration, electrolyte imbalance, weight loss, ketoacidosis, and nutritional deficiencies.
    *   May require hospitalization for intravenous fluids and medications.
2.  **Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (Preeclampsia)**:
    *   High blood pressure developing after 20 weeks of pregnancy (systolic ≥140 mmHg and/or diastolic ≥90 mmHg), accompanied by proteinuria (protein in urine).
    *   Can lead to serious complications including **eclampsia** (seizures), stroke, organ damage, and placental abruption.
    *   Requires close monitoring and may necessitate early delivery.
3.  **Gestational Diabetes**:
    *   Develops during pregnancy due to hormonal changes affecting insulin function.
    *   Increases the risk of **macrosomia** (large baby), birth complications, and cesarean delivery.
    *   Can lead to type 2 diabetes later in life for the mother.
    *   Requires dietary management and possibly insulin therapy.

### Factors Influencing Nutrient Requirements (5 Marks)

**Answer:**

*   **Age**: Different life stages have varying nutrient needs. Children and adolescents require more nutrients for growth, while elderly people may need adjusted amounts due to decreased absorption and activity.
*   **Physiological State**: **Pregnancy** and **lactation** significantly increase nutrient requirements to support fetal development and milk production.
*   **Health Status**: **Illness** and **disease** increase nutrient requirements for immune function and tissue repair. Chronic diseases may also affect nutrient absorption and utilization.
*   **Sex**: Males typically require more energy and certain nutrients due to larger muscle mass and higher metabolic rates compared to females.
*   **Geographic Location**: People living in areas with food insecurity or specific environmental conditions may have different nutrient requirements or availability.

### Nutritional Deficiency Disorders (3 Marks)

**Answer:**

*   **Vitamin C Deficiency**: Causes **Scurvy**, characterized by bleeding gums, poor wound healing, and weakness.
*   **Iron Deficiency**: Causes **Anemia**, resulting in fatigue, weakness, pale skin, and reduced oxygen-carrying capacity.
*   **Iodine Deficiency**: Causes **Goiter** (thyroid gland enlargement) and can lead to hypothyroidism and developmental problems.
