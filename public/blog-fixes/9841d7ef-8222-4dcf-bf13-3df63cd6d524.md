# Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma)

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Rheumatology**

> **In one line:** systemic sclerosis is an autoimmune connective tissue disease in which **small-vessel damage (vasculopathy), immune activation and fibroblast over-activity** cause obliteration of small vessels and excess collagen deposition (fibrosis) in the skin and internal organs. **Raynaud's phenomenon** is nearly universal, and lung and kidney disease cause most deaths.

## What you must know

- Multisystem autoimmune disease of **small arteries, microvessels and fibroblasts**, giving vascular obliteration and fibrosis of skin and organs. "Systemic sclerosis" is preferred to "scleroderma".
- Female : male **4 : 1** (8 : 1 in the child-bearing years); peak age 35 to 64.
- Two patterns: **limited cutaneous (LCSS, CREST)** with skin changes distal to the elbows and knees, and **diffuse cutaneous (DCSS)** with proximal and truncal skin disease and more organ involvement.
- **Anti-centromere** is typical of limited disease (about 60% of CREST); **anti-Scl-70 (topoisomerase I)** of diffuse disease (about 30%).
- **Lung disease** (interstitial lung disease, pulmonary hypertension) is now the leading cause of death; **scleroderma renal crisis** is the lethal renal complication and is treated with **ACE inhibitors**.
- Avoid high-dose steroids (more than 15 mg prednisolone daily): they raise the risk of renal crisis.

## 1. Definition and types

- **Systemic sclerosis (SSc)** is a multisystem autoimmune disease affecting small arteries, microvessels and fibroblasts, resulting in vascular obliteration and fibrosis of skin and internal organs.
- **Localised scleroderma** affects mainly the skin (and may involve muscle and bone) without internal organ disease, for example morphoea and linear scleroderma.
- **Systemic sclerosis** also involves the gut, heart, lungs and kidneys.

| Feature | Limited cutaneous (LCSS) | Diffuse cutaneous (DCSS) |
|---|---|---|
| Skin thickening | **Distal** to elbows and knees; face and perioral skin (pursing of lips); trunk spared | **Proximal** to elbows and knees; involves trunk |
| Organ involvement | Less; late | More likely and earlier |
| Typical complications | Isolated **pulmonary hypertension**, oesophageal disease, telangiectasia, calcinosis | **Interstitial lung disease** and **renal crisis** |
| Antibody | **Anti-centromere** | **Anti-Scl-70**, anti-RNA polymerase III |
| Syndrome | **CREST** | |

**CREST**: **C**alcinosis, **R**aynaud's, **o**(e)sophageal dysmotility, **S**clerodactyly, **T**elangiectasia.

## 2. Epidemiology

- Peak age 35 to 64, but occurs in children and the very old; onset is younger in women and in diffuse disease.
- Female : male = 4 : 1, rising to 8 : 1 in the child-bearing years.

## 3. Aetiology

- **Unknown**; genetic factors play at least a limited role.
- Environmental exposures:
  - **Silica** (increased risk in men)
  - **Vinyl chloride**
  - Aniline-contaminated rapeseed oil (toxic oil syndrome, Spain)
  - Silicone breast implants: no definite risk identified
- Drugs: **bleomycin, L-tryptophan, pentazocine**.

## 4. Pathogenesis

Three processes drive disease, and it is not clear which is primary:

1. **Severe fibroproliferative vascular lesions** of small arteries and arterioles.
2. **Excessive, often progressive deposition of collagen** and other extracellular matrix in skin and organs.
3. **Altered humoral and cellular immunity.**

General sequence:

- An endogenous or exogenous trigger stimulates antigen-presenting cells, which activate **CD4+ T cells**.
- Cytokines (TGF-beta and others) stimulate growth factors, which drive **fibroblasts to make collagen**.
- Endothelial damage leads to **intimal thickening and luminal narrowing**, then **ischaemia**.
- Ischaemia and reperfusion injury drive platelet aggregation; platelets stick to endothelium and release **PDGF**, which is chemotactic and mitogenic for fibroblasts, so fibrosis and vasculopathy feed each other.

## 5. Clinical features

### 5.1 Raynaud's phenomenon

- Episodic **vasoconstriction** of fingers and toes (also nose tip, ear lobes). Triggers: cold, temperature change, stress, vibration. Worse in winter.
- Typical attack: **pallor, then cyanosis, then erythema** (vasoconstriction, then ischaemia, then reperfusion).
- More common in women.

| Primary (Raynaud's disease) | Secondary (Raynaud's phenomenon in SSc) |
|---|---|
| No underlying cause; exaggerated physiological response to cold | Complication of SSc or another connective tissue disease |
| Positive family history | Onset **after 30 years** |
| No digital necrosis, ulcer or gangrene | More severe, with digital ischaemia and infarction |
| **ANA negative** | ANA positive; abnormal nail-fold capillaries |

### 5.2 Skin

**Early:** perivascular T-cell infiltrate, then skin swelling (puffy fingers) which becomes thickening, starting in the hands and feet.

**Late:**
- Skin firm, thick and tight; fingers affected = **sclerodactyly**; thickening moves proximally.
- Fibroblasts and collagen deposition; hair, sweat glands and wrinkles over the area disappear; loss of transverse creases over the dorsum of the fingers; **fixed flexion deformity** of the fingers.
- Face: smooth, tight, mask-like (**"Mauskopf"** appearance), thin radially furrowed lips, **beak-like nose**, small mouth (microstomia).
- Skin atrophy, slow-healing ulcers over extensor surfaces of PIP joints and elbows.
- **Digital pits** (healed ischaemic ulcers) from prolonged Raynaud's; may become infected.
- **Acro-osteolysis**: resorption of terminal phalanges.
- **Calcinosis** (calcium deposits in skin and soft tissue) and **telangiectasia**.
- Skin disease may regress over years, in reverse order (trunk first, then proximal limbs, then distal).

### 5.3 Musculoskeletal

- Morning stiffness; arthritis in over 50% (swelling, stiffness, pain in hand joints).
- Contractures from skin thickening restrict hand movement.
- **Polymyositis** in overlap or mixed connective tissue disease; **carpal tunnel syndrome** may be present.

### 5.4 Lung (leading cause of death)

Presenting symptom: **exertional dyspnoea**.

**Interstitial lung disease (ILD)**
- Most common in **diffuse** disease with **anti-Scl-70**.
- Inflammatory phase: ground-glass opacities and linear infiltrates in the **lower two-thirds** of the lungs on CT. Late fibrotic phase: **honeycombing**.
- Pulmonary function tests: **restrictive** pattern with low FVC, low residual volume and **low DLCO**.
- Diagnosis by PFTs and **high-resolution CT**; BAL and lung biopsy are often not required.

**Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH)**
- Occurs in **10 to 15%**, typically in limited disease and late.
- Definition (right heart catheterisation): **mean PA pressure above 25 mmHg at rest** or above 30 mmHg with exercise. Echocardiogram estimate: systolic PA pressure above 35 mmHg.
- Pathology: intimal fibrosis and medial hypertrophy of pulmonary arterioles and arteries.

> **Check:** current guidelines define PAH as a mean PA pressure above 20 mmHg (with pulmonary vascular resistance above 2 Wood units); the slide uses the older 25 mmHg cut-off.

### 5.5 Kidney: scleroderma renal crisis

- A main cause of death: **malignant hypertension with rapidly progressive renal failure**.
- Presentation: abrupt severe hypertension, severe headache, blurred vision, chest pain.
- Urine: proteinuria, microscopic haematuria; blood film shows fragmented red cells (microangiopathic haemolysis).
- Followed by rapidly progressive **oliguric** renal failure.
- Differential: **TTP** is commonly misdiagnosed.
- **Creatinine above 3 mg/dL** at presentation means a poor prognosis, with permanent dialysis and high mortality.
- **Prompt ACE inhibitor** therapy to control BP before renal failure improves prognosis.

**Risk factors for renal crisis:** rapidly progressive skin thickening in the first 2 to 3 years, **steroid use (prednisolone above 15 mg)**, **anti-RNA polymerase III antibody**, pericardial effusion.

### 5.6 Gastrointestinal

- Many are asymptomatic, or present with weight loss.
- **Oesophageal dysmotility in up to 90%**: smooth muscle atrophy and fibrosis reduce lower oesophageal sphincter tone and cause distal dilatation. Symptoms: dysphagia, **GORD** with erosive oesophagitis, which may lead to stricture and **Barrett's oesophagus**.
- **Stomach:** early satiety, delayed emptying, occasional outlet obstruction. **Gastric antral vascular ectasia (GAVE, "watermelon stomach")**: diffuse small-vessel vasculopathy causing chronic occult GI bleeding and iron-deficiency anaemia.
- **Small bowel:** bacterial overgrowth causes bloating, pain and **malabsorption** (fat, protein, vitamin B12).
- **Large and small bowel dilatation** from autonomic neuropathy causes **pseudo-obstruction**; recurrent acute abdominal pain should be distinguished from this.
- Investigations: oesophageal manometry, barium swallow (oesophagram), CT.

### 5.7 Cardiac

- Myocardial, pericardial and conduction disease; may also be secondary to renal or pulmonary disease.
- Silent until **heart failure** (mostly right-sided) or arrhythmia occurs.
- **Conduction defects** from fibrosis of the conduction system; pericardial effusion (rarely tamponade); myocarditis with inflammatory myositis.

## 6. Investigations

- **The diagnosis is primarily clinical.**
- **ANA positive in about 70%.**
- **Anti-topoisomerase I (anti-Scl-70)** in about 30% of diffuse SSc; **anti-centromere** in about 60% of CREST.
- Anti-RNA polymerase III: diffuse disease with high renal crisis risk.
- Raised IgG is common; **CRP is usually normal** unless there is severe organ disease or infection.
- Nail-fold capillaroscopy; FBC (anaemia), urea, creatinine, urinalysis, BP monitoring.
- Organ screening: PFTs with DLCO, HRCT chest, echocardiogram, ECG, oesophageal studies.

### ACR classification criteria (older)

**One major or two or more minor criteria.**

| Type | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **Major** | **Proximal scleroderma**: skin changes proximal to the MCP/MTP joints, which may affect the whole limb, face, neck and trunk |
| Minor | **Sclerodactyly**: thickening and tightening limited to the fingers |
| Minor | **Digital pitting scars** or loss of substance of the finger pad (ischaemia) |
| Minor | **Bibasilar pulmonary fibrosis** (reticular or linear densities in the basal lungs on CXR, not due to primary lung disease) |

## 7. Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing points |
|---|---|
| **Scleredema** | No Raynaud's, negative antibodies; seen in insulin-dependent diabetes; proximal thickening of trunk, shoulders and back |
| **Scleromyxoedema** | Thickening on head, neck, arms and trunk; **monoclonal gammopathy** (myeloma, AL amyloid); skin biopsy differentiates |
| Endocrine | Diabetes (diabetic cheiroarthropathy gives sclerodactyly), hypothyroid myxoedema |
| **POEMS** syndrome | Polyneuropathy, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, monoclonal gammopathy, skin changes |
| **Nephrogenic systemic fibrosis** | Chronic kidney disease plus gadolinium MRI contrast; hands and feet may be involved |
| **Eosinophilic fasciitis** | Hands and feet spared, peripheral eosinophilia, peau d'orange skin; skin biopsy diagnoses |
| **Graft-versus-host disease** | Previous bone marrow transplant, no Raynaud's; skin biopsy |

## 8. Management

Treatment depends on the organ involved, whether disease is aggressive or stable, and whether the process is reversible inflammation or fixed vasoconstriction and fibrosis. There is no cure.

### 8.1 Raynaud's and digital ulcers

- Avoid cold; use conventional or heated mittens; stop smoking.
- **Calcium channel blockers (nifedipine, amlodipine)** first line; angiotensin II receptor blockers (valsartan) also vasodilate.
- Topical nitroglycerin patches; **sildenafil** for refractory disease (not in combination with nitrates).
- **Parenteral vasodilators (iloprost, epoprostenol infusion)** for severe disease with impending digital ischaemia.
- Prompt antibiotics for infected ulcers.

### 8.2 Gastrointestinal

- GORD: **proton pump inhibitors** and prokinetics such as **metoclopramide**.
- Delayed gastric emptying and dysmotility: supportive, with promotility agents; treat bacterial overgrowth with rotating antibiotics.

### 8.3 Renal and hypertension

- Aggressive BP control with **ACE inhibitors** (captopril is classic), including in renal crisis.

### 8.4 Pulmonary

| Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|
| ILD with active inflammation | **Mycophenolate, azathioprine or IV cyclophosphamide**; low-dose steroid (about 10 mg daily) if RNA polymerase III negative; **avoid steroids if RNA polymerase III positive** |
| Pulmonary hypertension | Vasodilators: **bosentan** (oral endothelin-1 antagonist), sildenafil, epoprostenol, treprostinil, iloprost; heart-lung transplant if severe |

### 8.5 Myositis and cardiac disease

- Overlap polymyositis or MCTD: methotrexate or azathioprine with low-dose steroids; keep prednisolone at about **10 mg or less** to avoid renal crisis.
- Pericarditis: NSAIDs; drain effusion if tamponade.
- Myocarditis (raised CK-MB and troponin): if coronary disease is excluded and MRI or biopsy confirms, low-dose prednisolone (10 mg/day) and cyclophosphamide; nifedipine may help.

### 8.6 Skin

- Stable disease: no treatment.
- Advancing diffuse skin disease: **methotrexate, mycophenolate**, tocilizumab (trial), **D-penicillamine 125 mg/day**; antifibrotic research includes imatinib.

> **Exam tip:** the one drug class that changes outcome in renal crisis is the ACE inhibitor; the one drug to avoid in high doses is prednisolone.

## 9. Prognosis

- Diffuse disease and early organ involvement carry the worst prognosis. Lung disease (ILD and PAH) is now the leading cause of death, since ACE inhibitors improved the survival of renal crisis.
- Limited disease has a better prognosis, except when isolated pulmonary hypertension develops.

## Key points to revise

1. SSc = vasculopathy + autoimmunity + fibrosis of skin and organs; female : male 4 : 1.
2. Limited (CREST, distal skin, anti-centromere, PAH) versus diffuse (proximal and truncal skin, anti-Scl-70, ILD, renal crisis).
3. Raynaud's (pallor, cyanosis, erythema) is almost universal; secondary type starts after 30 with positive ANA and digital ulcers.
4. Lung disease is the leading cause of death: ILD shows restrictive PFTs with low DLCO and basal fibrosis.
5. Renal crisis presents with malignant hypertension and oliguric renal failure; give ACE inhibitors; avoid prednisolone above 15 mg.
6. GI disease is common: oesophageal dysmotility, GORD, GAVE, bacterial overgrowth, pseudo-obstruction.
7. Treat by organ: CCBs for Raynaud's, PPIs for GORD, bosentan for PAH, mycophenolate/cyclophosphamide for ILD.
8. Differentials: scleredema, scleromyxoedema, eosinophilic fasciitis, nephrogenic systemic fibrosis, GVHD.
