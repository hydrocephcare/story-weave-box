# HIV Global Epidemiology: UNAIDS Fact Sheet 2022

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Paediatric HIV and Epidemiology**

> **In one line:** by the end of 2022 about 39 million people lived with HIV, new infections and deaths had fallen sharply because of antiretroviral therapy (ART), but children, men and key populations still lag behind on testing and treatment, and sub-Saharan Africa (including Kenya) carries the biggest burden. The global target is the **95-95-95** cascade: know status, be on treatment, be virally suppressed.

## What you must know

- **39.0 million** people were living with HIV in 2022: **37.5 million adults** and **1.5 million children (0 to 14 years)**; 53% were women and girls.
- **1.3 million** new infections and **630 000** AIDS-related deaths in 2022; **29.8 million** were on ART.
- New infections are down **59%** since the 1995 peak and **38%** since 2010. AIDS deaths are down **69%** since the 2004 peak and **51%** since 2010.
- **Children lag behind adults:** 57% of children are on ART versus 77% of adults. Paediatric new infections fell 58% since 2010 to 130 000.
- **82%** of pregnant women with HIV received ART to prevent mother-to-child transmission (PMTCT).
- In **sub-Saharan Africa**, women and girls are **63%** of new infections; **4000 adolescent girls and young women** (15 to 24 years) are infected every week globally, 3100 of them in sub-Saharan Africa.
- 95-95-95 in 2022 (all people): **86% know status, 76% on treatment, 71% virally suppressed**.

- Median adult (15 to 49 years) HIV prevalence globally was **0.7%**.

## 2. Trends over time

| | 2000 | 2005 | 2010 | 2021 | 2022 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| People living with HIV | 26.6 million | 28.9 million | 31.5 million | 38.7 million | 39.0 million |
| New infections (all) | 2.8 million | 2.5 million | 2.1 million | 1.4 million | 1.3 million |
| New infections (adults 15+) | 2.3 million | 2.0 million | 1.8 million | 1.3 million | 1.2 million |
| New infections (children 0 to 14) | 530 000 | 480 000 | 310 000 | 140 000 | 130 000 |
| AIDS-related deaths | 1.7 million | 2.0 million | 1.3 million | 660 000 | 630 000 |

- The number living with HIV keeps rising even as new infections fall, because **ART keeps people alive** (deaths fall, so prevalence accumulates).
- **New infections:** 3.2 million at the 1995 peak (2.5 to 4.3 million) to 1.3 million in 2022 (a 59% fall). Since 2010: 2.1 million to 1.3 million (38% fall).
- **Children:** new infections fell 58% since 2010, from 310 000 to 130 000, largely because of PMTCT.
- **AIDS-related deaths:** 2.0 million in 2004 (peak), 1.3 million in 2010, 630 000 in 2022. Since 2010 the fall is 55% in women and girls and 47% in men and boys.

> **Remember:** prevalence rises while incidence and mortality fall. This is the expected result of effective treatment, not a failure of prevention.

## 3. Treatment coverage

- At the end of December 2022, **29.8 million** people were on ART, up from 7.7 million in 2010.
- Overall coverage: **76%** (65 to 89%) of people living with HIV.

| Group | On ART in 2022 |
|---|---|
| Adults 15 years and older | 77% |
| Children 0 to 14 years | 57% |
| Women 15 years and older | 82% |
| Men 15 years and older | 72% |
| Pregnant women (ART for PMTCT) | 82% |

- **Paediatric gap:** children are diagnosed late (many exposed infants are never tested) and have fewer suitable formulations. Early infant diagnosis, family-based testing and paediatric dolutegravir-based regimens close the gap.
- **Men** present late, test less often and are less likely to be on treatment than women.

## 4. New infections and AIDS deaths

- 2022 new infections: 1.3 million overall; women and girls were **46%** of the total.
- Region matters:
  - **Sub-Saharan Africa:** women and girls are **63%** of new infections.
  - **All other regions:** more than **70%** of new infections are in men and boys.
- **Adolescent girls and young women (15 to 24 years):** 4000 new infections every week globally, 3100 of them in sub-Saharan Africa. Drivers include age-disparate partnerships, gender-based violence, lack of schooling and economic dependence.
- Deaths: about 630 000 in 2022, 69% below the 2004 peak.

## 5. Key populations

Median HIV prevalence is higher than the 0.7% adult average in every key population:

| Key population | Median prevalence |
|---|---|
| Transgender persons | 10.3% |
| Gay men and other men who have sex with men | 7.7% |
| People who inject drugs | 5.0% |
| Sex workers | 2.5% |
| People in prisons | 1.4% |

- These groups face stigma, criminalisation and poor access to services, so targeted, rights-based programmes are needed.

## 6. The 95-95-95 targets

The goal is **95%** of people living with HIV know their status, **95%** of those are on ART, and **95%** of those on ART are virally suppressed.

| Group | Know status | On ART (of those who know) | Virally suppressed (of those on ART) |
|---|---|---|---|
| All people living with HIV | 86% | 89% | 93% |
| Children 0 to 14 years | 63% | 91% | 81% |
| Women | 90% | 91% | 93% |
| Men | 83% | 86% | 94% |

- Of **all** people living with HIV: 86% knew their status, **76%** were on treatment and **71%** were virally suppressed.
- The weakest step for children is the **first 95** (knowing status) and the viral suppression step (81%), reflecting late diagnosis and adherence and regimen problems.

> **Exam tip:** the second and third 95s are percentages of the step before, not of all people with HIV. Be ready to state the difference (76% of all on ART versus 89% of those who know their status).

## 8. Financing

- At the end of 2022, **US$ 20.8 billion** (constant 2019 dollars) was available for the AIDS response in low- and middle-income countries; about **60%** came from domestic sources.
- UNAIDS estimates **US$ 29 billion** will be needed by 2025 to be on track to end AIDS as a public health threat.

## 9. Why this matters for paediatrics

- Most childhood HIV is acquired by **mother-to-child transmission** (pregnancy, delivery, breastfeeding). PMTCT with maternal ART, infant prophylaxis and early infant diagnosis (HIV DNA PCR) is the main route to an HIV-free generation.
- Adolescents, especially girls, are the next high-risk group; adolescent-friendly services and adherence support are key.
- In Kenya, national guidelines follow the "test and treat" approach for all ages, with dolutegravir-based first-line regimens.

> **Remember:** every child who is HIV-exposed needs infant prophylaxis and testing at birth-linked schedules (6 weeks onward), and every child who tests positive should start ART immediately.
