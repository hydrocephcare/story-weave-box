# Developmental Milestones from Birth to Five Years

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Growth and Development**

> **In one line:** development proceeds in a predictable order across four domains (gross motor, fine motor and vision, language and hearing, social and emotional); knowing the expected age for each milestone lets you tell normal variation from developmental delay, which needs early assessment.

## What you must know

- Newborns lose up to **10% of birthweight** in the first days and should **regain birthweight by 2 weeks**; they then gain at least **30 g/day**, slowing to about **20 g/day** at 2 to 6 months.
- **Social smile** is the first social milestone (about 6 to 8 weeks); early smiling in the first weeks is involuntary.
- **Head control** by about 3 to 4 months, **sitting unsupported** by about 6 to 8 months, **crawling** around 8 months, **walking** around 12 months.
- **Object permanence** appears in the second half of infancy (slides call it "object consistency") and is firmly established by 18 to 24 months.
- **Separation anxiety** begins at 6 to 12 months and peaks in the second year.
- Language: babbling with many syllables by 6 to 12 months; **2-word sentences** by 18 to 24 months; words per sentence about equal to age in years up to 5.
- Preschool years: gain about **2 kg and 7 cm per year**; all primary teeth by **3 years**; daytime bladder control before bowel control, girls before boys.

## 1. Principles of development

- Development follows a **cephalocaudal** (head to toe) and **proximodistal** (trunk to extremities) sequence.
- **Primitive reflexes** disappear as voluntary control matures; persistence beyond the expected age is abnormal.
- Rate varies between children, so the **range** of normal matters more than a single age. A child who misses a milestone by a wide margin (for example no walking by 18 months, no words by 18 months, no social smile by 3 months) needs formal assessment.
- Premature infants are assessed using **corrected age** until about 2 years.

> **Remember:** assess all four domains at each visit. Delay in one domain only is called isolated delay; delay in two or more domains is **global developmental delay**.

## 2. The first year

### 2.1 Birth to 2 months

**Physical**

- About **10% weight loss** from birthweight, from loss of excess extravascular fluid and low intake in the first days.
- Should **regain or exceed birthweight by 2 weeks**.
- Should gain **at least 30 g/day**.
- **Purposeless hand opening**; the **smile is involuntary** (reflex).
- By 2 months most infants wake **2 to 3 times a night** to feed.

**Cognitive**

- Recognise facial expressions (smiles).
- **Habituate** to the familiar: they respond less to repeated stimuli and attend more when the stimulus changes.
- Can tell apart similar colours, patterns and consonants.

**Emotional**

- Learn **basic trust** when urgent needs are met regularly.
- A consistent, trusted adult sets up **secure attachment**.

### 2.2 2 to 6 months

- **Growth slows** to about **20 g/day**.
- **Early (primitive) reflexes recede.**
- **Head control** is gained; they hold objects voluntarily and release them; they **roll from back to front**.
- Sleep: **14 to 16 hours/day**.
- **Cognitive**: increasing interest in surroundings; explore their own bodies (staring at hands, blowing bubbles, touching ears and face).
- **Emotional and communication**: express distinct emotions with facial expressions, such as anger, joy, fear and disgust.

### 2.3 6 to 12 months

- Growth slows further.
- **Sits unsupported**; **crawls around 8 months**; **walks around 1 year**; teeth start to erupt (lower central incisors first, from about 6 months).
- **Cognitive**: everything goes to the mouth. A major milestone is **object permanence** (objects continue to exist when out of sight).
- **Emotional**: **separation anxiety**; demand for autonomy (wants to hold the spoon); **tantrums** begin.
- **Communication**: range of emotions, responds to tone of voice and facial expression, **babbles with many syllables**.

## 3. The second year

### 3.1 12 to 18 months

- Growth rate slows further; **appetite falls** and baby fat is lost with greater mobility.
- **Exaggerated lumbar lordosis** makes the abdomen protrude (normal).
- Early gait is **wide-based**, knees bent, arms flexed at the elbow, and the whole torso rotates with each stride.
- **Cognitive**: stacks blocks, puts things in containers, imitates parents and older siblings.
- **Language**: by 15 months can point to major body parts and use **4 to 6 words** spontaneously and correctly; also **polysyllabic jargon**.

> **Check:** most references expect 4 to 6 words at about 15 to 18 months and body part pointing by 18 to 24 months. The slide wording ("paint" to body parts) means "point to".

### 3.2 18 to 24 months

- Better balance and agility: **runs, jumps, climbs**.
- **Object permanence firmly established**; better grasp of cause and effect.
- Separation anxiety worsens; tantrums ("terrible twos").
- **Language**: vocabulary expands, **2-word sentences**, follows instructions.

## 4. Preschool years (2 to 5 years)

- Growth: average gain **2 kg and 7 cm per year**.
- The abdomen flattens and the body becomes leaner.
- Sleep: **11 to 13 hours/day**.
- **All 20 primary teeth** erupted by **3 years**.
- Most children **run steadily by 3 years**.
- **Handedness** is established by 3 years (a clear hand preference before 12 months suggests a weakness on the other side, for example hemiplegia).
- **Bladder and bowel control**: **daytime bladder control comes before bowel control; girls before boys**.
- **Bed wetting** is normal up to **4 years in girls and 5 years in boys**; persistent beyond this is nocturnal enuresis.
- **Sentence length** (number of words) matches age: 2 words at 2 years, 3 at 3 years, and so on.

## 5. Quick reference table

| Age | Gross motor | Fine motor and cognition | Language and social |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 months | Reflex movements | Hand opening without purpose | Involuntary smile; recognises faces |
| 2 to 6 months | Head control, rolls front to back, then back to front | Holds and releases objects; explores own body | Distinct emotional expressions |
| 6 to 12 months | Sits unsupported, crawls (8 months), walks (about 12 months) | Mouths everything; object permanence | Babbles; separation anxiety |
| 12 to 18 months | Wide-based gait | Stacks blocks, imitates | 4 to 6 words; jargon |
| 18 to 24 months | Runs, jumps, climbs | Cause and effect | 2-word sentences; follows instructions; tantrums |
| 2 to 5 years | Steady running by 3 | Hand preference by 3 | Sentence length equals age; toilet control |

## 6. When to worry

- No social smile by 2 to 3 months; no head control by 4 to 6 months; not sitting by 9 months; not walking by 18 months.
- No babble by 9 to 12 months, no words by 18 months, no 2-word phrases by 2 years.
- Loss of previously acquired skills (**regression**) is always significant.
- Persistent primitive reflexes, strongly asymmetrical movements, floppiness or stiffness.
- Concerns about hearing or vision should prompt formal screening early.

> **Exam tip:** never interpret milestones without considering gestation (use corrected age), sensory deficits and environment; check head circumference, hearing and vision first in a child with speech delay.

## Key points to revise

1. Birthweight is regained by 2 weeks; weight gain is at least 30 g/day early, then about 20 g/day by 2 to 6 months.
2. Head control and rolling occur at 2 to 6 months; sitting unsupported, crawling (8 months) and walking (12 months) in the second half of the first year.
3. Object permanence and separation anxiety appear at 6 to 12 months and consolidate by 18 to 24 months.
4. Language: babble by 6 to 12 months, 4 to 6 words at about 15 months, 2-word sentences by 2 years.
5. Preschoolers gain 2 kg and 7 cm per year and need 11 to 13 hours of sleep.
6. All primary teeth by 3 years; steady running and handedness by 3 years.
7. Daytime bladder control precedes bowel control; girls achieve control before boys.
8. Regression or marked delay needs formal developmental assessment.
