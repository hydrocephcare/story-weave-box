# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Respiratory Medicine**

> **In one line:** COPD is a preventable and treatable lung disease in which chronic inflammation from inhaled noxious particles (mainly tobacco smoke, and biomass smoke in Africa) causes airflow limitation that is not fully reversible, through small-airway disease (chronic bronchitis) and parenchymal destruction (emphysema). Spirometry confirms it; smoking cessation is the only measure that slows decline in lung function.

## What you must know

- COPD = **chronic bronchitis** and/or **emphysema**, with airflow obstruction that is **not fully reversible** and progressive.
- **Chronic bronchitis:** cough and sputum on most days for at least **3 consecutive months in 2 successive years**.
- **Emphysema:** permanent enlargement of airspaces distal to the terminal bronchioles, with wall destruction and **no obvious fibrosis**.
- Cigarette smoking is the main cause; it is unusual to develop COPD with under **10 pack-years**. In low-income settings, **biomass fuel** smoke is a major cause.
- Diagnosis is by **post-bronchodilator spirometry** (FEV1/FVC below 0.70).
- **Only smoking cessation** slows the FEV1 decline; **long-term oxygen** (over 15 hours a day) is the only other therapy shown to improve survival.
- Inhaled corticosteroids reduce exacerbations but do **not** alter the decline in FEV1.
- Think of **alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency** in young patients with basal emphysema.

## 1. Definition

- A preventable and treatable lung disease with significant **extrapulmonary effects**.
- The pulmonary component is airflow limitation that is not fully reversible, progressive, and linked to an abnormal inflammatory response of the lung to noxious particles or gases.
- Extrapulmonary effects: impaired nutrition, weight loss, skeletal muscle dysfunction (also cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis, depression, anaemia).

## 2. Epidemiology

- Prevalence follows the prevalence of **tobacco smoking**; in low- and middle-income countries, **biomass fuel** use is added.
- About 80 million people worldwide have moderate to severe disease; more than 3 million deaths in 2005 (5% of global deaths); forecast as the 3rd leading cause of death by 2020 (it is now the 3rd).
- The burden is rising fastest in **Asia and Africa** because of increasing tobacco use.

## 3. Aetiology and risk factors

Cigarette smoking is the most significant cause. Risk relates to the **amount and duration** (pack-years; 1 pack-year = 20 cigarettes a day for a year). Not all smokers develop COPD, so individual susceptibility matters.

| Group | Risk factors |
|---|---|
| **Exposures** | Tobacco smoke; **biomass solid fuel fires** (wood, animal dung, crop residues, coal) giving high indoor air pollution; occupational dusts (coal miners, cadmium workers); outdoor and indoor air pollution; cannabis smoking |
| **Lung growth** | Low birth weight (lower maximal lung function in early adult life); childhood infections; maternal smoking |
| **Infections** | Recurrent infection may accelerate FEV1 decline; persistence of adenovirus in lung tissue may alter local inflammation; **HIV** is associated with emphysema; tuberculosis |
| **Host factors** | **Alpha-1 antiproteinase deficiency**; airway hyper-reactivity |
| **Social** | Low socioeconomic status; nutrition (independent role unclear) |

## 4. Pathophysiology (brief)

- Inhaled irritants cause chronic inflammation (neutrophils, macrophages, CD8+ T cells), mucus gland hypertrophy and goblet cell hyperplasia, small-airway narrowing and fibrosis (obstruction), and an **imbalance of proteases and antiproteases** that destroys alveolar walls (emphysema).
- Loss of elastic recoil causes airway collapse on expiration, **air trapping** and **hyperinflation**.
- Loss of alveolar surface reduces gas transfer. Ventilation-perfusion mismatch leads to hypoxia and later hypercapnia.
- Chronic hypoxia causes pulmonary vasoconstriction, pulmonary hypertension and **cor pulmonale**.

## 5. Clinical features

COPD is suspected in anyone **over 40** with chronic cough, sputum or breathlessness.

- **Cough and sputum:** the first symptoms ("smoker's cough").
- **Breathlessness:** usually what brings the patient to medical attention. Graded by the **MRC dyspnoea scale**.
- **Haemoptysis:** may complicate exacerbations but must not be attributed to COPD without thorough investigation (exclude cancer, TB, bronchiectasis).
- Advanced disease: ankle oedema and **morning headaches** suggest **hypercapnia**.
- **Signs are non-specific, correlate poorly with lung function and appear only in advanced disease:** hyperinflated barrel chest, pursed-lip breathing, use of accessory muscles, quiet breath sounds, wheeze, reduced cricosternal distance.
- **Crackles** may accompany infection; **persistent crackles** raise the possibility of **bronchiectasis**.
- Oedema reflects failure of salt and water excretion by the hypoxic, hypercapnic kidney (and cor pulmonale).
- Record **BMI** (prognostic).

### Two classical phenotypes (they overlap in practice)

| | Pink puffer | Blue bloater |
|---|---|---|
| Build | Thin, breathless | Often overweight |
| PaCO2 | Normal until late | Hypercapnia earlier (tolerates it) |
| Features | Predominantly emphysema, hyperinflation | Oedema, **secondary polycythaemia**, cor pulmonale |

## 6. Investigations

- **Spirometry (confirms diagnosis):** post-bronchodilator **FEV1/FVC below 0.70** shows airflow obstruction that is not fully reversible. Severity is graded by post-bronchodilator FEV1.
- **Chest X-ray:** no reliable signs correlating with the severity of obstruction. Essential to find alternative diagnoses (cardiac failure), other smoking complications (**lung cancer**), and **bullae**.
- **Full blood count:** exclude anaemia; document **polycythaemia**.
- **Alpha-1 antitrypsin level** in younger patients, predominantly basal emphysema, or family history.
- **Lung volumes:** hyperinflation (raised TLC, RV). The helium dilution method may **underestimate** volumes in severe COPD with large bullae, so use **body plethysmography**.
- **Gas transfer (DLCO) is low** in emphysema.
- **Exercise tests:** objective exercise tolerance, baseline for response to bronchodilators or rehabilitation, and prognosis.
- **HRCT:** detects, characterises and quantifies emphysema; more sensitive than CXR for bullae.
- Arterial blood gases in severe disease or exacerbations.
- Also ECG and echocardiography for cor pulmonale.

### Spirometric classification (GOLD, post-bronchodilator FEV1)

| GOLD grade | Severity | FEV1 (% predicted) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mild | 80% or more |
| 2 | Moderate | 50 to 79% |
| 3 | Severe | 30 to 49% |
| 4 | Very severe | Below 30% |

(All with FEV1/FVC below 0.70.)

> **Exam tip:** COPD does not show a big bronchodilator reversibility (that favours asthma). Diagnosis is made on **post-bronchodilator** values.

## 7. Differential diagnosis

Asthma, bronchiectasis, heart failure, tuberculosis, lung cancer, obliterative bronchiolitis, and (in young non-smokers) alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency.

## 8. Management

**Aims:** improve breathlessness, reduce the frequency and severity of exacerbations, improve health status, and improve prognosis.

### 8.1 Smoking cessation

- Explain the role of smoking in the development and progression.
- Reducing the number of cigarettes has little effect on the course; **complete cessation** improves lung function and slows FEV1 decline.
- Offer counselling, nicotine replacement, varenicline or bupropion.
- Promote **non-smoking cooking devices** and alternative fuels (key in Kenya for biomass exposure).

### 8.2 Bronchodilators (central to treating breathlessness)

- Inhaled route preferred; choose by patient preference and inhaler technique.

| Class | Short-acting (milder disease) | Long-acting (moderate to severe) |
|---|---|---|
| β2-agonists | Salbutamol, terbutaline | **Salmeterol, formoterol** (LABA) |
| Anticholinergics | **Ipratropium** bromide | **Tiotropium** (LAMA) |

- Breathlessness may improve significantly despite minimal change in FEV1 (less hyperinflation).
- **Oral options** for those who cannot use inhalers: **theophylline** (limited by side effects, unpredictable metabolism and drug interactions), bambuterol (pro-drug of terbutaline). Highly selective phosphodiesterase inhibitors were under development (roflumilast is now available).

### 8.3 Corticosteroids

- **Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS)** reduce frequency and severity of exacerbations. Recommended in severe disease (**FEV1 below 50%**) with **two or more exacerbations a year needing antibiotics or oral steroids**.
- Regular use gives a small FEV1 improvement but **does not alter the natural decline**. Usually given as a fixed **ICS plus LABA** combination.
- **Oral steroids** are useful in exacerbations; **maintenance** oral steroids cause osteoporosis and skeletal muscle weakness and should be avoided.
- An oral steroid trial helps diagnose asthma but does **not** predict response to ICS in COPD.

### 8.4 Pulmonary rehabilitation

- Encourage exercise at all stages; reassure that breathlessness is distressing but not dangerous.
- Multidisciplinary programme: physical training, education and nutritional advice. Improves symptoms, health status and confidence.
- Usually 2 to 3 sessions a week for 6 to 12 weeks, with sustained gains in exercise tolerance.

### 8.5 Oxygen therapy

- **Long-term domiciliary oxygen therapy (LTOT):** best given by an **oxygen concentrator**, for **at least 15 hours a day** (greater benefit over 20 hours). Improves survival in selected patients.
- **Ambulatory oxygen** for patients who desaturate on exercise and show improvement with oxygen.
- Titrate to SpO2 above 90% or PaO2 at least **8 kPa (60 mmHg)**.

> **Remember:** the standard indications for LTOT are PaO2 below 7.3 kPa (55 mmHg), or 7.3 to 8 kPa with polycythaemia, pulmonary hypertension or peripheral oedema, measured when clinically stable. In an acute exacerbation with hypercapnia, use controlled oxygen targeting 88 to 92%.

### 8.6 Surgery

| Procedure | Selection |
|---|---|
| **Bullectomy** | Young patient, large bullae compressing normal lung, minimal airflow limitation, no generalised emphysema |
| **Lung volume reduction surgery (LVRS)** | Resect peripheral emphysematous tissue; predominantly **upper-lobe** emphysema, preserved gas transfer, no pulmonary hypertension |
| **Lung transplantation** | Advanced disease; limited by donor shortage |

Bullectomy and LVRS can be done thoracoscopically; endoscopic volume reduction techniques are under development.

### 8.7 Palliative and supportive care

- **Morphine** preparations for breathlessness in advanced disease; low-dose **benzodiazepines** for anxiety.
- **Annual influenza vaccine** and pneumococcal vaccine.
- Identify and improve obesity, poor nutrition, depression and social isolation.
- Mucolytics (acetylcysteine) or antioxidants occasionally used.

### 8.8 Acute exacerbations (add-on knowledge)

Increased breathlessness, sputum volume or purulence. Give short-acting bronchodilators, a short course of oral prednisolone (about 5 days), antibiotics when sputum is purulent, controlled oxygen, and non-invasive ventilation for hypercapnic acidosis.

## 9. Prognosis

- Variable but usually progressive; prognosis worsens with **age** and falls with **lower post-bronchodilator FEV1**.
- Other poor indicators: weight loss, pulmonary hypertension.
- **BODE index** predicts death: **B**ody mass index, degree of airflow **O**bstruction (FEV1), **D**yspnoea (MRC), **E**xercise capacity (6-minute walk).
- Common modes of death: respiratory failure, cardiac disease, lung cancer.

## Key points to revise

1. COPD = chronic bronchitis (3 months for 2 years) plus emphysema; airflow obstruction not fully reversible.
2. Main causes: tobacco and biomass smoke; consider alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency in the young.
3. Confirm with post-bronchodilator spirometry (FEV1/FVC below 0.70); DLCO is low in emphysema.
4. Pink puffer (emphysema, thin, normal CO2) versus blue bloater (hypercapnia, oedema, polycythaemia).
5. Smoking cessation slows decline; LTOT (over 15 hours a day) improves survival.
6. Treat with SABA/SAMA, then LABA/LAMA; add ICS if FEV1 below 50% with frequent exacerbations.
7. Surgery: bullectomy, LVRS (upper-lobe disease), transplantation.
8. Prognosis is estimated by the BODE index.
