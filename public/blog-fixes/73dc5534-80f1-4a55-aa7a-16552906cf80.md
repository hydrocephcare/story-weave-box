## VECTOR CONTROL METHODS
1. Physical/Mechanical
- Screened windows/doors, mosquito nets, draining pools, clearing long grass, emptying containers
2. Biological
- Toxorhynchites larvae — prey on Anopheles/Culex/Aedes larvae
- Gambusia affinis (fish) — eats larvae
- Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt), Bacillus sphaericus — bacterial larvicides
- Other natural predators
3. Genetic Engineering
- Sterile male technique
- Refractory vectors — gene modification blocks pathogen development → change in target site

**4. Chemical — LD50 classification:** 1–50 = extremely toxic, 50–500 = moderately toxic

| Class | Mechanism | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorinated hydrocarbons | Nerve poison, disrupts Na⁺ channels, lipid-soluble | DDT, Dieldrin, Endrin | Mostly banned — persistent, bioaccumulates |
| Organophosphates (OP) | Blocks cholinesterase | Malathion, Chlorpyrifos, Parathion, Diazinon, Dimethoate | Less persistent than organochlorines |
| Carbamates | Bind acetylcholinesterase (reversible) | Carbaryl, Propoxur, Bendiocarb | Lower toxicity, better than OPs on chronic exposure |
| Pyrethroids | Bind voltage-gated Na⁺ channels, delay inactivation | Permethrin (ITNs/LLINs), Deltamethrin | Rapid "knockdown" effect, safe outdoors |
| Insect Growth Regulators (IGRs) | Block chitin/sugar biosynthesis | Pyriproxyfen, Diflubenzuron | |
| Formamidines | Mimic octopamine, disordered neuronal excitation | Amitraz, Chlordimeform | Effective on resistant insects, mites/ticks |
| Avermectins | Inhibit nerve impulse transmission | Abamectin | Narrow spectrum; also anti-nematode |
| Neonicotinoids | Mimic nicotine, systemic + contact | Imidacloprid | Targets sucking insects |
| Miticides | Anti-mite | Bromopropylate, Cyhexatin, Tetradifon | |

**Application types:** contact poison, fumigant, systemic, stomach poison, respiratory poison

## KEY TERMINOLOGY
- **Anthropophilic** (human blood) vs **Zoophilic** (animal blood)
- **Endophagic** (feeds indoors) vs **Exophagic** (feeds outdoors)
- **Endophilic** (rests indoors) vs **Exophilic** (rests outdoors)
- **Autogenous** (eggs w/o blood meal) vs **Anautogenous** (needs blood meal)
- **Parthenogenesis** — asexual, no fertilization needed

## MOSQUITOES (Culicidae)

**Anopheles**
- Species: gambiae complex (supervector), funestus, arabiensis (zoophilic), melas, **stephensi** (supervector, Asia-origin, recently in Africa — heat/arid-tolerant, unlike native African species)
- Anthropophilic, endophagic, endophilic
- Life cycle: holometabolous (complete); optimal 25°C, 78% humidity
- Eggs: laid singly, float on surface
- Larvae: 4 instars (L1–L4), lie **parallel** to surface, breathe via body-side spiracles, feeding stage
- Pupa: comma-shaped, resting stage, emerges 2–3 days
- Adults rest at **45° angle**; males bushy/plumose antennae, females smooth/pilose
- Only females bite (anautogenous); males feed on sugar only; parthenogenesis possible
- **Vector:** Malaria (Plasmodium)
- **Control:** larvicides, Toxorhynchites (biological), refractory index/endosymbionts (genetic), draining, nets, IRS, trans-ovarian light traps, screening, cutting grass

**Culex**
- Species:
C. quinquefasciatus
- Exophagic, exophilic; feeds 3pm–6pm (nocturnal), diurnal feeding ~10am
- Breeds: contaminated water (sewage, drains)
- Eggs: laid simultaneously as a **raft**, float
- Larvae: siphon breathing, hang at **45° angle** (unlike Anopheles' parallel posture)
- Host detection: eyes + olfactory cues
- **Vector:** Wuchereria bancrofti (filariasis), Rift Valley fever, West Nile virus — transovarial transmission (adults emerge already infected)
- **Control:** sanitation/sewage systems, insecticide on larval habitats (7–10 days spray), light traps, wire mesh

**Aedes**
- Species: aegypti, albopictus
- Black with white spots, painful biters
- "Container breeders" — clean containers, tires, flower vases
- Bimodal biting: early morning + late evening; both sexes bite during oviposition
- **Vector:** Dengue, Chikungunya, Zika, Yellow fever, O'nyong-nyong, Japanese encephalitis; Rift Valley fever (mechanical); transovarial transmission of arboviruses
- **Control:** remove vegetation/containers, straighten rivers to increase flow, Bt insecticide, light traps, larvivorous fish

## SANDFLIES (Psychodidae: Phlebotominae)
- Old World: **Phlebotomus** (4 subgenera) | New World: **Lutzomyia** (e.g. L. umbratilis, Brazil) | **Sergentomyia** — zoophilic
- Distribution: worldwide — Mediterranean, Indian subcontinent, Sub-Saharan Africa, Asia, Central/South America
- Breed: cracks, holes in ground, termite mound bases, chicken coops, animal burrows, rubbish heaps; need high humidity, can't survive desiccation
- Weak fliers, short mouthparts, bite exposed skin; anthropophilic; some autogenous
- Bimodal biting: early morning + early evening
- **Vector:**
 - L. donovani → visceral leishmaniasis
 - L. tropica → cutaneous leishmaniasis (Old World)
 - L. braziliensis complex → mucocutaneous leishmaniasis
 - Bartonella bacilliformis → Carrion's disease
 - Sandfly fever virus (Phlebovirus)
- **Control:** remove termite mounds, DEET/Picaridin, protective clothing, time outdoor activity, spray cracks/burrows, destroy rodent reservoirs

## BITING MIDGES (Ceratopogonidae) — "no-see-ums"
- Genera: Leptoconops (tropics), Culicoides (worldwide)
- Breed in wet mud/sand (fresh + salt water)
- Both diurnal + nocturnal, active in wind, exophagic/exophilic; some autogenous
- **Vector:** Mansonella ozzardi (filarial nematode); monkey = reservoir in Africa
- **Control:** hard to control with insecticide; avoid peak times, repellents, screens

## BLACK FLIES (Simuliidae)
- Genera: Simulium, Prosimulium, Cnephia
- Breed: riverbanks, fast-flowing, well-oxygenated water
- Both sexes feed on plant juices; females need blood for eggs; few autogenous
- Bimodal: early morning + early evening; exophilic/exophagic; bite head/arms more than legs
- **Vector:** Onchocerca volvulus → **river blindness (onchocerciasis)**; S. metallicum → Mansonella ozzardi (non-pathogenic)
- Bites cause bleeding → predispose to secondary bacterial infection
- **Control:** protective clothing, repellents, timing outdoor activity, insecticide on breeding/resting sites, larvicides (15–30 min), Bti, straighten rivers/remove obstructions

## HORSE FLIES / DEER FLIES (Tabanidae)
- Tabanus (horse fly — green head, breeds in swamps) | Chrysops (deer fly — yellow/brown) | Haematopota (clegs)
- Females feed by day in bright sunlight; locate host by sight + smell
- Purely exophagic **except** Chrysops silacea (attracted by darkness, hangs from objects)
- Painful bites (large mouthparts)
- **Vector:** Tabanus → Bacillus anthracis (anthrax), also implicated in Borrelia burgdorferi (Lyme — primarily tick-borne); Chrysops silacea/dimidiata → **Loa loa** (West/Central Africa)
- **Control:** barriers, canopy traps, aerial sprays (dry season), drainage of larval habitat (costly), repellents, protective clothing

## TSETSE FLIES (Glossinidae)
- Sub-Saharan Africa, 15°N–20°S
- **Forest/Fusca group:** G. palpalis, G. tachinoides, G. fuscipes
- **Savanna/Morsitans group:** G. morsitans (most widespread), G. swynnertoni, G. pallidipes, G. longipalpis (smallest, causes dermatitis in Angola)
- **Riverine/Palpalis group:** G. palpalis, G. tachinoides, G. fuscipes
- **Reproduction:** adenotrophic viviparity (larviparous, unique) — larva nourished via milk gland, deposited in shaded soil/cracks (2.5cm deep), pupates immediately, adult emerges 4–5 weeks
- Both sexes feed on blood every 2–3 days; diurnal, sight-based host detection, attracted to dark moving objects; rest 23 hrs/day on vegetation
- **Vector:**
 - G. palpalis, G. tachinoides → T. b. gambiense (**West African/Gambian** sleeping sickness)
 - G. morsitans, G. swynnertoni, G. pallidipes → T. b. rhodesiense (**East African/Rhodesian** sleeping sickness)
- **Control:** barriers, aerial sprays, **Ngu traps**, bush/vegetation clearing along rivers, insecticides, sterile insect technique
- **Trap colours: black and blue**

## HOUSEFLIES & RELATIVES (Muscidae/Fanniidae)
- 5 genera: Musca domestica (housefly), Muscina stabulans (greater housefly), Stomoxys calcitrans (stable fly — biting), Fannia canicularis (lesser housefly), Fannia scalaris (latrine fly)
- Mechanical vectors — 3 transmission routes: body hairs/mouthparts, regurgitation on food, **defecation (most important)**
- Stomoxys bites → dermatitis, secondary bacterial infection
- Muscidae/Fanniidae → biologically transmit **Thelazia spp. (eyeworms)**; **Tabanus is the vector for Thelazia**
- **Control:** light-bulb traps, air curtains, source reduction (sealed garbage)

**Aedes trap colours: black and white**

## MYIASIS
Invasion of tissues/organs by fly larvae. Larvae feed on living tissue, dead tissue, or ingested food.

- **Accidental** — food contaminated with fly eggs/larvae (e.g. housefly); larvae may survive briefly in gut → diarrhoea, dysentery, abdominal pain, vomiting; no species truly adapted to this
- **Obligatory** — larvae must live in a live host for part of life cycle
- **Facultative** — normally free-living, attack carcasses but can infect living tissue

**Types by site:**
- **Urogenital** — flies attracted to dirty distal areas, eggs hatch, larvae enter urethra
- **Cutaneous/Dermal (furuncular)** — "creeping eruption," boil-like inflammation in wounds; remove larvae aseptically
- **Ophthalmic/Ocular** — eggs near dirty eyes; lacrimation, redness, pain; may need enucleation if damage severe; prevent with hygiene/eyewear
- **Nasopharyngeal** — via nose, larvae crawl to nasopharynx → tonsillitis
- **Intestinal** — via mouth, contaminated food swallowed

**Maggot therapy:** sterile Lucilia sericata larvae used to clean wounds (feed on dead tissue only)

## FLEAS
- Key genera: Xenopsylla cheopis, Ctenocephalides (felis/canis), Tunga penetrans, Pulex irritans
- **X. cheopis** → Yersinia pestis (plague), Rickettsia typhi (murine typhus)
- **Ctenocephalides** → intermediate host for Dipylidium caninum, Hymenolepis diminuta; also Bartonella henselae (cat scratch disease)
- **Tunga penetrans** ("jigger") → gravid female burrows into feet, deposits eggs in dermis
- **Pulex irritans** — human flea, causes allergic dermatitis
- Both sexes blood-feed
- **Life cycle:** eggs laid in host's dwelling/debris (sticky, fall off host) → larvae feed on organic matter + **partially digested blood meal** (regurgitated/faeces) essential for development → spin silken cocoon (covered in debris) → pupa (4–12 days, avg 7–8) → adult
- Movement: crawling on host; **resilin protein** in legs → powerful jumping
- Nuisance: bites on ankles/legs; dermatitis in hypersensitive people; inhaled faeces → allergy
- **Plague:** hunters handling infected wild mammals at risk
- **Murine typhus:** via rodent fleas, esp. X. cheopis
- **Tularemia** (Francisella tularensis) — transmissible via fleas
- **Control:** DEET on clothing, insecticidal dusts (carbaryl, pyrethroids, OPs) on rodent burrows/pets

## LICE
- 3 blood-sucking human types: **Body louse** (Pediculus humanus corporis), **Head louse** (P. humanus capitis), **Pubic/crab louse** (Pthirus pubis)
- Worldwide, linked to poor hygiene/dense slums
- **Pediculosis** — repeated saliva injection on biting → itchiness, dermatitis, possible asthma/bronchitis, secondary impetigo (contagious), fever from scratching
- **Body louse vectors:** Epidemic typhus (Rickettsia prowazekii), Relapsing fever (Borrelia recurrentis), Trench fever (Bartonella quintana) — all **transcutaneous**
- **Body louse control:** wash/iron/dry-clean clothing, permethrin-impregnated clothing, oral ivermectin
- **Head louse** → pediculosis capitis, can cause alopecia; control: combing, shaving, insecticidal shampoo
- **Nits (eggs):** body lice on clothing seams; head lice on hair shaft; **crab lice may lay on a single hair strand** — found on beard, armpits, occasionally chest hair
- **Crab lice** → pediculosis pubis, faecal deposits → small blue maculae; control: shave pubic hair, dusts/lotions, malathion (irritant), petroleum jelly on eyelashes (8–10 days)

## BEDBUGS (Cimicidae)
- Species: Cimex lectularius (tropical + non-tropical), Cimex hemipterus (Old/New World tropics), Leptocimex (mainly Africa)
- Rising infestations due to insecticide resistance + warmer climates
- Both sexes feed at night; hide by day under wallpaper, mattresses, wall cracks; rest in groups
- **Medical importance:** sleepless nights, iron deficiency in infants/children/elderly from repeated feeding, **associated with Hepatitis B** — but **no confirmed disease transmission** to humans
- **Control:** hot water wash, ironing/sun-drying, freezing (48hrs), insecticide dusting

## COCKROACHES (Blattidae)
- Genera: Blattella germanica (German), Blatta orientalis (Oriental), Periplaneta americana (American), Supella longipalpa
- **Mechanical vectors:** Shigella,
E. coli, Salmonella
- Ecdysis (molting) → allergic reactions/asthma; ~10% of non-asthmatics show cockroach allergy (sneezing, rash, sore eyes, respiratory distress, severe cases → shortness of breath)
- **Control:** IGRs, contact poisons (boric acid), spraying walls/floors, sanitation

## TRIATOMINE (KISSING) BUGS (Reduviidae)
- Genera: Triatoma (infestans, dimidiata), Rhodnius prolixus, Panstrongylus megistus
- **Vector:** Trypanosoma cruzi → **Chagas disease** (American trypanosomiasis)
- Mechanism: bug feeds then defecates near bite → faeces contain parasite (contamination, not bite itself)
- Heavy biting → anaemia; hypersensitivity → oedema, pruritus, erythema
- Prefer warm areas (chicken sheds, goat pens), dark hiding places
- **Control:** spraying interior wall/floor surfaces, reduce hiding places, fumigation

## TICKS

**General:** Arachnids — no antennae, chelicerate mouthparts, body = cephalothorax + abdomen, hemimetabolous (incomplete metamorphosis)

**ARGASIDAE (Soft ticks)**
- Genera: Ornithodoros (O. moubata), Argas
- Found in warm areas; **nidicolous** (stay in host's nest/dwelling)
- Multi-host tick; multiple nymphal stages (N1–N4, usually 4–5 instars)
- Feed briefly (hours) compared to hard ticks
- **Vector:** Borrelia duttoni → tick-borne relapsing fever; also other spirochetes, viruses; tick paralysis (saliva toxin)
- **Control:** DEET, permethrin-impregnated nets, acaricides on furniture/walls

**IXODIDAE (Hard ticks)**
- Genera: Dermacentor, Amblyomma, Haemaphysalis, Rhipicephalus, Hyalomma, Boophilus
- Worldwide, mostly temperate regions
- Non-nidicolous, **1 nymphal stage only**, stays on host longer than soft ticks
- **3-host tick:** each stage (larva/nymph/adult) feeds on a different host — Dermacentor, Amblyomma, Rhipicephalus, Hyalomma
- **2-host tick:** larvae molt to nymph on 1st host, nymph seeks 2nd host — Rhipicephalus, Hyalomma
- **1-host tick:** all stages on same host — Boophilus
- Larvae quest by climbing vegetation, waiting for host contact
- **Vector:** tick paralysis (breathing/swallowing/speech difficulty), Q fever, tick-borne encephalitis, poliomyelitis-like illness in allergic people, **Lyme disease (Borrelia burgdorferi)**
- **Control:** DEET, regular acaricide dipping of livestock, garden spraying, proper tick removal, permethrin clothing
- **Why hard to control:** survive long periods without feeding, adapt to multiple host types

## MITES

**Sarcoptes scabiei (Sarcoptidae)** — causes **scabies** (humans) / **mange** (animals)
- Arachnid, no teeth on hypostome, has sensory setae
- Female burrows into skin, deposits eggs → narrow twisting tunnels (dark pepper-like tracks)
- Detection: ink test (smear + wipe reveals tunnels) or liquid tetracycline under UV (fluoresces yellow-green)
- **Classic scabies:** papular, non-painful rash — interdigital webs, wrists, elbows, armpits, genitals; never above the head; severe nocturnal pruritus, worse after hot bath; scratching → secondary infection
- **Crusted/Norwegian scabies:** highly contagious, huge mite burden, scaly/hyperkeratotic skin, diagnosed via skin scraping
- **Treatment:**
 - Permethrin 5% cream — 2 applications, wash off 8–12 hrs
 - Benzyl benzoate — wash after 24 hrs, reapply day 2–3
 - Crotamiton — 5 daily applications, safe in children/pregnancy
 - Ivermectin — single topical dose
- **Control:** treat whole household, disinfect bedding/clothes (hot wash, dry-clean, freeze 48hrs), antihistamines for itch

**Trombiculid (Harvest) mites**
- Species: Leptotrombidium deliense, L. akamushi, L. fletcheri, Eutrombicula alfreddugesi (red bug), Trombicula autumnalis (harvest mite)
- **Only larvae are parasitic** — attach to small mammals (rodents = key hosts); nymphs/adults free-living
- Humans = accidental hosts, aid larval dispersal
- **Vector:** Rickettsia tsutsugamushi/Orientia tsutsugamushi → **scrub typhus**
- Also cause intense itching/welts (nuisance)
- **Control:** DEET, benzyl benzoate, permethrin-impregnated clothing, burning scrub vegetation, aerial insecticide

## TRANSMISSION TYPES (high-yield distinction)
- **Cyclopropagative** — cyclic development **+** multiplication (e.g. malaria)
- **Cyclodevelopmental** — cyclic development only, **no** multiplication (e.g. filarial worms)
- **Propagative** — multiplication only, **no** development (e.g. plague in fleas)
- **Mechanical/Passive** — no biological development, fomite-like (e.g. houseflies)

## OTHER EXAM-FAVORITE POINTS
- **Trap colours:** Aedes → black & white | Glossina (tsetse) → black & blue
- **Thelazia spp. (eyeworm) — vector = Tabanus**
- **Anopheles stephensi** — recently reported in Kenya (2022), invasive, heat/arid-tolerant
- **Container mosquito** = Aedes aegypti / albopictus
- **Blackwater fever** — linked to severe P. falciparum (Anopheles-transmitted)
