## SECTION A: MULTIPLE CHOICE QUESTIONS
**1.** Malignant neoplasms show all the following features **EXCEPT**:

A. Disorganized cell structureb) Encapsulationc) Invasion of local and distant tissuesd) Rapid, erratic growth

Answer: B. Encapsulation

Explanation: Malignant neoplasms are characteristically NOT encapsulated. Encapsulation is a feature of benign tumors that helps distinguish them from malignant ones. Malignant tumors show irregular borders, invasive growth patterns, and lack the fibrous capsule that typically surrounds benign tumors.

**2.** One of the following cells has the **least capacity for regeneration**:

A. Cardiac muscleb) Skeletal musclec) Neuronal cellsd) Liver cells

Answer: C. Neuronal cells

Explanation: Neuronal cells are permanent cells with minimal to no regenerative capacity once mature. While cardiac muscle also has limited regeneration, neurons in the central nervous system essentially cannot regenerate after injury, making them the most vulnerable to permanent loss.

**3.** The following are mediators of inflammation **EXCEPT**:

A. Interferonsb) Prostaglandinsc) Tumor Necrosis Factor (TNF)d) Myeloperoxidase enzyme

Answer: D. Myeloperoxidase enzyme

Explanation: Myeloperoxidase is an enzyme found in neutrophil granules that generates reactive oxygen species for antimicrobial activity, but it is not a mediator of inflammation. The other options are all inflammatory mediators that regulate various aspects of the inflammatory response.

**4.** Metaplasia refers to:

A. Irregular, atypical proliferative changes in epithelial or mesenchymal cellsb) Loss of cell substance producing shrinkage of the cellsc) Replacement of one type of adult cell by another type of adult celld) None of the above

Answer: C. Replacement of one type of adult cell by another type of adult cell

Explanation: Metaplasia is the reversible replacement of one differentiated cell type with another mature differentiated cell type. This typically occurs as an adaptive response to chronic irritation or inflammation, such as respiratory epithelium changing from ciliated columnar to squamous in smokers.

**5.** Metastatic calcification is associated with:

A. Dead or dying tissuesb) Atherosclerosisc) Pancreatic cancerd) Damaged heart valves

**Answer: None of the options correctly describe metastatic calcification**

Explanation: Metastatic calcification occurs in normal tissues due to elevated serum calcium or phosphate levels (hypercalcemia or hyperphosphatemia). The options listed describe dystrophic calcification, which occurs in damaged or necrotic tissues with normal serum calcium levels.

**6.** Which of these is an endogenous pigment?

A. Melaninb) Coalc) Tattooingd) Lipid

Answer: A. Melanin

Explanation: Melanin is an endogenous pigment produced by melanocytes. Coal and tattooing represent exogenous pigments introduced from outside the body, while lipid is not classified as a pigment but rather as a macromolecule.

**7.** Phagocytes include the following **EXCEPT**:

A. Neutrophilsb) Macrophagesc) Osteoclastsd) Lymphocytes

Answer: D. Lymphocytes

Explanation: Lymphocytes are primarily involved in adaptive immunity and do not function as professional phagocytes. Neutrophils and macrophages are classic phagocytes, while osteoclasts are specialized phagocytic cells that resorb bone matrix.

**8.** The commonest cause of infectious granulomas is:

A. Tuberculosisb) Fungal infectionsc) Viral infectionsd) Toxins

Answer: A. Tuberculosis

Explanation: Mycobacterium tuberculosis is the most common cause of infectious granulomatous inflammation worldwide. TB characteristically produces caseating granulomas with central necrosis, epithelioid cells, and Langhans giant cells.

**9.** The most common fixative used to preserve tissue in histopathology is:

A. 10% Formalinb) Ethylene Glycolc) Haematoxylind) 95% Ethanol

Answer: A. 10% Formalin

Explanation: 10% neutral buffered formalin is the most widely used fixative in histopathology. It cross-links proteins and nucleic acids to preserve tissue morphology and prevent autolysis and bacterial decay.

**10.** The following are oncogenic viruses in humans **EXCEPT**:

A. Human Papilloma Virus (HPV)b) Epstein Barr Virus (EBV)c) Hepatitis B Virusd) Polio Virus

Answer: D. Polio Virus

Explanation: Poliovirus is not oncogenic. HPV causes cervical and other cancers, EBV is associated with Burkitt lymphoma and nasopharyngeal carcinoma, and Hepatitis B virus is linked to hepatocellular carcinoma.

**11.** The **RB gene**, "the guardian of the genome," regulates the cell cycle at:

A. G1/S checkpointb) G2 checkpointc) G0 restriction checkpointd) Intra-S checkpoint

Answer: A. G1/S checkpoint

Explanation: The RB (retinoblastoma) gene product controls the G1/S checkpoint, preventing cells from entering S phase until appropriate growth signals are received. Note: p53 is actually called "the guardian of the genome," not RB.

**12.** TP53 is regulated and scavenged after completing its action by:

A. CDKN1Ab) MDM2c) RASd) E6

Answer: B. MDM2

Explanation: MDM2 (Mouse Double Minute 2) is the primary negative regulator of p53. It binds to p53 and promotes its degradation through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, creating a negative feedback loop.

**13.** Mutations in the **TGF-Beta signaling pathway** involving SMAD molecules usually result in:

A. Colon cancerb) Stomach cancerc) Lung cancerd) Breast cancer

Answer: A. Colon cancer

Explanation: TGF-β signaling pathway mutations, particularly involving SMAD2 and SMAD4, are commonly associated with colorectal carcinogenesis. TGF-β normally functions as a tumor suppressor in epithelial cells.

**14.** The most commonly mutated oncoprotein in all tumors and a key participant in abnormal signal transduction pathways is:

A. ABLb) TP53c) BRAFd) RAS

Answer: D. RAS

Explanation: RAS proteins are mutated in approximately 30% of all human cancers, making them the most commonly mutated oncoproteins. RAS mutations lead to constitutive growth signals and are found in many cancer types.

**15.** The **Warburg effect** in tumor cellular metabolism refers to:

A. Oxidative phosphorylation in tumor cellsb) Aerobic glycolysis in tumor cellsc) Mutations in enzymes regulating the Krebs cycled) Defective mitochondrial ATP synthesis

Answer: B. Aerobic glycolysis in tumor cells

Explanation: The Warburg effect describes the preference of cancer cells for glycolysis over oxidative phosphorylation for energy production, even in the presence of adequate oxygen. This metabolic reprogramming supports rapid cell division.

**16.** A granuloma is comprised of:

A. Fibroblasts and capillariesb) Capillaries and giant cellsc) Endothelial cells and neutrophilsd) Central necrotic area, epithelioid cells, and lymphocytes

Answer: D. Central necrotic area, epithelioid cells, and lymphocytes

Explanation: Granulomas are organized collections of epithelioid cells (activated macrophages) surrounded by lymphocytes, often with multinucleated giant cells and a central area of necrosis in caseating granulomas.

**17.** During autopsy, one of the following is **NOT** the role of the medical officer:

A. Identify the decedentb) Ascertain the cause of deathc) Document evidenced) Request for autopsy

Answer: D. Request for autopsy

Explanation: The medical officer performing the autopsy does not request the autopsy. Autopsy requests typically come from coroners, magistrates, families, or other authorized personnel. The pathologist's role is to perform and interpret the examination.

**18.** The postmortem interval can be estimated using the following **EXCEPT**:

A. Rigor mortisb) Algor mortisc) Mummificationd) Cadaveric spasm

Answer: C. Mummification

Explanation: Mummification is a late postmortem change that occurs over months to years in specific environmental conditions. It cannot be used to estimate postmortem interval in the acute setting, unlike the other early postmortem changes.

**19.** Exhumation of human remains in Kenya may only proceed on the authority of:

A. Valid court ordersb) The National Police Servicec) The Chief Government Pathologistd) The Medical Superintendent

Answer: A. Valid court orders

Explanation: Exhumation of human remains requires proper legal authorization through valid court orders. This ensures proper legal procedures are followed and protects the dignity of the deceased and rights of families.

**20.** The following are chemical mediators of acute inflammation **EXCEPT**:

A. Leukotrienesb) Complement proteinsc) Interleukinsd) Hormones

Answer: D. Hormones

Explanation: While some hormones can influence inflammation, they are not primarily classified as mediators of acute inflammation. Leukotrienes, complement proteins, and interleukins are all specific inflammatory mediators.

**21.** The characteristic cells of chronic inflammation are all **EXCEPT**:

A. Plasma cellsb) Macrophagesc) Lymphocytesd) Polymorphonuclear cells (PMNs)

Answer: D. Polymorphonuclear cells (PMNs)

Explanation: PMNs (neutrophils) are the hallmark of acute inflammation, not chronic inflammation. Chronic inflammation is characterized by mononuclear cells including macrophages, lymphocytes, and plasma cells.

**22.** Regarding the pathologic assessment of tumors, which of the following is TRUE?

A. Grading is the degree of differentiation of tumorsb) Staging is the extent of spread of tumorsc) TNM and AJCC systems can be used for stagingd) All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: All statements are correct. Grading assesses histologic differentiation, staging determines extent of spread, and both TNM (Tumor, Node, Metastasis) and AJCC (American Joint Committee on Cancer) systems are used for staging.

**23.** Complications of cutaneous wound healing include:

A. Wound dehiscenceb) Ulcerationc) Formation of contracturesd) Deficient scar formation

**Answer: All options are complications of wound healing**

Explanation: All listed options represent potential complications of wound healing. Wound dehiscence is separation of wound edges, ulceration represents failure to heal, contractures result from excessive scarring, and deficient scar formation leads to weak repair.

**24.** One of the following statements is TRUE:

A. Large cutaneous wounds heal by first intentionb) Hormones such as glucocorticoids inhibit collagen synthesisc) Defective inflammation may cause increased susceptibility to infectiond) Atherosclerosis is a chronic inflammatory process

**Answer: Multiple correct answers (b, c, d)**

Explanation: Statement (a) is false - large wounds heal by second intention. Statements (b), (c), and (d) are all true: glucocorticoids inhibit collagen synthesis and wound healing, defective inflammation increases infection risk, and atherosclerosis involves chronic inflammatory processes.

**25.** The following statements are TRUE of ischemia:

A. Results from critical lack of blood supply to an areab) May occur in atherosclerosisc) May be reversible when blood supply is restoredd) All of the above

Answer: D. All of the above

Explanation: All statements about ischemia are correct. It results from inadequate blood supply, commonly occurs in atherosclerosis due to arterial narrowing, and can be reversible if blood flow is restored before irreversible cell death occurs.

## SECTION B: SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS
**1. Define the following terms:**

**a) Apoptosis:** Programmed cell death characterized by controlled cellular suicide involving DNA fragmentation, cell shrinkage, membrane blebbing, and formation of apoptotic bodies that are phagocytosed without inflammatory response.

**b) Necrosis:** Pathologic cell death resulting from severe cell injury, characterized by cellular swelling, membrane disruption, organelle dysfunction, and release of cellular contents causing inflammation.

**c) Proto-oncogene:** Normal cellular genes that regulate cell growth, proliferation, and differentiation. When mutated, activated, or overexpressed, they become oncogenes that promote malignant transformation.

**d) Oncogene:** Mutated or abnormally expressed versions of proto-oncogenes that contribute to malignant transformation by promoting uncontrolled cell proliferation and survival.

**e) Tumor suppressor gene:** Genes that normally function to prevent uncontrolled cell growth by regulating cell cycle checkpoints, promoting apoptosis, or maintaining genomic stability. Loss of function leads to cancer predisposition.

**2. Compare and contrast apoptosis and necrosis. (5 Marks)**

**Similarities:** Both result in cell death and involve loss of cellular function.

**Differences:**

**Apoptosis:**

- Programmed, controlled process
- Single cells or small groups affected
- Cell shrinkage and membrane blebbing
- DNA laddering pattern
- No inflammatory response
- Phagocytosis by macrophages
- Energy-dependent process
- Maintains membrane integrity initially

**Necrosis:**

- Pathologic, uncontrolled process
- Large areas of tissue affected
- Cell swelling and membrane rupture
- Random DNA degradation
- Significant inflammatory response
- Release of cellular contents
- Energy-independent process
- Early membrane integrity loss

**3. Outline the steps involved in preparation of cytology specimens. (5 Marks)**

**Collection:** Obtain cells through various methods including fine needle aspiration, scraping, brushing, or fluid collection from body cavities.

**Fixation:** Immediate fixation using alcohol-based fixatives or air-drying depending on staining method to preserve cellular morphology.

**Preparation:** Spread cells thinly on glass slides, either as direct smears or using liquid-based cytology methods for even distribution.

**Staining:** Apply appropriate stains such as Papanicolaou stain for gynecologic specimens or Romanowsky stains for hematologic samples.

**Examination:** Microscopic evaluation by cytopathologist to assess cellular morphology, nuclear features, and overall pattern for diagnostic interpretation.

**4. Write short notes on amyloidosis. (5 Marks)**

**Definition:** Amyloidosis is a group of diseases characterized by extracellular deposition of misfolded proteins in a characteristic β-pleated sheet configuration.

**Types:**

- **AL Amyloidosis:** Derived from immunoglobulin light chains, associated with plasma cell disorders
- **AA Amyloidosis:** Secondary to chronic inflammatory conditions
- **Hereditary:** Various genetic mutations causing protein misfolding

**Morphology:** Amorphous, eosinophilic extracellular deposits that show characteristic apple-green birefringence under polarized light with Congo red staining.

**Clinical Effects:** Organ dysfunction depending on distribution - cardiac (restrictive cardiomyopathy), renal (proteinuria, nephrotic syndrome), neurologic, or systemic involvement.

**Diagnosis:** Congo red staining with polarized microscopy, electron microscopy showing fibrillar structure, and protein typing for classification.

**5. List the principal mediators of acute inflammation and outline their functions. (5 Marks)**

**Histamine:** Released from mast cells and basophils, causes immediate vasodilation and increased vascular permeability.

**Prostaglandins:** Especially PGE2 and PGI2, cause vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, and pain sensitization.

**Leukotrienes:** LTB4 is a potent neutrophil chemoattractant, while cysteinyl leukotrienes cause vasoconstriction and increased permeability.

**Complement System:** C3a and C5a are anaphylatoxins causing mast cell degranulation, while C5a is a powerful chemoattractant for neutrophils.

**Cytokines:** TNF-α and IL-1 cause endothelial activation, fever, and systemic acute phase responses.

**6. State five (5) cytomorphologic features of malignant cells. (5 Marks)**

**Nuclear Pleomorphism:** Variation in nuclear size, shape, and staining characteristics within the cell population.

**Increased Nuclear-to-Cytoplasmic Ratio:** Enlarged nuclei relative to cytoplasmic volume, reflecting increased metabolic activity.

**Nuclear Hyperchromasia:** Dark, intensely staining nuclei due to increased chromatin content and altered chromatin distribution.

**Prominent Nucleoli:** Enlarged, irregular nucleoli reflecting increased protein synthesis and ribosomal RNA production.

**Abnormal Mitotic Figures:** Increased mitotic activity with abnormal, atypical mitotic configurations indicating genomic instability.

**7. Outline the major steps involved in the conduction of an autopsy. (5 Marks)**

**External Examination:** Document body measurements, identifying features, external injuries, and signs of disease or decomposition.

**Internal Examination:** Systematic dissection of body cavities (thoracic, abdominal, pelvic) with in-situ examination of organ relationships.

**Organ Removal:** Careful removal and weighing of organs using standard techniques, documenting gross pathologic findings.

**Tissue Sampling:** Collection of representative tissue samples for histopathologic examination and special studies as indicated.

**Documentation:** Detailed recording of findings, photography of significant lesions, and preparation of comprehensive autopsy report with cause of death determination.

## SECTION C: LONG ANSWER QUESTIONS
**1. Define neoplasia and describe the process of transformation of a normal cell to a malignant cell. (10 Marks)**

**Definition of Neoplasia:** Neoplasia is the process of abnormal new growth resulting in the formation of neoplasms (tumors). It represents a fundamental disturbance in cell growth and differentiation, where cells show partial or complete loss of responsiveness to normal growth controls.

**Characteristics of Neoplasia:**

- Autonomous growth independent of physiologic stimuli
- Monoclonal origin from single transformed cell
- Genetic alterations affecting growth control mechanisms
- Progressive accumulation of mutations over time

**Transformation Process - Multi-Step Carcinogenesis:**

**Initiation Phase:**

- DNA damage by carcinogens (chemical, physical, viral)
- Mutations in critical genes (oncogenes, tumor suppressor genes)
- Single cell acquires growth advantage
- Reversible if DNA repair mechanisms effective

**Promotion Phase:**

- Clonal expansion of initiated cells
- Additional genetic alterations accumulate
- Cells become increasingly autonomous
- Development of preneoplastic lesions

**Progression Phase:**

- Further accumulation of mutations
- Acquisition of invasive capabilities
- Development of metastatic potential
- Angiogenesis and tissue invasion

**Molecular Mechanisms:** **Oncogene Activation:** Growth-promoting genes become overactive through point mutations, amplifications, or translocations.

**Tumor Suppressor Inactivation:** Loss of growth-inhibiting genes like p53 and Rb through deletions, mutations, or epigenetic silencing.

**DNA Repair Defects:** Mutations in mismatch repair genes leading to genomic instability and accelerated mutation accumulation.

**Apoptosis Evasion:** Resistance to programmed cell death allowing survival of damaged cells.

**2. Discuss the processes involved in cutaneous wound healing and repair. (10 Marks)**

**Types of Wound Healing:** **Primary Intention:** Clean, closely approximated wounds heal with minimal scar formation. **Secondary Intention:** Large wounds with tissue loss heal by contraction and epithelialization.

**Phases of Wound Healing:**

**Hemostasis and Inflammation (0-3 days):**

- Immediate vasoconstriction followed by vasodilation
- Platelet aggregation and fibrin clot formation
- Neutrophil infiltration for debris clearance
- Release of inflammatory mediators and growth factors

**Proliferative Phase (3-21 days):** **Angiogenesis:** Formation of new blood vessels through endothelial cell proliferation and migration, stimulated by VEGF and other angiogenic factors.

**Fibroblast Proliferation:** Migration and proliferation of fibroblasts producing collagen, elastin, and ground substance forming granulation tissue.

**Epithelialization:** Migration and proliferation of keratinocytes from wound edges to restore epithelial continuity.

**Maturation and Remodeling (21 days-2 years):**

- Collagen cross-linking and reorganization
- Conversion from type III to type I collagen
- Reduction in cellularity and vascularity
- Scar contraction and strengthening

**Growth Factors and Regulation:** **PDGF:** Promotes fibroblast and smooth muscle cell proliferation **TGF-β:** Stimulates collagen synthesis and wound contraction **FGF:** Promotes angiogenesis and epithelial cell proliferation **EGF:** Accelerates epithelialization

**Factors Affecting Healing:**

- Adequate blood supply and oxygenation
- Nutritional status (protein, vitamin C, zinc)
- Age and comorbidities
- Infection and foreign bodies
- Mechanical factors and wound tension

**3. Describe the vascular and cellular events of the acute inflammatory response. (20 Marks)**

**Definition and Purpose:** Acute inflammation is the immediate and early response to injurious agents, characterized by vascular changes, cellular recruitment, and molecular mediator release. Its primary purposes are to eliminate the injurious stimulus, limit tissue damage, and initiate repair processes.

**Vascular Events:**

**Vasodilation:**

- Immediate transient vasoconstriction (seconds)
- Sustained vasodilation affecting arterioles and venules
- Mediated by histamine, prostaglandins, and nitric oxide
- Results in increased blood flow and tissue warmth/redness

**Increased Vascular Permeability:** **Immediate Response (0-30 minutes):** Histamine and leukotrienes cause endothelial cell contraction creating intercellular gaps.

**Delayed Response (2-12 hours):** Direct endothelial damage from severe injury, toxins, or complement activation.

**Delayed Prolonged Response:** Endothelial cell retraction, reorganization, and increased transcytosis.

**Hemodynamic Changes:**

- Initial increased blood flow followed by stasis
- Plasma protein extravasation leading to edema formation
- Increased blood viscosity due to hemoconcentration
- Margination of leukocytes along vessel walls

**Cellular Events:**

**Neutrophil Recruitment - Multi-Step Process:**

**Margination and Rolling:**

- Decreased axial flow allows neutrophils to move toward vessel walls
- Selectins (P-selectin, E-selectin) on activated endothelium bind to neutrophil ligands
- Rolling interaction allows sampling of chemotactic gradients

**Adhesion:**

- Firm adhesion mediated by β2 integrins (LFA-1, MAC-1) on neutrophils
- Binding to ICAM-1 and VCAM-1 on activated endothelium
- Strengthened by chemokine activation of neutrophils

**Transmigration (Diapedesis):**

- Migration between endothelial cells through intercellular junctions
- PECAM-1 and other junctional molecules facilitate passage
- Basement membrane penetration via enzymatic degradation

**Chemotaxis:**

- Directional migration toward site of injury
- Guided by chemoattractant gradients (C5a, LTB4, bacterial products)
- Involves cytoskeletal reorganization and pseudopod formation

**Neutrophil Activation and Function:** **Phagocytosis:** Recognition, engulfment, and intracellular killing of pathogens and debris through opsonization (antibodies, complement).

**Degranulation:** Release of preformed mediators including elastase, myeloperoxidase, lactoferrin, and antimicrobial peptides.

**Respiratory Burst:** Generation of reactive oxygen species (superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, hypochlorous acid) for microbial killing.

**NETosis:** Formation of neutrophil extracellular traps combining DNA, histones, and antimicrobial proteins to trap pathogens.

**Molecular Mediators:**

**Cell-Derived Mediators:**

- **Histamine:** Immediate vasodilation and permeability
- **Prostaglandins:** Vasodilation, pain, fever
- **Leukotrienes:** Vasoconstriction, permeability, neutrophil chemotaxis
- **Platelet-Activating Factor:** Platelet aggregation, neutrophil activation

**Plasma-Derived Mediators:** **Complement System:** C3a and C5a cause mast cell degranulation and neutrophil chemotaxis, while membrane attack complex causes cell lysis.

**Coagulation System:** Thrombin generation, fibrin formation, and activation of protease-activated receptors.

**Kinin System:** Bradykinin causes vasodilation, increased permeability, and pain.

**Resolution of Acute Inflammation:**

- Cessation of mediator production
- Neutrophil apoptosis and clearance by macrophages
- Lymphatic drainage of edema fluid
- Tissue repair and regeneration initiation

**Outcomes:** **Complete Resolution:** Return to normal tissue architecture **Healing by Scarring:** Replacement with fibrous tissue **Chronic Inflammation:** Persistent inflammatory response **Abscess Formation:** Localized suppurative inflammation
