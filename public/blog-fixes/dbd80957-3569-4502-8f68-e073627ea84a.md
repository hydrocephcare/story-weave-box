# Physiological Changes of Pregnancy by System

**Year 4 · Obstetrics and Gynaecology · Maternal physiology**

> **In one line:** pregnancy is a state of planned adaptation in which almost every system changes (driven by **progesterone, oestrogen, hCG, human placental lactogen and the placental circulation**) to supply the foetus, protect the mother from haemorrhage at delivery and prepare for lactation. Knowing the normal values stops you mistaking physiology for disease, or missing disease because a result looks "normal".

## What you must know

- **Blood volume +40-50%** (plasma rises more than red cells, so Hb falls: dilutional anaemia); peak at 32-34 weeks.
- **Cardiac output +30-50%**; blood pressure **falls** in the first and second trimesters and returns to baseline at term.
- **GFR +50%**: creatinine and urea fall, so a "normal" creatinine of 90 µmol/L is abnormal in pregnancy.
- **Tidal volume +40%**, PaCO2 about 30 mmHg (compensated respiratory alkalosis); vital capacity unchanged.
- **Hypercoagulable state** (raised fibrinogen and factors VII, VIII, X; fall in protein S) gives a higher risk of VTE.
- **Insulin resistance** from the second trimester (human placental lactogen) explains why gestational diabetes presents then.
- Use the **left lateral tilt** after 20 weeks to avoid supine hypotension syndrome.

## 1. The principle

Each change has a purpose: more blood and cardiac output to perfuse the placenta, more ventilation to clear foetal CO2, more renal clearance to remove foetal waste, and a hypercoagulable state to limit bleeding at placental separation. Disease is recognised by comparing a result with the **pregnancy-specific** normal range, not the non-pregnant one.

## 2. Cardiovascular system

- **Blood volume** rises by 40-50% (plasma more than red cells), peaking at 32-34 weeks. This protects against the blood loss of delivery (a normal vaginal delivery loses up to about 500 mL, a caesarean about 1,000 mL).
- **Cardiac output** rises by 30-50% from early in the first trimester, first through a higher **stroke volume** and then a higher **heart rate** (by 10-20 beats per minute).
- **Blood pressure** falls in the first and second trimesters because systemic vascular resistance drops (progesterone, nitric oxide, the low-resistance placental bed). It returns to baseline by term.
- **Supine hypotension syndrome**: the gravid uterus compresses the inferior vena cava when the woman lies flat, so venous return and output fall (dizziness, pallor, sweating, faintness). Nurse her in the **left lateral tilt** (about 15 degrees, or a wedge under the right hip).
- **Normal findings**: ejection systolic flow murmur, third heart sound, ankle oedema, a laterally displaced apex beat.

> **Remember:** a pathological murmur is a **diastolic** murmur or a loud systolic murmur with a thrill; do not dismiss those as "pregnancy".

### During labour
- Cardiac output rises further with each contraction (autotransfusion from the uterus) and is highest immediately after delivery, when caval compression is relieved. Women with cardiac disease are most at risk then.

## 3. Haematological changes

- Physiological **dilutional anaemia**: plasma expands more than red cell mass. Anaemia in pregnancy is defined as Hb **below 11 g/dL** in the first and third trimesters and **below 10.5 g/dL** in the second.
- **Iron demand** rises sharply (about 1 g in total across pregnancy), so supplementation is routine, especially in Kenya where iron deficiency and malaria are common.
- **Hypercoagulable state**: fibrinogen and factors VII, VIII and X rise, and protein S falls. Fibrinolysis is reduced. This explains the higher risk of **venous thromboembolism**.
- White cell count and ESR rise (**ESR is unreliable** in pregnancy). Platelet count may fall slightly (gestational thrombocytopenia).

## 4. Respiratory system

- **Tidal volume rises about 40%**, so minute ventilation increases. PaCO2 falls to about **30 mmHg** (a compensated respiratory alkalosis; bicarbonate also falls).
- **Residual volume falls** as the diaphragm rises, but **vital capacity is unchanged**. The respiratory rate stays normal.
- Hyperventilation is driven by **progesterone**. Physiological dyspnoea is common.
- Oxygen consumption rises about 20%, and the woman has less reserve, so she desaturates quickly under apnoea (important at anaesthesia).

> **Exam tip:** a "normal" PaCO2 of 40 mmHg in a dyspnoeic pregnant woman suggests she is tiring or has respiratory disease.

## 5. Renal system

- **GFR rises by 50%** and renal plasma flow by 60-80%. Serum **creatinine and urea fall** (creatinine about 40-60 µmol/L), so a value that is normal outside pregnancy may signal renal impairment.
- Dilatation of the calyces, renal pelvis and ureters (**right more than left**, from progesterone and compression at the pelvic brim) predisposes to **pyelonephritis** and asymptomatic bacteriuria needing treatment.
- **Glycosuria** is common because the filtered glucose load exceeds tubular reabsorption; it is not by itself diagnostic of diabetes.
- Protein excretion rises mildly; significant proteinuria (for example urine protein:creatinine ratio above 30 mg/mmol) points to pre-eclampsia or renal disease.

## 6. Gastrointestinal system

- Reduced **lower oesophageal sphincter tone** causes reflux and heartburn.
- **Gastric emptying is slowed in labour**, so assume a full stomach at anaesthesia (risk of aspiration).
- Constipation (progesterone relaxes smooth muscle), gum hypertrophy and a higher risk of **gallstones** (slow gallbladder emptying) also occur.
- Nausea and vomiting of early pregnancy are linked to hCG.

## 7. Endocrine and metabolic changes

- The placenta makes **hCG, human placental lactogen (hPL), oestrogen and progesterone**.
- **Insulin resistance** develops from the second trimester (hPL), which is why **gestational diabetes** appears then.
- The thyroid enlarges slightly, and **total T4 rises** with thyroid-binding globulin, but **free T4 stays normal**.
- **Weight gain** of 11-16 kg is normal in a woman of normal BMI (foetus, placenta, liquor, uterus, breasts, blood volume, fluid and fat).

## 8. Genital tract and breasts

- The **uterus** grows from about 70 g to 1,100 g and its capacity from 10 mL to 5 L (hypertrophy and hyperplasia of muscle early, stretching later).
- The **cervix** softens (**Goodell's sign**), turns bluish (Chadwick's sign) and forms a mucus plug that seals the canal.
- The **breasts** enlarge, with darker areolae and prominent **Montgomery's tubercles**.

## 9. Skin

Linea nigra, **chloasma (melasma)**, striae gravidarum, palmar erythema and spider naevi, all from oestrogen and melanocyte-stimulating activity. Most fade after delivery.

## 10. Summary table of key changes

| System | Change | Practical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Blood volume | +40-50% | Tolerates delivery blood loss; dilutional anaemia |
| Cardiac output | +30-50% | Flow murmur; cardiac disease decompensates |
| Blood pressure | Falls, then returns to baseline | BP at term is not "low normal" |
| Tidal volume | +40% | PaCO2 about 30 mmHg |
| GFR | +50% | Low creatinine and urea normal |
| Coagulation | Hypercoagulable | VTE risk; prophylaxis in high-risk women |
| Insulin sensitivity | Falls from second trimester | Screen for gestational diabetes |
| Gastric emptying | Slow in labour | Aspiration risk |

## Key points to revise

1. Blood volume +40-50%, cardiac output +30-50%, GFR +50%, tidal volume +40%.
2. Creatinine and urea fall; a "normal" creatinine may be abnormal.
3. Anaemia: Hb below 11 g/dL (first and third trimesters), below 10.5 g/dL (second).
4. Left lateral tilt after 20 weeks prevents supine hypotension.
5. Hypercoagulability (fibrinogen, VII, VIII, X up; protein S down) raises the risk of VTE.
6. Insulin resistance from the second trimester (hPL) explains gestational diabetes.
7. Dilated right-sided ureter and glycosuria are physiological but predispose to pyelonephritis.
8. Assume a full stomach in labour because gastric emptying is slowed.
