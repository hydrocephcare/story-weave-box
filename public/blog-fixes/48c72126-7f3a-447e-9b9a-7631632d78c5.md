## TOPIC 1: HAEMATOPOIESIS**Etymology:** *Haema* (Greek) = blood | *Poiesis* = making/production
**Definition:**
- Continuous process of **blood cell production** from pluripotent stem cells (★)
- Replaces cells lost through senescence, destruction, or haemorrhage
- Produces ~200 billion RBCs and ~100 billion WBCs daily
-

**SITES OF HAEMATOPOIESIS — BY DEVELOPMENTAL STAGE** (★)
| Phase | Site | Period |
|---|---|---|
| Mesoblastic | Yolk sac | 0–2 months fetal |
| Hepatic | Liver (primary), spleen, lymph nodes | 2–7 months fetal |
| Myeloid | Bone marrow | 5 months → adult life |
- In healthy adults: **bone marrow is the sole site** (★)
- Active marrow in adults: sternum, vertebrae, pelvis, ribs, skull, proximal femur and humerus
- **Extramedullary haematopoiesis** = reactivation of liver/spleen in disease states (e.g. myelofibrosis, thalassaemia major) (★)
-

**THE HAEMATOPOIETIC STEM CELL (HSC)**
- Pluripotent and self-renewing
- Surface marker: **CD34+** (★) — also CD38−, CD117+, HLA-DR+
- Differentiates into two main progenitors:
-
 - **Common Myeloid Progenitor (CMP)** → RBCs, platelets, neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, monocytes
- **Common Lymphoid Progenitor (CLP)** → T-cells, B-cells, NK cells
**Common CD markers — know the difference:** (★)
- CD34 = HSC marker
- CD3 = T-cell marker
- CD4 = T-helper cell
- CD19, CD20 = B-cell markers
- CD33 = myeloid marker
-

**GROWTH FACTORS & CYTOKINES** (★)
| Factor | Primary Target | Clinical Use |
|---|---|---|
| Erythropoietin (EPO) | RBC production (erythroid line) | Anaemia of CKD |
| Thrombopoietin (TPO) | **Platelet** production via megakaryocytes (★) | — |
| G-CSF (Filgrastim) | **Neutrophil** production and recovery (★) | Post-chemotherapy neutropenia |
| GM-CSF | Neutrophils + monocytes | Stem cell mobilisation |
| IL-3 | Multi-lineage stimulation | — |
| SCF (Stem cell factor) | HSC survival and proliferation | — |
- G-CSF = Granulocyte Colony-Stimulating Factor → promotes **neutrophil recovery after chemotherapy** (★)
- Thrombopoietin → acts on megakaryocytes → **platelets** (★)
-

## TOPIC 2: ANAEMIA — GENERAL + IRON DEFICIENCY ANAEMIA (IDA)**Etymology:** *An-* = without | *haima* = blood
**Definition of Anaemia:**
- Reduction in haemoglobin concentration below normal for age and sex
- WHO thresholds: Hb <13 g/dL (adult male) | <12 g/dL (adult non-pregnant female) | <11 g/dL (pregnant)
-

**CLASSIFICATION OF ANAEMIA BY MCV** (★)
| Type | MCV | Causes |
|---|---|---|
| Microcytic (<80 fL) | ↓ | IDA, thalassaemia, anaemia of chronic disease, sideroblastic |
| Normocytic (80–100 fL) | Normal | Aplastic anaemia, haemolytic anaemia, acute blood loss, CKD |
| Macrocytic (>100 fL) | ↑ | B12/folate deficiency, liver disease, hypothyroidism, drugs (zidovudine, hydroxyurea, methotrexate) |

**IRON DEFICIENCY ANAEMIA (IDA)** (★)
**Definition:** Most common anaemia worldwide — due to negative iron balance leading to insufficient haem synthesis
**Iron Metabolism — The Pathway:**
- Dietary iron: haem iron (meat) absorbed 20–30% | non-haem iron (plant) absorbed 5–10%
- Absorbed in **duodenum and proximal jejunum** as Fe²⁺ (ferrous form)
- Carried in blood bound to **transferrin**
- Stored as **ferritin** (soluble, reflects body stores — best early marker) and **haemosiderin** (insoluble)
- Regulated by **hepcidin** — liver hormone that blocks ferroportin → ↓ iron absorption and release
-

**Stages of IDA:** (★)
- Stage 1: ↓ ferritin (depleted stores) — no anaemia yet
- Stage 2: ↓ serum iron, ↑ TIBC, ↓ transferrin saturation — iron-deficient erythropoiesis
- Stage 3: ↓ Hb, microcytic hypochromic anaemia — overt IDA
-

**Causes:**
- ↑ Loss: heavy menstrual bleeding (commonest in premenopausal women), GI bleeding (peptic ulcer, colorectal Ca, hookworm)
- ↓ Intake: poor diet, malnutrition
- ↓ Absorption: coeliac disease, gastrectomy, achlorhydria, H. pylori
- ↑ Demand: pregnancy, infancy, adolescent growth spurt
-

**Clinical Features:**
- General anaemia: fatigue, pallor, dyspnoea on exertion, palpitations, headache, dizziness
- IDA-specific signs: (★)
-
 - **Koilonychia** — spoon-shaped nails
- **Angular cheilitis** — cracking at corners of mouth
- **Glossitis** — smooth, beefy-red, painful tongue
- **Pica** — craving non-food substances (ice, soil, clay, paper)
- **Plummer-Vinson syndrome** — IDA + dysphagia + post-cricoid oesophageal web (★)
**Laboratory Findings:** (★)
| Test | IDA Result |
|---|---|
| Hb | ↓ |
| MCV | ↓ (<80 fL) — microcytic |
| MCH | ↓ — hypochromic |
| Serum ferritin | ↓ ← earliest and most sensitive indicator |
| Serum iron | ↓ |
| TIBC (transferrin capacity) | ↑ |
| Transferrin saturation | ↓ (<15%) |
| Reticulocyte count | ↓ or normal |
| PBF | Microcytes, hypochromia, pencil/cigar cells, anisocytosis, poikilocytosis |
| Serum soluble transferrin receptor | ↑ (useful in pregnancy) |
**Confirmatory Tests:**
- Serum ferritin — most sensitive and specific
- Serum iron + TIBC
- Bone marrow aspirate with Prussian blue stain — absent iron stores (gold standard, rarely done)
-

**Treatment:**
- Oral ferrous sulphate (FeSO₄) — first line
- Parenteral iron — if oral not tolerated or malabsorption
- Blood transfusion — only if severely symptomatic or haemodynamically unstable
- Address underlying cause
-

## TOPIC 3: APLASTIC ANAEMIA**Etymology:** *A-* = without | *plastic* = forming — failure to form blood cells
**Definition:**
- Pancytopenia resulting from **bone marrow failure** — destruction or suppression of HSCs
- Results in ↓ RBCs + ↓ WBCs + ↓ platelets
-

**Causes:** (★)
- Idiopathic (~70%) — autoimmune T-cell mediated destruction of HSCs
- **Drugs:** chloramphenicol (★), NSAIDs, anticonvulsants (carbamazepine, phenytoin), chemotherapy, gold
- **Infections:** Hepatitis (non-A, non-B, non-C — most common viral cause), EBV, CMV, **Parvovirus B19** (causes pure red cell aplasia — affects erythroid precursors only) (★)
- Radiation exposure
- Chemicals: benzene
- Inherited: Fanconi anaemia (autosomal recessive, chromosome fragility)
-

**Clinical Features:**
- Anaemia → fatigue, pallor, dyspnoea
- **Neutropenia → recurrent/severe infections** (★)
- **Thrombocytopenia → petechiae, purpura, mucosal bleeding, easy bruising** (★)
- No splenomegaly (distinguishes from other causes of pancytopenia)
-

**Bone Marrow Findings:** (★)
- **Fatty replacement** of haematopoietic tissue
- **Hypocellularity** (<25% cellularity)
- Reduced all cell lines
- Remaining cells are morphologically normal
-

**Investigations:**
- FBC: pancytopenia
- Reticulocyte count: ↓ (hypoproliferative)
- Bone marrow aspirate + trephine biopsy: confirms fatty replacement and hypocellularity (★)
- Cytogenetics: rule out MDS and Fanconi anaemia
-

**Treatment:**
- Mild-moderate: supportive (transfusions, antibiotics, G-CSF)
- Severe: **allogenic bone marrow transplant (BMT)** — curative (★)
- If no donor: **anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG) + ciclosporin** (immunosuppression)
-

## TOPIC 4: HAEMOLYTIC ANAEMIA**Definition:**
- Premature destruction of RBCs — normal RBC lifespan 120 days; in haemolysis significantly shorter
- Compensatory ↑ erythropoiesis (reticulocytosis)
-

**Classification:** (★)
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Intravascular haemolysis | ABO incompatibility, G6PD deficiency, PNH, mechanical valves |
| Extravascular haemolysis | Hereditary spherocytosis, AIHA, sickle cell, thalassaemia |
| Intrinsic (intracorpuscular) | Hereditary spherocytosis, G6PD deficiency, sickle cell, thalassaemia |
| Extrinsic (extracorpuscular) | AIHA, transfusion reactions, microangiopathic HA (TTP, HUS) |
**Laboratory Findings:** (★)
| Test | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Hb | ↓ | RBC destruction |
| Reticulocyte count | **↑** | Compensatory erythropoiesis |
| Serum LDH | **↑** (★) | Released from lysed RBCs |
| Unconjugated bilirubin | ↑ | Hb breakdown → bilirubin |
| Haptoglobin | ↓ | Binds free Hb → consumed |
| Urobilinogen in urine | ↑ | — |
| Haemoglobinuria | Present (intravascular) | Free Hb in urine |
| PBF | Spherocytes / schistocytes / sickle cells depending on cause | — |
- Key: **↑ LDH + ↑ reticulocytes + ↓ haptoglobin + ↑ unconjugated bilirubin** = haemolytic picture (★)
-

## TOPIC 5: THALASSAEMIA**Etymology:** *Thalassa* (Greek) = sea | *Haima* = blood — first described in Mediterranean populations
**Definition:**
- Inherited disorders of **globin chain synthesis** leading to ineffective erythropoiesis and haemolysis
- Result: unstable or insufficient haemoglobin
-

**Normal Haemoglobin:**
- HbA (adult, major) = α₂β₂ — 95–97% of adult Hb
- HbA₂ = α₂δ₂ — 2–3%
- HbF (fetal) = α₂γ₂ — <1% in adults
-

**Types:** (★)
| Type | Defect | Genetics |
|---|---|---|
| α-thalassaemia | Deletion of α-globin genes | Chromosome 16 (HBA1/HBA2) |
| β-thalassaemia | Point mutations in β-globin gene | Chromosome 11 (HBB) |
**β-Thalassaemia Spectrum:** (★)
| Genotype | Disease | Features |
|---|---|---|
| β/β (normal) | — | Normal |
| β/β+ or β/β° | Thalassaemia trait (minor) | Mild microcytic anaemia, target cells, asymptomatic |
| β+/β° or β°/β° | Thalassaemia major (Cooley's anaemia) | Severe transfusion-dependent anaemia |
| Intermediate forms | Thalassaemia intermedia | Between minor and major |
**Thalassaemia Trait — Features:** (★)
- Mild microcytic hypochromic anaemia
- **Target cells (codocytes)** on PBF (★)
- ↑ HbA₂ (>3.5%) on Hb electrophoresis — diagnostic
- Common in Mediterranean, Middle East, SE Asia, sub-Saharan Africa
- Patients often asymptomatic; detected incidentally
-

**Thalassaemia Major — Features:** (★)
- Presents at 6 months (when HbF → HbA switch occurs and no functioning β-chains)
- Severe anaemia — transfusion dependent
- **Primary mechanism: defective globin chain synthesis** (★)
- Excess unpaired α-chains → precipitate → damage RBC membrane → ineffective erythropoiesis + haemolysis
- Hepatosplenomegaly (extramedullary haematopoiesis)
- Bone deformities: **frontal bossing, chipmunk facies** (marrow expansion)
- Iron overload from repeated transfusions → heart failure, liver cirrhosis, endocrinopathies
- PBF: severe microcytosis, hypochromia, target cells, nucleated RBCs, tear-drop cells
-

**Diagnosis:**
- Hb electrophoresis / HPLC (★)
- ↑ HbF, absent or ↓ HbA, ↑ HbA₂ (β-thal trait)
- DNA analysis for specific mutations
- Serum ferritin (iron overload monitoring)
-

**Treatment:**
- Trait: no treatment; genetic counselling
- Major: regular transfusions + **iron chelation** (desferrioxamine or deferasirox) + **allogenic BMT** (curative) (★)
-

## TOPIC 6: VITAMIN B12 DEFICIENCY & PERNICIOUS ANAEMIA**Definition:**
- B12 (cobalamin) deficiency → impaired DNA synthesis → megaloblastic anaemia + neurological damage
- **Pernicious anaemia** = autoimmune destruction of gastric parietal cells → loss of intrinsic factor (IF) → B12 malabsorption (★)
-

**B12 Absorption Pathway:** (★)
- Dietary B12 (meat, dairy, eggs) binds R-protein in stomach
- Gastric **parietal cells** secrete **intrinsic factor (IF)**
- B12–IF complex absorbed in **terminal ileum**
- Chronic atrophic gastritis → no parietal cells → no IF → no B12 absorption → pernicious anaemia
-

**Causes of B12 Deficiency:**
- Pernicious anaemia (autoimmune — most common in developed world) (★)
- Strict veganism / vegetarianism
- Gastrectomy (loss of parietal cells)
- Crohn's disease / ileal resection (loss of absorption site)
- Bacterial overgrowth (fish tapeworm — Diphyllobothrium latum)
-

**Clinical Features:**
- Macrocytic anaemia: fatigue, pallor, glossitis
- **Subacute combined degeneration of spinal cord (SACD)** — B12 needed for myelin synthesis (★)
-
 - Dorsal column loss: vibration, proprioception
- Lateral column loss: spastic weakness
- Peripheral neuropathy
- Psychiatric: depression, memory loss ("megaloblastic madness")
-

**Lab Findings:** (★)
| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Hb | ↓ |
| MCV | ↑ (macrocytic >100 fL) |
| PBF | Macro-ovalocytes, **hypersegmented neutrophils** (★) (>5 lobes) |
| Serum B12 | ↓ |
| Homocysteine | ↑ |
| Methylmalonic acid (MMA) | ↑ (specific for B12 deficiency, not folate) |
| Intrinsic factor antibodies | Positive in pernicious anaemia |
| Parietal cell antibodies | Positive (less specific) |
| Bone marrow | Hypercellular, megaloblasts, giant metamyelocytes |
**Treatment:**
- IM hydroxocobalamin injections (lifelong if pernicious anaemia) (★)
- Oral B12 if dietary deficiency
