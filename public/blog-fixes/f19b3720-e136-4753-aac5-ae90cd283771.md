# Fundamentals of Tuberculosis

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Infectious Diseases and Respiratory Medicine**

> **In one line:** tuberculosis is an airborne infection with *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. After inhalation, most people contain the bacilli as latent infection (granuloma formation by cell-mediated immunity), and about 10% progress to disease, mostly in the lungs. Diagnosis rests on sputum (smear, GeneXpert, culture), the chest X-ray and the tuberculin test, and cure needs a multi-drug regimen (RHZE) taken fully, ideally under observation.

## What you must know

- Causative organism: ***M. tuberculosis***, spread by **droplet nuclei** (cough, sneeze, singing); prolonged contact is needed.
- About **10% of infected people** develop disease over a lifetime (5% in the first 1 to 2 years, 5% later). **HIV is the strongest risk factor.**
- **85%** of TB is pulmonary. **Extrapulmonary TB is not infectious.**
- **Latent TB (LTBI):** no symptoms, not infectious. **TB disease:** bacilli multiply, symptoms, infectious if pulmonary.
- Diagnosis: sputum (3 specimens, before treatment): smear for AFB, **GeneXpert MTB/RIF** (also detects rifampicin resistance), culture. CXR supports but does not confirm.
- Treatment of disease: **RHZE** (rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide, ethambutol). Never add a single drug to a failing regimen. **DOT** gives best adherence.
- **TST:** 0.1 mL of 5 TU PPD intradermally; read induration at 48 to 72 h; cut-offs **5, 10, 15 mm** depending on risk.
- LTBI treatment: **isoniazid daily for 9 months** (or rifampicin 4 months).

## 1. Epidemiology

- TB is a leading infectious cause of death worldwide, and can involve any part of the body. India has the highest burden of TB (about 20% of global cases in the slides).
- **Kenya:** WHO estimated **169,000 cases and 29,000 deaths in 2016** (excluding HIV/TB deaths), making TB the fourth-leading cause of death in the country. About **31% of TB patients with known HIV status are HIV positive**.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa, 2019:** nearly 2.5 million people developed TB and over 660,000 died.

> **Check:** the lecture says TB is the second leading infectious cause of death after HIV/AIDS. Globally TB has ranked first among single infectious agents in most recent years (apart from COVID-19), and India's share is now closer to 25%.

**Factors contributing to the increase in TB**

- The HIV epidemic.
- Immigration from high-prevalence countries.
- Transmission in congregate settings (prisons, long-term care).
- Deterioration of public health infrastructure.

## 2. Transmission and pathogenesis

- Spread person to person through **droplet nuclei** when an infectious person coughs, sneezes, laughs or sings.
- Probability of transmission depends on **infectiousness** of the case, the **environment**, and **length of exposure**. Not everyone exposed becomes infected.

**Sequence of events**

1. Bacilli reach the **alveoli** and are taken up by macrophages, where they begin to replicate (**primary infection**).
2. **Cell-mediated immunity (CMI)** activates and surrounds the infected cells to form a **granuloma** (about 3 weeks).
3. **Caseous necrosis** develops at the site of infection (the **Ghon focus**). Involvement of the draining hilar lymph nodes makes the **Ghon (primary) complex**.
4. The complex usually heals with **calcification**.
5. If immunity contains the bacilli, the result is **latent TB infection**. If not, bacilli multiply and **TB disease** follows.

| | **Latent TB infection (LTBI)** | **TB disease** |
|---|---|---|
| Bacilli | Contained by the immune system | Multiply rapidly |
| Symptoms | None | Present |
| Infectious | No | Yes (if pulmonary) |
| Tuberculin test | Usually positive | Usually positive |
| Chest X-ray | Usually normal | Usually abnormal |
| Sputum smear/culture | Negative | May be positive |
| Treatment | Treat LTBI to prevent disease | Multi-drug treatment |

### Sites of disease

- **Pulmonary TB** (85% of cases).
- **Extrapulmonary TB:** larynx, lymph nodes, brain (meningitis), spine (Pott's disease), kidneys, bones and joints.
- **Miliary TB:** bacilli enter the bloodstream and spread to all organs.

## 3. Who is at risk

Two groups: those **recently infected** and those with conditions that increase the **risk of progression from LTBI to disease**.

**Recent infection**

- Close contacts of infectious TB.
- Skin test converters (within 2 years).
- Recent immigrants from endemic areas.
- Children aged 5 years or younger with a positive TST.
- Residents and staff of high-risk congregate settings (prisons, homeless shelters, healthcare facilities).

**Increased risk of progression**

- **HIV infection**.
- Prior untreated TB.
- Underweight or malnutrition.
- Injection drug use.
- **TNF-alpha antagonists** (rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease).
- Other conditions: diabetes, silicosis, chronic kidney disease, malignancy, immunosuppression.

## 4. Infectiousness and infection control

**Infectious if:**

- Undergoing cough-inducing procedures.
- Sputum smear positive for AFB and: not on treatment, just started treatment, or poor clinical or bacterial response.
- **Cavitary disease.**

**Not infectious if all of:** adequate treatment for 2 to 3 weeks, favourable clinical response, and 3 consecutive negative sputum smears from different days.

**Extrapulmonary TB patients are not infectious** (unless there is concurrent pulmonary disease).

**Reducing transmission**

- Cover the mouth when coughing or sneezing; wear a mask as instructed.
- Open windows for ventilation.
- Avoid work, school and public places until cleared; limit visitors; isolation at home or hospital as ordered.

## 5. Clinical features

| Pulmonary (commonly) | General |
|---|---|
| **Prolonged productive cough** | Fever and chills |
| Chest pain | **Night sweats** |
| **Haemoptysis** | Fatigue |
| | Loss of appetite, **weight loss** |

> **Remember:** in Kenya, cough for 2 weeks or more should trigger TB screening.

## 6. Investigations

**Evaluation:** history, examination, Mantoux test, chest X-ray, bacteriology (smear and culture).

### Chest X-ray

An abnormal chest X-ray alone **cannot confirm** TB; use with other indicators. Obtain for positive TST or suggestive symptoms.

| Finding | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cavity** formation | Post-primary (reactivation) disease |
| Pneumonia-like picture | Primary TB |
| Bilateral hilar adenopathy | Primary pulmonary TB |
| **Miliary** pattern: numerous small nodules like millet seeds | Miliary TB |
| Atypical lesions | HIV-associated TB |
| Dense nodules in hilar and upper lobes | Healed and latent TB |
| Lesions in the **posterior segment of the right upper lobe** | Reactivation TB |
| Pleural effusion | Pleural TB |

### Sputum

- Essential to confirm TB. Must be **lung secretions, not saliva**.
- Collect **3 specimens on 3 different days**, preferably spontaneous early-morning sputum (better than induced). **Collect before treatment.**
- **Smear** for acid-fast bacilli (AFB; Ziehl-Neelsen stain): strongly consider TB if positive. Repeat smears assess **infectiousness and response to treatment**.
- **GeneXpert MTB/RIF:** a cartridge-based nucleic acid amplification test detecting *M. tuberculosis* DNA and **rifampicin resistance** at the same time.
- **Culture:** confirms the diagnosis; culture **all specimens, even smear-negative**. The initial isolate is used for **drug susceptibility testing**.

### Extrapulmonary work-up

- Biopsy (for example bone marrow, lymph node).
- **Lumbar puncture** for TB meningitis.
- **CT/MRI** for brain involvement or Pott's disease.
- Urinalysis and urine cultures for genitourinary TB.

### Tuberculin skin test (TST)

**Targeted testing:** detects LTBI in people who would benefit from treatment; avoid mass screening of low-risk groups; test only if there is an intent to treat.

**Groups to test:** people with or at risk for HIV, close contacts of infectious TB, those with relevant medical conditions, injection drug users, foreign-born people from high-prevalence areas, medically underserved low-income populations, residents of high-risk congregate settings, locally identified high-prevalence groups.

**Technique:** Mantoux method, **0.1 mL of 5 TU PPD** intradermally with a 27-gauge needle, producing a **6 to 10 mm wheal**.

**Reading:** at **48 to 72 hours**; measure **induration, not erythema**. Positive reactions can be read up to 7 days; negative reactions only up to 72 hours. Record in **millimetres**, not "positive/negative", and only trained staff should read.

| Induration | Positive in |
|---|---|
| **5 mm or more** | HIV infected; close contacts of infectious TB; CXR consistent with prior untreated TB; organ transplant recipients; immunosuppressed (equivalent of more than 15 mg/day prednisone for 1 month, or TNF-alpha antagonists) |
| **10 mm or more** | Recent immigrants (within 5 years) from a high-prevalence country; injection drug users; other high-risk medical conditions; residents or staff of high-risk congregate settings; mycobacteriology laboratory staff; children under 4 years; infants, children and adolescents exposed to high-risk adults |
| **15 mm or more** | People with no known risk factors |

**False positive TST:** non-tuberculous mycobacteria (usually 10 mm or less); **BCG vaccination** (wanes with time; a positive result is likely TB infection if risk factors are present).

**False negative TST:** **anergy** (weak immune system, for example advanced HIV), recent TB infection (2 to 10 weeks after exposure), very young age (newborns), recent live virus vaccination, poor technique (too shallow or deep, small wheal).

**Boosting and two-step testing**

- In people with old LTBI, immunity to PPD may fade; the first TST can "boost" it, so later positive tests may be misread as new infection.
- **Two-step testing** (for baseline testing of adults who will be re-tested, for example health workers): if the 1st TST is positive, consider infected; if negative, repeat in **1 to 3 weeks**; if the 2nd is positive, consider infected; if the 2nd is negative, uninfected at baseline.

**Interferon-gamma release assay (QuantiFERON-TB Gold):** a modern alternative to the TST that distinguishes *M. tuberculosis* from other mycobacteria and **BCG**.

## 7. Management

### Latent TB infection

- **Isoniazid daily for 9 months**; monitor for hepatitis and peripheral neuropathy (give pyridoxine).
- Alternative: **rifampicin for 4 months**.

### TB disease

- Initial regimen contains **four first-line drugs: isoniazid (H), rifampicin (R), pyrazinamide (Z), ethambutol (E)**.
- Adjust when susceptibility results are available or for intolerance.
- **Never add a single drug to a failing regimen.**
- Promote adherence and make sure treatment is completed.

> **Remember:** the standard Kenya regimen for new drug-susceptible pulmonary TB is **2 months RHZE then 4 months RH** (2RHZE/4RH), using fixed-dose combinations. Longer courses (for example 12 months) are used for TB meningitis and bone TB. Check the current national guidelines for doses by weight.

| Drug | Main adverse effects |
|---|---|
| **Isoniazid** | Hepatitis, **peripheral neuropathy** (prevent with pyridoxine) |
| **Rifampicin** | Hepatitis, orange discolouration of urine and secretions, **enzyme induction** (drug interactions, including antiretrovirals and contraceptives) |
| **Pyrazinamide** | Hepatitis, hyperuricaemia and arthralgia |
| **Ethambutol** | **Optic neuritis** (visual acuity, colour vision) |

**Directly observed therapy (DOT)**

- A health worker watches the patient swallow each dose. It is the **best way to ensure adherence**, should be used with all intermittent regimens, and **reduces relapse and acquired drug resistance**.

**Clinical monitoring: tell patients to report immediately**

- Rash.
- Nausea, loss of appetite, vomiting, abdominal pain.
- **Persistently dark urine** (hepatitis).
- Fatigue or weakness.
- Persistent numbness of hands or feet.

### Drug resistance

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Infection with a strain already resistant to one or more drugs |
| **Acquired** | Resistance that develops because of inappropriate or inadequate treatment |

- The underlying cause is the mycobacterium's genetic machinery: spontaneous mutation at a certain rate while still growing, so organisms survive otherwise effective drugs. Poor adherence, wrong regimens and single-drug additions select resistant strains.
- **MDR-TB:** resistance to at least rifampicin and isoniazid (GeneXpert shows rifampicin resistance). Needs second-line drugs for a prolonged period, with counselling on adherence and adverse effects.

### Adherence

- **Barriers:** stigma, long duration of treatment, adverse reactions, fear of toxicity, lack of knowledge.
- Adherence is the **responsibility of the provider, not the patient**. Measures: patient education, DOT, case management, incentives and enablers.
- Develop an individualised plan; give culturally and linguistically appropriate care; educate on TB, dosing and adverse reactions; use incentives and enablers; facilitate access to health and social services.
- **Completion of therapy** is based on the **total number of doses**, not the duration; extend or restart if there were frequent or prolonged interruptions.

## 8. Prevention (the TB control cycle)

1. Assess risk factors.
2. If risk is present, perform a TST.
3. If TST is positive, **rule out active disease**.
4. If active disease is ruled out, start treatment for LTBI.
5. If treatment starts, ensure completion.

Other measures: BCG vaccine at birth, infection control, contact tracing, and ART with isoniazid preventive therapy in people with HIV.

## Key points to revise

1. TB is airborne; 10% of the infected develop disease, and HIV is the major driver (about 31% of Kenyan TB patients are HIV positive).
2. Primary infection forms a granuloma and Ghon focus; healing is by calcification; failure of immunity leads to disease.
3. Pulmonary TB is 85% of cases; extrapulmonary TB is not infectious; cavitary smear-positive disease is the most infectious.
4. Diagnose with three sputum specimens: smear, GeneXpert (detects rifampicin resistance), culture; CXR alone cannot confirm.
5. TST cut-offs 5, 10 and 15 mm by risk group; read induration at 48 to 72 hours; BCG and NTM cause false positives, anergy causes false negatives.
6. LTBI: isoniazid 9 months or rifampicin 4 months.
7. TB disease: RHZE initially, then RH; never add a single drug to a failing regimen; use DOT.
8. Resistance is primary or acquired; MDR-TB is at least rifampicin plus isoniazid resistance; completion is by doses taken.
