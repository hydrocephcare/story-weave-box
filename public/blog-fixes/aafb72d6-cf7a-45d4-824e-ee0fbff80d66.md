## MEDICAL ENTOMOLOGY
### Vector Control Methods
#### 1. Physical/Mechanical Control
- Screened windows and doors
- Mosquito nets on walls
- Draining pools of water
- Removing long grass
- Emptying containers

#### 2. Biological Control
- Using living organisms to control vectors
- **Toxorhynchites mosquito species** - larvae feed on larvae of Anopheles, Culex, and Aedes
- **Gambusia affinis** (fish) - feeds on mosquito larvae
- **Bacillus thuringiensis** (Bt bacteria) - bacterial form
- **Bacillus sphaericus**
- Other predators

#### 3. Genetic Engineering
- **Sterile male technique**
- **Refractory vectors** - modifying genes of vectors so that pathogens cannot develop
- Result: change in target site

#### 4. Chemical Control - Insecticides
##### Classification Based on Toxicity (LD50)
- **LD50 1-50**: Extremely toxic/dangerous
- **LD50 50-500**: Moderately toxic
- **LD50 >5000**: Safe

##### Types of Insecticides
**A) Chlorinated Hydrocarbons**

- Relatively stable
- Act as nerve poisons
- Lipid/fat soluble
- Disrupt Na+ channels
- Most are banned (e.g., DDT) due to long persistence and bioaccumulation in fatty tissues
- Examples: DDT, Dieldrin, Endrin

**B) Organophosphates (OP)**

- Less persistent, don't accumulate in fatty tissues
- Block cholinesterase (acetylcholine won't be broken down)
- Examples: Malathion, Chlorpyrifos, Parathion, Diazinon, Dimethoate

**C) Carbamates**

- MOA similar to organophosphates
- Bind to acetylcholinesterase
- Better biodegradability, availability, and solubility
- Examples: Carbaryl, Propoxur, Bendiocarb
- Lower mammalian/human toxicity
- **Advantage over OP**: Chronic exposure to carbamates is less likely to cause illness than organophosphates; inhibiting action is reversible

**D) Pyrethroids**

- Synthetic/semi-natural compounds
- Examples: Permethrin (used in ITNs, LLINs), Deltamethrin
- They bind to voltage gated sodium channels and delay their inactivation. increasing permeability
- Results in excitation, lack of coordination, and paralysis
- Relatively safe for outdoor use
- Effect is rapid (knockdown effect)

**E) Insect Growth Regulators (IGRs)**

- Block insect sugar/chitin biosynthesis pathway
- Example: Pyriproxyfen, Diflubenzuron

**F) Formamidines**

- Mimic activity of octopamine (neurotransmitter/neurohormone/neuromodulator)
- They bind to octopamine receptors in insects, leading to disordered neuronal excitation and death
- MOA is different, useful in resistant insects
- Example: Amitraz
- Effective against mites and ticks
- Examples: Chlordimeform, Amitraz

**G) Avermectins**

- Cause paralysis and death by inhibiting transmission of nerve impulses
- Narrow spectrum, active only against specific invertebrates
- Can also be used against parasitic nematodes
- Example: Abamectin

**H) Neonicotinoids**

- Both systemic and contact activity against sucking insects
- Mimic nicotine
- Example: Imidacloprid

**I) Miticides**

- Effective against mites
- Examples: Bromopropylate, Cyhexatin, Tetradifon

##### Types of Insecticides by Application
- **Contact poison**
- **Fumigant**
- **Systemic**
- **Stomach poison**
- **Respiratory poison**

## MOSQUITO-BORNE DISEASES
### Family: CULICIDAE
#### Genus: ANOPHELES
**Key Species:**

- *Anopheles gambiae* complex (supervector)
- *An. funestus*
- *An. arabiensis* - zoophilic
- *An. melas*
- *An. stephensi* - supervector, transmits malaria mostly in Asia; recently reported in Africa (unlike other African species, can withstand high temperatures and arid conditions)

**Behavior:**

- Anthropophilic
- Endophagic (feeds indoors)
- Endophilic (rests indoors)

**Life Cycle:**

- Complete metamorphosis (holometabolous)
- Duration depends on temperature (optimal 25°C) and humidity (78%)

**Breeding:**

- Stagnant water with vegetation and algae
- Eggs laid singly and laid one at a time
- Float on water surface

**Larval Stage:**

- 4 instars (L1-L4)
- Larva lies parallel to water surface (breathes through spiracles on body side)
- Feeding stage

**Pupal Stage:**

- Resting stage
- Comma-shaped
- Emerges after 2-3 days

**Adult Stage:**

- Emerges and mates post-emergence
- **Females**: Feed on sugar solutions AND blood (require blood meal for egg development - anautogenous)
- **Males**: Only feed on sugar solutions
- Parthenogenesis possible (egg development without fertilization)

**Identification:**

- **Resting position**: 45° angle to surface
- **Antennae**: Males - bushy/plumose; Females - smooth/pilose
- **Feeding**: Only females take blood meals
- Both sexes feed on sugar solutions

**Geographic Distribution:**

- Determined by location of parasite

**Medical Importance:**

- **Primary**: Transmission of malaria (genus *Plasmodium*)

**Control Measures:**

- **Chemical**: Larvicides, breeding site treatment
- **Biological**: *Toxorhynchites* species (T. rutilus) prey on mosquito larvae
- **Genetic**: Refractory index - use of endosymbionts/bacteria that kill mosquito, preventing *Plasmodium* survival
- **Physical/Mechanical**: Draining/removal of containers
- Mosquito nets
- IRS (Indoor Residual Spraying)
- Trans-ovarian light traps
- Screening windows
- Cutting tall grass/weeds
- Mesh on walls

#### Genus: CULEX
**Key Species:**

- *Culex quinquefasciatus*

**Behavior:**

- Exophagic (feeds outdoors)
- Exophilic (rests outdoors)
- Feeding time: 3pm-6pm, nocturnal
- Diurnal feeding: 10am

**Breeding:**

- Contaminated water (sewage, drains)

**Life Cycle:**

- Complete metamorphosis
- Eggs, larvae, pupae, adult stages
- **Eggs**: Laid simultaneously, attached to each other forming a raft; float on water as a raft
- **Larvae**: Breathe via siphon; feed on particles and algae; hang at 45° angle to surface (unlike *Anopheles*)

**Host Detection:**

- Eyes and olfactory cues

**Medical Importance:**

- Transmission of **filarial nematodes** (*Wuchereria bancrofti*)
- Transmission of **arboviruses** (Rift Valley fever, West Nile virus)
- Can transmit virus from female to eggs (transovarial transmission), so adults emerge with virus

**Control:**

- **Breeding site management**: Improving sanitation, installing proper sewage systems
- **Chemical**: Insecticides on larval habitats (spray over 7-10 days; oils/films less effective in presence of organic matter)
- **Genetic engineering**: Light traps
- **Physical**: Wire mesh screens

#### Genus: AEDES
**Key Species:**

- *Aedes aegypti*
- *Aedes albopictus*

**Appearance:**

- Black color with white spots
- Painful biters

**Behavior:**

- "Container breeders" - prefer breeding in clean containers
- Biting time: Early morning and late evening
- Bimodal biting behavior

**Breeding:**

- Clean containers, tires, flower vases
- Both males and females take blood meals during oviposition

**Life Cycle:**

- Similar to *Culex*
- Complete metamorphosis

**Medical Importance:** Both species transmit:

- **Dengue fever**
- **Chikungunya**
- **Zika virus**
- **Yellow fever**
- **O'nyong-nyong**
- **Japanese encephalitis**
- Arboviruses transmitted vertically (transovarial)
- **Rift Valley fever** (mechanical transmission)

**Control:**

- **Physical**: Remove vegetation, predators, drain containers; straighten rivers and remove obstructing material to increase water velocity
- **Chemical**: Insecticides on water surfaces (15-30 min duration)
- Use of Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis)

- **Biological**: Light traps, mosquito-eating fish

### Family: PSYCHODIDAE (Sandflies)
#### Subfamily: PHLEBOTOMINAE
**Distribution:**

- Worldwide
- Old World: Mediterranean, Indian subcontinent, Sub-Saharan Africa, Asia
- New World: Central and South America

**Genera:**

- **Phlebotomus** (Old World) - 4 subgenera
- **Lutzomyia** (New World) - *Lutzomyia umbratilis* (Brazil)
- **Sergentomyia** (zoophilic)

**Bionomics:**

- Oviposition in small cracks, holes in ground, base of termite mounds
- Can breed in chicken coops, poultry houses, animal burrows, rubbish heaps
- Prefer high humidity areas; cannot withstand desiccation
- Complete life cycle

**Behavior:**

- **Females** require blood meal
- Some species are autogenous
- Anthropophilic
- Weak fliers
- Short mouthparts
- Bite exposed body parts
- Bimodal biting pattern: feeding times in early morning and early evening

**Medical Importance:**

- Transmission of **leishmaniasis** (3 forms):*Leishmania donovani* - visceral leishmaniasis
- *Leishmania tropica* - cutaneous leishmaniasis (Old World)
- *Leishmania braziliensis* complex - mucocutaneous leishmaniasis

- Transmission of **Carrion's disease** (*Bartonella bacilliformis*)
- Sandfly fever virus (Phlebovirus)

**Control:**

- **Physical**: Removal of termite mounds
- Personal protection
- Applying efficient insect repellents (DEET, Picaridin)
- Protective clothing
- Timing outdoor activities

- **Chemical**: Spraying insecticides on cracks in walls, termite mounds, animal burrows

- **Habitat Control**: Attempts to destroy reservoir hosts (rodents are reservoir hosts involved in zoonotic transmission)

### Family: CERATOPOGONIDAE (Biting Midges)
**Common Name:** Biting midges, "no-see-ums"

**Distribution:**

- Worldwide in tropics, subtropics, Caribbean, Pacific

**Genera:**

- **Leptoconops** - tropics and continents
- **Culicoides** - worldwide distribution

**Breeding:**

- Prefer ovipositing in wet mud/sand (both salt and fresh water)
- Pupae in soil or mud

**Behavior:**

- Both diurnal and nocturnal
- Very active in wind
- Females prefer feeding on exposed body parts
- Both exophagic and exophilic
- Some *Culicoides* are autogenous

**Medical Importance:**

- Transmission of **Mansonella ozzardi** (filarial nematode)
- In Africa, monkey acts as reservoir host
- Painful bites
- *Culicoides* are biting pests with larvae found in various habitats

**Control:**

- Difficult to achieve by insecticides
- Can be achieved by avoiding activity during peak times
- Personal protection with repellents
- Use of screens/netting

### Family: SIMULIIDAE (Black Flies)
**Common Name:** Black flies

**Distribution:** Worldwide

**Breeding:**

- Along riverbanks in fast-flowing, well-oxygenated water

**Genera:**

- *Simulium*
- *Prosimulium*
- *Cnephia*

**Bionomics:**

- Both males and females feed on plant juices
- Females require blood meal for egg viability
- Few members are autogenous
- Bimodal biting pattern: early morning and early evening (diurnal)
- Exophilic and exophagic
- Prefer feeding on exposed body parts (head and arms more than legs)

**Medical Importance:**

- Transmission of **Onchocerca volvulus** (causes river blindness/onchocerciasis)
- *Simulium metallicum* can transmit **Mansonella ozzardi** (non-pathogenic)
- Bites cause bleeding of skin; wounds predispose to secondary bacterial infection

**Control:**

- **Physical**: Protection by wearing clothing that covers exposed parts
- Application of repellents
- Timing outdoor activities (avoid early morning/evening)

- **Chemical**: Spraying insecticides on larval breeding sites and resting areas
- Applying larvicides on water surfaces (15-30 min duration)
- Use of Bti (Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis)

- **Physical habitat modification**: Straightening rivers
- Remove obstructing material to increase water velocity

### Family: TABANIDAE (Horse Flies, Deer Flies)
**Common Name:** Horse flies, deer flies

**Genera:**

- **Tabanus** (horse fly) - green head, breeds in swamps
- **Chrysops** (deer fly) - yellow/brown
- **Haematopota** (clegs)

**Bionomics:**

- Females feed during day in bright sunlight
- Locate host by sight and olfactory cues
- Purely exophagic except *Chrysops silacea* (attracted by darkness, must hang from objects)
- Diurnal; sight plays major role in attraction along with olfactory cues
- Large object detectors
- Painful bites due to large mouthparts

**Medical Importance:**

- *Tabanus* implicated in transmission of **Bacillus anthracis** (anthrax)
- Also **Borrelia burgdorferi** (Lyme disease - though primarily tick-borne)
- *Chrysops* transmits **Loa loa** in West and Central Africa (*C. silacea* and *C. dimidiata*)

**Control:**

- **Physical**: Creating barriers
- Use of traps (canopy traps)

- **Chemical**: Aerial sprays
- Spraying usually in dry season

- **Habitat management**: Efficient drainage of larval habitats
- Might be problematic due to large areas
- Often cost-prohibitive

- **Personal protection**: Insect repellents
- Use of tags/protective clothing

## KEY CONCEPTS IN VECTOR BIOLOGY
### Vector Types and Transmission
**Based on Maturation of Pathogen:**

- **Cyclopropagative**Disease agent undergoes cyclic development AND multiplies in vector
- Pathogen number increases and develops

- **Cyclodevelopment**Disease agent undergoes cyclic development when fertilized by vector
- Cyclic change but does not multiply in vector body

- **Propagative**Only multiplication stage of disease transmission
- No development, only pathogen number increases

**Passive Vectors:**

- **Mechanical** - no biological development
- Composed of outdoor components only
- Non-living vectors (fomites)

### Development of Resistance
Theme is development of resistance by vectors, leading to:

- Change of target site
- Behavioral resistance

### Sensitivity and Specificity
In relation to thin vs thick blood smears for parasite detection

### Anthrophilic vs Zoophilic
- **Anthropophilic** - preference for human blood meals
- **Zoophilic** - preference for animal blood meals

### Feeding and Resting Behavior
- **Endophagic** - feeds indoors
- **Exophagic** - feeds outdoors
- **Endophilic** - rests indoors
- **Exophilic** - rests outdoors

### Mosquito Egg Development
- **Autogenous** - can develop eggs without blood meal
- **Anautogenous** - requires blood meal for egg development

### Reproduction
- **Parthenogenesis** - form of asexual reproduction where offspring develop without fertilization

## MYIASIS
**Definition:** The invasion of organs and tissues of humans and animals by fly larvae

**Larvae feed on:**

- Living tissues
- Dead tissues of host
- Ingested food (in case of intestinal myiasis)

### Types of Myiasis
#### 1. Accidental Myiasis
- Occurs when an individual consumes food contaminated with eggs or larvae of flies that are not parasitic mammals, such as houseflies
- Larvae may survive for some time in the intestine and result in pathological effects:Diarrhea
- Dysentery
- Abdominal pain
- Vomiting

- Larvae may be passed alive or vomited
- No species is adapted to cause intestinal myiasis in humans

#### 2. Obligatory Myiasis
- Occurs in mammals, not just humans
- Maggots/larvae must live in a live host for at least part of their life cycle

#### 3. Facultative Myiasis
- Larvae are free-living
- Attack carcasses but can infect living tissues

### Pathogenic Forms
#### A) Urogenital Myiasis
- Flies are attracted to distal areas which are dirty (in hospital areas, especially)
- Fly deposits eggs, which hatch immediately and larvae enter urethra

#### B) Cutaneous/Dermal Myiasis (Furuncular Myiasis)
- Also called "creeping eruption"
- Boil-like inflammation of wound infected with larvae
- Only wounds attract flies which lay eggs; eggs hatch and larvae develop under skin

**Treatment:** Remove larvae under aseptic conditions

#### C) Ophthalmic/Ocular Myiasis
- Flies are attracted to eyes which are dirty
- Can cause larvae (maggots) to invade and develop in eyes in both humans and animals
- Fly deposits eggs on/near the eye, the larvae feed on host's living eye tissues
- **Symptoms:** Lacrimation, redness, pain
- **Treatment: **Removal of larvae
- If larvae deeply embedded in tissues or mucous membranes, eyes may need to be enucleated
- If damage by larvae is irreversible, result is eye removal

- **Prevention:** Personal hygiene, eyewear

#### D) Nasopharyngeal Myiasis
- Major route is nose
- Larvae crawl down to nasopharynx and can cause:Tonsillitis
- Tonsillar irritation

#### E) Intestinal Myiasis
- Route of entry is mouth
- Food contaminated with eggs/larvae is swallowed

### Maggot Therapy
**Application:** Live and sterile larvae of *Lucilia sericata* used in wound management

- Larvae clean the wounds by feeding on dead tissue

## ORDER: SIPHONAPTERA (Fleas)
### Distribution
- Worldwide distribution
- Associated with people and animals

### Medical Importance - Key Genera
- **Xenopsylla cheopis**
- **Ctenocephalides** (*C. felis*, *C. canis*)
- **Tunga penetrans** (chigoe/jigger flea)
- **Pulex irritans** (human flea)

### Xenopsylla Cheopis
- Vector for **Yersinia pestis** (plague)
- Common in the tropics due to warm temperature
- Also transmits **Rickettsia typhi** (murine typhus)

### Ctenocephalides
**Species:** *C. felis* (cat flea), *C. canis* (dog flea)

**Medical Importance:**

- Fleas of this genus may be intermediate hosts of cestodes:*Dipylidium caninum*
- *Hymenolepis diminuta*

- Also transmit bacteria causing cat scratch disease (*Bartonella henselae*)

### Tunga Penetrans
- Also known as "chigoe" or "jigger flea"
- Burrows into people's feet
- The gravid female flea penetrates the host and deposits eggs in the dermis

### Pulex Irritans
- Human flea
- Parasite causing dermatitis in allergic individuals

### Bionomics (General for Fleas)
**Feeding:**

- Both sexes feed on blood
- Both are ectoparasites

**Life Cycle:**

- **Egg Stage: **Female flea locates the host and deposits eggs in the debris/dust in the habitat/dwelling places (e.g., rodent burrow)
- For human or domestic animal fleas, they lay eggs while still on the host
- Since the eggs are sticky, they soon fall off the host and are found in areas where host spends their time

- **Larval Stage: **Eggs hatch into larvae (feed on any organic matter/debris)
- To successfully achieve full development to adulthood, the larvae must feed on partly digested blood meal regurgitated from alimentary canal of adult fleas or feces
- At the end of larval period, the larvae spin a silken cocoon from protein secreted by its salivary gland
- Being sticky, it becomes covered with fine particles of dust and debris/sand picked up from floor of host's dwelling

- **Pupal Stage:**4-12 days (duration 7-8 days) is spent in pupal stage inside the cocoon
- Time is dependent on temperature and stimulus

- **Adult Stage: **Emerges and seeks host

**Host Preference:**

- Although feeding preference and host blood type keeps flea alive, their fertility is determined by preferred host
- Some can withstand considerable hunger/starvation
- When no suitable host is available, they rapidly abandon their (dead) hosts in search of living host
- Presenting a new wave of plague transmission
- Portions move and feed on horses/livestock in absence of humans

**Movement:**

- On the host, they move rapidly by crawling
- Have a protein called **resilin** in their legs - highly compressed, produces rapid expansion by the compressed state, giving the power of jumping

### Medical Importance of Fleas
#### 1. Flea Nuisance
- Cat flea (*Ctenocephalides felis*) and dog flea bite people on ankles/lower legs or any exposed body part
- Hypersensitive people develop dermatitis
- Inhalation of flea feces causes allergic reactions

#### 2. Plague
- Medically most important: **bubonic plague**
- People have contracted plague when they are hunters handling wild mammals infected with plague

#### 3. Murine Typhus (*Rickettsia typhi*)
- Transmitted by rodent fleas, especially *X. cheopis*

#### 4. Tularemia (*Francisella tularensis*)
- Can be transmitted by examining the site

### Control of Fleas
- **Repellents** - DEET on clothing
- **Rodent fleas and larvae** - use hand-operated dusters to apply insecticidal dusts such as:Carbaryl
- Pyrethroids
- Organophosphates applied to animals/pets

- **For cat and dog fleas:** Insecticides (pyrethroids and OPs) are applied to animals

**Three types of blood-sucking lice in humans:**

- **Body Louse** - *Pediculus humanus corporis*
- **Head Louse** - *Pediculus humanus capitis*
- **Pubic/Crab Louse** - *Pthirus pubis*

### BODY LOUSE (*Pediculus humanus corporis*)
#### Epidemiology
- Worldwide distribution
- Common in densely populated slums
- Associated with poor hygiene conditions

#### Medical Importance
- **Pediculosis** - condition known as "lousy disease"They feed on blood due to repeated injection of saliva when they bite
- Bite several times a day
- Results in intense itchiness/irritability or pessimistic mood
- Dermatitis, severe itching
- May have some type of asthma or bronchitis

- **Risk of secondary bacterial infection** such as impetigo (which is contagious)
- **Results into fever** due to lateral scratching
- **Vector-borne diseases transmitted transcutaneously:****Epidemic typhus** (*Rickettsia prowazekii*)
- **Relapsing fever** (*Borrelia recurrentis*)
- **Trench fever** (*Bartonella quintana*)

#### Control of Body Louse
- Louse-infested clothing is subjected to washing or ironing
- Dry cleaning
- Impregnation of clothing with pyrethroid insecticides may provide long-lasting protection (permethrin)
- Trials have shown that orally administered ivermectin kills lice

### HEAD LOUSE (*Pediculus humanus capitis*)
#### Medical Importance
- Causes **pediculosis capitis**
- Can result in loss of hair (alopecia)

#### Control
- Regular combing of hair
- Shaving of head hair
- Use of insecticidal formulas/lotions/shampoos

### PUBIC/CRAB LOUSE (*Pthirus pubis*)
#### Description
- Eggs are attached to lice and composed of both louse attached to hair shaft
- Unlike head lice, crab lice may be laid on a single hair strand
- Can be found on:Beard
- Armpits
- Occasionally chest hair

#### Medical Importance
- Cause **pediculosis pubis** (pubic lice infestation)
- Due to deposition of feces around genital area
- Small blue spots/maculae may appear on infested part of skin

#### Control
- **Shaving** pubic hair
- **Insecticidal formulations: **Dusts
- Lotions
- Emulsions (not alcohol-based in genital region)
- Malathion (may cause irritation in genital area)

- **On eyelashes:** Apply small amount of petroleum jelly twice a day for 8-10 days
- After pubic lice treatment, spray/dust or apply emulsion/wash affected areas with water, then iron and dry in sunlight

## ORDER: HEMIPTERA
### Family: CIMICIDAE (Bedbugs)
#### Key Species
- **Cimex lectularius** - widely distributed in tropical and non-tropical countries
- **Cimex hemipterus** - commonly found in old and new world tropics; although *C. lectularius* is found in warm areas of some non-tropical countries (e.g., Florida)
- **Leptocimex** - found mainly in Africa; also recorded in Asia

#### Epidemiology
**Increase in bedbug infestation due to:**

- Global resistance to insecticides
- Warmer temperatures

#### Bionomics
**Feeding:**

- Both sexes take blood meals
- They prefer to feed during the night
- During sunshine, they often hide
- However, in temperate regions, they may remain active

**Resting:**

- During the day, adults and nymphs hide underneath:Loose wallpaper
- Mattresses
- Beds
- Cracks in walls

- They rest as a group

#### Medical Importance
- **Cause restless/sleepless nights**
- **Repeated and vigorous feeding** can contribute to iron deficiency, especially in:Infants
- Young children
- Elderly people

- **Hepatitis B**But no evidence that they transmit any disease to people

#### Control
- **Mechanical transmission** of enteropathogens (*Shigella*, *E. coli*, *Salmonella*)

## TICKS
### Two Main Families
- **ARGASIDAE (Soft Ticks)**
- **IXODIDAE (Hard Ticks)**

### ARGASIDAE (Soft Ticks)
#### Key Genera
- *Ornithodoros* - *O. moubata*
- *Argas*

#### Distribution
- Found in warm areas
- Vector of **tick-borne relapsing fever** (*Borrelia duttoni*)

#### Characteristics
**Nidicolous Ticks:**

- As opposed to non-nidicolous (hard ticks) which quest from the host environment
- Nymphs and adults are blood-feeding stages
- Remain for short period of time (hours) on host compared to hard ticks
- Have **multiple host types** - called "multi-host tick"
- **Multiple nymphal stages** (N1, N2, N3, N4) - usually 4 or 5 nymphal instars

#### Medical Importance
- Transmit bacteria **Borrelia** (relapsing fever)
- Transmission of spirochetes
- Viruses
- **Tick paralysis** due to injection of toxins in saliva

#### Control
- **Repellents** - DEET
- **Impregnated nets** with permethrin
- **Use of acaricides** on furniture/walls which harbor ticks

### IXODIDAE (Hard Ticks)
#### Distribution
- Worldwide but most common in temperate regions

#### Key Genera
- **Dermacentor**
- **Amblyomma**
- **Haemaphysalis**
- **Rhipicephalus**
- **Hyalomma**
- **Boophilus** (1-host tick)

#### Characteristics
**Life Cycle:**

- **Hemimetabolous** (incomplete metamorphosis)
- **Non-nidicolous**
- **1 nymphal stage**
- Host stays on host for longer period compared to soft ticks

**Tick Types Based on Host Number:**

##### 3-Host Tick
- Eggs → Larvae → Nymphs → Adult
- Each stage feeds on different host
- **Examples:** *Dermacentor*, *Amblyomma*, *Rhipicephalus*, *Hyalomma*

##### 2-Host Tick
- Eggs hatch into larvae
- Larvae molt into nymphs on first host
- Nymphs drop off and seek second host
- **Examples:** *Rhipicephalus*, *Hyalomma*

##### 1-Host Tick
- Eggs hatch into larvae on the host
- Larvae molt into nymphs on host
- Nymphs molt into adults on host
- **Example:** *Boophilus*

**Questing Behavior:**

- Larvae have questing behavior
- Climb up vegetation (grass, stems, limbs)
- Wait for host fur contact
- Drop onto host

#### Medical Importance
- **Tick paralysis** - toxins in female saliva pumped into host during feeding periodFollowed by difficulties in:Breathing
- Swallowing
- Speaking

- **Transmission of arboviruses: **Q fever
- Tick-borne encephalitis
- In people with allergies - can cause poliomyelitis

- **Lyme disease** (*Borrelia burgdorferi*)

#### Control
- **Repellents** - DEET
- **Regular dipping of livestock** with acaricides
- **Regular spraying of gardens**
- **Proper removal** - pulling it out carefully
- **Impregnated clothing** with permethrin

### Why are ticks hard to control?
- **Nutrient requirements** - can survive long periods without feeding
- **Host adaptability** - ticks have the ability to adapt to various host types
- This increases their chances of survival as parasites
- Vectors for getting hosts for endurance

## MITES
### Family: SARCOPTIDAE
**Causative Agent:** *Sarcoptes scabiei* - scabies mite

#### Distribution
- Worldwide distribution
- Causes **mange** in animals
- Causes **scabies** in humans

#### Characteristics
- Are arachnids
- Don't have teeth on the hypostome of their mouth
- Have **setae** (bristles) on body structures for sensing

### Diagnosis
**By detection of:**

- **Female mites** burrowing into the skin to deposit eggs
- **Narrow, twisting tunnels** on the skin may be visible as dark pepper-like tracks
- **On dark skin:** Smear a drop of ink on suspected areaAfter a few minutes when ink is wiped off, tunnels will be visible

- **Alternative:** Use liquid tetracycline under UV light - tunnels fluoresce bright yellow-green

### Types of Scabies
#### 1. Classic Scabies Rash
- Papular eruption (non-painful)
- Mainly affects interdigital web spaces
- Also affects:Wrists
- Elbows
- Armpits
- Genitals
- Other body parts

- Does **NOT** appear at sites above the head

**Response produced:** Due to bite of the burrowing mite

**After first exposure:**

- A few number of mites can cause rash
- One may have severe pruritus (itching) followed by:Constant itching and scratching, especially at night
- Worse after taking hot bath
- Scratching can lead to secondary bacterial infections

**Control:** Use of scabicide on surface layer

#### 2. Crusted/Norwegian Scabies
- Highly contagious
- As a result, numerous numbers of mites on skin
- Skin becomes scaly and hyperkeratotic
- Can spread to other people easily
- Mite adheres to patients and infects others

**Scraping:** Affected skin areas with a scalpel can reveal the mites

### Treatment of Scabies
#### 1. Creams and Emulsions
**A) Permethrin Cream (5%):**

- Careful application (2 applications - one now, one later)
- Apply from neck downwards
- Wash off after 8-12 hours
- For complicated cases: Re-application after 24 hours

**B) Benzyl Benzoate:**

- Apply, then wash after 24 hours for parent/adult
- Re-application then wash on 2nd and 3rd day, then cure

**C) Crotamiton Cream/Lotion:**

- 5 daily applications for one week
- Can be used in young children and pregnant women

**D) Ivermectin:**

- Single dose
- Topical application

### Control of Scabies
- **It's contagious** - treat all family members/community members
- **Clothes and bedding** should be disinfected:Dry cleaning
- Dusting the clothes/beddings with insecticides

- **Bed linen, clothes, and infant mattresses: **Wash with hot water
- Then iron and dry in sunlight
- Can also be wiped in sealed plastic bag and placed in freezer for 48 hours to kill the bedbugs

- **Antihistamines** to control itching
- **Proper construction of houses**

## Family: TROMBICULIDAE (Chigger Mites)
### Key Species
- *Leptotrombidium deliense*
- *L. akamushi*
- *L. fletcheri*
- *Eutrombicula alfreddugesi* (red bug)
- *Trombicula autumnalis* (harvest mite)

### Life Cycle
**Adults:**

- Adults bite and feed on vertebrates inhabiting arthropod-rich areas
- Female lays 1-5 spherical eggs in moist but well-drained soil (e.g., riverbank)

**Larval Stage:**

- Only larvae are parasitic
- Attach to small mammals so they will have hosts on which to feed
- Rodents are important local hosts

**Nymphs and Adults:**

- Free-living
- Inhabit habitats containing suitable small arthropods for feeding

### Ecology
- Humans are **accidental hosts**, important in aiding dispersal of larvae to other areas
- Support for larvae away from vegetation
- Mites are frequently found in agricultural areas/pastures which create scrub vegetation following agricultural processes

### Medical Importance
- **Nuisance:** Harvest mites (*Trombicula autumnalis*), red bugs (*Eutrombicula alfreddugesi*) cause:Intense irritation
- Itchy welts/rash

- **Transmission of scrub typhus** (caused by *Rickettsia tsutsugamushi*/*Orientia tsutsugamushi*)

### Control
- **Repellents:** DEET, benzyl benzoate
- **Clothing impregnated/sprayed** with repellents or permethrin
- **Burning scrub vegetation**
- **Insecticidal applications** by aircraft
