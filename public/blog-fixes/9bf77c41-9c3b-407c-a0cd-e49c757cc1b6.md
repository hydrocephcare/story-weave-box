# Approach to a Child with Chronic Diarrhoea and Constipation

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Paediatric gastroenterology**

> **In one line:** chronic diarrhoea (lasting over 2 weeks) in a child is approached by **age of onset, relation to feeds, growth and the mechanism** (osmotic, secretory, dysmotility or inflammatory); early-onset diarrhoea is **congenital or infective**, diarrhoea starting with a new food points to **intolerance, allergy or malabsorption**, and the first question is always whether the child is **growing normally**. Constipation is approached separately, mostly as a functional problem.

## What you must know

- Chronic diarrhoea = persisting **more than 2 weeks**; split cases into **with** and **without** growth failure.
- Four mechanisms: **osmotic, secretory, dysmotility, inflammatory**.
- **Early diarrhoea (from day 1)**: congenital (chloride diarrhoea, sodium diarrhoea, microvillus inclusion disease, tufting enteropathy, autoimmune enteropathy) or infection.
- **Milk-triggered**: glucose-galactose malabsorption, lactase deficiency, cow's milk protein allergy.
- **Cereal-triggered** after 6 months: **coeliac disease** (anti-tTG/EMA positive + villous atrophy; HLA-DQ2/DQ8).
- **Pink, well-grown toddler with food particles in the stool**: toddler diarrhoea (chronic non-specific diarrhoea); reassure.
- **Pale, underweight child**: investigate.
- Cystic fibrosis: sweat chloride **above 70 mmol/L**.
- Do not treat amoebic **cysts** as disease; amoebiasis needs trophozoites ingesting red cells.
- Nutrition is the cornerstone of treatment whatever the cause.

## 1. Introduction and pathophysiology

Recurrent, chronic infantile diarrhoea with malnutrition kills about **4.6 million** children globally each year. Prevention has improved through renewed emphasis on **breastfeeding**, less use of partial starvation regimens during diarrhoea, and wider availability of age-appropriate infant food.

> **Remember:** early diarrhoea is either **congenital or infective**.

| Mechanism | Explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Osmotic** | Failure to absorb a luminal solute; water is retained across an osmotic gradient. Stops on fasting | Lactose intolerance, sugar malabsorption |
| **Secretory** | Net secretion of electrolytes and fluid without compensatory absorption. **Continues despite fasting** | Congenital chloride diarrhoea, microvillus inclusion disease, VIPoma, cholera |
| **Dysmotility** | Intestinal transit time is short, so there is less time for absorption though absorptive ability is intact | Hyperthyroidism |
| **Inflammatory** | May combine all of the above | IBD, infection |

## 2. General approach

1. **History**: onset (neonatal, with milk, with fruit juice, with cereals), stool character (watery, bloody, fatty, food particles), relation to feeds, duration, associated symptoms (cough, rashes, abscesses, fever), diet, water source, antibiotic use, family history and consanguinity.
2. **Examination**: pallor, weight and height (plot growth), oedema, abdominal distension, clubbing, rashes, lymph nodes, spleen, perianal region.
3. **Triage**: pale and underweight children need investigation. A **pink child with normal weight** can usually be reassured.
4. **Investigations** (see section 14) guided by the likely diagnosis.

## 3. Case 1: diarrhoea from day 1 of life

| Condition | Key features |
|---|---|
| **Congenital chloride diarrhoea** | Autosomal recessive defect in chloride transport. Maternal **polyhydramnios**, prematurity, abdominal distension, diarrhoea. **Hypokalaemia, hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis**, faecal chloride greater than faecal sodium plus potassium, **hyperbilirubinaemia in 90%**. Treatment: **sodium chloride and potassium chloride supplements** |
| **Congenital sodium diarrhoea** | Autosomal recessive defect of the jejunal sodium/proton exchanger causing severe watery diarrhoea; **SPINT2** gene (19q13.1; a serine protease inhibitor). **Polyhydramnios** is the first manifestation. **Hyponatraemia, metabolic acidosis** |
| **Microvillous atrophy (microvillus inclusion disease)** | **Watery diarrhoea despite the patient being nil by mouth.** Congenital form: onset in the first week; late-onset: after the neonatal period (1-2 months). Biopsy: villous atrophy and **intracytoplasmic inclusions lined by intact microvilli**. Treatment: **total parenteral nutrition (TPN)**, **intestinal transplantation** |
| **Intestinal epithelial dysplasia (tufting enteropathy)** | Early severe intractable watery diarrhoea within days; growth impaired; no history of hydramnios; **dysmorphic features**; associated with choanal or oesophageal atresia, imperforate anus. Biopsy: persistent villous atrophy, epithelial disorganisation, **focal enterocyte crowding resembling tufts**, dilated crypts with pseudo-cysts. Treatment: TPN, intestinal transplant |
| **Autoimmune enteropathy** | Severe protracted watery diarrhoea in infancy or toddlerhood, alone or with diabetes as part of **IPEX** (immune dysregulation, polyendocrinopathy, enteropathy, X-linked; **FOXP3** mutation). Circulating antibodies to enterocytes, anti-smooth muscle, antithyroid and islet cell antibodies. Treatment: TPN, **prednisone, ciclosporin, azathioprine**, intestinal transplant |

## 4. Case 2: diarrhoea starting with milk feeds

Milk contains lactose (broken into glucose and galactose), protein, vitamins, immunoglobulins, lipids, water and minerals.

### 4.1 Glucose-galactose (monosaccharide) malabsorption
- Rare, autosomal recessive; onset with the first milk feeds (breast or bottle).
- Watery diarrhoea, dehydration, metabolic acidosis; **hypernatraemia** and **nephrocalcinosis**; can cause a very febrile infant.
- **Diarrhoea stops within an hour of stopping lactose, glucose and galactose and returns on reintroduction.**
- Treatment: **fructose-based formula** (fructose is absorbed passively), with immediate response.

### 4.2 Lactase deficiency

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **Developmental** | Relative deficiency in preterm infants under **34 weeks** or low birth weight (immature gut) |
| **Primary** | Relative or absolute absence of lactase; develops in childhood at different ages in different racial groups; **commonest cause of lactose malabsorption** |
| **Secondary** | After small bowel injury: acute gastroenteritis, persistent diarrhoea, small bowel overgrowth, cancer chemotherapy; at any age, commoner in infancy |

- Treatment: remove lactose or pre-digest it with lactase enzyme (**lactose-free formula**). Provide **calcium** from non-dairy sources or supplements.
- Chemical **napkin dermatitis** may be secondary to disaccharide malabsorption (acidic stools; positive reducing substances).

## 5. Case 3: diarrhoea with fruit juice

**Congenital sucrase-isomaltase deficiency**: watery osmotic diarrhoea with fruit, fruit juice, gummies or sweets; abdominal distension; irritability in older children; growth may be normal. Treatment: **avoid sucrose and fructose-containing foods**; supplement **sacrosidase** (not available locally). Sorbitol (diabetic sweeteners) can also cause diarrhoea, distension and gas.

## 6. Case 4: persistent diarrhoea after gastroenteritis

### 6.1 Post-gastroenteritis syndrome
A transient condition giving persistent diarrhoea (over 14 days) after gastroenteritis, from temporary lactose intolerance and cow's milk protein sensitisation with villous damage. Diagnose with **positive Clinitest and negative Clinistix** (reducing substances but no glucose). Resolves with a **cow's milk protein-free and lactose-free diet**.

### 6.2 Bacterial causes of chronic diarrhoea

| Organism | Source | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Aeromonas** | Untreated water | 1 week to 1 year |
| **Campylobacter** | Raw poultry, animals, unpasteurised milk, water | 5 days to chronic |
| **Clostridium difficile** | Antibiotic use; nosocomial | 10% relapse |
| **Plesiomonas shigelloides** | Untreated water, shellfish | 2 weeks to months |
| **Salmonella** | Poultry, faecal-oral, water | 5 days to months in infants |
| **Yersinia enterocolitica** | Raw pig intestines (chitterlings) | 3 weeks to 3 months |
| **E. coli** | Enteric pathotypes may become chronic from persistent bowel injury; **enterotoxigenic and mucosa-adherent** strains cause watery, prolonged diarrhoea; **enterohaemorrhagic** strains cause colitis and **haemolytic-uraemic syndrome** | |

The commonest bacterial causes are **E. coli, Salmonella and Shigella**.

### 6.3 Parasitic causes

| Organism | Source | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Giardia lamblia** | Diapered infants, faecal-oral, water | 2 weeks to years |
| **Cryptosporidium parvum** | Child care, petting zoos, swimming pools | 1-2 weeks (6 weeks reported) |
| **Cyclospora cayetanensis** | Raspberries, water, unpasteurised cider | 1 week to 1 month or more |
| **Entamoeba histolytica** | Faecal-oral, water | Weeks |
| **Isospora belli** | Faecal-oral, water | Chronic |
| **Strongyloides stercoralis** | Developing countries | Chronic |
| **Blastocystis** | Uncertain if a pathogen | |

> **Exam tip:** **Entamoeba histolytica** is widely over-diagnosed. Amoebic **cysts** are found in about 80% of some populations and mean nothing; true amoebiasis is diagnosed by seeing amoebae **ingesting red blood cells**. Do not give a 5-week course of metronidazole for cysts.

**Giardiasis**: diarrhoea (64-100%), malaise and weakness (72-97%), abdominal distension (42-97%), flatulence (35-97%), cramps (44-81%), nausea (14-79%), **foul-smelling greasy stools** (15-79%), anorexia (41-73%), weight loss (53-73%), vomiting (14-35%); rarely anasarca from protein-losing enteropathy. Diagnosis: microscopy of stool (or biopsy); antigen tests are more sensitive.

**Cryptosporidium**: infection follows ingestion from faecal contamination of hands; combined Giardia-Cryptosporidium antigen tests have better sensitivity.

### 6.4 Intractable diarrhoea of infancy (IDI)
Also called post-enteritis enteropathy, protracted diarrhoea of infancy or secondary disaccharidase deficiency. Enteric infection plus poor intake and absorption causes loss of digestive and absorptive capacity. Recurrent diarrhoea and failure to regain weight. Suspect further when there is **no breastfeeding**, diluted or clear liquid feeds, or restricted intake in a misguided effort to reduce diarrhoea or vomiting; severe malnutrition causes malabsorption. Treatment: **lactose-free, sucrose-free formula**, short IV hydration, **TPN** if no improvement.

## 7. Case 5: diarrhoea with chronic cough and recurrent skin abscesses

Think **immunodeficiency**. Chronic diarrhoea is a common complication. Examine lymph nodes, spleen, skin, peripheral blood film and joints. Associated disorders:
- HIV infection
- SCID (Rag1, Rag2, JAK3, ZAP70, Omenn syndrome)
- X-linked agammaglobulinaemia
- Hyper IgM syndrome
- Common variable immunodeficiency
- **Chronic granulomatous disease** (recurrent skin abscesses)
- Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome
- MHC class II deficiency
- Selective IgA deficiency
- IPEX syndrome

> **Remember:** in Kenya, always do an HIV test in a child with chronic diarrhoea and failure to thrive.

## 8. Case 6: diarrhoea with rash and wheeze after formula or fruit (dietary protein enteropathy)

- **Onset** depends on age of exposure. Cow's milk and soy cause failure to thrive up to 2 years.
- **Proteins**: cow's milk (commonest), soy, cereal, egg, fish.
- **Pathology**: variable villous injury, increased crypt length, patchy; intra-epithelial lymphocytes; few eosinophils. Associated with IgA deficiency, IgG subclass abnormalities and atopy.
- **Features**: even bloody diarrhoea, malabsorption, failure to thrive, vomiting, distension, anaemia, oedema, **hypoproteinaemia (protein-losing enteropathy)**, skin rashes and wheeze. About **40%** with cow's milk protein allergy are also allergic to soy.
- **Diagnosis**: negative anti-endomysial antibody; radiology showing small bowel oedema; **food challenge** (normal formula after hypoallergenic formula) giving vomiting or diarrhoea within **40-72 hours**; raised IgE and eosinophilia; or a **2-week trial of cow's milk protein elimination** without biopsy.
- **Treatment**: strict elimination of the offending antigen with **hypoallergenic (HA) formula**; most resolve in **2-3 years**.

> **Remember:** the baby may react to maternal diet through breast milk, but it is poor practice to tell a mother to avoid chicken, fish and milk wholesale (malnutrition). Treat the baby first and remove only the food proven to be the problem.

## 9. Case 7: diarrhoea after cereals and biscuits (coeliac disease)

- **Onset** depends on when gluten is introduced, typically **after 6 months**.
- **Antigen**: gluten in wheat, rye, barley and possibly oats.
- **Pathology**: extensive **villous atrophy**, elongated crypts, increased intraepithelial lymphocytes.
- **Genetics**: **HLA-DQ2** (and DQ8).
- **Presentation**: chronic diarrhoea (now often absent and never bloody), abdominal distension, short stature, **iron or folate deficiency anaemia**, rickets, failure to thrive (marasmus), oedema (protein-losing enteropathy), petechiae (vitamin K deficiency), abdominal pain, hepatomegaly with raised LFTs, buttock wasting, irritability.
- **Associations**: dermatitis herpetiformis, type 1 diabetes, thyroid disease, Down syndrome, IgA deficiency (screen these patients).
- **Diagnosis (new criteria)**: positive **anti-tissue transglutaminase or endomysial antibodies** plus **villous atrophy on small bowel biopsy**. (Old criteria needed three biopsies: at diagnosis, after gluten-free diet, and after gluten challenge.)
- **Treatment**: lifelong **gluten elimination**.

> **Check:** check total IgA (or use IgG-based tests) because IgA deficiency gives false-negative anti-tTG.

## 10. Case 8: chronic diarrhoea with lymphoedema or ataxia

### 10.1 Intestinal lymphangiectasia
Dilated intestinal lymphatics with impaired fat absorption; may be familial or secondary to fibrosis. Features: **protein-losing enteropathy**, lymphoedema and diarrhoea, **hypoalbuminaemia, hypogammaglobulinaemia, lymphopenia**, whitish spots on the mucosa, chylous ascites, systemic infections, generalised lymphatic abnormalities. Diagnosis: barium follow-through (oedema of the intestine), protein loss by **Cr-labelled albumin**, biopsy showing lymphatic dilatation. Treatment: low-fat diet with MCT.

### 10.2 Abetalipoproteinaemia
Autosomal recessive; **MTP gene (4q22)**, which transfers lipid to apolipoprotein B. Features: **ataxia and retinitis pigmentosa**, fat malabsorption, failure to thrive. Diagnosis: very low cholesterol, triglycerides and phospholipids; **acanthocytes** on blood film; biopsy with normal villi but fat droplets in enterocytes. Treatment: **low-fat diet with MCT** and **fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K**.

## 11. Case 9: diarrhoea with skin rash and hair loss

**Acrodermatitis enteropathica**: autosomal recessive; **SLC39A4** (8q24.3), a zinc transporter. Chronic diarrhoea and failure to thrive, dermatitis around the mouth and anus (perioral and perianal), **alopecia**. Diagnosis: low plasma zinc and **low alkaline phosphatase**. Treatment: **zinc sulphate 150 mg/day orally**.

## 12. Case 10: diarrhoea with growth failure and chest infections

### 12.1 Cystic fibrosis
- Autosomal recessive.
- **Neonatal**: meconium ileus or bowel perforation, intra-abdominal calcification, ileal atresia, cholestasis.
- **Respiratory**: recurrent or persistent cough with wheeze, **staphylococcal pneumonia**; later Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
- Older children: chest or bowel disease, **meconium ileus equivalent** (distension and constipation), nasal polyps, male sterility, arthritis.
- **Malabsorption**: large, pale, bulky, offensive, watery or foamy stools; failure to thrive; **rectal prolapse**; electrolyte imbalance with **hyponatraemia**; rarely heat stroke.
- **Diagnosis**: **sweat chloride above 70** (mmol/L) or genetic testing.
- **Treatment**: chest physiotherapy and antibiotics, **pancreatic enzyme replacement**, high-calorie diet, fluid and salt intake (especially in hot weather).

### 12.2 Short gut syndrome
Surgical resection of small bowel, volvulus, adhesions (also congenital short gut with malrotation).

### 12.3 VIP-secreting tumours
Paediatric causes: ganglioneuroma (rare), ganglioneuroblastoma, phaeochromocytoma, mastocytoma, non-beta cell hyperplasia, medullary thyroid carcinoma. **VIPoma**: age 1-3 years, chronic high-volume watery diarrhoea with **hypokalaemia and alkalosis (WDHA)**; VIP strikingly elevated; imaging shows an adrenal mass or mass along sympathetic ganglia in abdomen or chest.

> **Check:** hypokalaemic, hyperchloraemic metabolic **acidosis** (from stool bicarbonate loss) is the usual finding in secretory diarrhoea; the slide's "alkalosis" in WDHA is the classic recalled mnemonic.

## 13. Case 11: toddler with loose stools and normal growth, and IBD

### 13.1 Chronic non-specific diarrhoea (toddler diarrhoea, irritable bowel of childhood)
- Onset **6-18 months**.
- Loose, explosive stools containing **food particles**, 6-12 per day.
- **Growth normal** (unless on a restrictive diet); the child is pink, not pale.
- Chronic if persists over 2 weeks.
- **Red flags** (not compatible with CNSD): haematochezia or melaena, persistent fever, weight loss or growth arrest, anaemia.
- Diet trials: restrict apple juice, restrict lactose (trial only). Tests: tTG or EMA, faecal Giardia antigen.
- **Treatment**: reassurance, reduce sugar and milk, **avoid restrictive diets**.

### 13.2 Inflammatory bowel disease (5-year-old with bloody diarrhoea and growth failure)
Once rare, now seen several times a week in some centres.

| Feature | Ulcerative colitis | Crohn's disease |
|---|---|---|
| Gross rectal bleeding | Often, may be severe | Rare / absent |
| Diarrhoea | Less frequent presentation | Almost always |
| Pain | Mild or moderate | Can be severe |
| Anorexia | Moderate | Severe |
| Weight loss | Usually mild | Often pronounced |
| Growth retardation | Common | Common |
| Distribution | **Colon only** | **Mouth to anus**; oral and oesophageal ulcers, perianal disease |
| Biopsy | **Crypt abscesses** | **Granulomas** |
| Extra-intestinal | Arthritis, uveitis, pyoderma gangrenosum | Same; unexplained fever, short stature |

## 14. Differential diagnosis and investigations

### 14.1 Prolonged diarrhoea of infancy: differential
Congenital chloride diarrhoea, congenital sodium diarrhoea, microvillus inclusion disease, tufting enteropathy, autoimmune enteropathy, immunodeficiency, abetalipoproteinaemia, VIPoma, cystic fibrosis, IBD, carbohydrate malabsorption, cow's milk protein allergy, coeliac disease, intractable diarrhoea of infancy, enteric infection, intestinal lymphangiectasia, congenital short gut (malrotation), acrodermatitis enteropathica, chronic non-specific diarrhoea.

### 14.2 Which test for which diagnosis

| Investigation | Diagnoses |
|---|---|
| Stool culture, microscopy, antigen (bacterial, viral, protozoal) | Infectious enteritis |
| Stool pH and reducing substances; breath hydrogen; oral sugar tolerance | Carbohydrate malabsorption |
| Stool electrolytes | Chloride-losing diarrhoea |
| Lymphocyte count, immunoglobulins, macrophage function | Immunodeficiency, lymphangiectasia |
| Sweat chloride; pancreatic function tests | Cystic fibrosis, pancreatic disorders |
| Duodenal intubation | Bacterial overgrowth, enteric infection |
| Colonoscopy | IBD |
| Barium studies | Surgical disorders, IBD |
| Intestinal biopsy (histology) | Milk protein allergy (pre/post challenge), coeliac disease, lymphangiectasia |
| Urinary catecholamines; VIP immunoassay | Secretory tumours |
| Serum zinc | Acrodermatitis enteropathica |
| Lipid profile | Abetalipoproteinaemia |
| PT, PTT | Vitamin K malabsorption |
| Stool fat | Fat malabsorption |
| Stool alpha-1-antitrypsin | Protein-losing enteropathy |

## 15. Management principles

### 15.1 Nutrition
- Give **enough calories for catch-up growth**; use continuous enteral feeding or parenteral nutrition if oral intake is inadequate or malabsorption prevents adequate intake.
- Replace **micronutrients**: vitamin A, zinc, folic acid, copper, selenium (deficiency impairs immunity).

### 15.2 Medications
- **Probiotics**: some use; the role of antibiotics is unclear.
- **Antidiarrhoeal drugs**: avoid in children (sedation, toxic megacolon; they prolong excretion of the organism and may promote haemolytic-uraemic syndrome in enterohaemorrhagic E. coli).
- **Somatostatin (octreotide)**: for severe secretory diarrhoea (neuroendocrine tumours, microvillus inclusion disease, enterotoxin-induced diarrhoea).

## 16. Constipation in children (added from standard references)

The slides cover chronic diarrhoea; the same approach (history, growth, red flags) applies to constipation.

- **Definition**: infrequent (fewer than 3 per week), hard or painful stools, often with withholding and overflow soiling.
- **Functional constipation (over 90%)**: fear of pain, toilet training, low fibre and fluid, change of diet or routine.
- **Organic causes (red flags)**: delayed passage of meconium beyond 48 hours (**Hirschsprung disease**, cystic fibrosis), ribbon stools, abdominal distension, failure to thrive, abnormal anal position, **hypothyroidism**, hypercalcaemia, spinal lesions, anal stenosis or ectopia.
- **Examination**: abdominal faecal mass, anus, lumbosacral spine, neurological examination of the legs, growth.
- **Treatment**: reassurance and education; **disimpaction** (macrogol/polyethylene glycol at high dose, or enemas if needed), then **maintenance** with macrogol (or lactulose) and a stimulant laxative if needed; regular toilet routine with reward charts; adequate fluid and fibre. Continue for months and wean slowly.

## Key points to revise

1. Chronic diarrhoea lasts over 2 weeks; assess growth first (pale and underweight children need work-up).
2. Four mechanisms: osmotic, secretory, dysmotility, inflammatory.
3. Day-1 diarrhoea: infection or congenital (chloride diarrhoea with hypokalaemic alkalosis; sodium diarrhoea; microvillus inclusion disease; tufting enteropathy; autoimmune enteropathy/IPEX).
4. Milk-triggered: glucose-galactose malabsorption (fructose formula), lactase deficiency (lactose-free), cow's milk protein allergy (hypoallergenic formula).
5. Coeliac disease: gluten after 6 months, anti-tTG/EMA plus villous atrophy, HLA-DQ2/DQ8, lifelong gluten-free diet.
6. Cystic fibrosis: sweat chloride above 70; enzymes, physiotherapy, high calories.
7. Acrodermatitis enteropathica: zinc sulphate 150 mg/day; abetalipoproteinaemia: acanthocytes, MCT diet and vitamins A, D, E, K.
8. Toddler diarrhoea: food particles, normal growth, reassure; red flags exclude it.
9. Nutrition and micronutrients are central; avoid antidiarrhoeals.
