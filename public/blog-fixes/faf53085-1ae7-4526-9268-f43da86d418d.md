## SECTION 1: CRANIAL NERVES, EYES, GENERAL/VITAL SIGNS, RESPIRATORY SYSTEM
## A. Cranial Nerves (full set, *4 — heavily tested)
| CN | Name | Function |
|---|---|---|
| I | Olfactory | Smell |
| II | Optic | Vision |
| III | Oculomotor | Motor to most eye muscles (SR, IR, MR, IO), levator palpebrae, pupil constriction (parasympathetic) |
| IV | Trochlear | Motor to Superior Oblique (SO4) |
| V | Trigeminal | Sensory: face, forehead, cheeks, jaw. Motor: muscles of mastication |
| VI | Abducens | Motor to Lateral Rectus (LR6) |
| VII | Facial | Motor: facial expression. Sensory: taste anterior 2/3 tongue. Parasympathetic: lacrimal/salivary glands |
| VIII | Vestibulocochlear | Hearing & balance |
| IX | Glossopharyngeal | Taste posterior 1/3 tongue, pharynx sensation, swallowing |
| X | Vagus | Parasympathetic to thorax/abdomen, gag reflex, voice |
| XI | Accessory | Motor: sternocleidomastoid, trapezius |
| XII | Hypoglossal | Motor to tongue muscles |
**Mnemonic for eye muscles:** SO4, LR6, rest are CN III (oculomotor).
**Key exam logic (*4 tested heavily on V, VI, VII, IX):**
- Sensory face + motor mastication → **Trigeminal (V)**
- Taste anterior 2/3 tongue → **Facial (VII)**
- Taste posterior 1/3 tongue → **Glossopharyngeal (IX)**
- Lateral rectus weakness (can't abduct eye) → **Abducens (VI)** lesion
- Ptosis + dilated pupil + poor accommodation + poor light reflex → **Oculomotor (III)** lesion (consider Horner's as differential if pupil is constricted, not dilated)
**Facial nerve palsy — UMN vs LMN (*3, frequently tested):**
| Feature | UMN (e.g. stroke) | LMN (e.g. Bell's palsy) |
|---|---|---|
| Forehead | Spared (can still wrinkle) | Affected (cannot wrinkle) |
| Cause examples | Stroke, brain tumour, subdural haematoma | Bell's palsy, otitis media, parotid disease, infections (HSV/EBV/Ramsay Hunt), bulbar polio |
| Reason | Bilateral cortical innervation to upper face | Single nerve trunk supplies entire side |
**Meninges (superficial → deep):** Dura mater → Arachnoid mater → Pia mater (Dura is most superficial — frequently tested trap).

## B. Eye Examination
- **Pupillary light reflex:** Direct = constriction of the pupil light is shone into. Consensual = constriction of the opposite pupil. On accommodation, pupils constrict and converge bilaterally.
- **PERRLA** = Pupils Equal, Round, Reactive to Light and Accommodation.
- **Snellen chart** = tests visual acuity, NOT colour blindness (Ishihara plates test colour blindness — common distractor).
- **Nystagmus** = repetitive involuntary eye movement; can be congenital/idiopathic or acquired (cerebellar, vestibular, drug-induced).
- **Ptosis (drooping eyelid):**
 - Can be congenital or acquired
 - Causes: injury/stretching of muscle, **Myasthenia gravis**, **apical lung tumour (Pancoast tumour → Horner's syndrome)**, CN III palsy
- **Horner's syndrome triad:** ptosis + miosis (constricted pupil) + anhidrosis.
- **CN III (Oculomotor) palsy:** ptosis (complete), dilated fixed pupil, eye "down and out," poor accommodation.

## C. General Examination / Vital Signs
- **Vital signs:** temperature, pulse, BP, respiratory rate, O2 saturation, weight — essential for assessing diagnostic state; abnormal parameters point to underlying pathology.
 - BP >200 systolic or >120 diastolic → malignant hypertension.
- **Pallor:** check via capillary refill or palpebral conjunctiva — positive (pale) in anaemia.
- **Jaundice:** best checked at the **sclera** (*3 — repeatedly tested; not conjunctiva, nail beds, or palm).
- **Cyanosis:**
 - Central — tongue, mucous membranes, lips → serious cardiac/respiratory disease.
 - Peripheral — fingers/toes → cold exposure, poor peripheral circulation.
- **Oedema:** can be pitting or non-pitting; pressed 5cm above medial malleolus; suggests kidney failure, heart failure, or hypoalbuminaemia (Kwashiorkor in children = generalized oedema).
- **Capillary refill:** normal = less than 3 seconds in someone with good cardiac function and perfusion.
- **BMI** = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²)
 - Underweight: <18.5
 - Normal: 18.5–24.9
 - Overweight: 25–29.9
 - Obese: ≥30
- **Posture** is the best indicator of acute or chronic respiratory distress (better than height/weight/hair loss).

## D. Respiratory System Examination
**Order of chest exam:** Inspection → Palpation → **Percussion → Auscultation** (note: this differs from the abdomen order — see Section 2).
### Percussion notes
| Note | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Resonance | Normal air-filled lung |
| Hyperresonance | Pneumothorax, COPD, emphysema, bronchial asthma |
| Dullness | Consolidation, pneumonia |
| Stony dullness | Pleural effusion |
| Tympany | Pneumothorax |
### Breath sounds
- **Vesicular** — normal, soft, low-pitched, heard over most of both lungs.
- **Bronchial** — louder, harsher, with a clear pause between inspiration and expiration; normal over the trachea, abnormal in the periphery (suggests consolidation).
- High-pitched sounds best heard with the **diaphragm**; low-pitched with the **bell**.
### Added (adventitious) sounds
- **Stridor** = partial obstruction of larynx/trachea — medical emergency (*3 tested).
- **Rhonchi** = low-pitched, from secretions in larger airways — heard in asthma, COPD, bronchitis.
- **Monophonic wheeze** = obstruction of a single bronchus/bronchiole — suggests tumour or foreign body (localized/fixed).
- **Polyphonic wheeze** = widespread airway narrowing — asthma, COPD.
- **Pleural friction rub** = rubbing/grating/squeaky sound, "like two pieces of leather rubbing."
- **Crackles** = fluid/secretions in small airways or alveoli (pulmonary oedema, pneumonia, fibrosis).
### Vocal resonance / fremitus
- **Consolidation** → increased vocal fremitus and resonance; **egophony present** (E→A change, "above the level" of consolidation) (*3).
- **Pleural effusion** → decreased/absent vocal fremitus and resonance.
- **Pneumothorax** → decreased breath sounds, hyperresonant, decreased tactile fremitus.
### Classic vignettes
- **Lobar pneumonia / consolidation (*3):** dullness to percussion + bronchial breath sounds + increased vocal fremitus + egophony.
- **Tension/spontaneous pneumothorax (*2 — "teenage boy, sharp pain, trouble breathing"):** cyanosis, tachypnoea, tracheal deviation AWAY from affected side, decreased tactile fremitus, hyperresonance, decreased breath sounds on affected side.
- **Pleural effusion clinical findings:** stony dull percussion, decreased/absent vocal resonance, decreased breath sounds, tracheal deviation away from effusion (if large).
### Causes — quick lists
- **Dullness to percussion:** consolidation, pleural effusion, lobar collapse.
- **Wheeze:** asthma, COPD/bronchitis (diffuse, polyphonic); tumour or foreign body (fixed, monophonic).
- **Bilateral reduced chest expansion:** bilateral pleural effusion, end-stage pulmonary fibrosis, COPD.
- **Finger clubbing — respiratory causes:** lung cancer (incl. mesothelioma), pulmonary fibrosis, suppurative lung disease (bronchiectasis, lung abscess, empyema), cystic fibrosis.
### Abnormal respiratory patterns
- **Cheyne-Stokes:** slow shallow → rapid → slower, with apnoeic periods (~20 sec). Causes: heart failure, opioid overdose, renal failure, meningitis, severe head injury.
- **Kussmaul breathing:** deep, rapid, laboured — classic in diabetic ketoacidosis (metabolic acidosis).
### Spinal curvature terms (relevant to chest exam)
- **Kyphosis** = exaggerated posterior thoracic curvature.
- **Lordosis** = exaggerated anterior lumbar curvature ("swayback").
- **Scoliosis** = lateral spinal curvature.

## SECTION 2: CARDIOVASCULAR, ABDOMINAL & HEPATO-GI EXAMINATION
## A. Cardiovascular System Examination
### Pulses and Neck Vessels
- **Arterial vs venous pulsation in the neck — features suggesting it's arterial:**
 - No variation with posture
 - Not reduced by pressure at the root of the neck
 - Does NOT show "wagging ear lobe" sign or positive hepatojugular reflux (those suggest venous)
- **JVP examination essentials:**
 - Patient lying at 45°
 - Neck muscles relaxed
 - Good lighting
 - Time the pulsation against the **internal jugular waveform**, NOT against the radial pulse (common trap)
- **Hepatojugular reflux:**
 - Elevated in constrictive pericarditis, restrictive cardiomyopathy, left ventricular failure
 - A rise should NOT last more than a few cycles (sustained rise = abnormal/positive)
### Precordium / Apex Beat
- **Normal apex beat:** 5th intercostal space, mid-clavicular line.
- **Displaced apex beat** (left/inferior): suggests LV dilatation/hypertrophy.
- **Tapping apex beat:** mitral stenosis (palpable S1).
- **Palpable left parasternal heave:** Right Ventricular Hypertrophy.
### Heart Sounds
- **S1 ("lubb"):** closure of AV valves (mitral + tricuspid) → start of systole. Best heard at apex.
- **S2 ("dubb"):** closure of semilunar valves (aortic + pulmonary) → end of systole. Best heard at base.
### Murmurs and Valve Disease
- **Mixed mitral disease, MR-predominant:** displaced apex, soft S1, pan-systolic murmur, short mid-diastolic murmur.
- **Aortic regurgitation:** collapsing pulse, wide pulse pressure, displaced/hyperdynamic apex, early diastolic murmur (left sternal edge).
- **Aortic stenosis:** narrow pulse pressure, ejection systolic murmur. Radio-femoral delay = coarctation, NOT aortic stenosis (trap).
### Pulse Pressure
- Narrow: aortic stenosis, cardiac tamponade.
- Wide: aortic regurgitation (longstanding → bounding pulse).
### Vascular Signs
- **Bruit:** swishing sound over artery from turbulent flow (narrowed/atherosclerotic vessel).
- **Thrill:** palpable vibration over an artery — NOT a normal finding on carotid exam; suggests significant stenosis.
### High Output Heart Failure — Causes (*2)
Anaemia, sepsis, thyrotoxicosis/hyperthyroidism, systemic arteriovenous fistula, Paget's disease, beriberi (thiamine deficiency).
**NOT a cause:** iron overload.
### Heart Failure (essay-style — 56yo lady vignette)
**Symptoms:** dyspnoea (exertional/orthopnoea/PND), fatigue, chest pain, palpitations, ankle swelling, weight gain, reduced exercise tolerance.
**Exam findings:** raised JVP, displaced apex, S3 gallop, bibasal crackles, peripheral oedema, hepatomegaly, ascites, cool peripheries, tachycardia.
**Cause-finding history:** prior MI/IHD, hypertension, valvular disease, arrhythmias, alcohol use, thyroid disease, anaemia, infections (viral myocarditis), cardiotoxic drugs.
### MI — Chest Pain Features
Most suggestive: **sweating and vomiting** (autonomic features) — more specific than pain severity/duration alone.
### Alcoholic Patient Signs
- **Dupuytren's contracture** — associated with alcohol dependence.
- Tender hepatomegaly does NOT necessarily mean portal hypertension.
- A shrunken liver does NOT rule out cirrhosis.
- Raised GGT/MCV alone do NOT establish diagnosis.

## B. Abdominal Examination
### Order of Examination
**Inspection → Auscultation → Percussion → Palpation** (*3 — reverse of chest order, to avoid stimulating bowel sounds before auscultating).
### Inspection
- Symmetrically distended abdomen → fluid (ascites) or obesity.
- Visible peristalsis → intestinal obstruction (not peritonitis).
- **Caput medusae** → dilated periumbilical veins, cirrhosis with portal hypertension.
- Visible scars → prior surgery.
- Enlarged organ on right hypochondrium → likely the **liver** (not spleen).
### Percussion of Organs
- Kidney: tympanic (overlying bowel gas).
- Spleen: dull.
- Liver: normally dull.
### Distinguishing Kidney from Spleen
| Feature | Spleen | Kidney |
|---|---|---|
| Direction of enlargement | Diagonally toward umbilicus/RIF | Inferiorly |
| Mobility with respiration | Mobile | Not mobile |
| Notch | Present | Absent |
| Getting above it | Cannot | Can |
| Ballotable | No | Yes |
| Percussion | Dull | Tympanic |
### Ascites — Eliciting Clinically
- **Inspection:** symmetrical distension, bulging flanks (patient supine).
- **Percussion — flank dullness:** percuss umbilicus → flanks; note tympanic-to-dull transition.
- **Shifting dullness:** mark dullness, roll patient to opposite side, wait, re-percuss — dullness shifts if ascites present.
- **Fluid thrill:** one hand flat on one flank, tap opposite flank; assistant's hand edge placed midline (dampens wall transmission); fluid wave felt as impulse if significant ascites present.
### Abdominal Distension — "5 F's"
Fat, Faeces, Foetus, Flatus, Fluid.
### Abdominal Mass — Key Facts
- Pulsatile mass NOT always an aortic aneurysm.
- Enlarged (pathological) kidney is dull to percussion.
- Pelvic mass disappearing after catheterization → distended bladder.
- Indentible mass → faecal loading of colon.
### Abdominal Pain Patterns
- Peptic ulcer → epigastric (not hypogastrium).
- IBS → poorly localized (not well localized).
- **Oesophagitis → retrosternal.**
- Pancreatitis → radiates to the back.
### Classic Abdominal Signs
- **Psoas sign:** pain on hip flexion against resistance — inflamed (retrocaecal) appendix.
- **Obturator sign:** pain on internal rotation of flexed hip — appendix touching obturator internus.
- **Rovsing's sign:** LLQ palpation → RLQ pain — appendicitis (*2).
- **Murphy's sign:** "catch" in breath on RUQ palpation during deep inspiration — acute cholecystitis (*2).
- **Cullen's sign:** periumbilical bruising — haemorrhagic pancreatitis (late sign), ruptured ectopic, ruptured AAA.
- **Grey Turner's sign:** flank bruising — haemorrhagic/acute pancreatitis, retroperitoneal bleed.
- **Charcot's triad:** jaundice + fever (rigors) + RUQ pain → ascending cholangitis.
### Hernias / Groin Mass — Causes
Inguinal hernia, femoral hernia, femoral aneurysm, enlarged lymph node.
### Cholecystitis Vignette
40yo, pain worse after fatty/spicy food, unrelieved by antacids → **Murphy's sign** raises suspicion.

## C. Hepato-GI Signs
### Chronic Liver Disease — Signs on General Exam (*2 — "list 5 signs")
Gynaecomastia, hair loss, spider naevi, testicular atrophy, Dupuytren's contracture, palmar erythema, leuconychia, finger clubbing, caput medusae, bruising, cachexia, pallor, excoriations, jaundice.
### Hepatomegaly
- Causes include emphysema (diaphragm displacement, mimics), cirrhosis, malignancy, congestion.
- Liver enlarges **downward from the right hypochondrium** (not left).
- Jaundice + spider naevi + purpura together → suggest **alcohol** as cause.
- Liver is normally **dull** to percussion.
### Jaundice
- Pale stools + dark urine → **obstructive jaundice** (not haemolytic).
- Bilirubin is a breakdown product of Hb, not used in RBC synthesis.
- Pruritus may signal obstructive jaundice.
- "Danger of infection" sticker on IVDU blood samples with jaundice is **mandatory**, not optional.
### Other Hepato-GI Signs
- Koplik spots — buccal mucosa (pathognomonic for measles).
- Pharyngitis = inflammation of throat/pharynx (not tongue).
- Smooth glossy tongue (atrophic glossitis) → iron deficiency (or B12/folate deficiency).
- Angular stomatitis — iron deficiency, B-complex deficiency, liver disease.
- Trauma, B12 deficiency, folate deficiency → all linked to aphthous ulceration.
- Oral candidiasis NOT exclusive to HIV/AIDS.
### Hands Examination
- **Leuconychia** — most commonly liver disease/hypoalbuminaemia (not trauma).
- **Asterixis** — uraemia, hepatic encephalopathy, CO2 retention.
- Finger clubbing is NOT only respiratory/cardiac — also seen in GI disease.
### Finger Clubbing — Full Cause List
**Respiratory:** lung cancer (incl. mesothelioma), pulmonary fibrosis, suppurative lung disease, cystic fibrosis.
**Cardiac:** cyanotic congenital heart disease, infective endocarditis.
**GI:** IBD, coeliac disease, cirrhosis, GI lymphoma.
### Spider Naevi
- Can be normal in pregnancy (and small numbers in healthy people).
- Multiple (>5, above nipple line) → chronic liver disease.
- Janeway lesions — palms/soles (painless), infective endocarditis (not face).
- Aortic dissection — associated with radio-femoral delay.
### Virchow's Node / Troisier's Sign
- Left supraclavicular node = **Virchow's node** — associated with **gastric/GI malignancy**.
- **Right** supraclavicular node → more associated with **lung/thoracic malignancy**.
- Not typically linked to CNS malignancy.
### Renal Failure — Symptoms & Signs (*high yield)
Decreased urine output, fluid retention (leg/ankle/feet swelling), shortness of breath, fatigue, confusion, nausea, weakness, seizures/coma (severe), bloody/frothy urine, high BP, dry/itchy skin.
**Cardiac (not renal) clues:** finger clubbing, palpitations, dizziness/syncope, angina, splinter haemorrhages, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea.
### Hydration Status Assessment
Skin turgor, intraocular tension, mucous membranes/tongue moisture, BP (incl. postural drop), JVP, heart rate, capillary refill, urine output.
### Female Reproductive System
- Vagina ≈ **7.5–10cm** long (not 5cm).
- Labia majora = fat pads covered with hair.
- Vulva = labia majora + labia minora.
- Uterus = muscular, pear-shaped, midline structure.
- Abnormal vaginal bleeding: anaemia not always present; iron deficiency anaemia IS a recognized association; oligomenorrhoea IS a feature of PCOS.
### Rectal Examination
- Sacrum palpated **posteriorly**.
- Pubic bone palpated anteriorly (both sexes).
- In males, **prostate** felt anteriorly.
- Cervix can sometimes be felt anteriorly in females.
- Optimum position: **left** lateral, knees **flexed**.
- Chaperone refusal generally cannot simply be overruled.
- Useful for GI disorders AND neurological assessment (anal tone/reflex).
### Haemorrhoids
- Bleeding → bright red PR bleeding (not melena).
- Pregnancy is a predisposing factor.
- First-degree bleeding usually **painless**.
- Second-degree haemorrhoids CAN be manually reduced.
### Haematemesis
- Low BP (<90 systolic) + tachycardia (>100/min) = worrying.
- Pulse of 80/min on **Bisoprolol** is NOT reassuring (masks tachycardic response).
- Abdominal pain not always present.
- Alcohol history IS essential (varices risk).
