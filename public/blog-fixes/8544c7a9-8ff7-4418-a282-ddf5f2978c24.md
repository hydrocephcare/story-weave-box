## SECTION 4: GI & Pancreatic Disorders, Liver Disease, and Body Fluid Cytology (Pages 154–223)

## 1. Gastrointestinal (GI) Disorders
**Definition:** Conditions affecting the digestive tract — oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, colon, rectum — disrupting digestion, absorption, and waste elimination.
**Common symptoms:** Abdominal pain, nausea/vomiting, diarrhoea or constipation, bloating, unexplained weight loss, appetite changes.
### Categories of GI Disorders
**Inflammatory:**
- **Gastritis** — stomach lining inflammation; causes: H. pylori, NSAIDs, alcohol, autoimmune
- **Crohn's Disease** — transmural inflammation, any part of GI tract (commonly terminal ileum); can cause strictures and fistulas
- **Ulcerative Colitis** — continuous mucosal inflammation of colon/rectum only
- **Peptic Ulcer Disease** — gastric or duodenal ulcers; caused by H. pylori or NSAIDs
**Functional (no structural cause):**
- **IBS** — abdominal pain + altered bowel habits (IBS-D, IBS-C, IBS-M)
- **Functional Dyspepsia** — chronic upper abdominal pain, bloating, early satiety; no organic pathology
**Structural:**
- **Diverticulitis** — inflamed colonic pouches; pain, fever, altered bowel habit
- **Hiatal Hernia** — stomach herniation through diaphragm → GERD, heartburn
- **Achalasia** — failure of lower oesophageal sphincter to relax → dysphagia, regurgitation
**Neoplastic:**
- **Colorectal Cancer** — altered bowel habit, rectal bleeding, pain
- **Pancreatic Cancer** — jaundice, weight loss, back pain, new-onset diabetes
- **Oesophageal Cancer** — dysphagia, weight loss, chest pain; subtypes: squamous cell and adenocarcinoma
**Metabolic/Endocrine:**
- **Coeliac Disease** — autoimmune reaction to gluten → small intestinal damage → malabsorption
- **Pancreatic Insufficiency** — inadequate digestive enzymes → malabsorption, steatorrhoea
**Motility:**
- **Gastroparesis** — delayed gastric emptying; nausea, vomiting, early satiety; common in diabetics
- **Oesophageal Spasm** — abnormal contractions → chest pain, dysphagia
**Obstructive:**
- **Bowel Obstruction** — adhesions, tumours, hernias → severe pain, vomiting, inability to pass stool/gas
- **Gallstones** — obstruct bile ducts → pain, jaundice, cholecystitis

## 2. Laboratory Evaluation of GI Disorders
| Test | Purpose | Key Indicators |
|---|---|---|
| **CBC** | Overall health, detect anaemia/infection | Low Hb → GI bleeding or malabsorption; elevated WBC → infection |
| **BMP/CMP** | Electrolytes, kidney function, metabolic status | Electrolyte losses from diarrhoea/vomiting; glucose for pancreatic function |
| **LFTs** | Liver/biliary function | ALT/AST → hepatitis; ALP/GGT → bile duct obstruction; bilirubin → jaundice |
| **Serum Amylase & Lipase** | Pancreatitis diagnosis | Lipase more specific and stays elevated longer |
| **Fecal Occult Blood Test (FOBT)** | Screen for GI bleeding | Hidden blood → colorectal cancer or ulcer |
| **Fecal Immunochemical Test (FIT)** | Colorectal cancer screening | Detects human haemoglobin in stool |
| **Stool Cultures** | Infectious diarrhoea | Identify bacteria, viruses, parasites |
| **Fecal Fat Test** | Malabsorption | Steatorrhoea → pancreatic insufficiency |
| **Anti-tTG IgA** | Coeliac disease | Elevated → coeliac |
| **H. pylori tests** | Peptic ulcer/gastritis | Serology, urea breath test, stool antigen |
| **Lactose Tolerance Test** | Lactose intolerance | Low post-ingestion glucose rise |
| **Hydrogen Breath Test** | Carbohydrate malabsorption | Elevated H₂ → malabsorption |
| **CEA, CA 19-9** | GI cancer monitoring | CEA → colorectal; CA 19-9 → pancreatic |
| **Gastrin Levels** | Zollinger-Ellison syndrome | Elevated → gastrin-secreting tumour |

## 3. Pancreatic Disorders
**The pancreas has two roles:**
- **Exocrine** — secretes digestive enzymes (amylase, lipase, trypsin) into the duodenum
- **Endocrine** — secretes insulin and glucagon into the blood
### Types of Pancreatic Disorders
**Inflammatory:**
- **Acute Pancreatitis** — sudden inflammation; causes: gallstones (most common), alcohol, drugs, trauma. Features: severe epigastric pain radiating to the back, raised amylase/lipase, nausea, fever. Complications: necrosis, abscess, systemic sepsis
- **Chronic Pancreatitis** — progressive permanent damage; cause: chronic alcohol use or genetic. Features: persistent pain, steatorrhoea, weight loss, diabetes. Complications: exocrine insufficiency, pseudocysts
**Neoplastic:**
- **Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma** — most common; often presents late (jaundice, weight loss, back pain, new-onset diabetes). Poor prognosis
- **Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumours (PNETs)** — less common; may be functional (e.g., insulinoma, gastrinoma)
**Functional:**
- **Pancreatic Insufficiency** — inadequate enzyme production → malabsorption, steatorrhoea, weight loss; caused by chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis, or pancreatic cancer
**Metabolic:**
- **Diabetes Mellitus Type 1** — autoimmune destruction of beta cells → no insulin
- **Diabetes Mellitus Type 2** — insulin resistance + relative deficiency
**Structural:**
- **Pancreatic Cysts** — simple, pseudocysts (post-pancreatitis), cystic neoplasms; may be asymptomatic or cause pain
- **Pseudocysts** — fluid collections after pancreatitis, surrounded by fibrous wall; require drainage if symptomatic
**Genetic:**
- **Cystic Fibrosis** — CFTR gene mutation → thick secretions → pancreatic insufficiency + recurrent lung infections; managed with enzyme replacement
- **Hereditary Pancreatitis** — recurrent episodes from childhood
**Endocrine (Rare):**
- **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome** — gastrinoma (gastrin-secreting tumour) → excessive gastric acid → recurrent severe peptic ulcers, diarrhoea
### Lab Evaluation of Pancreatic Disorders
| Test | Role |
|---|---|
| **Serum Amylase** | Rises in acute pancreatitis; also rises in salivary disease, bowel obstruction |
| **Serum Lipase** | More specific for pancreas; stays elevated longer — preferred test |
| **Fecal Fat (72-hr collection)** | Steatorrhoea → pancreatic insufficiency |
| **Fecal Elastase** | Low = pancreatic insufficiency (chronic pancreatitis, cancer) |
| **Fasting Blood Glucose + HbA1c** | Assess endocrine dysfunction; secondary diabetes |
| **CA 19-9** | Tumour marker for pancreatic cancer (not specific — also elevated in chronic pancreatitis, cholestasis) |
| **CEA** | Secondary tumour marker |
| **Gastrin Levels** | Diagnose Zollinger-Ellison syndrome |
| **Secretin Stimulation Test** | Assesses exocrine function — abnormal response → insufficiency |
| **LFTs (ALP, GGT, bilirubin)** | Detect biliary obstruction associated with pancreatic disease |
| **Hydrogen Breath Test** | Malabsorption screen |

## 4. Liver Disease
### Functions of the Liver
- Detoxification of drugs, alcohol, and metabolic waste
- Synthesis of plasma proteins (albumin, clotting factors)
- Bile production for fat digestion
- Carbohydrate and lipid metabolism
- Iron storage (ferritin)
### Types of Liver Disease
**Hepatitis (Inflammation):**
- **Acute** — sudden onset; causes: viral (Hep A, B, C), toxins, alcohol, autoimmune
- **Chronic** — >6 months; causes: Hep B, Hep C, autoimmune hepatitis
**Fatty Liver Disease:**
- **NAFLD** — fat accumulation unrelated to alcohol; associated with obesity, diabetes, metabolic syndrome. Can progress to NASH → cirrhosis
- **Alcoholic Liver Disease (ALD)** — spectrum: fatty liver → alcoholic hepatitis → cirrhosis
**Cirrhosis:**
- End-stage scarring from chronic liver damage; irreversible; causes: viral hepatitis, alcohol, NAFLD
- Complications: portal hypertension, ascites, varices, hepatic encephalopathy, HCC
**Liver Cancer:**
- **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)** — most common primary liver cancer; arises on background of cirrhosis or viral hepatitis
- **Cholangiocarcinoma** — bile duct cancer
**Genetic Liver Diseases:**
- **Haemochromatosis** — iron overload → liver damage, diabetes, cardiomyopathy; diagnose with ferritin + transferrin saturation
- **Wilson's Disease** — copper accumulation (defective ATP7B gene) → liver disease, neuropsychiatric symptoms, Kayser-Fleischer rings; diagnose with caeruloplasmin + 24-hr urinary copper
- **Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency** — can cause cirrhosis and emphysema
**Autoimmune:**
- **Autoimmune Hepatitis** — immune attack on hepatocytes; ANA/SMA positive
- **Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC)** — bile duct destruction; AMA (anti-mitochondrial antibody) positive; causes pruritus
- **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC)** — bile duct scarring; associated with IBD
**Biliary:**
- **Gallstones** — obstruct bile flow → jaundice, cholecystitis
- **Biliary Atresia** — congenital absence/closure of bile ducts in infants
### Clinical Features of Liver Disease
| Feature | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Jaundice | Elevated bilirubin → yellowing of skin and sclera |
| Dark urine | Conjugated bilirubin excreted in urine |
| Pale stools | Bile not reaching intestine (obstructive) |
| Ascites | Portal hypertension + low albumin → fluid in abdomen |
| Hepatic Encephalopathy | Ammonia accumulation → confusion, flap (asterixis) |
| Spider angiomata / Palmar erythema | Oestrogen accumulation in cirrhosis |
| Pruritus | Bile salt deposition in skin (PBC especially) |
| Fatigue | Chronic disease, anaemia |
### Laboratory Diagnosis of Liver Disease
| Test | Significance |
|---|---|
| **ALT** | Most liver-specific; elevated in hepatitis, hepatocellular damage |
| **AST** | Elevated in liver + heart + muscle damage; AST:ALT >2 suggests alcoholic hepatitis |
| **ALP** | Elevated in cholestatic disease (bile duct obstruction, PBC, PSC) |
| **GGT** | Sensitive for biliary/liver disease; elevated with alcohol |
| **Bilirubin (total, direct, indirect)** | Direct ↑ → obstructive/conjugated; indirect ↑ → pre-hepatic (haemolysis) |
| **Albumin** | Low → chronic liver failure (poor synthetic function) |
| **PT/INR** | Prolonged → liver can't synthesise clotting factors; severity marker |
| **Viral hepatitis serology** | HBsAg, anti-HCV, HBV DNA PCR, HCV RNA PCR |
| **ANA, SMA, AMA** | Autoimmune hepatitis (ANA/SMA), PBC (AMA) |
| **Caeruloplasmin + urinary copper** | Wilson's disease |
| **Ferritin + transferrin saturation** | Haemochromatosis (ferritin high, sat >60%) |
| **Liver biopsy** | Grade and stage fibrosis; confirm diagnosis where uncertain |
| **FibroScan (Elastography)** | Non-invasive assessment of liver stiffness/fibrosis |

## 5. Body Fluid Cytology
### Types of Body Fluids
- **Pleural** — chest cavity; tapped by thoracentesis
- **Peritoneal/Ascitic** — abdomen; tapped by paracentesis
- **Pericardial** — around heart; pericardiocentesis
- **CSF** — surrounds brain/spinal cord; lumbar puncture
- **Synovial** — joint cavities; arthrocentesis
- **Urine, bile, gastric juice, saliva, sweat** — other analysable fluids
### Transudate vs Exudate (Light's Criteria)
| Feature | Transudate | Exudate |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | <2.5 g/dL | >2.5 g/dL |
| Specific gravity | <1.012 | >1.020 |
| LDH | Low | High |
| Appearance | Clear, pale yellow | Turbid, cloudy, may be bloody |
| Nature | Non-inflammatory | Inflammatory |
| Causes | Heart failure, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome | Infection, malignancy, TB, pneumonia |
**Light's Criteria formally classifies exudate if any of:** pleural fluid protein/serum protein >0.5, pleural LDH/serum LDH >0.6, pleural LDH >2/3 upper normal serum LDH.
### Body Fluid Cytology Processing
1. **Sample collection** — method depends on fluid type; immediate fixation if needed to preserve cells
2. **Cytocentrifugation** — concentrates sparse cells onto a slide in a uniform thin layer
3. **Smearing** — if cytocentrifuge not used; thin smear made manually
4. **Fixation** — methanol or alcohol-based fixative applied immediately to preserve morphology
5. **Staining** — Pap stain (detailed nuclear morphology), Wright's stain (blood/marrow), Diff-Quik (rapid)
6. **Drying and coverslipping**
7. **Microscopy and interpretation** — look for malignant cells, inflammatory cells (neutrophils → bacterial infection; lymphocytes → viral/TB/malignancy), organisms, crystals (e.g., urate in gout for synovial fluid)
### Clinical Significance of Body Fluid Cytology
- **Malignant effusions** — detect cancer cells in pleural/peritoneal fluid (lung, ovarian, breast cancer commonly shed cells)
- **Infectious** — organisms identified in CSF (bacteria, fungi) or stool
- **Inflammatory** — WBC differential helps identify cause (neutrophilia → bacterial; lymphocytosis → viral, TB, lymphoma)
- **CSF analysis specifically:** cell count, protein, glucose (CSF glucose normally 60–70% of serum; low glucose → bacterial/fungal meningitis), Gram stain, culture
