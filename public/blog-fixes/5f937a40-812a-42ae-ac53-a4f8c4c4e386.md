**Q1.** 17-year-old boy, delayed puberty, anosmia, low LH/FSH, low testosterone.
A. Klinefelter syndrome
B. Kallmann syndrome
C. Turner syndrome
D. Androgen insensitivity syndrome
E. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia
## Answer: B — Kallmann syndrome
- Anosmia + hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (low LH/FSH with low testosterone) is the classic combination, due to defective GnRH neuron migration. Klinefelter (A) shows *elevated* gonadotropins, not low; Turner (C) is a female condition.
**Q2.** Polyuria/polydipsia after head trauma; low urine osmolality, rises after desmopressin.
A. Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus
B. Central diabetes insipidus
C. SIADH
D. Psychogenic polydipsia
E. Addison disease
## Answer: B — Central diabetes insipidus
- Response to desmopressin confirms the kidney can concentrate urine when ADH is supplied — the deficiency is central (post-trauma pituitary/hypothalamic damage). Nephrogenic DI (A) would NOT respond to desmopressin since the kidney itself is unresponsive.
**Q3.** 45-year-old, suprasellar mass, headaches, bitemporal hemianopia.
A. Rathke pouch remnants
B. Schwann cells
C. Thyroid follicular cells
D. Adrenal cortex
E. Pancreatic β-cells
## Answer: A — Rathke pouch remnants
- Craniopharyngioma, the classic suprasellar mass causing chiasmal compression, arises from remnants of Rathke's pouch (embryological precursor of the anterior pituitary).
**Q4.** 28-year-old woman, amenorrhoea, galactorrhoea, markedly raised prolactin.
A. Prolactinoma
B. Graves disease
C. Adrenal adenoma
D. Pancreatic NET
E. Primary ovarian failure
## Answer: A — Prolactinoma
- Classic triad of amenorrhoea-galactorrhoea with markedly elevated prolactin points directly to a prolactin-secreting pituitary adenoma.
**Q5.** Pituitary macroadenoma compressing the optic chiasm most commonly causes:
A. Unilateral blindness
B. Bitemporal hemianopia
C. Homonymous hemianopia
D. Central scotoma
E. Diplopia only
## Answer: B — Bitemporal hemianopia
- Compression at the chiasm affects the decussating nasal retinal fibres bilaterally, causing loss of the temporal visual fields on both sides.
**Q6.** 52-year-old, enlarged hands, prognathism, sweating, elevated IGF-1 — most common cause?
A. Ectopic GHRH secretion
B. Pituitary somatotroph adenoma
C. Adrenal carcinoma
D. Thyroid carcinoma
E. Pancreatic insulinoma
## Answer: B — Pituitary somatotroph adenoma
- The vast majority of acromegaly cases arise from a GH-secreting pituitary adenoma; ectopic GHRH (A) is a rare cause.
**Q7.** 24-year-old woman, weight loss, tremor, palpitations, exophthalmos; TSH suppressed, free T4 elevated.
A. Hashimoto thyroiditis
B. Graves disease
C. Subacute thyroiditis
D. Thyroid lymphoma
E. Iodine deficiency goitre
## Answer: B — Graves disease
- Exophthalmos plus hyperthyroid biochemistry (suppressed TSH, high T4) is specific to Graves disease. Hashimoto (A) typically causes hypothyroidism, not this picture.
**Q8.** Graves disease is driven primarily by antibodies to the:
A. Thyroglobulin
B. TSH receptor
C. Insulin receptor
D. ACTH receptor
E. PTH receptor
## Answer: B — TSH receptor
- Stimulating autoantibodies against the TSH receptor drive continuous thyroid hormone overproduction, the hallmark of Graves disease.
**Q9.** 46-year-old woman, painless goitre, hypothyroid symptoms, positive anti-TPO antibodies.
A. Graves disease
B. Hashimoto thyroiditis
C. Medullary carcinoma
D. Follicular adenoma
E. Subacute granulomatous thyroiditis
## Answer: B — Hashimoto thyroiditis
- Anti-TPO positivity with painless goitre and hypothyroidism is classic for Hashimoto's autoimmune thyroiditis.
**Q10.** 40-year-old woman, hypertension, hypokalaemia, low renin.
A. Addison disease
B. Conn syndrome
C. SIADH
D. Cushing disease
E. Diabetes insipidus
## Answer: B — Conn syndrome
- Primary hyperaldosteronism causes hypertension with hypokalaemia and suppressed renin (due to autonomous aldosterone excess feeding back on the renin-angiotensin axis).
**Q11.** Conn syndrome results from excess:
A. Cortisol
B. Aldosterone
C. Catecholamines
D. ACTH
E. Thyroxine
## Answer: B — Aldosterone
- By definition, Conn syndrome is primary hyperaldosteronism.
**Q12.** 38-year-old, episodic headaches, sweating, palpitations, severe hypertension — best screening test?
A. Serum cortisol
B. Plasma/urinary metanephrines
C. Serum calcitonin
D. Serum TSH
E. Urinary 5-HIAA
## Answer: B — Plasma/urinary metanephrines
- This presentation is classic for pheochromocytoma; metanephrines (catecholamine metabolites) are the standard screening test. Calcitonin (C) screens for medullary thyroid carcinoma; 5-HIAA (E) screens for carcinoid syndrome.
**Q13.** 19-year-old, abdominal pain, Kussmaul breathing, high ketones, metabolic acidosis.
A. Hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state
B. Diabetic ketoacidosis
C. Addison crisis
D. Thyroid storm
E. SIADH
## Answer: B — Diabetic ketoacidosis
- Kussmaul breathing (compensatory hyperventilation) with ketosis and metabolic acidosis is the textbook triad of DKA, typically in Type 1 diabetics.
**Q14.** Hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state is most associated with:
A. Type 1 diabetes
B. Type 2 diabetes
C. Insulinoma
D. Glucagonoma
E. Pheochromocytoma
## Answer: B — Type 2 diabetes
- HHS occurs in patients with sufficient residual insulin to prevent ketosis (typical of Type 2 diabetes), unlike DKA which is typical of Type 1.
**Q15.** Recurrent fasting hypoglycaemia relieved by glucose; insulin inappropriately high during symptoms.
A. Glucagonoma
B. Insulinoma
C. Somatostatinoma
D. VIPoma
E. Gastrinoma
## Answer: B — Insulinoma
- Inappropriately elevated insulin during hypoglycaemic episodes (failure of normal insulin suppression) is diagnostic of an insulin-secreting pancreatic tumour.
**Q16.** 65-year-old heavy smoker, hyponatraemia, low plasma osmolality, concentrated urine.
A. Diabetes insipidus
B. Ectopic ADH secretion
C. Addison disease
D. Hyperaldosteronism
E. Primary hyperparathyroidism
## Answer: B — Ectopic ADH secretion
- SIADH from small cell lung cancer (common in smokers) causes water retention → dilutional hyponatraemia with inappropriately concentrated urine, despite low plasma osmolality.
**Q17.** Ectopic ACTH secretion is most commonly associated with:
A. Thyroid carcinoma
B. Small cell carcinoma of the bronchus
C. Renal cell carcinoma
D. Breast carcinoma
E. Pancreatic adenocarcinoma
## Answer: B — Small cell carcinoma of the bronchus
- Small cell lung cancer is the classic paraneoplastic source of ectopic ACTH, causing Cushing syndrome.
**Q18.** Elevated urinary 5-HIAA is most indicative of:
A. Phaeochromocytoma
B. Carcinoid syndrome
C. Medullary thyroid carcinoma
D. Insulinoma
E. Gastrinoma
## Answer: B — Carcinoid syndrome
- 5-HIAA is the breakdown product of serotonin, elevated specifically in serotonin-secreting carcinoid tumours.
**Q19.** MEN1 most commonly involves tumours of:
A. Thyroid, adrenal, pancreas
B. Parathyroid, pancreas, pituitary
C. Adrenal, thyroid, parathyroid
D. Pituitary, thyroid, adrenal
E. Pancreas, kidney, thyroid
## Answer: B — Parathyroid, pancreas, pituitary
- The classic "3 Ps" of MEN1: Parathyroid hyperplasia/adenoma, Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours, and Pituitary adenomas.
**Q20.** MEN2 is associated with mutation of the:
A. MEN1 gene
B. RET proto-oncogene
C. p53 gene
D. BRCA1 gene
E. KRAS gene
## Answer: B — RET proto-oncogene
- RET mutations are the defining genetic cause of MEN2 (both 2A and 2B), driving medullary thyroid carcinoma and pheochromocytoma.
**Q21.** Hyperparathyroidism in MEN1 typically results in:
A. Hypocalcaemia
B. Hypercalcaemia
C. Hypokalaemia
D. Hyponatraemia
E. Hypoglycaemia
## Answer: B — Hypercalcaemia
- Excess PTH increases bone resorption and renal calcium reabsorption, causing hypercalcaemia — the hallmark biochemical finding.
**Q22.** Klinefelter syndrome typically shows:
A. XX karyotype
B. XY karyotype
C. XXY karyotype
D. XYY karyotype
E. XO karyotype
## Answer: C — XXY karyotype
- Klinefelter syndrome is defined by an extra X chromosome in a phenotypic male (47,XXY). XO (E) is Turner syndrome instead.
**Q23.** Clitoromegaly is most characteristic of:
A. Mild hirsutism
B. Virilisation
C. Hypothyroidism
D. Hyperprolactinaemia
E. Ovarian failure
## Answer: B — Virilisation
- Clitoromegaly reflects significant androgen excess causing masculinisation of female genitalia, a hallmark of virilising conditions (e.g., CAH, androgen-secreting tumours).
**Q24.** Infertility is defined as failure to conceive after:
A. 6 months
B. 9 months
C. 12 months
D. 18 months
E. 24 months
## Answer: C — 12 months
- Standard clinical definition: 12 months of regular unprotected intercourse without conception (or 6 months if the woman is over 35).
**Q25.** Elevated FSH in a woman with infertility suggests:
A. Hypothalamic dysfunction
B. Pituitary tumour
C. Ovarian failure
D. Hyperprolactinaemia
E. PCOS
## Answer: C — Ovarian failure
- High FSH reflects loss of negative feedback from declining ovarian function (primary ovarian insufficiency/failure), unlike hypothalamic causes (A) which typically show low/normal FSH.
**Q26.** Progesterone >30 nmol/L seven days before menses indicates:
A. Anovulation
B. Ovulation occurred
C. Ovarian failure
D. Pregnancy
E. PCOS
## Answer: B — Ovulation occurred
- A significant mid-luteal progesterone rise confirms that ovulation has taken place that cycle.
**Q27.** 40-year-old man, severe flank pain and haematuria — most likely diagnosis?
A. Pyelonephritis
B. Renal calculi
C. Nephrotic syndrome
D. Acute glomerulonephritis
E. CKD
## Answer: B — Renal calculi
- Severe colicky flank pain with haematuria is the classic presentation of renal/ureteric stones causing obstruction and mucosal trauma.
**Q28.** A patient with nephrotic syndrome is most likely to have which of the following?
A. Haematuria and hypertension only
B. Proteinuria >3.5 g/day
C. Hypernatraemia
D. Low cholesterol
E. Metabolic alkalosis
## Answer: B — Proteinuria >3.5 g/day
- This is the defining diagnostic threshold for nephrotic-range proteinuria. Nephrotic syndrome actually causes hypercholesterolaemia (not D), and haematuria/hypertension (A) are more typical of nephritic syndrome.
**Q29.** A "peak" sample is taken:
A. Before dose
B. At the expected maximum concentration after dose
C. Only at night
D. Randomly
E. Only in renal failure
## Answer: B — At the expected maximum concentration after dose
- By definition, peak drug level monitoring is timed to capture the highest expected serum concentration following administration (contrasted with a "trough," taken just before the next dose).
**Q30.** In hypoalbuminaemia, total phenytoin may be:
A. Falsely high relative to free level
B. Falsely low relative to free level
C. Unchanged always
D. Not measurable
E. Always toxic
## Answer: B — Falsely low relative to free level
- Phenytoin is highly protein-bound; with less albumin available for binding, a greater proportion circulates as free (active) drug, so the measured *total* level underestimates the true free/active concentration — risking under-treatment or missed toxicity if only total levels are checked.
