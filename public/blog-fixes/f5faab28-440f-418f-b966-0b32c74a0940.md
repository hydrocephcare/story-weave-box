# Evaluation of the Well Child: Surveillance, Screening, Immunisation and Anticipatory Guidance

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Community and preventive paediatrics**

> **In one line:** a well-child visit confirms that a healthy child is growing and developing normally, prevents disease (immunisation, supplements, safe practices), screens for hidden problems and supports the family. It follows a fixed set of components that double as an OSCE checklist.

## What you must know

- Five jobs: **monitor growth and development, prevent illness, detect disease early, give anticipatory guidance, support the family**.
- The **Mother and Child Health Booklet** is the child's record; check the immunisation card at every contact.
- **Always plot growth** against WHO standards and look at the **trend**.
- **Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months**; **vitamin A every 6 months from 6 to 59 months**; **deworming from 12 months**.
- Blood pressure from **3 years**; developmental tool at **9, 18 and 24 to 30 months**; M-CHAT-R/F at 18 and 24 months.
- A **minor illness is not a contraindication** to immunisation.
- General danger signs (IMCI): not able to drink or breastfeed, vomits everything, convulsions, lethargic or unconscious.

## 1. Aims of well-child care

1. **Monitor growth and development** so problems are found early.
2. **Prevent illness:** immunisation, micronutrient supplementation, deworming, safe practices.
3. **Detect disease early:** screen vision, hearing, anaemia, HIV, TB exposure, hypertension.
4. **Anticipatory guidance** on what comes next (feeding, sleep, safety, discipline, school).
5. **Support parents and caregivers**; recognise family and social problems, including abuse.

## 2. Schedule of visits

In Kenya the **Mother and Child Health Booklet** holds birth details, immunisation record, growth chart, development milestones, vitamin A and deworming dates, and health education messages.

- **Newborn:** within the first week and at 6 weeks (postnatal and baby check).
- **Immunisation visits:** 6, 10 and 14 weeks, then 9 and 18 months.
- **Growth monitoring and promotion:** monthly in the first year, every 3 months to 2 years, then 6-monthly to 5 years.
- **School entry and school age:** annually, plus vision and hearing screening at school entry.
- **Adolescent visit:** about 10 to 14 years (including HPV vaccination).

## 3. The history

| Component | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Concerns** | What worries the caregiver? |
| **Birth and neonatal** | Gestation, birth weight, delivery, neonatal problems, newborn screening, maternal HIV status and PMTCT |
| **Feeding and nutrition** | Exclusive breastfeeding to 6 months, complementary feeding, diet diversity, formula and water safety, appetite, picky eating |
| **Growth** | Previous weights and lengths (from the booklet) |
| **Development** | Milestones in each domain; school progress; behaviour |
| **Immunisation** | Review the card: given, due, missed |
| **Illness** | Past infections, admissions, allergies, medications, TB contacts, malaria prevention |
| **Sleep, play, screen time, toilet training** | |
| **Family and social** | Who lives at home, caregivers, income, housing, water and sanitation, education, parental health including mental health, violence |
| **School age and adolescents** | Attendance, performance, friends, bullying; **HEADSS** done privately (Home, Education and Employment, Activities, Drugs, Sexuality, Suicide and mood) |

## 4. The examination

Examine the child **undressed, on the caregiver's lap** if small; keep it gentle and playful.

1. **Observation:** general appearance, alertness, interaction, parent-child bonding.
2. **Growth:** weight, length or height, head circumference (under 2 years or if abnormal), MUAC from 6 months; **plot every measurement**.
3. **Vital signs:** temperature, pulse, respiratory rate; **blood pressure from 3 years** (earlier if risk factors).
4. **General:** pallor, jaundice, oedema, lymph nodes, hydration, skin, hair, nails, mouth and teeth.
5. **Head and neck:** fontanelles, head shape, eyes (red reflex, squint), ears, throat, thyroid.
6. **Chest and heart:** air entry, murmurs, femoral pulses.
7. **Abdomen:** liver, spleen, kidneys, hernias; genitalia (undescended testes, hypospadias; pubertal staging in older children).
8. **Back and limbs:** spine, hips (Ortolani and Barlow in infants), feet, gait.
9. **Neurological and development:** tone, reflexes, primitive reflexes, motor milestones, and a **developmental assessment** (Denver II or milestone checklist).

## 5. Growth monitoring and nutrition

- Compare each point with **WHO growth standard** curves; judge the **trend**, not a single point.
- A flat or falling curve, or crossing two centile lines, calls for a dietary history and a search for illness. Early action prevents malnutrition.
- **Breastfeed exclusively for 6 months**, then add **complementary foods** and continue breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond.
- **Vitamin A** every 6 months from 6 to 59 months (100,000 IU at 6 to 11 months; 200,000 IU from 12 months).
- **Deworming** with albendazole every 6 months from 12 months.
- Check for **anaemia** (pallor, haemoglobin); give **iron**, and zinc with ORS in diarrhoea.
- **Malaria prevention:** insecticide-treated nets; the malaria vaccine is offered in some endemic counties.

## 6. Immunisation (Kenya Expanded Programme on Immunisation)

| Age | Vaccines |
|---|---|
| **Birth** | **BCG**, **OPV 0** |
| **6 weeks** | **OPV 1**, **Pentavalent 1** (DPT-HepB-Hib), **PCV10 1**, **Rotavirus 1** |
| **10 weeks** | OPV 2, Pentavalent 2, PCV10 2, Rotavirus 2 |
| **14 weeks** | OPV 3, Pentavalent 3, PCV10 3, **IPV** (one dose) |
| **9 months** | **Measles-rubella (MR) 1**; **yellow fever** in high-risk counties |
| **18 months** | **Measles-rubella 2** |
| **10 years (girls)** | **HPV**, two doses 6 months apart |

> **Check:** the Ministry of Health schedule is revised from time to time; always confirm against the current Kenya schedule and the child's own card.

### Practical points
- Give catch-up doses at the **first contact** with an unvaccinated child. Minor illness (cold, mild fever, mild diarrhoea) is **not** a contraindication.
- **True contraindications:** severe allergic reaction to a previous dose or vaccine component; for live vaccines, severe immunosuppression. **HIV-exposed or infected children should still receive BCG, measles and the rest** unless severely immunosuppressed (assessed individually).
- Keep the **cold chain** (2 to 8 °C), discard open multidose vials per policy, record every dose in the booklet.
- Counsel on expected effects (fever, local swelling) and when to return.

> **Exam tip:** "mild illness is not a contraindication" is the commonest immunisation question; severe allergy to a previous dose is the true one.

## 7. Screening

| What | When and how |
|---|---|
| **Vision** | Newborn **red reflex**; **corneal light reflex** and cover test for squint; acuity at school entry; refer any squint after 3 to 4 months |
| **Hearing** | Newborn screening where available (otoacoustic emissions); ask about response to sound; test any child with speech delay or risk factor (meningitis, prematurity, jaundice, aminoglycosides) |
| **Development** | Surveillance at every visit; formal tool at **9, 18 and 24 to 30 months**; M-CHAT-R/F for autism at 18 and 24 months |
| **Anaemia** | Haemoglobin where pallor or risk factors, and in high-risk areas |
| **HIV** | Early infant diagnosis (PCR) for HIV-exposed infants at 6 weeks; antibody testing from 18 months and for any child at risk |
| **TB** | Symptoms and contacts at every visit; isoniazid preventive therapy for eligible contacts |
| **Hip dysplasia, scoliosis, undescended testes** | Examination at each stage |
| **Blood pressure** | From 3 years, annually |
| **Dental** | Clean teeth from the first tooth; dental checks from 1 year |
| **Newborn metabolic and sickle cell screening** | Where available |

## 8. Anticipatory guidance by age

| Age | Topics |
|---|---|
| **Newborn to 6 months** | Exclusive breastfeeding; safe sleep on the back; cord care; kangaroo mother care for small babies; hand-washing; bonding; talk and sing to the baby; danger signs (not feeding, fits, fever, fast breathing, yellow palms) |
| **6 to 12 months** | Complementary foods; start cup feeding; baby-proofing (falls, burns, choking, poisoning); stranger anxiety; play and reading |
| **1 to 3 years** | Toddler diet; discipline; toilet training; tantrums; language stimulation; safety around water and fires; limit screens |
| **3 to 5 years** | Pre-school readiness; safe play; road safety; dental care; reading together; screens limited to 1 hour a day |
| **School age** | Nutrition and activity; school progress and bullying; sleep; **injury prevention** (road, drowning, burns); body safety; puberty education |
| **Adolescents** | Puberty and sexual health; HPV; substance use; mental health; confidential care; injury and violence prevention |

**Nurturing care:** good health, good nutrition, **responsive caregiving**, **opportunities for early learning**, and **safety and security**. Talking, singing, playing, reading and eye contact all support brain development.

## 9. Danger signs (IMCI)

Check and teach **general danger signs** in children aged 2 months to 5 years: **not able to drink or breastfeed, vomits everything, convulsions, lethargic or unconscious**. Any of these needs urgent referral. Also teach when to return: fast or difficult breathing, fever, diarrhoea with blood or dehydration, worsening illness.

## 10. Ending the visit

- Summarise and **explain** the findings to the caregiver.
- Plot and **record** everything in the booklet.
- Give a **plan**: immunisation due, supplements, follow-up date.
- Answer questions and check understanding.

## Key points to revise

1. A well-child visit has five jobs: monitor, prevent, screen, guide and support.
2. Always plot growth and compare with previous visits.
3. Use the Mother and Child Health Booklet and check the immunisation card every time.
4. Exclusive breastfeeding to 6 months; vitamin A every 6 months from 6 months; deworm from 12 months.
5. Screen hearing, vision, development, anaemia, HIV and TB exposure at the right ages; blood pressure from 3 years.
6. Mild illness is not a reason to withhold a vaccine.
7. Teach IMCI general danger signs to every caregiver.
