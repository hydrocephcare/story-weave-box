# Bone Marrow Failure (Aplastic Anaemia)

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Haematology**

> **In one line:** the bone marrow stops making blood cells, so the patient becomes **pancytopenic** (low red cells, white cells and platelets) while the marrow itself is empty and fatty. The commonest form is **aplastic anaemia**. Think of it as anaemia + infections + bleeding with no spleen, no lymph node enlargement and no blast cells.

## What you must know

- Bone marrow failure and aplastic anaemia are used almost as the same thing: a **hypoplastic or aplastic marrow** with **peripheral blood cytopenias**.
- Most cases are **secondary** (drugs, chemicals, viruses, radiation). Idiopathic ("primary") cases are fewer.
- The mechanisms are a **stem-cell defect, a damaged marrow environment, and immune suppression of the marrow** (the immune one is the main reason immunosuppression works).
- Diagnosis: FBC (pancytopenia with **normal red cell indices**), blood film, **bone marrow aspirate and trephine** (hypocellular, fatty marrow).
- Treatment: supportive care for everyone, then **bone marrow transplant (BMT)** for the young with an HLA-matched donor, or **immunosuppression (ATG/ALG + ciclosporin + steroid)** for the rest.

## 1. Definition

Bone marrow failure is a failure of the marrow stem cells to produce enough mature cells, resulting in **cytopenias in the peripheral blood**. Aplastic anaemia is the classic form: the marrow is replaced by fat and the blood shows pancytopenia.

## 2. Classification

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Idiopathic (cause unknown) |
| **Secondary** | Exposure to toxic agents (drugs, chemicals, viruses) |
| **Associated conditions** | Fanconi anaemia, infective hepatitis, pancreatic insufficiency (Shwachman-Diamond), **paroxysmal nocturnal haemoglobinuria (PNH)**, pure red cell aplasia (congenital or acquired) |

## 3. Causes

1. **Predictable ("inevitable")**: ionising radiation and cytotoxic drugs. The damage is dose-related and happens in everyone given enough.
2. **Idiosyncratic**: idiopathic, drug-induced, viral (hepatitis viruses, EBV, HIV, HPV, dengue, parvovirus B19), some mycobacteria, commercial solvents. These happen only in susceptible people.
3. **Inherited**: Fanconi anaemia, dyskeratosis congenita, Diamond-Blackfan anaemia, others.
4. **Industrial**: **benzene**.
5. **Immune**: drug-induced, viral (EBV), **SLE**.
6. **Marrow infiltration** (the marrow is crowded out, so this is really "replacement", not aplasia):
   - Infective: leishmaniasis (kala-azar), **miliary TB**
   - Storage disorders: **Gaucher disease**
   - Malignancy: lymphomas, plasma cell dyscrasias, metastatic carcinoma, myeloproliferative disorders (leukaemic or not)

### Drugs strongly linked to aplastic anaemia

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| Antibiotics | **Chloramphenicol**, sulphonamides including **cotrimoxazole** |
| NSAIDs | Phenylbutazone, gold salts, indomethacin, benoxaprofen, piroxicam |
| Antithyroid drugs | Thiouracils, **carbimazole** |
| Antimalarials | Amodiaquine, pyrimethamine |
| Anticonvulsants | **Phenytoin** (also carbamazepine) |
| Antidepressants / antipsychotics | Chlorpromazine, prothiaden (dothiepin) |

> **Exam tip:** chloramphenicol can cause two kinds of marrow damage: a **dose-related, reversible** suppression and a rare **idiosyncratic aplastic anaemia** that does not depend on dose and may appear weeks after stopping.

## 4. Pathology

Three mechanisms are proposed, and they overlap:

1. **A qualitative defect of the common stem cell**
2. **A defective marrow microenvironment** (the "soil" cannot support the stem cells)
3. **Immune suppression**: activated T cells (releasing interferon-gamma and TNF) destroy the stem cells. This is why **immunosuppressive therapy works** in most patients.

## 5. Differential diagnosis of acquired marrow failure

Other causes of pancytopenia must be excluded:

- **PNH** (flow cytometry for CD55 and CD59 loss)
- **Myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS)**: hypercellular or dysplastic marrow
- **Large granular lymphocytic leukaemia**
- Immune pancytopenia in **connective tissue disease**, e.g. refractory anaemia in SLE
- **Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML)** (aleukaemic phase)
- **Hairy cell leukaemia** (B-cell; pancytopenia with splenomegaly)
- Megaloblastic anaemia, hypersplenism, marrow infiltration

## 6. Clinical features

- **Primary** disease is much less common than secondary. It can occur at any age but is **rare in infants and children**. It may be acute or chronic.
- **Secondary** disease can occur at any age, acute or chronic.

### Features caused by the low counts

| Cell line | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Red cells (anaemia)** | Lassitude, fatigue, breathlessness, pallor |
| **White cells (neutropenia / leukopenia)** | Fever, chills, mouth ulcers, recurrent and severe infections |
| **Platelets (thrombocytopenia)** | Bruises (ecchymoses), petechiae, bleeding from gums, nose, gut and urine |

### Features of the cause

- Chemical toxicity: rash, itching, joint pain
- Drug-induced: the signs of the condition the drug was treating
- Marrow infiltration: the features of the infiltrating disease (e.g. bone pain, weight loss, lymphadenopathy)
- Features of associated diseases (e.g. short stature and thumb abnormalities in Fanconi anaemia)

> **A clue in the examination:** in pure aplastic anaemia there is **no splenomegaly and no lymphadenopathy**. If there is, think of leukaemia, lymphoma, infiltration or hypersplenism instead.

## 7. Laboratory findings

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Full blood count** | Low Hb and RBC with **normal indices (normocytic)**; low total WBC with **neutropenia**; **low platelets** |
| **Reticulocytes** | Low (a clue to failure of production) |
| **Blood film** | **Normocytic, normochromic**, no polychromasia (no reticulocytes); no blasts, no abnormal cells |
| **ESR** | Raised |
| **Bone marrow aspirate** | Hypoplastic or aplastic marrow, or a **dry tap**, or infiltration |
| **Trephine biopsy** | **Reduced haematopoietic tissue with increased fat spaces and stroma**; this is the best test of cellularity |
| **Others** | Tests for the associated or infiltrative condition: chromosome breakage (Fanconi), flow cytometry (PNH), hepatitis and HIV serology, ANA |

### Severity (Camitta criteria for severe aplastic anaemia)

The marrow cellularity is below 25% **and** at least two of: neutrophils below 0.5 × 10⁹/L, platelets below 20 × 10⁹/L, reticulocytes below 20 × 10⁹/L (corrected). **Very severe** if neutrophils are below 0.2 × 10⁹/L. Severity decides treatment urgency.

## 8. Treatment

### A. Supportive (everyone)

| Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Anaemia** | **Packed red cells** if symptomatic or in failure; irradiated, leucodepleted blood if a transplant may follow |
| **Infection** | Prompt, relevant antibiotics; treat neutropenic fever as an emergency |
| **Bleeding** | **Platelet concentrates** |
| **Splenectomy** | Only if the patient is **transfusion-dependent or not responding** to therapy |

Stop all suspected causative drugs at once. Avoid unnecessary transfusion from family members who might be transplant donors.

### B. Definitive treatment

1. **Bone marrow transplantation (BMT):** for the **young with an HLA-matched sibling donor**. This is the only curative treatment.
2. **Immunosuppression** (for patients without a matched donor, or older patients):
   - **Antithymocyte / antilymphocyte globulin (ATG or ALG)**, always **with corticosteroids to prevent serum sickness**
   - **High-dose methylprednisolone** (20 mg/kg/day with a rapid taper) if ATG or ALG is too expensive
   - **Ciclosporin** 200 to 400 mg/day: reported **85% haematological remission** rate when combined with ATG
   - Cytotoxic drugs (cyclophosphamide) in selected cases
3. **Androgens** (oxymetholone, nandrolone decanoate, testosterone propionate) and steroids: poorly tolerated by **women and children** because of masculinisation. **Danazol** is a non-masculinising androgen that may help.
4. **Haematopoietic growth factors** (G-CSF, GM-CSF) in neutropenic cases
5. **Treat the cause or the original disease**, and stop the offending drug.

> **Choosing between BMT and immunosuppression:** young patient + matched sibling donor = BMT. Older patient, or no donor = ATG + ciclosporin. Relapse and clonal evolution (to PNH, MDS or leukaemia) can follow immunosuppression.

### Prognosis

Without treatment, severe aplastic anaemia is fatal within a year in most cases, usually from infection or haemorrhage. With BMT or immunosuppression, about 70 to 90% of patients survive.

## Key points to revise

1. **Pancytopenia + empty, fatty marrow** = aplastic anaemia.
2. Causes: drugs (chloramphenicol, sulphonamides, NSAIDs, carbimazole, phenytoin), viruses, radiation, benzene, inherited (Fanconi), immune.
3. Anaemia, infections and bleeding are the three clinical problems; **no splenomegaly**.
4. Diagnosis is by **trephine biopsy**; exclude PNH, MDS, leukaemia and infiltration.
5. Supportive care for all; **BMT** for the young with a matched donor; **ATG + ciclosporin + steroids** otherwise.
