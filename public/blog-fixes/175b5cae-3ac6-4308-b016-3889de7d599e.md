# Growth and Development

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · General paediatrics and child development**

> **In one line:** growth is the increase in size or mass of tissues (more cells, bigger cells, more ground substance), while development is the maturation of function and acquisition of skills, driven largely by myelination. Both are continuous, orderly and cephalocaudal, are shaped by genes, hormones and environment, and are tracked by serial measurement on charts and by milestone screening.

## What you must know

- **Growth** = net increase in size or mass; **development** = maturation of function and skills (myelination of neurons).
- Birth weight about **3 kg**; doubles by **5 months**, trebles by **1 year**, quadruples by **2 years**. Length 50 cm at birth, 75 cm at 1 year, 100 cm at 4 years.
- Head circumference: **35 cm at birth, 45 cm at 12 months, 48 cm at 24 months**.
- Four developmental domains: **gross motor, fine motor (adaptive), social, language** (cognition cuts across them).
- Key gross motor milestones: neck holding 3 months, sitting without support 8 months, cruising 10 months, walking without support about 14 months, running 18 months.
- A single measurement does not show growth rate: **velocity** from serial measurements on a growth chart (Road to Health card) is what detects problems early.
- Developmental delay is diagnosed in three steps: **clinical assessment, screening tool, psychometric scale**.

## 1. Definitions and basic principles

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Growth** | Net increase in size or mass of tissues. Due to increase in **cell number** (cells multiply enormously between birth and adulthood), increase in **cell size**, and increase in **ground substance** |
| **Development** | Maturation of function and acquisition of skills. Due to **myelination of neurons** and brain maturation |

Growth and development together distinguish children from adults.

### Characteristics

- Continuous and orderly, but the rate is **not uniform**: there are periods when growth accelerates, slows or is steady (fastest in infancy and at puberty).
- **Generalised mass activity gives way to specific responses** (the primitive reflexes are lost as voluntary control appears).
- Proceeds in a **cephalocaudal direction** (head control before sitting before walking) and from proximal to distal.
- **Different tissues grow at different rates** (neural tissue early, lymphoid tissue peaks in mid-childhood, genital tissue at puberty, general body growth in infancy and puberty).

## 2. Factors affecting growth and development

| Genetic | Environmental |
|---|---|
| Parental phenotype and familial patterns | **Intrauterine:** maternal nutrition, anaemia, pre-eclampsia and hypertension, tobacco, alcohol, drugs, infections |
| Race | **Postnatal:** nutrition, infections, trauma, emotional factors, cultural practices |
| Sex | **Hormones:** growth hormone, insulin, thyroid hormone, sex hormones |
| Genetic disorders: chromosomal and single-gene defects | |

> **Remember:** nutrition and infection are the commonest causes of poor growth in Kenya. Thyroid hormone and growth hormone are key in infancy and childhood; sex hormones drive the pubertal spurt.

## 3. Assessment of physical growth

### 3.1 Measurements and technique

| Parameter | Technique |
|---|---|
| **Weight** | Child weighed **nude** on a lever or electronic scale. A spring balance is less accurate |
| **Length** (under 2 years) | Supine on a rigid table or **infantometer**; legs straightened, feet at right angles, foot board brought up to the heels |
| **Height** (2 years and over) | Standing upright against a wall or **stadiometer**; heels, buttocks and back touching the vertical surface; head in the **Frankfurt plane** (line from the floor of the external auditory meatus to the floor of the orbit is horizontal); head piece firmly over the vertex |
| **Crown-rump length** | Vertex to ischial tuberosity (sitting height) |
| **Head circumference** | Maximum circumference from the occipital protuberance to the forehead |
| **Chest circumference** | At the level of the nipples, midway between inspiration and expiration, in the recumbent position |
| **Mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC)** | Relatively constant between 1 and 5 years, independent of age |

### 3.2 Body proportions

The **upper segment : lower segment (US:LS)** ratio falls as the legs grow faster than the trunk:

| Age | US:LS |
|---|---|
| Birth | 1.7 : 1 |
| 3 years | 1.3 : 1 |
| 6 to 7 years | 1 : 1 |

### 3.3 MUAC

- Normal is about 16.5 to 17.5 cm in 1 to 5 years; **under 12.5 cm indicates malnutrition**.
- Z scores express the child's value as standard deviations from the median reference.

> **Check:** the slide figure for normal MUAC is garbled. In Kenyan practice, MUAC **under 11.5 cm = severe acute malnutrition**, **11.5 to 12.5 cm = moderate acute malnutrition**, over 12.5 cm normal (for ages 6 to 59 months).

### 3.4 Weight-for-height and height-for-age (Waterlow classification)

This separates **acute** (wasting) from **chronic** (stunting) malnutrition:

| Weight for height | Height for age | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 80% or more | 90% or more | Normal |
| 80% or more | Under 90% | **Stunted** (chronic) |
| Under 80% | 90% or more | **Wasted** (acute) |
| Under 80% | Under 90% | **Wasted and stunted** |

## 4. Growth charts, percentiles and standards

- Growth tables and charts come from cross-sectional or longitudinal studies of large populations. Growth varies between individuals; like all biological measurements, growth parameters follow a **normal distribution**.
- **Percentiles:** if 100 children are lined up by weight or height, the 50th percentile child has equal numbers above and below. The child is placed relative to a comparable population.
- **Standard deviation (SD)** shows the permissible limits of normal:
  - ±1 SD includes **68%**; ±2 SD includes **95%**; ±3 SD includes **99%** of the population.
  - +1 SD corresponds to the 84th centile.
  - Usually **±2 SD** form the limits of normal; beyond 2 to 3 SD is abnormal.
- **Standards:** country-specific standards are often drawn from children of high socioeconomic class; the international standard used before was NCHS (USA).

### WHO growth charts (2006)

- Multi-country study (six countries, developed and developing; about 8,500 children) up to 5 years.
- Parameters: weight for age, height for age, weight for height, BMI, head circumference, mid-arm and triceps and subscapular skinfolds, plus **6 key motor milestones**.
- Based **only on breast-fed babies with no environmental constraints to growth**; cross-sectional plus longitudinal data. Breast-fed infants have lower weight for length, z scores and skinfolds.
- **Prescriptive** (how children should grow) rather than descriptive. Detects both **undernutrition and obesity**.

### Velocity of growth

- Serial measurements over time (from longitudinal studies). **One measurement does not show growth rate**; an abnormal percentile may appear only once the growth-retarding factors are profound or prolonged.
- Plotting growth over time is a good epidemiological tool for early detection of malnutrition, infection and growth disorders, e.g. the **Road to Health chart** (the child health card used in Kenya).

> **Exam tip:** a child who is crossing percentile lines downwards (faltering growth) needs action even if still within the normal range.

## 5. Growth rules of thumb

### Weight

- Average birth weight **3 kg**. Loses up to **10%** in the first days and **regains birth weight by about 10 days**.
- Gains **25 to 30 g/day for the first 3 months**, then about **400 g/month** to the end of the first year.
- Birth weight **doubles at 5 months, trebles at 1 year, 4 times at 2 years, 6 times at 5 years, 10 times at 10 years**.
- Alternatively: gains about **2 kg/year from 3 to 7 years**, then about **3 kg/year** until the pubertal spurt.

### Height

| Age | Length / height |
|---|---|
| Birth | 50 cm |
| 3 months | 60 cm |
| 1 year | 75 cm |
| 4 years | 100 cm |
| 4 to 10 years | gains about 5 cm/year |

### Head and chest circumference

| Age | Head circumference |
|---|---|
| Birth | 35 cm |
| 3 months | 40 cm |
| 12 months | 45 cm |
| 24 months | 48 cm |
| 12 years | 52 cm |

- Chest circumference is **3 cm less than the head at birth**, **equal at 1 year**, and exceeds the head thereafter. A chest smaller than the head after 1 year suggests malnutrition.

## 6. Dentition

| Primary (milk) teeth | Age |
|---|---|
| Lower central incisors | 5 to 8 months |
| Upper central incisors | about 1 month later |
| Lateral incisors | within the next 3 months |
| First molars | 12 to 15 months |
| Canines | 18 to 21 months |
| Second molars | 21 to 24 months |

| Permanent teeth | Age |
|---|---|
| First molar | 6 years |
| Central and lateral incisors | 6 to 8 years |
| Canines and premolars | 9 to 12 years |
| Second molar | 12 years |
| Third molar | 18 years or later |

There are 20 primary teeth; a rough rule is age in months minus 6 gives the number of teeth in the first 2 years.

## 7. Bone age

- Indicator of **physiological development**, distinct from chronological age. More advanced in girls (by about 1 year in early childhood, 2 years in mid-childhood).
- Assessed by the **number, shape and size of ossification centres**, and the density, size and shape of the ends of bones.
- Which bone to X-ray:

| Age | X-ray |
|---|---|
| Newborn | Foot and knee |
| Infant 3 to 9 months | Shoulder |
| 1 to 13 years | Wrist and hand |
| 12 to 14 years | Elbow and hip |

- Bone age is delayed in hypothyroidism, growth hormone deficiency and constitutional delay; advanced in precocious puberty and congenital adrenal hyperplasia.

## 8. Behavioural (developmental) milestones

- As the child ages, motor coordination improves and the child reacts willfully to the environment. Milestones are reached at an anticipated age (give or take a few months). **Primitive reflexes are lost.**
- **Four domains:** gross motor, fine motor (adaptive), social, language.
- **Cognitive development:** the ability to think, understand and reason; memory, information processing and problem-solving emerge through childhood.
- **Gross motor** skills use larger muscles (walking, running, balance); **fine motor** skills use small muscles of fingers, toes and eyes (drawing, writing, throwing, waving, catching).

### 8.1 Gross motor

Tested by position:

| Test | Findings by age |
|---|---|
| **Ventral suspension** (held prone and lifted) | Newborn: head flops down. 4 to 12 weeks: head comes to the plane of the body, then above it |
| **Pull to sit** (supine, pulled up by the arms) | Newborn: **head lag**. By 16 to 20 weeks: head in line with the body or ahead, back straight |
| **Prone** | Newborn: turns head to one side. 1 month: lifts chin briefly. 3 months: lifts head and upper chest. 6 months: lifts head and chest. 5 to 8 months: rolls over (first back to side, then front). 8 months: crawls |
| **Sitting** | 5 months: sits with support. 8 months: sits steadily with straight back, no support. 10 months: pulls from lying to sitting |
| **Standing** | 4 months: bears weight on legs. 9 months: early stepping, pulls to stand with furniture. 10 months: cruising. 13 to 15 months: walks unsupported. 15 months: walks sideways or backwards |
| **Stairs** | 2 years: climbs up, 2 feet per step. 3 years: climbs up, one foot per step. 4 years: climbs down, one foot per step |

**Key gross motor milestones:**

| Age | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 3 months | Neck holding |
| 5 months | Sitting with support |
| 8 months | Sitting without support |
| 9 months | Standing with support |
| 10 months | Cruising |
| 12 months | Standing without support |
| 14 months | Walking without support |
| 18 months | Running |
| 24 months | Walking upstairs |

### 8.2 Fine motor (adaptive)

Covers eye coordination, hand-eye, hand-mouth coordination and manipulation. Tested with a red ring, pen torch, red cubes (2.5 cm), pellet, cup with handle, spoon, book with thick pages, crayon, paper, wooden blocks, doll and mirror.

| Area | Milestones |
|---|---|
| **Eye** | 4 weeks: regards a torch or red ring 20 cm in front. 6 weeks: follows side to side unsteadily. 2 to 3 months: follows with steady eye movements. **Binocular vision** by 3 to 6 months |
| **Hand-eye** | 4 months: tries to grasp a dangling ring, may overshoot. 5 months: reaches and grasps with the **ulnar** side. 6 months: **radial grasp**, transfers hand to hand. 10 months: **pincer grasp** |
| **Hand-mouth** | 1 year: tries to feed with a spoon, spills. 15 months: feeds with spoon. 18 months: drinks from a cup |
| **Book** | 13 months: turns 2 to 3 pages at a time. 24 months: one page at a time |
| **Scribbling and copying** | 12 to 24 months: scribbles. 2 years: copies a vertical line. 2.5 years: horizontal line. 3 years: circle. 4 years: cross and rectangle. 5 years: triangle (and copies cross) |

### 8.3 Personal and social

| Age | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1 month | Regards mother's or caregiver's face |
| 2 months | **Social smile**; briefly calms self (hands to mouth, sucks hand); tries to look at parent |
| 3 months | Recognises mother or caregiver |
| 6 months | Enjoys mirror |
| 7 to 8 months | Separation (stranger) anxiety |
| 9 months | Waves bye-bye |

### 8.4 Language

| Age | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1 month | Turns head towards sound |
| 3 to 5 months | Vowel sounds, gurgles |
| 6 months | Monosyllables (ba, da) |
| 9 months | Bisyllables (mama, dada) |
| 10 months | Understands spoken speech |
| 12 months | Two words with meaning |
| 18 months | About 20 words |
| 24 months | Joins 2 to 3 words in a short sentence |
| 3 years | About 250 words |

### 8.5 Bowel and bladder control

- Early months: the **gastrocolic reflex** causes defaecation after each feed.
- From about 7 months, stooling is unrelated to feeds.
- Toilet training is possible by **18 months to 2 years**.

> **Exam tip:** the commonest exam question is "what can a child of X months do?" Learn the key anchors: social smile 2 months, sits unsupported 8 months, pincer 10 months, walks about 12 to 14 months, 2 words at 12 months, sentences at 2 years.

## 9. Developmental delay and screening

- **Three-step diagnosis:** (1) clinical assessment (history, examination, observation), (2) screening tools, (3) psychometric scales.
- **Developmental screening tests:**
  - **Denver Developmental Screening Test**: most widely used; 4 scales, 125 items.
  - **Baroda Development Screening Test**: adapted from the Bayley scales for Indian children.
  - **Trivandrum Development Screening Test**.
- Red flags: no social smile by 3 months, no head control by 4 months, not sitting by 9 months, not walking by 18 months, no words by 18 months, any loss of previously acquired skills (regression), persistent primitive reflexes.
- Evaluate for causes: prematurity and perinatal insult, hypothyroidism, hearing and vision impairment, genetic syndromes, CNS infection, malnutrition, deprivation.

## Key points to revise

1. Growth is increase in size (cell number, cell size, ground substance); development is maturation of function driven by myelination.
2. Growth is continuous, orderly, cephalocaudal and tissue-specific; the rate is not uniform.
3. Weight: 3 kg at birth, doubles at 5 months, trebles at 1 year, quadruples at 2 years; length 50, 75 and 100 cm at birth, 1 year and 4 years.
4. Head circumference 35, 45 and 48 cm at birth, 12 and 24 months; chest equals head at 1 year.
5. Waterlow classification: low weight-for-height = wasting (acute); low height-for-age = stunting (chronic); MUAC under 12.5 cm indicates malnutrition.
6. Within ±2 SD (about 95%) is normal; WHO 2006 charts are prescriptive and based on breast-fed infants.
7. Anchor milestones: social smile 2 months, neck holding 3, sits unsupported 8, pincer 10, walks 12 to 14, words at 12, running 18, sentences 24 months.
8. Developmental delay is diagnosed by clinical assessment, screening (Denver) and psychometric scales.
