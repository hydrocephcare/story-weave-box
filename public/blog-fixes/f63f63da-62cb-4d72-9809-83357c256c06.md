## ONCOPATHOLOGY
Question 1 — Hallmarks of Cancer (5 marks — need 5 clear points)
1. Self-sufficiency in growth signals — oncogene activation (RAS, MY
C) → proliferates without external growth signals
2. Insensitivity to anti-growth signals — loss of tumour suppressors (p53, R
B) → ignores stop signals
3. Evasion of apoptosis — p53 mutation, ↑BCL-2 → resists programmed cell death
4. Limitless replicative potential — telomerase reactivation → immortalization, avoids senescence
5. Sustained angiogenesis — VEGF secretion → new vessel formation for nutrient supply
6. Invasion and metastasis — ↓E-cadherin, matrix metalloproteinase secretion → basement membrane breach, spread
(Extra, for a stronger answer beyond the classic 6): evasion of immune destruction, reprogrammed energy metabolism (Warburg effect), genome instability, tumour-promoting inflammation.

Question 2 — Classification of Carcinogens (10 marks — needs categories + examples)
1. Chemical carcinogens
- Direct-acting: react with DNA directly, no activation needed (e.g. alkylating chemotherapy agents)
- Indirect-acting (procarcinogens, need metabolic activation):
 - Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (tobacco smoke, smoked food) → lung, skin cancer
 - Aromatic amines/azo dyes → bladder cancer
 - Nitrosamines (cured meats) → GI cancer
 - Aflatoxin B1 (*Aspergillus*, stored grains) → hepatocellular carcinoma
 - Vinyl chloride → liver angiosarcoma
 - Asbestos → mesothelioma, bronchogenic carcinoma
2. Physical carcinogens
- UV radiation → skin cancers (pyrimidine dimers)
- Ionizing radiation → leukaemia, thyroid, bone cancer
- Chronic irritation/foreign bodies
3. Biological carcinogens
- Viruses: HPV 16/18 (cervical Ca — E6/E7 degrade p53/RB), Hep B/C (HCC), EBV (Burkitt lymphoma, nasopharyngeal Ca), HTLV-1 (adult T-cell leukaemia), HHV-8 (Kaposi sarcoma)
- Bacteria: *H. pylori* → gastric adenocarcinoma, MALT lymphoma
- Parasites: *Schistosoma haematobium* → bladder SCC; liver flukes → cholangiocarcinoma
4. Hormonal carcinogens
- Unopposed oestrogen → endometrial carcinoma
- DES in utero → vaginal clear cell adenocarcinoma
Mechanism (bonus line): initiation (irreversible mutation) → promotion (clonal expansion) → progression (further mutations → malignant phenotype).

Section B: Nomenclature of Tumours (15 marks — needs structure + exceptions + examples)
General rule: named by tissue of origin + suffix denoting benign or malignant behaviour.
**Benign tumours** — suffix "-oma"
- Epithelial: adenoma (glandular), papilloma (surface projection), cystadenoma (cystic)
- Mesenchymal: lipoma (fat), fibroma (fibrous tissue), leiomyoma (smooth muscle), chondroma (cartilage), osteoma (bone), haemangioma (vessels)
**Malignant tumours**
- Epithelial origin → **carcinoma** (squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma)
- Mesenchymal origin → **sarcoma** (liposarcoma, fibrosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, angiosarcoma)
**Mixed and special tumours**
- Mixed tumour: >1 neoplastic cell line, single germ layer, e.g. pleomorphic adenoma of salivary gland
- Teratoma: from totipotent germ cells, tissue from all 3 germ layers (mature = benign, immature = malignant)
**Exceptions to the naming convention (important — examiners love these)**
- Sound benign ("-oma") but are **malignant**: lymphoma, melanoma, mesothelioma, seminoma
- Hamartoma: benign, disorganized overgrowth of native tissue in its normal site
- Choristoma: benign, normal tissue in an abnormal location
- Leukaemia: malignant, named by "-aemia" not "-oma"
- Meningioma: benign, despite naming pattern
**Prefix guide (tissue of origin)**
- Adeno- = gland, Lipo- = fat, Fibro- = fibrous tissue, Chondro- = cartilage, Osteo- = bone, Angio- = blood vessels, Rhabdomyo- = skeletal muscle, Leiomyo- = smooth muscle
