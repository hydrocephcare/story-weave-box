# Vasculitides

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Rheumatology**

> **In one line:** vasculitis is inflammation of blood vessels that causes narrowing (stenosis, ischaemia, infarction) or dilatation (aneurysm) depending on vessel size. The disease is named and classified by the size of vessel involved: large (giant cell arteritis, Takayasu), medium (polyarteritis nodosa, Kawasaki) and small (IgA vasculitis, ANCA-associated vasculitis). Treatment is immunosuppression in an induction phase then maintenance.

## What you must know

- Vasculitis = inflammation of vessel walls, causing **stenosis** and **aneurysm**. In children the commonest are **IgA vasculitis (Henoch-Schonlein purpura)** and **Kawasaki disease**.
- **IgA vasculitis:** palpable purpura on legs and buttocks, colicky abdominal pain, arthralgia, haematuria / proteinuria. Small vessel, **type III hypersensitivity**.
- **Kawasaki disease:** leading cause of acquired heart disease in developed countries; **coronary aneurysms** in 15 to 25% untreated. **IVIG plus aspirin** lowers this to 4 to 9%.
- **ANCA:** **c-ANCA (anti-PR3)** in granulomatosis with polyangiitis; **p-ANCA (anti-MPO)** in microscopic polyangiitis and EGPA.
- **Takayasu arteritis:** large vessel (aorta and branches), absent or asymmetric pulses, BP difference over 10 mmHg between limbs.
- **Giant cell arteritis:** temporal pain, jaw claudication, visual loss; treat promptly with **steroids**.
- Management: **induction** (prednisone plus cyclophosphamide, about 6 months), then **maintenance** (azathioprine, MMF, prednisone); rituximab for refractory disease.

## 1. Introduction and definition

- **Vasculitis** is inflammation of blood vessels, which may lead to **narrowing (stenosis)** and **dilatation (aneurysms)**.
- **IgA vasculitis (Henoch-Schonlein purpura, HSP)** and **Kawasaki disease** are the most common; HSP incidence is 10 to 20 per 100,000.
- Primary vasculitides are rare, especially in children.

### Classification by vessel size (Chapel Hill, general knowledge)

| Vessel size | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Large** | Giant cell arteritis, **Takayasu arteritis** |
| **Medium** | **Polyarteritis nodosa**, **Kawasaki disease** |
| **Small, ANCA-associated** | **Granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA, Wegener's)**, **microscopic polyangiitis (MPA)**, **eosinophilic GPA (EGPA, Churg-Strauss)** |
| **Small, immune-complex** | **IgA vasculitis (HSP)**, cryoglobulinaemic vasculitis |

## 2. "Could this be vasculitis?" clinical clues

| System | Clues |
|---|---|
| **Systemic** | Fever, weight loss, fatigue |
| **Vascular** | Hypertension, absent or asymmetric pulses, palpable aneurysms, tissue gangrene |
| **Cutaneous** | **Palpable purpura**, painful nodules, **livedo reticularis**, ulceration |
| **Pulmonary** | Infiltrates or haemorrhage |
| **Neurological** | Headache, **mononeuritis multiplex**, focal CNS signs |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Arthralgia, arthritis, myalgia, myositis |
| **Renal** | Haematuria, hypertension, renal failure |
| **Laboratory** | Abnormal urinalysis, **ANCA**, raised ESR and CRP |

## 3. Giant cell arteritis (GCA) and polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR)

### Giant cell arteritis (temporal arteritis)

- Large vessel vasculitis (granulomatous) affecting the temporal and other cranial arteries.
- Symptoms: **pain over the temporal arteries**, **jaw claudication when eating**, **visual disturbance / visual loss**.
- Treatment: **steroids**, started immediately without waiting for biopsy.

> **Check:** the slide says "occurs in people aged under 50". GCA occurs in people **aged over 50** (mean about 70). Temporal artery biopsy confirms the diagnosis; untreated, it can cause irreversible blindness (anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy).

### Polymyalgia rheumatica

- Closely related to GCA; mainly in those **over 70** (usually over 50), commoner in **women**.
- **Pain, stiffness and inflammation of the muscles of the pelvic and shoulder girdles**; also fever, tiredness, loss of appetite, weight loss and depression.
- Treatment: **steroids** (low-dose prednisolone, with rapid response). ESR/CRP markedly raised.

## 4. IgA vasculitis (Henoch-Schonlein purpura)

- **Small vessel vasculitis** with **type III hypersensitivity** and **IgA deposition**.
- Classification features: **purpura mainly on lower limbs; abdominal pain; haematuria or proteinuria; arthritis or arthralgia; IgA on biopsy**.
- Course variable and often relapsing: **up to a third relapse within 4 months**. May cause **end-stage renal disease**.

### Clinical features

- **Palpable purpura**, mainly lower limbs and buttocks.
- **Colicky abdominal pain** with vomiting, haematemesis, melaena (risk of intussusception).
- **Arthritis or arthralgia**, mainly lower limbs, in up to **60%**.
- **Renal involvement in 25 to 60%**: proteinuria, haematuria.
- Others: orchitis, pulmonary haemorrhage.
- Usually self-limited with supportive care (analgesia, hydration); steroids for severe abdominal or renal disease.

## 5. Kawasaki disease (KD)

- **Second commonest paediatric vasculitis**; medium vessel vasculitis.
- **Leading cause of acquired heart disease in developed countries.**
- **IVIG and aspirin** reduce coronary artery aneurysm from **25% to 4 to 9%**.
- Aetiology unknown. Worldwide, with bias to Asian / Japanese populations.
- Incidence: Japan **184.6 per 100,000 in under-fives**; UK 5 to 8 per 100,000.

### Clinical features

- Classic features (standard knowledge): fever for 5 days or more with at least 4 of: bilateral non-purulent conjunctivitis, oral changes (strawberry tongue, red cracked lips), cervical lymphadenopathy, polymorphous rash, and changes in the extremities (oedema, erythema, later peeling).
- **Coronary involvement** occurs in **15 to 25%** of untreated cases and is the most important manifestation.
- **Irritability** is an important sign.
- **Reactivation / erythema and induration of the BCG scar** (mechanism unknown).
- Other: aseptic meningitis, arthritis, sterile pyuria, **macrophage activation syndrome**.

### Differential diagnosis

Systemic JIA, scarlet fever, measles and other viral infections, SLE.

> **Check:** the slide lists "hand, foot and mouth syndrome" under scarlet fever; these are separate conditions, and both are in the differential for fever with rash.

## 6. ANCA-associated vasculitis

### ANCA patterns

| Pattern | Target antigen | Associated diseases |
|---|---|---|
| **c-ANCA** | **PR3** | **GPA (30 to 90%)**, EGPA (25 to 50%) |
| **p-ANCA** | **MPO** (also lactoferrin, elastase, cathepsin G) | **MPA (25 to 75%)**, EGPA (50 to 75%), ulcerative colitis (40 to 80%), sclerosing cholangitis (65 to 85%), Crohn's (10 to 40%) |
| p-ANCA (BPI) | Bactericidal permeability-increasing protein | Cystic fibrosis (80 to 90%) |
| p-ANCA (actin) | Actin | Autoimmune hepatitis type I (70 to 75%) |

### Granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA, formerly Wegener's)

- **Granulomatous inflammation of the respiratory tract** with **necrotising vasculitis of small to medium vessels**.
- **Classification criteria (any 2 give sensitivity 88.2% and specificity 92.0%):**
  1. **Nasal or oral inflammation:** oral ulcers, purulent or bloody nasal discharge
  2. **Abnormal chest X-ray:** nodules, fixed infiltrates or cavities
  3. **Abnormal urine sediment:** more than 5 RBC per high power field or RBC casts
  4. **Granulomatous inflammation** on biopsy: granuloma in or around an artery or arteriole

| Airway disease in GPA | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Sinusitis | 60% |
| Epistaxis | 50% |
| Saddle nose deformity | 25% |
| Oral ulcers | 20% |
| Otitis media | 30% |
| Subglottic stenosis | 30% |
| Pulmonary haemorrhage | 30% |

| Systems affected in 65 children with GPA | Frequency |
|---|---|
| ENT | 94% |
| Pulmonary | 80% |
| Renal | 67% |
| Joint | 58% |
| Ocular | 54% |
| Skin | 51% |
| Venous thrombosis | 16% |

- **Limited disease:** confined to the upper airways and lungs.
- **Severe disease:** rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis, pulmonary haemorrhage, mononeuritis multiplex, scleritis or peripheral ulcerative keratitis.

### Microscopic polyangiitis (MPA)

- Small vessel vasculitis, often with **pulmonary capillaritis**; **rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis**; **p-ANCA (anti-MPO) positive**. No granulomas.

### Eosinophilic GPA (EGPA, Churg-Strauss)

- **Eosinophilic granulomatous inflammation** of the respiratory tract.
- Often **late-onset asthma** and **eosinophilia**.

## 7. Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN)

- **Necrotising vasculitis of medium-sized muscular arteries** with **aneurysm formation** along vessel walls.
- Third commonest childhood vasculitis after IgA vasculitis and Kawasaki disease; peak age **7 to 11 years**.
- Some cases, especially in adults, are associated with **hepatitis B** infection. ANCA is usually negative.
- Clinical: **systemic** (fever, weight loss, myalgia, rash, abdominal pain, arthritis); **skin** (livedo reticularis, tender subcutaneous nodules, infarction); **renal** (hypertension, proteinuria, haematuria); **GI** (haemorrhage, pain); **neurological** (focal deficits, hemiplegia); **testicular pain**.
- Diagnosis: biopsy or **angiography showing microaneurysms**.

## 8. Takayasu arteritis

- **Inflammation of the aorta and its large branches**, with **stenosis, dilatation and aneurysm**.
- Named after **Mikito Takayasu**, a Japanese ophthalmologist who described retinal vasculitis with absent pulses ("pulseless disease"). Typically young women.

| Phase | Features |
|---|---|
| **Acute** | Fever, weight loss, malaise, arthralgia, myalgia; rash (erythema nodosum, pyoderma gangrenosum); arthritis; hypertension; thrombosis, MI |
| **Chronic** | **Asymmetric or absent pulses**; **BP difference over 10 mmHg between limbs** (measure all 4 limbs); systemic hypertension; arterial bruits; heart failure; claudication; abdominal pain; CNS ischaemia (dizziness, headache, seizures, TIA, stroke); eye (diplopia, blurred vision); renal (renovascular hypertension, renal failure) |

**Differentials:** other vasculitides, fibromuscular dysplasia, Williams syndrome, aortic stenosis, TB.

## 9. Investigations

- Raised **ESR and CRP**.
- **Urine dipstick:** proteinuria, haematuria; renal and liver function.
- **ANCA, ANA, anti-dsDNA** (also complement, hepatitis B and C, cryoglobulins).
- **Biopsy:** kidney, respiratory tract, skin, temporal artery.
- **Radiology:** chest X-ray for cavities and nodules; CT or X-ray of sinuses for erosions; **ECG, CXR, echo**.
- **MRA, CTA, conventional angiography** for large and medium vessel disease.

## 10. Management

| Phase | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Supportive** | Pain relief, **blood pressure control**, aspirin |
| **Induction (about 6 months)** | **Prednisone** plus **cyclophosphamide** (rituximab as an alternative in ANCA disease) |
| **Maintenance** | **Azathioprine**, mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), prednisone, aspirin |
| **Biologics** | **Rituximab**, anti-TNF |
| **Kawasaki disease** | **IVIG plus aspirin** (within 10 days of fever onset) |
| **Takayasu arteritis** | Steroids; **surgery** (bypass or angioplasty) for critical stenosis |
| GCA and PMR | **Steroids** |

> **Exam tip:** in Kawasaki disease give IVIG and aspirin early; echocardiography at diagnosis and follow-up. In suspected GCA give steroids immediately to prevent blindness.

## 11. Complications

- **Renal failure**
- **Tissue or digital gangrene**
- **Stroke** (thrombotic or hypertensive)
- Coronary aneurysms (Kawasaki)
- **Treatment-related:** steroid toxicity, **gonadal failure with cyclophosphamide** (also haemorrhagic cystitis, infection, malignancy)

## Key points to revise

1. Vasculitis = vessel inflammation causing stenosis and aneurysm; classify by vessel size.
2. IgA vasculitis: purpura, abdominal pain, arthritis, nephritis; IgA deposits; relapses in a third.
3. Kawasaki: fever, conjunctivitis, rash, lymphadenopathy; coronary aneurysm in 15 to 25%; IVIG plus aspirin.
4. c-ANCA / PR3 = GPA; p-ANCA / MPO = MPA, EGPA (and IBD, autoimmune liver disease).
5. GPA = upper airway, lung and kidney; saddle nose, subglottic stenosis, RPGN.
6. PAN = medium vessel, aneurysms, hepatitis B; Takayasu = pulseless disease, BP difference over 10 mmHg.
7. GCA/PMR = steroids; do not delay for biopsy.
8. Treat in two phases: induction (prednisone plus cyclophosphamide, about 6 months) then maintenance (azathioprine / MMF).
