# Osteoporosis

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Rheumatology and Metabolic Bone Disease**

> **In one line:** osteoporosis is a skeletal disease of low bone mass and damaged bone micro-architecture, caused by bone resorption exceeding bone formation (classically from oestrogen deficiency after the menopause or ageing). It is silent until a fragility fracture occurs, is diagnosed by DXA (T-score of -2.5 or below), and is treated by reducing fracture risk with calcium, vitamin D, lifestyle change and anti-resorptive drugs such as bisphosphonates.

## What you must know

- **Definition:** low bone mass plus micro-architectural deterioration, causing bone fragility and fracture. Bone is **porous and brittle** (in osteomalacia it is soft).
- **WHO definition (DXA T-score):** normal above -1; osteopenia -1 to -2.5; **osteoporosis -2.5 or below**; severe (established) osteoporosis is -2.5 or below with a fragility fracture.
- **Primary** osteoporosis (commonest): postmenopausal (type I, oestrogen deficiency) and senile (type II, ageing and calcium deficiency). **Secondary:** disease, deficiency or drugs (steroids, hypogonadism, hyperthyroidism, myeloma).
- The **vertebral fracture** is the commonest; **two thirds are painless**, and patients present with height loss and kyphosis.
- Female to male ratio about **4:1**; half of postmenopausal women have an osteoporotic fracture in their lifetime.
- Baseline tests exclude secondary causes and mimics (FBC, calcium, TFTs, vitamin D, protein electrophoresis, urine calcium).
- **First-line drug: bisphosphonates** (alendronate, risedronate, zoledronate), with calcium and vitamin D.
- HRT is no longer first-line (breast cancer, stroke, VTE).

## 1. Definition

- National Osteoporosis Foundation: a disease characterised by **low bone mass and micro-architectural deterioration of bone tissue, leading to bone fragility and increased susceptibility to fracture**.
- The bone has lower density and higher fracture risk.
- **WHO** definition is based on **bone mineral density (BMD)** measured by DXA, expressed as a T-score (standard deviations from the mean of a young healthy adult):

| Category | T-score |
|---|---|
| Normal | -1.0 or above |
| Osteopenia (low bone mass) | between -1.0 and -2.5 |
| **Osteoporosis** | **-2.5 or below** |
| Severe (established) osteoporosis | -2.5 or below plus one or more fragility fractures |

## 2. Epidemiology

- In the USA, more than 10 million adults aged 50 and above have osteoporosis and more than 43 million have low BMD.
- Globally it is **by far the commonest metabolic bone disease**, affecting over **200 million** people.
- Risk **rises with age** as BMD declines.
- **Women** are at much higher risk: half of postmenopausal women will have an osteoporosis-related fracture. **Female to male ratio about 4:1.**
- Whites (especially northern European) and Asians have higher risk.

## 3. Aetiology

### Primary osteoporosis (most common)

- **Juvenile osteoporosis**
- **Idiopathic**, subdivided into:
  - **Postmenopausal (type I):** mainly **oestrogen deficiency**; affects trabecular bone (vertebrae, distal radius).
  - **Age-associated or senile (type II):** mainly the **ageing skeleton and calcium deficiency**; affects cortical and trabecular bone (hip).

### Secondary osteoporosis

- Occurs when an underlying **disease, deficiency or drug** causes bone loss. **Renal hypercalciuria** is one of the most important causes and is treatable with **thiazide diuretics**.
- Other causes (general knowledge): glucocorticoids, hypogonadism, hyperthyroidism, hyperparathyroidism, Cushing's syndrome, myeloma, coeliac disease and malabsorption, chronic liver and kidney disease, rheumatoid arthritis, anticonvulsants, heparin, alcohol, smoking, immobility, anorexia nervosa, HIV and antiretrovirals (tenofovir).

### Risk factors

Older age, female sex, early menopause or oophorectomy, family history (hip fracture in a parent), low BMI, previous fragility fracture, smoking, excess alcohol, low calcium and vitamin D intake, sedentary life, long-term steroids, recurrent falls.

## 4. Pathophysiology

- **Composition of bone:** organic part is **collagen** and the cells (**osteoblasts, osteoclasts, osteocytes**); inorganic part is mainly **calcium phosphate** (hydroxyapatite).
- Osteoporosis arises from **resorption (osteoclasts) exceeding formation (osteoblasts)**.
- It results from a **failure to reach peak bone mass** and/or **accelerated bone loss**.
- Bone mass rises during growth to a **peak between 20 and 40 years**, then falls.
- In women, **oestrogen deficiency after the menopause** causes an accelerated phase of loss through **uncoupling** of resorption and formation: osteoclasts remove bone faster than osteoblasts replace it.
- **Age-related bone loss** (both sexes): resorption is not especially increased, but **formation is reduced** and cannot keep up. Marrow stem cells differentiate more into **adipocytes** than osteoblasts, so fat accumulates in the marrow space.

## 5. Clinical features

- Osteoporosis is a **"silent disease"**: it is not apparent until a fracture occurs.
- **Vertebral fracture is the commonest.** **Two thirds are painless**, though patients notice **stooped posture and height loss**.

| Fracture site | Presentation |
|---|---|
| **Vertebral** | Acute back pain; gradual height loss; **kyphosis** with chronic pain. Acute pain may radiate to the anterior chest or abdominal wall (can mimic MI or abdominal disease) but is **worse on movement with local tenderness** |
| **Peripheral** (distal radius/Colles, humerus, hip) | Local pain, tenderness, deformity, often after **minimal trauma** |
| **Hip** | Affected leg **shortened and externally rotated** |

## 6. Diagnosis

Approach: **history** (risk factors), **examination**, **investigations**.

### Baseline laboratory tests (to find secondary causes and mimics)

| Test | Reason |
|---|---|
| FBC | Anaemia |
| Serum chemistry (calcium, phosphate, ALP), LFTs | Metabolic bone disease, liver disease |
| **TSH** | Thyroid dysfunction is associated with osteoporosis |
| **25-hydroxyvitamin D** | Vitamin D insufficiency predisposes |
| **Serum protein electrophoresis** | Multiple myeloma |
| **24-hour urine calcium/creatinine** | Hypercalciuria |
| **Testosterone, LH/FSH** | Male hypogonadism |

### Bone turnover markers

| Formation markers | Resorption markers |
|---|---|
| Serum total ALP | Urinary hydroxyproline |
| Bone-specific ALP | Urinary total pyridinoline (PYD) |
| Serum osteocalcin | Urinary free deoxypyridinoline (DPD) |
| Serum type 1 procollagen | Urinary collagen type 1 (e.g. N-telopeptide) |

Used mainly to monitor response to treatment.

### Imaging

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| Plain radiography | Late sign (30 to 40% bone loss); shows fractures, wedge or biconcave vertebrae |
| **DXA** | Gold standard; BMD at **lumbar spine, hip and proximal femur** |
| Quantitative CT | Volumetric BMD |
| Quantitative ultrasonography | **No radiation**, but less accurate |
| Single-photon emission CT, MRI | Fracture age, other pathology |
| **Bone scan** | Radionuclide; detects increased osteoblastic activity (fracture, metastases) |
| Bone biopsy | Reserved for unclear cases |

## 7. Differential diagnosis

Osteomalacia, tumours, osteonecrosis, bone infection, bone-softening metabolic disorders (renal osteodystrophy, hyperparathyroidism), **metastatic bone disease** and myeloma.

> **Exam tip:** osteoporosis = **porous and brittle** bone with normal mineralisation and normal calcium, phosphate and ALP. Osteomalacia = **soft** bone from defective mineralisation, with low vitamin D and often raised ALP.

## 8. Management

The aim is to **reduce the risk of fracture**.

### Non-pharmacological

- Stop smoking, moderate alcohol.
- Adequate dietary calcium; weight-bearing exercise.
- Patients with recurrent falls or unsteadiness: refer to a **multidisciplinary falls prevention team** (including occupational therapy).
- **Hip protectors** can reduce hip fracture risk.

### Drug treatment

| Drug | Mechanism and notes |
|---|---|
| **Bisphosphonates**: **alendronate, risedronate, zoledronate**, etidronate | Bind **hydroxyapatite** on the bone surface; released inside osteoclasts when they resorb bone, inhibiting signalling essential for osteoclast function. **First line.** Zoledronate is effective in postmenopausal osteoporosis. Take orally on an empty stomach with water and stay upright 30 minutes (oesophagitis); rarely jaw osteonecrosis and atypical femoral fracture |
| **Denosumab** | Monoclonal antibody (anti-RANKL); powerful inhibitor of resorption; reduces fractures |
| **Calcium and vitamin D** | Widely used as an **adjunct** to other treatments |
| **Parathyroid hormone (teriparatide, PTH 1-34)** | **Anabolic**; stimulates new bone formation; **20 µg once daily subcutaneously** |
| **HRT** (cyclical oestrogen with progestogen), **raloxifene, tibolone** | HRT prevents postmenopausal loss and reduces vertebral and non-vertebral fractures |
| **Raloxifene** | **SERM**; reduces bone resorption via oestrogen receptors; reduces invasive breast cancer; increases **DVT, stroke and hot flushes** |
| **Calcitonin** | Reduces osteoclast activity; for women more than 5 years post-menopause with low bone mass; **not first line**; reserved for those who refuse or cannot take bisphosphonates; give with calcium and vitamin D |

- **HRT** is mainly for prevention in women with **early menopause** and for treatment in women in their early fifties with troublesome menopausal symptoms. **Avoid in older women with established osteoporosis** (breast cancer, cardiovascular disease, stroke, VTE).
- Oestrogen-progestin is no longer first-line; it is recommended only if the patient also needs relief of menopausal symptoms.
- Regular monitoring (DXA at intervals, markers) may be helpful.

## 9. Prevention

- Prevention **starts in childhood** (build peak bone mass).
- Behaviour: stop smoking, **weight-bearing exercise**, adequate calcium and vitamin D, less alcohol, **fall prevention**.
- Drugs: calcium supplementation; **raloxifene or bisphosphonates (alendronate or risedronate)**. Bisphosphonates should be considered **first-line** for prevention.

## Key points to revise

1. Osteoporosis = low bone mass plus architectural damage; T-score -2.5 or below.
2. Resorption exceeds formation; oestrogen deficiency uncouples the two after menopause.
3. Type I (postmenopausal) versus type II (senile); look for secondary causes, including renal hypercalciuria.
4. Silent until fracture; vertebral fractures are commonest and two thirds are painless.
5. DXA is the diagnostic test; baseline labs exclude myeloma, thyroid, vitamin D and hypogonadism.
6. First-line treatment: bisphosphonates plus calcium and vitamin D; teriparatide is the anabolic drug.
7. Raloxifene reduces breast cancer but increases DVT and stroke; HRT is avoided in older women.
8. Prevention starts in childhood: exercise, calcium, vitamin D, no smoking, falls prevention.
