## HERPESVIRIDAE

## OVERVIEW
**Definition:** Large, enveloped, double-stranded DNA viruses with icosahedral capsids. DNA replicates in the host cell nucleus.
**Name origin:** From Greek *herpein* = "to creep" → reflects spreading nature of skin lesions.
**Significance:** Second leading cause of human viral disease after influenza/cold viruses.
**8 Human Pathogens:**
- HSV-1 and HSV-2
- Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV)
- Cytomegalovirus (CMV)
- Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV)
- HHV-6, HHV-7, HHV-8
**General Characteristics (All Herpesviruses):**
- Most primary infections are asymptomatic
- Establish **latency** after primary infection — never fully cleared
- All capable of **reactivation** causing recurrent disease
- In tissue culture: produce intranuclear inclusion bodies + cytopathic effect (ballooning, rounding → cell death)

## HERPES SIMPLEX VIRUS (HSV)
### Types
| Feature | HSV-1 | HSV-2 |
|---|---|---|
| Primary site | Oral/facial | Genital |
| Transmission | Skin contact, inhalation, autoinoculation | Sexual intercourse |
| Latency site | Trigeminal ganglion | Sacral ganglia |
> Structurally identical; serological cross-reaction exists; share gene sequence homology.
### Pathogenesis
1. Replication at initial infection site
2. Virus migrates via neurons → sensory ganglia → **latency**
3. Reactivation triggered by: UV light, fever, stress, hormonal changes, surgical trauma
4. Antibodies do **not** prevent reactivation

### HSV-1 Diseases
**1. Oropharyngeal Infections**
- *Acute gingivostomatitis* (children): fever + painful vesicles on gums, lips, oral mucosa → rupture → red-based ulcers → may get secondary candida infection (white coat)
- *Herpes labialis*: cold sores / fever blisters (reactivation)
**2. Herpetic Keratoconjunctivitis**
- Corneal ulcers + conjunctival lesions
- Recurrence → dendritic ulcer pattern
- Repeated recurrence → corneal scarring → **blindness**
**3. Encephalitis**
- Rare but high mortality
- Involves the **temporal lobe**
**4. Herpetic Whitlow**
- Herpes infection of the fingers
- Occupational risk: dentists, nurses
**5. Eczema Herpeticum**
- Herpes spreading into pre-existing eczematous skin
- Risk of bacterial superinfection
**6. Disseminated Infection**
- In immunocompromised patients

### HSV-2 Diseases
**1. Genital Herpes**
- Vesiculoulcerative lesions: penis (males); cervix, vulva, vagina, perineum (females)
**2. Aseptic Meningitis**
- Self-limited
**3. Neonatal Herpes**
- Acquired in utero, during, or after delivery
- Serious — can disseminate to CNS and organs

### Immune Evasion (HSV)
- Mutation of membrane proteins
- Blocks interferon induction
- Blocks dendritic cell maturation
- Blocks complement activation

### Lab Diagnosis (HSV)
**Specimen:** Vesicular fluid, corneal scrapings
| Method | Finding |
|---|---|
| Tzanck smear (toluidine blue) | Multinucleated giant cells, "ground glass" chromatin (Tzanck cells) |
| Direct Immunofluorescence | Bright green intranuclear particles under UV |
| Viral isolation (tissue culture) | Human diploid fibroblasts / amnion / embryonic kidney → syncytium CPE in 24–48 hrs |
| Serology | IgM = primary infection |

### Treatment (HSV)
- **Acyclovir** — inhibits viral DNA polymerase
- Routes: topical, oral, IV
- Does **not** eliminate latency

## VARICELLA-ZOSTER VIRUS (VZV)
### Varicella (Chickenpox)
**Epidemiology:** Mild, highly contagious; mainly children
**Transmission:**
- Airborne droplets
- Direct contact with varicella patients
- Vesicular fluid of zoster patients (source for susceptible children)
**Pathogenesis:**
1. Infects upper respiratory mucosa
2. Multiplies in regional lymph nodes
3. Primary viremia → liver and spleen
4. Secondary viremia → skin → rash
5. Remains **latent in dorsal root ganglia** for life
**Clinical Picture:**
- Incubation: 10–21 days
- Mild fever + rash
- Rash progression: trunk → face → limbs
- Macules → papules → vesicles → crusts → shed without scarring
- **Cropping** = characteristic (lesions at different stages simultaneously)
**Complications:**
- Pneumonia (adults; may be fatal)
- Fulminant encephalitis (rare) — can be part of **Reye's syndrome** if salicylates given
- **Congenital Varicella Syndrome** (1st trimester): skin lesions, limb hypoplasia, chorioretinitis, CNS defects
- **Neonatal Varicella** (infection near delivery): disseminated, 35% mortality
- If maternal rash ≥1 week before delivery: maternal IgG crosses placenta → protects baby

### Zoster (Shingles)
- Reactivation of latent VZV in adults or immunocompromised patients
- Rash limited to a single **dermatome** (distribution of one dorsal root ganglion)
**Complications:**
- Trigeminal nerve involvement → keratitis, conjunctivitis, iritis
- Cranial nerve involvement → **Bell's palsy**
- **Post-herpetic neuralgia**: severe pain lasting months after rash resolves; likely due to nerve damage; does **not** respond to antivirals

### Lab Diagnosis (VZV)
| Method | Finding |
|---|---|
| Tzanck smear | Multinucleated giant cells (same as HSV) |
| Direct Immunofluorescence | Bright green intranuclear particles |
| PCR | Detects viral DNA (most specific) |
| Serology | VZV-specific antibodies via CFT, neutralisation, or ELISA |

### Treatment & Prevention (VZV)
- **DOC: Acyclovir** (inhibits DNA polymerase)
- Used in: immunocompromised, complicated chickenpox (pneumonia, keratitis), neonatal varicella
- Does **not** affect latency
**Prevention:**
- **Active:** Live attenuated varicella vaccine — single dose, age 1–12 years
- **Passive:** VZIG (Varicella-Zoster Immunoglobulin) — given to immunocompromised children exposed to infection, and mothers infected near term

## CYTOMEGALOVIRUS (CMV)
**Key fact:** Most common congenital viral infection; prevalent but usually asymptomatic in healthy hosts.
### Transmission
- Close contact with body fluids (saliva, urine, vaginal secretions, semen)
- Transplacental / perinatal (infected birth canal or breastfeeding)
- Sexual contact
- Blood transfusion
- Organ transplantation
### Pathogenesis
1. Infects upper respiratory tract + local lymphocytes
2. Lymphocytes spread virus to spleen and lymph nodes
3. Spreads to epithelial cells: salivary glands, renal tubules, testes, cervix
4. Establishes latency; both humoral and cell-mediated immunity maintain latency
### Diseases
**Healthy Adults:** Asymptomatic or mild flu-like illness; occasionally heterophil-negative infectious mononucleosis (similar to EBV)
**Immunocompromised (transplant, HIV):**
- Life-threatening pneumonia
- CMV retinitis → blindness
**Congenital/Neonatal (most serious):**
- Transmitted at any stage of pregnancy (primary or reactivated infection)
- **Cytomegalic Inclusion Disease (CID):** microcephaly, brain calcification, convulsions, retinitis, hepatosplenomegaly
- High mortality; survivors: deafness, ocular defects, mental retardation
- Can also cause abortion or stillbirth
### Lab Diagnosis (CMV)
**Specimen:** Urine, saliva, peripheral blood mononuclear cells
| Method | Finding |
|---|---|
| Histology/cytology | Multinucleated giant cells with **"owl's eye"** intranuclear inclusion bodies |
| Direct IF / ELISA | Detection of viral antigens |
### Treatment & Prevention (CMV)
- **Ganciclovir** — inhibits viral DNA polymerase
- Used in immunocompromised patients
- Does **not** affect latency
**Prevention:**
- Good hand hygiene (after diapers/oral secretions)
- Isolate newborns with CID
- Screen transplant donors and recipients for CMV antibodies
- Vaccines (live and recombinant) — still under trial

## EPSTEIN-BARR VIRUS (EBV)
**Prevalence:** Infects >95% of the human population
**Key feature:** Oncogenic virus
**Transmission:** Intimate contact with infected saliva ("kissing disease"); not airborne or blood-borne normally
### Pathogenesis
1. Replication in nasopharyngeal epithelium
2. Spreads to salivary glands and oropharyngeal lymphoid tissue
3. Viremia → infects B-lymphocytes and reticuloendothelial system (liver, spleen)
4. Host response: **CTLs (cytotoxic T-lymphocytes)** = atypical lymphocytes in blood → destroy EBV-infected B-cells
5. Latency in B-lymphocytes and oropharyngeal epithelial cells
6. In some cases: EBV DNA integrates into host chromosome → **indefinite B-cell proliferation → malignancy**
### Diseases
**1. Infectious Mononucleosis ("Mono"):**
- Fever, malaise, pharyngitis, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly ± jaundice ± rash
- Severity inversely related to age (younger = milder)
**2. EBV-Associated Malignancies:**
- **Burkitt's lymphoma** — Africans
- **Nasopharyngeal carcinoma** — Asians
### Lab Diagnosis (EBV)
**Blood picture:** Absolute lymphocytosis with **atypical lymphocytes (Downey cells)**
**Specimen:** Saliva, peripheral blood, lymphoid tissue
| Method | Finding |
|---|---|
| Direct IF | Viral antigens in cell scrapings |
| Paul-Bunnell / Monospot test | Detects non-specific heterophil antibodies (agglutinate sheep RBCs) — transient |
| ELISA / Indirect IF | VCA-IgM = recent/acute infection; VCA-IgG = lifelong past infection |
### Treatment (EBV)
- **No antiviral therapy available**
- **No vaccine available**
- Management is supportive

## HIGH-YIELD BUZZWORDS
- Tzanck cells (HSV, VZV) — multinucleated giant cells
- Owl's eye inclusion bodies — CMV
- Downey cells / atypical lymphocytes — EBV
- Dermatome distribution — Zoster
- Post-herpetic neuralgia — does not respond to antivirals
- Heterophil antibodies — EBV (Paul-Bunnell/Monospot)
- Kissing disease — EBV
- Reye's syndrome risk — aspirin + VZV infection
- Cropping — characteristic of varicella rash
- Acyclovir does NOT eliminate latency (none of the antivirals do)
