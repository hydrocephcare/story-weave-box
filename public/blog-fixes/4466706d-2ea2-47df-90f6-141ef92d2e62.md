# Basic Paediatric Protocols (Kenya MoH, 5th Edition, 2022)

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Emergency and Hospital Paediatrics (children up to 5 years)**

> **In one line:** the Kenya Ministry of Health Basic Paediatric Protocols are the national standard for the first 24 to 48 hours of care of a sick newborn or child under 5 years: triage, ABCDE resuscitation, then protocol-driven treatment of the big killers (pneumonia, diarrhoea, malaria, severe acute malnutrition, meningitis, HIV, TB and neonatal illness) with weight-based dosing. Know the algorithms and the doses; they are examined directly.

## What you must know

- **Triage** every sick child at the door: emergency signs (airway/breathing, circulation, coma/convulsions, severe dehydration) get immediate treatment; priority signs go to the front of the queue.
- All doses are **weight-based**; prescribe with the frequency written as 6hrly, 8hrly, 12hrly (not QID/TID). Review severely ill children within **6 hours** of admission.
- **Oxygen** if SpO2 below 90% or any danger sign. Target **90 to 95%** (91 to 95% neonates, 94 to 98% post-resuscitation).
- **Convulsions:** diazepam, repeat once at 5 minutes, then phenobarbitone 15 mg/kg. Always check glucose.
- **Severe malaria:** IV/IM artesunate (3 mg/kg if 20 kg or less, 2.4 mg/kg if over 20 kg) at 0, 12, 24 h then daily; then oral AL.
- **Dehydration:** Plan A, B, C. Shock and Plan C use **Ringer's lactate**. No bolus in malnutrition or severe anaemia (unless shock from diarrhoea or dehydration criteria are met).
- **Newborn:** Golden Minute (ventilation within 60 seconds), vitamin K 1 mg IM, chlorhexidine 7.1% cord care, caffeine citrate for apnoea of prematurity, phototherapy by nomogram.
- Every admission is offered an **HIV test**, **TB screen**, immunisation check, developmental assessment and a check for abuse or neglect.

## 1. Principles of good care and hospital policies

### Twelve principles of good care

1. Facilities keep basic equipment and drugs in stock at all times, with adequate staff skilled in paediatric care.
2. Sick children are **triaged immediately**, assessed and given emergency treatment as soon as possible.
3. Assessment of diagnosis and severity is thorough, treatment carefully planned, and every stage documented.
4. Protocols are a minimum standard for most but **not all** common problems; look for and treat the less common conditions.
5. Prescribe clearly, usually from a **measured weight**, on treatment sheets with doses checked by nurses. Write frequency as 6hrly, 8hrly, 12hrly.
6. Parents and caretakers must understand the illness and treatment; being polite to them improves communication.
7. A child needing inpatient care should **not be discharged against medical advice**; transfer to another facility instead.
8. Assess response to treatment. Severely ill children are reviewed within **6 hours** of admission and progress documented.
9. Supportive care (feeding, oxygen, fluids) is as important as disease-specific care.
10. Use laboratory tests appropriately and avoid unnecessary drugs.
11. Make a discharge and follow-up plan.
12. Good hand hygiene and a clean environment improve outcomes.

### Specific policies

- Every admitted child or newborn has an **inpatient number** and a standard admission record.
- Medical records are legal documents: clear, accurate, signed, dated and timed.
- All paediatric admissions are offered **HIV testing** and screened for **TB**.
- All newborn admissions aged 14 days or less receive **vitamin K** unless already given.
- Check **immunisation** status and give missed vaccines before discharge.
- Refer promptly any child whose condition cannot be managed with available resources.
- Assess for **abuse, neglect or maltreatment** and refer to the social worker.
- Every child has a **developmental assessment**; those with special needs are managed or referred.

### Admission assessment

- Record **weight** (used for fluids, feeds and doses). Measure length/height and record weight-for-height Z-score (WHZ).
- Use **MUAC** for nutritional assessment above 6 months of age.
- Record temperature, SpO2, pulse, respiratory rate (counted for a full **1 minute**) and blood pressure.
- Assess consciousness with **AVPU** or a paediatric GCS.
- Check **blood glucose** in every sick child. If not possible and AVPU is below A, treat for hypoglycaemia.
- Keep the sickest children near the nursing station and re-assess them first.

### Infection prevention and control (IPC)

- Clean hands with **soap and running water** or an **alcohol rub (70%)**. Dirty hands are washed with soap and water first; let the rub dry to be effective.
- **Gloves do not protect patients** and are not a substitute for hand hygiene.
- Follow the **five moments of hand hygiene**. The full technique takes **40 to 60 seconds**: wet, soap, palm to palm, right palm over left dorsum (and reverse), interlaced fingers, backs of fingers, rotational rubbing of thumbs, then fingertips in palm, rinse, dry with a single-use towel, and use the towel to turn off the tap.
- Decontaminate equipment according to national IPC guidelines.

### Clinical audit

- Audit is for **self-improvement, not blame**. The audit team has 4 to 8 members led by a senior clinician (nurses, administrators, laboratory staff, nutritionists); one or two rotating members (interns or nurses) do the audit.
- The audit cycle has six steps: identify cases, collect information, analyse, recommend solutions, implement change, monitor and evaluate. Audit **deaths and survivors weekly**.
- The audit framework classifies modifiable factors as **administrative** (no guidelines, equipment or supplies, staffing, transport), **health-worker** (knowledge gaps, delays, medical errors, poor communication or documentation) and **patient-related** (poor access, delay in seeking care, refusal of treatment).
- Compare care with these protocols. **If it is not written down, it was not done.**

## 2. Triage of sick children

Protect the cervical spine if there is a history of trauma.

| Emergency signs (immediate transfer to emergency area, start life support) | Priority signs (front of the queue) |
|---|---|
| **Airway and breathing:** obstructed breathing, central cyanosis, severe respiratory distress, weak or absent breathing | **Tiny:** sick infant under 2 months |
| **Circulation:** cold hands with any of capillary refill over 3 s, weak and fast pulse, slow (below 60/min) or absent pulse | **Temperature:** very high, above 39.5 C |
| **Coma, convulsing or confusion:** AVPU = P or U | **Trauma:** major trauma |
| **Diarrhoea with sunken eyes and skin pinch over 2 s** (treat as severe dehydration) | **Pain:** severe pain; **Poisoning:** reported by mother |
| | **Pallor:** severe palmar pallor |
| | **Restless, irritable or floppy**; **Respiratory distress** |
| | **Referral:** urgent referral letter |
| | **Malnutrition:** visible severe wasting; **oedema of both feet** |
| | **Burns:** severe burns |

Children with no emergency or priority signs are **non-urgent** and wait in the queue. Emergency cases are weighed if possible and given oxygen; priority cases get clinical review as soon as possible, weight and baseline observations.

## 3. Resuscitation

### Basic life support (infant and child)

1. Ensure safety, **stimulate, shout for help**, move to the emergency area.
2. Open the airway in the **sniffing position** (head tilt, chin lift). If trauma, protect the cervical spine and use **jaw thrust**.
3. Look, listen and feel for breathing for **5 seconds**.
4. If not breathing normally: give **5 rescue breaths** with a BVM on 100% oxygen. If the chest does not rise, check the airway and the mask position. Ensure at least 2 good breaths.
5. Check pulse and signs of life for 5 to 10 seconds.
6. If no pulse, or a slow pulse below 60/min: **15 chest compressions to 2 breaths** for 2 minutes, then reassess ABC.
7. Adrenaline: consider **0.1 ml/kg of 1 in 10,000** IV every 3 to 5 minutes (1 mg in 10 ml; make by diluting 1 ml of 1 in 1000 in 9 ml water for injection). Consider a fluid bolus if shock is likely, treat hypoglycaemia, continue CPR in cycles of 2 to 3 minutes.
8. If pulse is palpable and above 60/min but breathing is inadequate, continue ventilation at **20 to 30 breaths per minute** and look for dehydration, poor circulation or hypoglycaemia.
9. After recovery: oxygen via non-rebreather mask 10 to 15 L/min (target SpO2 **94 to 98%**), vital signs every 15 minutes, full history and examination.

### Child with signs of life: structured ABCDE

| Step | Assess | Decide and act |
|---|---|---|
| **Safety, stimulate** | Eye contact, movements. If not alert (AVPU below A) shout for help and place on resuscitation couch | |
| **A: Airway** | Stridor or airway noises; look in mouth if not alert | Suction (not in an alert child), sniffing position only if not alert, oropharyngeal airway only if unconscious with no gag reflex |
| **B: Breathing** | Rate for 1 min, head nodding, nasal flaring, central cyanosis, grunting, lower chest wall indrawing, deep acidotic breathing, SpO2 below 90%, wheeze or crackles | Oxygen by nasal prongs titrated to SpO2 **90 to 95%**; bronchodilator if wheeze |
| **C: Circulation** | Pulse (very fast or very slow), temperature gradient, capillary refill, peripheral pulses, pallor, dehydration, wasting, oedema, MUAC | See fluid rules below |
| **D: Disability** | AVPU, bedside glucose, ability to drink | **10% dextrose 5 ml/kg** if hypoglycaemic, or AVPU below A when glucose cannot be checked |
| **E: Exposure** | Quick head to toe exam | Note abnormalities; then full history, document all interventions and times |

**Circulation rules (very high yield):**

- Severely impaired circulation **plus diarrhoea with sunken eyes or prolonged skin pinch** (hypovolaemic shock): **Ringer's lactate 20 ml/kg over 15 minutes** as a rapid bolus, then proceed to Plan C step 2.
- Severely impaired circulation **without** diarrhoea, dehydration, severe acute malnutrition or severe anaemia: Ringer's lactate **20 ml/kg over 2 hours**.
- Severely impaired circulation **with** severe acute malnutrition: Ringer's lactate with 5% dextrose **20 ml/kg over 2 hours**.
- Respiratory distress and circulatory compromise **with severe pallor**: organise immediate **transfusion**; while waiting, give maintenance fluids or feeds only.
- Impaired circulation (some but not all signs) without diarrhoea or severe anaemia, with or without SAM: **maintenance fluids or feeds only. DO NOT BOLUS.**

> **Exam tip:** the fluid bolus rate depends on the cause. Fast (15 minutes) only for hypovolaemic shock from diarrhoea; slow (2 hours) for other shock; no bolus at all when circulation is only partly impaired.

### Intraosseous (IO) access

If IV access fails in circulatory compromise, an IO line is a rapid, safe route for blood samples, drugs, fluids and blood.

- Needle: IO or bone marrow needle 15 to 18G; if unavailable a 16 to 21G hypodermic needle. Use sterile gloves and clean the site.
- Site: middle of the **antero-medial flat surface of the tibia at the junction of the upper and middle thirds**.
- Bevel towards the toes, introduce at **90 degrees**, advance slowly with a rotating movement, stop at a **sudden give**, aspirate with a 5 ml syringe, inject 3 ml normal saline watching for leakage under the skin, then connect fluids and strap down.
- Boluses need a 20 or 50 ml syringe. Watch for **calf swelling** (extravasation, compartment syndrome). Replace with an IV line within **8 hours**.

### Oxygen therapy

- Hypoxaemia (SpO2 below 90%) is a major risk factor for death regardless of diagnosis.
- **Give oxygen** for: central cyanosis, head nodding, nasal flaring, grunting, severe lower chest wall indrawing, respiratory rate above 70/min, SpO2 below 90%, convulsions, post-resuscitation.
- Target SpO2: **91 to 95% neonates, 90 to 95% older children, 94 to 98% post-resuscitation**. Titrate every 15 to 30 minutes by 0.5 L/min.
- If the target is not met at the highest flow rate, refer for advanced care (high-flow nasal cannula, CPAP, ventilation).
- When stable (SpO2 above 90%, no increased work of breathing, no emergency signs) wean by 0.5 L/min every 30 minutes; **recheck SpO2 one hour after stopping** (late desaturation). Discharge only after 24 hours stable on room air with SpO2 90% or more.

| Device | Flow rates | FiO2 delivered |
|---|---|---|
| Nasal prongs, standard flow | Neonates 0.5 to 1 L/min; infants 1 to 2; child 1 to 4 | About 35% |
| Nasal prongs, high flow | Preterm 1 L/min; term neonate 2; infant 4; child 4 to 8 | About 50% |
| Nasal catheter | Not recommended in neonates; infant/child 1 to 2 L/min | About 40% |
| Face mask with reservoir (non-rebreather) | 10 to 15 L/min (bag must not deflate) | 80 to 95% |

Humidify at high flow rates, check regularly for abdominal distension, and document flow rate, device, monitoring frequency and target SpO2.

## 4. Convulsions (age over 1 month)

1. **ABCD:** lateral position and suction; oxygen via non-rebreather; check temperature gradient and pallor; check glucose or give **5 ml/kg of 10% dextrose**.
2. Up to 2 fits each lasting under 5 minutes do not need emergency drugs. A **3rd short convulsion within 2 hours** (multiple short seizures) is treated like a prolonged fit, as is any convulsion over **5 minutes**.
3. **Diazepam 0.3 mg/kg IV slowly over 1 minute, or 0.5 mg/kg rectally** (alternatives: IV lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg, buccal midazolam).
4. If still fitting after 5 minutes: repeat diazepam once.
5. If still fitting 5 minutes after the second dose: **phenobarbitone 15 mg/kg IM (loading)**, then maintenance **2.5 mg/kg once daily for 48 hours** and review.
6. Phenytoin, levetiracetam and IV sodium valproate are alternatives to phenobarbitone.

> **Remember:** do not give more than **2 doses of diazepam in 24 hours** once phenobarbitone is used. Do **not** load phenobarbitone in a child already on maintenance phenobarbitone. Diazepam is **not used in neonates**.

### Anticonvulsant doses (formulary)

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| Diazepam IV | 0.3 mg/kg |
| Diazepam rectal | 0.5 mg/kg (insert full syringe barrel gently, to 4 to 5 cm depth) |
| Lorazepam IV | 0.1 mg/kg over 30 to 60 s (max 4 mg) |
| Midazolam buccal | 1 to 2 months 0.3 mg/kg (max 2.5 mg); 3 to 11 months 2.5 mg; 1 to 4 years 5 mg |
| Phenobarbitone | Load 15 mg/kg (20 mg/kg in neonates); maintenance 5 mg/kg daily for chronic therapy, 2.5 mg/kg daily for acute febrile illness |
| Phenytoin | Age 1 month to 12 years load 15 to 20 mg/kg IV at no more than 1 mg/kg/min (over 20 to 30 min); maintenance 2.5 to 5 mg/kg twice daily (max 150 mg twice daily) |
| Levetiracetam | Load 30 mg/kg IV over 15 min; maintenance 30 mg/kg/day in two doses starting 12 h later |
| Sodium valproate | Status epilepticus 30 mg/kg IV over 5 min. Child 1 month to 12 years: start 10 to 15 mg/kg (max 600 mg) daily in 1 to 2 doses (max 60 mg/kg/day); maintenance 25 to 30 mg/kg/day in 2 doses. Neonate: 20 mg/kg once daily, maintenance 10 mg/kg twice daily |
| Carbamazepine | 1 month to 12 years: 5 mg/kg at night, increase by 2.5 to 5 mg/kg every 3 to 7 days; maintenance 5 mg/kg 2 to 3 times daily; avoid abrupt withdrawal |

## 5. Malaria

**Severe malaria:** fever plus any of: AVPU = V, P or U; unable to drink; respiratory distress with severe anaemia or acidotic breathing; hypoglycaemia (glucose below 2.5 mmol/L); more than 2 convulsions; Hb below 5 g/dL.

> **Remember:** if a good quality blood slide is **negative** but signs of severe malaria are present, start treatment, **repeat the slide 24-hourly up to 3 times**, and stop treatment if the 3rd test is negative.

### Management of severe malaria

1. **Artesunate** IV/IM (quinine if artesunate unavailable). Check the dosage chart; give a loading dose if using quinine.
2. Treat hypoglycaemia; give maintenance fluids or feeds.
3. **No bolus IV fluids** unless diarrhoea with signs of shock.
4. If respiratory distress or cardiac failure and Hb below 5 g/dL: transfuse **10 ml/kg packed cells (or 20 ml/kg whole blood)** urgently.
5. Artesunate is given for a **minimum of 24 hours** (3 doses). When the child can eat or drink, switch to a full course of ACT (artemether-lumefantrine), 8 to 12 hours after the last artesunate dose.

| Drug | Dosing |
|---|---|
| **Artesunate** (IV/IM) | **3 mg/kg/dose if 20 kg or less; 2.4 mg/kg/dose if over 20 kg**, at 0, 12 and 24 h, then once daily (maximum 7 days) until oral therapy is possible |
| **Artemether IM** | Load 3.2 mg/kg stat; maintenance 1.6 mg/kg 24-hourly |
| **Quinine** | Loading 20 mg/kg, then 10 mg/kg 8-hourly. Infuse at **no more than 5 mg/kg/hour** in at least 1 ml fluid per mg quinine (5% dextrose or saline); load takes 4 hours or longer, maintenance 2 hours or longer |
| **Artemether-lumefantrine (AL)**, 20/120 mg tablets | Take after meals: start at 0 h, then 8 h, then 12-hourly on days 2 and 3. Under 5 kg: half tablet (hospital use); 5 to 14 kg: 1 tablet; 15 to 24 kg: 2 tablets; 25 to 34 kg: 3 tablets |
| **Dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine** (second line), once daily for 3 days | 3 to 35 months: 1 paediatric tablet; 3 to 5 years: 2 paediatric tablets; 6 to 11 years: 1 adult tablet |
| Prophylaxis in sickle cell disease: proguanil | Under 1 year 25 mg daily; over 1 year to 5 years 50 mg daily |

**Preparation of artesunate:** 60 mg powder plus 1 ml of 5% sodium bicarbonate, then dilute with normal saline or 5% dextrose: **5 ml for IV (10 mg/ml)**, **2 ml for IM (20 mg/ml)**.

- Do **not** use water for injection; do **not** give if cloudy; do **not** give as a slow drip; use **within 1 hour** of preparation.
- IM quinine: take 1 ml of the 2 ml in a 600 mg vial, add 5 ml water for injection (50 mg/ml): loading 0.4 ml/kg, maintenance 0.2 ml/kg; maximum 3 ml per injection site.
- Oral quinine: 200 mg quinine sulphate = 200 mg quinine hydrochloride or dihydrochloride = 300 mg quinine bisulphate.

### Non-severe malaria

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Fever, none of the severe signs, able to drink, AVPU = A | Reliable test (blood slide or RDT). Positive: **AL** (or oral second line). Negative: no antimalarial; look for another cause and repeat if concerned |
| Severe anaemia (Hb below 5 g/dL or HCT below 15%) but alert, drinking and breathing comfortably | AL, iron, and if Hb below 4 g/dL transfuse 10 ml/kg packed cells or 20 ml/kg whole blood over 4 hours |
| Hb below 9 g/dL | Oral iron for 14 days; if respiratory distress develops with Hb below 5 g/dL transfuse urgently |

**Treatment failure:** consider other causes or co-morbidity. A child on oral antimalarials who develops severe signs (unable to sit or drink, AVPU V/P/U, respiratory distress) is changed to IV artesunate. Fever with a positive slide after **72 hours**: check compliance, and if true failure go to second-line treatment.

## 6. Meningitis (age over 60 days with fever)

**Lumbar puncture (LP) MUST be done** even if the malaria slide is positive, if any of: coma, inability to drink or feed, AVPU P or U, stiff neck, bulging fontanelle, fits if age under 6 months or over 6 years, partial seizures. Also do an LP for agitation, irritability or any convulsion, unless the mental state is completely normal after a febrile convulsion (review within 8 hours and repeat if doubt persists).

**Do not do LP if:** child requires CPR, pupils respond poorly to light, skin infection at the LP site.

| Class | Criteria | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Definite** | Turbid CSF (not a bloody tap), and/or laboratory: white cell count over 10 x 10^6/L, Gram-positive diplococci or Gram-negative cocco-bacilli | Ceftriaxone; **steroids not indicated** |
| **Probable** | Coma, stiff neck or bulging fontanelle, plus clear-looking CSF | Ceftriaxone; adjust to CSF cell count and Gram stain |
| **Possible** | No LP done | Ceftriaxone; senior review |
| No meningitis | All results normal | Look for another cause |

HIV exposed or infected children: also request CSF **cryptococcal** testing plus microscopy, culture and Ziehl-Neelsen stain.

## 7. Diarrhoea and gastroenteritis (age over 1 month, not severely malnourished)

**Hypovolaemic shock from diarrhoea** needs all four: weak or absent pulse, AVPU below A, cold hands with temperature gradient, capillary refill over 3 s, **plus** sunken eyes and slow skin pinch. Give **IV/IO Ringer's lactate 20 ml/kg over 15 min**, a second bolus if still in shock, then Plan C step 2. Treat hypoglycaemia. If Hb below 5 g/dL or severe pallor, transfuse urgently and **do not bolus**.

| Class | Signs | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Severe dehydration** | Unable to drink or AVPU below A, plus sunken eyes and/or skin pinch over 2 s | **Plan C** |
| **Some dehydration** | Drinks adequately but 2 or more of: sunken eyes, skin pinch 1 to 2 s, restless or irritable | **Plan B:** ORS by mouth **75 ml/kg over 4 hours**, continue breastfeeding, reassess after 4 h |
| **No dehydration** | Not enough signs for some dehydration | **Plan A:** ORS **10 ml/kg after each loose stool**, continue breastfeeding, encourage feeding if over 6 months |

**Plan C:**

- Step 1: Ringer's lactate **30 ml/kg** over 30 minutes if over 12 months, **60 minutes** if under 12 months.
- Step 2: Ringer's lactate **70 ml/kg** over 2.5 hours if over 12 months, **5 hours** if under 12 months. Give ORS 5 ml/kg/hr via NGT as soon as tolerated.
- If IV cannot be started: NGT ORS **120 ml/kg over 6 hours**.
- Reassess at least hourly and after 3 to 6 hours; reclassify.
- Consider immediate transfusion if severe pallor or Hb below 5 g/dL.

> **Exam tip:** **all** children with diarrhoea receive **zinc** (10 mg daily if 6 months or under, 20 mg daily if over 6 months, for 10 to 14 days). Antimicrobials are **not** indicated unless dysentery or proven amoebiasis or giardiasis.

HIV exposed or infected children with acute diarrhoea are treated the same way (fluids and zinc). For **persistent diarrhoea (14 days or more)** in a child 6 months or older use low-lactose or lactose-free milks.

## 8. Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)

**Features:** polyuria, polydipsia, enuresis, weight loss, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, reduced consciousness; dehydration, Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath, drowsiness.

**Diagnosis:** blood glucose above **11.1 mmol/L**, pH below **7.3** or bicarbonate below **15 mmol/L**, urine ketones above 2+ or serum ketones above 3 mmol/L.

| Severity | pH | Bicarbonate (mmol/L) |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | 7.2 to below 7.3 | 10 to below 15 |
| Moderate | 7.1 to below 7.2 | 5 to below 10 |
| Severe | below 7.1 | below 5 |

### Management

1. Assess dehydration; senior review.
2. **Shock** (AVPU below A, weak or absent pulses, prolonged CRT, cold extremities): airway, oxygen by non-rebreather, **0.9% saline 10 ml/kg over 30 minutes**; do not give more than **40 ml/kg within 4 hours**. Dehydration over 5% with acidotic breathing or vomiting, not in shock: saline 10 ml/kg over 1 hour, nil by mouth, calculate fluids over 48 hours.
3. **After the first hour** of fluids start **regular insulin infusion: under 5 years 0.03 to 0.05 IU/kg/hr; over 5 years 0.05 to 0.1 IU/kg/hr.** Without a pump: 50 units regular insulin in 50 ml saline (1 unit = 1 ml).
4. Check potassium; add **20 mmol KCl per 500 ml fluid** unless anuric or AKI.
5. Minimal dehydration and tolerating oral fluids: oral rehydration and subcutaneous regular insulin 0.2 IU/kg every 4 hours.
6. When glucose falls to **14 to 17 mmol/L**, or falls over 5 mmol/L per hour, change to **5% dextrose in 0.9% saline** (add 50 ml of 50% dextrose to 450 ml saline).
7. Monitor: hourly fluid input and output, hourly glucose, neurological status at least hourly, ECG for T-wave changes, electrolytes and blood gas 2 to 4 hourly. If febrile, give antibiotics. **Do not correct acidosis with bicarbonate.**
8. No improvement after 6 hours: re-evaluate fluids, ketones, insulin delivery and storage, need for more resuscitation, consider sepsis.

**Fluid maintenance (example of the chart):** DKA fluids = maintenance plus 5% of body weight per 24 hours (assuming 10% dehydration). For example, 10 kg: maintenance 780 ml/24 h, DKA total 1280 ml/24 h (53 ml/h); 20 kg: 1230 and 2230 ml/24 h (93 ml/h).

**Resolution of DKA:** clinically well, drinking and eating, pH above 7.3, serum ketones below 1.0 mmol/L.

**Transition to subcutaneous insulin:** total daily dose **0.5 to 1 unit/kg/day pre-pubertal, 1 to 2 units/kg/day pubertal.** Give 30% as long-acting (glargine) 24-hourly or intermediate 12-hourly, and 70% as regular insulin in 3 equal doses before meals. With regular insulin give the dose 30 minutes before a meal, then stop the infusion after 30 minutes; with long or intermediate-acting stop the infusion 2 hours after. Check sugars 30 minutes before and 2 hours after meals.

### Cerebral oedema

- **Warning signs:** headache, irritability, incontinence, slowing heart rate, reduced consciousness, specific neurological signs. First exclude hypoglycaemia (RBS below 4 mmol/L).
- **Treat:** call senior staff, head of bed at 30 degrees, oxygen, **mannitol 0.5 to 1 g/kg (max 200 g) over 15 min or 3% hypertonic saline 2.5 to 5 ml/kg over 15 min**, restrict IV fluids to 70% of daily fluids, move to ICU/PICU, cranial imaging when stable.

### Hypoglycaemia in a diabetic child

Blood glucose **below 4 mmol/L** with autonomic (shaking, sweating, palpitations, pallor), neuroglycopenic (poor concentration, blurred vision, slurred speech, confusion, seizure, coma), behavioural (irritability, agitation) or non-specific (hunger, headache) features.

| State | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Not alert** | IV 10% dextrose 5 ml/kg over 2 to 3 min; or glucagon IV/IM/SC (1 mg if over 25 kg, 0.5 mg if under 25 kg; contraindicated in prolonged fasting); or glucose gel/paste on the dependent cheek in lateral position |
| **Alert** | 0.3 g/kg rapid-acting carbohydrate (15 g = 3 glucose tablets, 3 teaspoons sugar or honey, or 175 ml juice). Retest after 15 minutes and repeat if no response |

Avoid chocolate, milk and fatty foods (slow absorption). If the next meal is over 1 hour away follow with 15 g slow carbohydrate (bread, milk, biscuits, fruit) and observe for recurrence.

## 9. Nutritional assessment and severe acute malnutrition (SAM)

- **MUAC** (left upper arm) is quick and used in emergencies. **Weight-for-height/length** Z-score detects wasting (acute malnutrition); measure length lying if under 2 years. Weight-for-age cannot separate acute from chronic malnutrition and is used for growth monitoring only. **Kwashiorkor = severe malnutrition at any age.**

| Acute malnutrition | MUAC (cm) | WHZ |
|---|---|---|
| None | above 13.5 | above -1 |
| At risk | 12.5 to 13.4 | -2 to -1 |
| Moderate | 11.5 to 12.4 | -3 to -2 |
| Severe | below 11.5 | below -3 |
| Kwashiorkor | bilateral pitting oedema | any |

Exclude other conditions that cause wasting (cardiac, gastrointestinal, endocrine).

### Complicated SAM (6 to 59 months): the 10 steps

Admit if acutely ill with MUAC below 11.5 cm (or visible severe wasting), WHZ below -3, or oedema or other kwashiorkor signs (flaky pale skin, hair changes).

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1. Hypoglycaemia | Glucose below 3 mmol/L: 5 ml/kg of 10% dextrose; if not alert and cannot test, treat. Give oral/NG glucose or feeds as soon as possible (within 30 min of admission) |
| 2. Hypothermia | Axillary temperature below 35 C: blankets, warm fluid bag or heater |
| 3. Dehydration and shock | Diarrhoea: shock gets IV fluids; no shock gets **ReSoMal**. **Transfuse if Hb below 4 g/dL**: 10 ml/kg whole blood over 3 h plus frusemide 1 mg/kg; if heart failure, 5 to 10 ml/kg packed cells over 3 h |
| 4. Electrolytes | Commercial F75; if unavailable, mineral mix plus 4 mmol/kg/day oral potassium. **Never use frusemide for oedema** |
| 5. Infection | All ill SAM children: IV penicillin (or ampicillin) **plus gentamicin**. Give gentamicin for 5 days; if improved change penicillin to amoxicillin 40 mg/kg 12-hourly PO at 48 h. Add nystatin or clotrimazole for thrush, albendazole after 7 days, TEO plus atropine eye drops for eye pus or ulceration |
| 6. Micronutrients | Vitamin A PO if eye signs on admission, and days 2 and 14; multivitamins for at least 2 weeks and folic acid 2.5 mg alternate days if no RUTF or F75/F100; **iron only when gaining weight** and if no RUTF |
| 7. Feeding | Prescribe feeds (below) and place NG tube |
| 8 to 10. Catch-up | Monitor appetite and weight; start catch-up feeding with RUTF or F100 (usually day 3 to 7); provide a caring, stimulating environment and educate the family for discharge |

### Fluids in SAM with diarrhoea

- **Shock** (AVPU below A, weak or absent pulse, CRT over 3 s, cold periphery): **Ringer's lactate with 5% dextrose 20 ml/kg over 2 hours** (add 50 ml of 50% dextrose to 450 ml Ringer's lactate). If severe anaemia, give blood instead.
- Not in shock, cannot take oral/NG: IV maintenance **4 ml/kg/hr**.
- Oral/NG: **ReSoMal 10 ml/kg/hour for 2 hours**, then 7.5 ml/kg over 1 hour, then introduce F75 and **alternate ReSoMal with F75 for 10 hours** (5 to 10 ml/kg/hr as tolerated). At 12 hours switch to 3-hourly F75 feeds.

### Feeding in SAM

- Under 6 months: expressed breast milk, term formula, or diluted F100 (add 35 ml water to each 100 ml F100).
- **F75** starter feeds: 130 ml/kg/day (100 ml/kg/day if severe oedema).
- When appetite returns and oedema is much improved: transition for about 2 days to F100 at the same volume (or RUTF), then **RUTF** (500 kcal in a 92 g packet) for rehabilitation; allow nibbling; can be mixed into uji. On day 3 of F100, increase each feed by 10 ml until some is left uneaten (usually about 200 ml/kg/day).
- **Danger sign during refeeding:** if pulse rises by 25 beats/min and breathing by 5 breaths/min on two successive 4-hourly readings, reduce the volume to 100 ml/kg/day for 24 hours. Reduce feeds if respiratory distress, worsening oedema or engorged jugular veins.

## 10. Pneumonia (2 to 59 months, without SAM)

| Class | Criteria | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Severe pneumonia** | Any danger sign: SpO2 below 90%, central cyanosis, unable to drink or breastfeed, AVPU V/P/U, grunting | Admit or refer; **oxygen; IV penicillin and gentamicin**; monitor SpO2; supportive care |
| **Pneumonia** | Lower chest wall indrawing OR fast breathing: **RR 50/min or more (2 to 11 months), 40/min or more (12 to 59 months)** | **High-dose amoxicillin DT 40 to 45 mg/kg/dose 12-hourly**; counsel on danger signs; review within **48 hours** (admit if review is not possible and there is indrawing) |
| No pneumonia (cough/cold) | None of the above | Reassure and counsel |

If there is wheeze, consider asthma or another diagnosis. For HIV exposed or infected children see the HIV protocol.

### Treatment failure definitions

| Time | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Any time | Progression to severe pneumonia (cyanosis or cannot drink) | Admit; change amoxicillin to penicillin and gentamicin |
| Any time | Obvious cavitation on CXR | Flucloxacillin and gentamicin IV (Staph aureus or Gram-negative) |
| 48 hours | Severe pneumonia worse | Re-assess, CXR (empyema, effusion, cavitation); switch to ceftriaxone unless Staphylococcus suspected (then flucloxacillin and gentamicin); suspect PCP especially if under 12 months and do HIV test |
| 48 hours | Pneumonia with no improvement in respiratory rate, indrawing, fever or ability to feed | Admit; change amoxicillin to penicillin and gentamicin |
| Day 5 | At least three of: fever over 38 C, RR over 60, cyanosed or SpO2 below 90% and no better, persistent indrawing, worsening CXR, cough over 14 days, persistent fever and distress | If on amoxicillin: admit, change to penicillin and gentamicin. If already on these: change to ceftriaxone. Suspect PCP (HIV test), consider TB (Mantoux, CXR) |

**HIV-exposed or infected children with severe pneumonia:** penicillin and gentamicin first line, ceftriaxone second line; plus **high-dose co-trimoxazole if under 5 years** for Pneumocystis (steroids not recommended for PCP).

## 11. Possible asthma

Wheeze plus cough or difficulty breathing. Asthma is much more likely if over 12 months with recurrent wheeze.

**Severe asthma** (any of SpO2 below 90%, central cyanosis, cannot drink or breastfeed, AVPU V/P/U, cannot talk or complete sentences, pulse over 200/min in 0 to 3 years or over 180/min in 4 to 5 years, silent chest): admit or refer; oxygen; **nebulised salbutamol 2.5 mg or 6 puffs via spacer and mask, repeat every 20 minutes for up to 3 doses**; **prednisolone 1 to 2 mg/kg** (3 to 5 days; max 20 mg/day if under 2 years, 30 mg/day if 2 to 5 years); consider **ipratropium bromide** 250 micrograms nebulised or 2 puffs of 80 micrograms every 20 min if poor response (repeat every 20 minutes for one hour if needed); antibiotics as for severe pneumonia.

**Mild or moderate asthma** (wheeze plus indrawing or fast breathing): salbutamol 2 puffs (or 2.5 mg nebulised) every 20 minutes up to 3 doses; monitor 1 to 2 hours. If mild, discharge on salbutamol MDI 2 puffs 6-hourly, counsel on deterioration and review within 48 hours. If no response or worsening, treat as severe.

**Before discharge:** consider inhaled corticosteroids (budesonide pMDI with spacer, low dose 200 micrograms daily) or adjust the dose; look for comorbidities; demonstrate MDI and spacer use (spacer with face mask under 3 years; face mask or mouthpiece at 4 to 5 years); arrange regular follow-up. IV salbutamol only in HDU with a monitor (under 2 years 5 micrograms/kg; 2 years and over up to 15 micrograms/kg, max 250 micrograms, over 5 minutes).

## 12. Tuberculosis

**Diagnosis:** screen every child for cough, fever, weight loss or poor weight gain, lethargy or reduced playfulness. **Suspect TB with two or more** of these. Ask about contact with an adult or adolescent with chronic cough or TB in the last **2 years**.

- Examine: temperature over 37.5 C, weight against growth curve, respiratory rate, respiratory system and other systems for extrapulmonary TB.
- Investigate: specimen for **Xpert MTB/RIF** (and culture); CXR; Mantoux; HIV test. Specimens: sputum (over 5 years), induced sputum, nasopharyngeal aspirate, gastric aspirate, stool; attempt in every child.
- **Bacteriologically confirmed TB:** positive specimen for MTB.
- **Clinical diagnosis of pulmonary TB:** two or more suggestive symptoms **plus** two or more of: positive contact, abnormal respiratory signs, abnormal CXR, positive Mantoux.
- Treat all confirmed and clinically diagnosed cases, including children with a negative or no Xpert result but suggestive clinical features.

| Category | Intensive phase | Continuation phase |
|---|---|---|
| All forms except TB meningitis and bone/joint TB | 2 months RHZE | 4 months RH |
| TB meningitis, bone and joint TB | 2 months RHZE | 10 months RH |
| Drug-resistant TB | Refer to DR-TB specialist | |

**Steroids** (prednisone **2 mg/kg**, max 60 mg/day, once daily for 4 weeks then taper over 2 weeks: 1 mg/kg for 7 days then 0.5 mg/kg for 7 days) for: TB meningitis and intracranial TB, pulmonary TB with respiratory distress or airway obstruction by hilar nodes, severe miliary TB, pericardial effusion.

**Fixed-dose tablets by weight** (RHZ 75/50/150 mg and E 100 mg in the intensive phase; RH 75/50 mg in continuation): under 2 kg: 1/4 tablet; 2 to 2.9 kg: 1/2; 3 to 3.9 kg: 3/4; 4 to 7.9 kg: 1; 8 to 11.9 kg: 2; 12 to 15.9 kg: 3; 16 to 24.9 kg: 4; over 25 kg: adult dosing. Weigh at every visit and adjust. **Pyridoxine** throughout: 5 to 7 kg quarter tablet (50 mg), 8 to 14 kg half, 15 kg and above one full tablet daily. Isoniazid preventive therapy: see national TB guidelines.

## 13. HIV in children

| Population | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Infants under 18 months | HIV testing at birth or first contact within 2 weeks; those tested at birth must be retested at the **6-week** immunisation visit. All HIV-exposed infants get **DNA PCR at 6 weeks** (or earliest contact). A positive PCR means presumed infected: start ART |
| Children 18 months to 9 years | Test all children of HIV-infected adults within 1 month of confirming the adult's status |

**Presumptive diagnosis (under 18 months, antibody positive and symptomatic, awaiting PCR):** two or more of oral candidiasis, severe pneumonia, severe sepsis; OR any WHO stage 4 condition, recent maternal death (if likely HIV-related) or advanced HIV in mother, or child CD4 below 25%.

**Early infant diagnosis algorithm:**

- Establish exposure (mother known positive; test mother if unknown; rapid antibody test on infant if mother's status cannot be established).
- Exposed child at 6 weeks: DBS for DNA PCR, **start or continue infant ARV prophylaxis, start cotrimoxazole prophylaxis (CPT)**.
- **PCR positive:** presumed infected; stop prophylaxis, start ART, continue CPT; collect a new sample for confirmatory PCR and baseline viral load at ART initiation. If confirmatory test negative, send a DBS for tie-breaker to the national reference laboratory.
- **PCR negative:** child remains HIV-exposed; continue follow-up; repeat DNA PCR at **6 months**, then **12 months** (until 18 months), earlier if symptomatic.
- Antibody test at **18 months** (and every 6 months while breastfeeding and 6 weeks after stopping). Positive: confirmed infected, start ART. Negative: HIV negative, stop CPT, review at 2 years.

**Infant prophylaxis:** AZT plus NVP for 6 weeks; **NVP continues until 6 weeks after complete cessation of breastfeeding**. Can stop after a minimum of 12 weeks of NVP if not breastfeeding.

| 2000 to 2499 g | 10 mg (1 ml) | 10 mg (1 ml) |
| 2500 g or more | 15 mg (1.5 ml) | 15 mg (1.5 ml) |
| Over 6 to 12 weeks | 20 mg (2 ml) | 60 mg (6 ml) |
| 12 weeks to 6 months (NVP) | 25 mg (2.5 ml) | |

AZT beyond 12 weeks: 3 to 5.9 kg 6 ml BD; 6 to 9.9 kg 9 ml BD; 10 to 13.9 kg 12 ml BD; 14 to 19.9 kg 15 ml BD. Over 12 months NVP: consult the national HIV clinical TWG (Uliza hotline 0800 72 48 48).

**Co-trimoxazole (CTX) for prophylaxis and PCP:** 1 to 4 kg: 2.5 ml syrup (240 mg/5 ml) or one 120 mg tablet or quarter of a 480 mg tablet; 5 to 8 kg: 5 ml, 2 x 120 mg or half 480 mg; 9 to 16 kg: 10 ml or one 480 mg tablet; 17 to 50 kg: two 480 mg tablets. **Prophylaxis once daily; PCP treatment 8-hourly for 3 weeks.** Trimethoprim 4 mg/kg 12-hourly (sulphamethoxazole 20 mg/kg) is the general dose.

## 14. Neonatal care

### Neonatal resuscitation

- Prepare equipment, warmth and help before delivery. **Immediate cord clamping** for newborns needing resuscitation; **delayed clamping (1 to 3 minutes)** if good heart rate and breathing.
- Dry, stimulate, keep warm. If crying/breathing with good tone: skin-to-skin, breastfeeding, essential newborn care.
- If not: clear airway (suction visible secretions), sniffing position, **shout for help**, start ventilation with **room air at 40 to 60 breaths/min** within the **Golden Minute (60 seconds)**; ensure the chest rises.
- After 1 minute check heart rate (5 to 10 s). **HR below 60:** connect 100% oxygen, give **1 breath for every 3 chest compressions** for 1 minute, then reassess. **HR above 60** with regular breathing (RR over 30) and HR above 100: give oxygen titrated to SpO2 90 to 95%, IV fluids or EBM, check glucose 2 hours after birth, maintain temperature 36.5 to 37.5 C, essential newborn care, treat infection, family-centred care, ensure HIV risk is known.

### Essential newborn care

1. Keep warm: **36.5 to 37.5 C**.
2. **Cord care:** chlorhexidine digluconate **7.1% gel** on the cord immediately after cutting, then once daily to day 7 (term) or until the cord falls off; preterm: one application. Clean the cord with lukewarm water and cotton wool before later applications; do not apply anything else; do not wash it off for 24 hours after the first application; stop if the cord is infected.
3. **Vitamin K:** 1 mg IM (0.5 mg if under 1.5 kg) to all newborns, and to any infant under 14 days not yet given it.
4. **Tetracycline eye ointment** to all newborns.
5. **Growth:** preterm babies gain 10 to 15 g/kg/day after day 7; term babies 20 to 30 g/day.
6. **Supplements** for all premature infants (under 36 weeks or under 2 kg) from full feeds or 2 weeks, for 6 months to 1 year: multivitamin 2.5 ml daily, folate 2.5 mg weekly, iron, vitamin D 400 IU daily, calcium 120 to 140 mg/kg/day elemental, phosphorus 60 to 90 mg/kg/day.
7. **Kangaroo mother care** (national KMC guidelines).

### Feeding and fluids in newborns

**Newborn 1.5 kg or more:**

- Well baby: immediate milk feeding. First feed 7.5 ml, increasing by that amount per feed to full volume.
- Total daily volume: day 1 **60**, day 2 **80**, day 3 **100**, day 4 **120**, day 5 **140**, day 6 **160**, day 7 **180 ml/kg/day**.
- Unstable baby: IV **10% dextrose** for the first 24 hours; give **2 ml/kg colostrum via NGT every 3 hours** once ABC are stable (do not subtract it from IV fluids). From day 2 start EBM via NGT at 30 ml/kg/day, increase by 30 ml/kg/day while reducing IV fluids. Increase total (IV plus EBM) by 20 ml/kg/day to a maximum of 150 ml/kg/day; once off IV fluids go to max 180 ml/kg/day (up to 200 with expert advice).
- IV fluids from day 2: add **sodium 2 to 3 mmol/kg/day** (19 ml/kg normal saline) and **potassium 1 to 2 mmol/kg/day** (0.5 to 1 ml/kg/day of 15% KCl) to 10% dextrose. **Maximum IV 150 ml/kg/day.** Always prefer EBM. Poor perfusion or fluid overload: seek senior opinion.

**Newborn under 1.5 kg:**

- Well babies: start EBM 5 ml and increase by 5 ml per 3-hourly feed to full volume (80 ml/kg/day on day 1, increasing 20 ml/kg each day).
- Unstable babies: IV 10% dextrose for 24 hours; same colostrum and EBM stepwise approach. Daily totals: day 1 **80**, day 2 **100**, day 3 **120**, day 4 **140**, day 5 **160**, day 6+ **180 ml/kg/day**.
- Fortifiers are not routinely required; for poor weight gain use hind milk. Investigate causes of failure to gain weight.

### Early-onset neonatal hypoglycaemia (0 to 72 hours)

- Check blood glucose at **2 hours of age** in high-risk neonates (preterm, IUGR, SGA, LGA, hypothermia, perinatal asphyxia, infant of diabetic mother, delayed feeding, maternal beta-agonists, maternal diabetes or obesity, sibling with seizures or sudden death) and immediately in all sick neonates.
- **Hypoglycaemia: glucose below 2.6 mmol/L.**
- **Asymptomatic with glucose 1.8 mmol/L or more:** **0.4 ml/kg of 50% dextrose gel buccal**, cup feed or NGT EBM; recheck in 1 to 2 hours, then before feeds; if normal on 3 consecutive readings, 6-hourly.
- **Symptomatic or glucose below 1.8 mmol/L:** buccal glucose, fix IV line, **10% dextrose 2 ml/kg mini-bolus over 3 minutes**, then maintenance IV fluids; recheck 30 minutes after the bolus then 3-hourly.
- Persistently low glucose: consult a paediatrician or endocrinologist and refer.
- **Signs:** mild to moderate: jitteriness, irritability, high-pitched cry, lethargy, hypotonia, tremors, hypothermia, tachypnoea, tachycardia, sweating, poor feeding, vomiting. Severe: seizures, apnoea, cyanosis, pallor and circulatory collapse, sudden death.

### Neonatal convulsions

- In the absence of clinical seizures, neonates with hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy do not need prophylactic phenobarbitone.
- Treat any clinical seizure over 3 minutes or brief serial seizures: ABC, oxygen, treat hypoglycaemia (consider glucose and calcium).
- **Phenobarbitone 20 mg/kg IM load.** Reassess at 1 hour; if still fitting, repeat **10 mg/kg** (maximum total load **40 mg/kg**).
- Controlled: maintenance **5 mg/kg/24 h** after at least 12 hours.
- Still fitting: **levetiracetam 30 mg/kg IV over 15 min** if available (reassess at 2 hours; then maintenance 30 mg/kg/day in 2 doses starting 12 h later), otherwise **phenytoin 15 mg/kg IV over 15 min at 1 mg/kg/min** (reassess at 1 hour; maintenance 5 mg/kg/day in 2 doses). Consult a neurologist if still uncontrolled.
- **Stopping:** consider after 72 hours seizure-free with normal examination or EEG; a single drug can be stopped abruptly; with multiple drugs withdraw one by one, phenobarbitone last.

### Neonatal jaundice

- Assess in bright natural light: eyes, blanched nose skin, sole of foot. Refer early if jaundice under 24 hours and phototherapy or exchange transfusion is unavailable.
- If bilirubin cannot be measured, start phototherapy for: a well baby with jaundice easily visible on the **sole**; a **preterm with any visible jaundice**; easily visible jaundice with inability to feed or neurological signs (consider immediate exchange transfusion).
- **Measure bilirubin within 2 hours** in: jaundice on day 1, on soles and palms, in preterm under 35 weeks, with any danger sign, sibling who needed phototherapy or exchange, or Rh incompatibility. Investigate cause in any jaundiced neonate on the NBU.
- **Risk factors for bilirubin encephalopathy:** dehydration, prematurity, respiratory distress, sepsis, hypoxia, seizures, acidosis, rapid rise of bilirubin.
- Plot the level on the correct **nomogram** (more than 38 weeks; 35+0 to 37+6; under 35 weeks by birth weight bands over 1999 g, 1500 to 1999 g, 1000 to 1499 g, under 1000 g). Use the **lower line** if sepsis, haemolysis, acidosis or asphyxia.

| Bilirubin result | Action |
|---|---|
| Below phototherapy line by 1 to 50 micromol/L | Repeat in 6 to 24 hours (24 hours if over 12 hours old) |
| Above phototherapy line, more than 50 micromol/L below exchange line | **Standard phototherapy** 25 to 30 microW/cm2/nm; short breastfeeding breaks (under 30 min every 3 h); no extra fluids |
| Above phototherapy line, less than 50 micromol/L below exchange line | **Intensive phototherapy** 30 to 35 microW/cm2/nm; feed via NGT or IV; prepare for exchange |
| Above exchange line, or signs of acute encephalopathy | **Exchange transfusion** (also if bilirubin not expected to fall below threshold after 6 hours of intensive phototherapy) |

- Check bilirubin 6 hours after starting phototherapy, then every 12 hours if stable or falling. **Stop phototherapy** when bilirubin is 50 micromol/L below the threshold; recheck after 24 hours.
- **Phototherapy checklist:** shield eyes (remove at feeds), baby naked except small napkin, one baby per machine at the centre of the cot, measure irradiance with a light metre (head, hands and feet), do not cover vents or lights, keep device clean, vital signs (especially temperature) 3-hourly, weight on alternate days, periodic serum bilirubin every 12 to 24 hours (visual and transcutaneous estimates unreliable), replace fluorescent tubes after 6 months or 2000 hours, blackened ends or flicker. LED lights produce less heat: watch for hypothermia, room temperature 25 to 28 C.

### Apnoea of prematurity

- **Definition:** cessation of breathing over 20 seconds, or a shorter pause with desaturation, central cyanosis or bradycardia.
- **Caffeine citrate** for: all preterms 30 weeks or under; preterms 30 to 34 weeks with repeated apnoea, before elective extubation, unscheduled extubation, or post-anaesthetic events.
- Dose: **load 20 mg/kg oral or IV, then 5 mg/kg daily from 12 to 24 hours later**; increase to **10 mg/kg/day** if apnoea persists.
- Monitor cardiorespiratory function; **SpO2 target 91 to 95%**. Treat causes (sepsis, hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, IVH).
- Persistent attacks: early CPAP, gentle tactile stimulation, bag-mask if slow response or cyanosis; refer for intubation if not controlled.
- **Withdraw** caffeine at 34 weeks if no apnoea, bradycardia or desaturation needing intervention for about 5 days; monitor 5 days after stopping.
- Aminophylline is the alternative (newborn load 6 mg/kg IV over 1 hour or rectal; maintenance 2.5 mg/kg 12-hourly at 0 to 6 days, 4 mg/kg 12-hourly at 7 to 28 days).

### CPAP

- **Delivery room:** spontaneous breathing, HR above 100: preterms under 28 weeks (under 1000 g) get prophylactic CPAP if resources exist (else oxygen 1 L/min); 28 to 30 weeks (1001 to 1300 g) prophylactic CPAP or oxygen then NBU; over 30 weeks (over 1300 g) use rescue CPAP if distressed.
- **NBU rescue CPAP** for respiratory distress: RR over 60, grunting, nasal flaring, sternal/intercostal recession, severe lower chest wall indrawing. Minimum **5 cmH2O**. Reassess HR, RR, SpO2 after 15 minutes, 1 hour, then 3-hourly; target SpO2 91 to 95%.
- Wean: after at least 24 hours with SpO2 above 95% and no distress, reduce FiO2 first; stop when stable at FiO2 30%, pressure 5 cm water; then oxygen 1 L/min by prongs and recheck at 15 minutes.
- **Do not give CPAP** to a neonate with **Apgar below 4 at 5 minutes**. **Defer** CPAP in uncontrollable seizures, apnoea or gasping, floppy infant.
- **Complications:** air leaks (pneumothorax), abdominal distension, nasal septum necrosis or distorted nares, hypoxia, oxygen toxicity.

### Neonatal sepsis (under 60 days)

| Class | Signs | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Severe neonatal sepsis** | Any of: unconscious, convulsions, unable to feed, apnoea, unable to cry or high-pitched cry, central cyanosis or SpO2 below 90%, bulging fontanelle, persistent vomiting | Admit; oxygen if SpO2 below 90%; keep warm if temperature below 35.5 C, expose if 38 C or above; check and treat hypoglycaemia; NGT feeds or IV fluids; blood and CSF cultures; **IV crystalline penicillin plus gentamicin** (or cefotaxime plus crystalline penicillin) for a minimum of **7 days** |
| **Neonatal sepsis** | Movement only when stimulated, not feeding well, temperature 38 C or above or below 35.5 C, severe chest wall indrawing | Admit; as above; IV penicillin and gentamicin for **2 days**; if improved and feeding, oral amoxicillin for 5 days as outpatient |
| Unlikely | None | Treat other illness; advise on danger signs; review within 24 hours |

- Use **flucloxacillin plus gentamicin** for suspected staphylococcal sepsis or extensive skin pustules, abscess or omphalitis. Add **metronidazole** if necrotizing enterocolitis is suspected.

| Problem | Duration |
|---|---|
| Possible infection, breastfeeding well | IV antibiotics may stop at 48 hours if all signs resolved, feeding well and LP (if done) normal; oral to complete 5 days; review at 48 hours |
| Skin infection with generalised illness | IV/IM can stop at 72 hours if feeding well, no fever and LP normal; oral for a further 5 days |
| Clinical or radiological pneumonia | IV/IM minimum 5 days or until well for 24 hours |
| Severe sepsis (LP done and clear) | Minimum 7 days or until completely well |
| Meningitis, or severe sepsis with no LP | Minimum **14 days**; **3 weeks IV if Gram-negative meningitis** suspected |

**Antibiotic prophylaxis** (crystalline penicillin plus gentamicin) immediately to all newborns (term and preterm) with: **PROM over 18 hours**, maternal fever above 38 C, suspected or confirmed chorioamnionitis, or maternal sepsis treatment in labour or the 24 hours before or after birth. Give for 48 to 72 hours (at least **4 doses of penicillin and 2 doses of gentamicin**) and stop if the baby stays well. Start investigations but do not delay antibiotics. No risk factors: no antibiotics. A well preterm or LBW baby with low risk does not need antibiotics.

**Ophthalmia neonatorum** (swollen red eyelids with pus): single dose ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg IM (max 125 mg), or kanamycin or spectinomycin 25 mg/kg (max 75 mg) IM.

> **Remember:** **ceftriaxone is not recommended in jaundiced newborns or under 6 days**; cefotaxime or ceftazidime are safer in the first 7 days. Gentamicin: check dose by weight and age in days; stop if no urine passed after 24 hours; penicillin is twice daily in babies under 7 days.

**Adrenaline and glucose preparation:** dilute 1 ml of 1 in 1000 adrenaline in 9 ml water for injection to make 10 ml of 1 in 10,000. For overweight children base doses on the median weight for age or height.

**Initial maintenance fluids (normal renal function):** weight 3 kg: 300 ml/24 h (13 ml/h); 5 kg: 500 (21); 10 kg: 1000 (42); 15 kg: 1250 (52); 20 kg: 1500 (63); 25 kg: 1625 (68). Start oral feeds as soon as safe (can rise to 150 ml/kg/day). Make Ringer's/5% dextrose by adding 50 ml of 50% dextrose to 450 ml Ringer's lactate.

**Weight reference:** use the weight-for-length (birth to 2 years) and weight-for-height tables to read Z-scores (for example, a 5-month-old boy of 48 cm weighing 2.5 kg is at -2 SD). Use the emergency weight-estimation table when the child cannot be weighed.

## Key points to revise

1. Triage first: emergency signs mean immediate resuscitation; priority signs mean front of the queue.
2. Oxygen if SpO2 below 90%; target 90 to 95% (91 to 95% in neonates); recheck SpO2 an hour after stopping.
3. Convulsions: diazepam 0.3 mg/kg IV (0.5 mg/kg PR), repeat once, then phenobarbitone 15 mg/kg IM; always check glucose; no more than 2 diazepam doses in 24 hours after phenobarbitone.
4. Severe malaria: artesunate 3 mg/kg (2.4 mg/kg if over 20 kg) at 0, 12, 24 h, minimum 24 hours, then full course of AL; repeat negative slides 3 times.
5. Dehydration: Plan A (10 ml/kg per stool), Plan B (75 ml/kg over 4 h), Plan C (30 then 70 ml/kg Ringer's lactate); zinc for all; no antibiotics unless dysentery.
6. SAM: 10 steps; no frusemide, no bolus unless shock; ReSoMal and F75, then RUTF; give penicillin and gentamicin to all ill children.
7. Pneumonia: fast breathing or indrawing gets high-dose amoxicillin; danger signs get oxygen, penicillin and gentamicin; know the 48-hour and day-5 failure rules.
8. Newborn: Golden Minute ventilation, vitamin K 1 mg IM, chlorhexidine 7.1% cord care, hypoglycaemia threshold 2.6 mmol/L, caffeine for apnoea, phototherapy and exchange by nomogram, antibiotic prophylaxis for PROM over 18 hours.
