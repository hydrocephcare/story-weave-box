## SECTION A: CARDIOVASCULAR PATHOLOGY---
### A1. ATHEROSCLEROSIS (recap + expansion from Part 1)- Stages: fatty streak → fibrous plaque → complicated plaque (calcification, ulceration, haemorrhage, thrombosis)

- Complicated plaque is what causes clinical events — MI, stroke, peripheral vascular disease
- Risk factors: hyperlipidaemia, hypertension, smoking, diabetes, age, male sex, family history

### A2. MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION**Timeline of morphological changes (classic exam table):**
| Time after MI | Gross/microscopic change |
|---|---|
| <24 hours | Coagulative necrosis (earliest change) — grossly may look normal |
| 1–3 days | Neutrophil infiltration, yellow pallor |
| 3–7 days | Macrophages clear debris, yellow-tan, soft |
| 1–3 weeks | Granulation tissue formation, red-grey border |
| Weeks–months | Collagen scar formation (fibrosis), grey-white, firm |
- Earliest identifiable change (<24h) — coagulative necrosis
- Cardiac marker timeline (troponin, CK-MB) — see Monday's Chemical Pathology notes

### A3. DILATED CARDIOMYOPATHY (essay-favourite)**Morphologic changes in the heart:**
- Cardiomegaly — all four chambers dilated
- Ventricular walls may be thinned or normal thickness (dilation predominates over hypertrophy)
- Mural thrombi commonly form in the dilated chambers (risk of embolism)
- Microscopically — non-specific myocyte hypertrophy and interstitial fibrosis
- Functional consequence — systolic dysfunction (poor contractility), leads to congestive heart failure

**Causes:** idiopathic (most common), viral myocarditis, alcohol, peripartum, genetic, doxorubicin toxicity

### A4. VASCULITIS — classified by vessel size| Vessel size | Examples |
|---|---|
| Large vessel | Takayasu arteritis (young Asian women, aortic arch), Giant cell (temporal) arteritis (elderly, headache, jaw claudication) |
| Medium vessel | Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN — necrotising, associated with Hep B), Kawasaki disease (children, coronary artery aneurysms) |
| Small vessel | Granulomatosis with polyangiitis/Wegener's (c-ANCA, upper/lower respiratory + renal), Microscopic polyangiitis (p-ANCA) |

### A5. RHEUMATIC HEART DISEASE**Pathogenesis:**
- Follows group A streptococcal pharyngitis (untreated)
- Molecular mimicry — antibodies against streptococcal M protein cross-react with cardiac tissue
- Causes acute rheumatic fever → chronic rheumatic heart disease with repeated episodes

**Morphology:**
- **Aschoff bodies** — pathognomonic granulomatous lesions containing Anitschkow cells (activated macrophages), found in the myocardium
- **Mitral valve is most commonly affected** — leads to mitral stenosis over time (fish-mouth/buttonhole deformity)
- Valve changes: leaflet thickening, fusion of commissures, chordae tendineae shortening

### A6. INFECTIVE ENDOCARDITIS — Acute vs Sub-acute (commonly compared)| Feature | Acute IE | Sub-acute IE |
|---|---|---|
| Organism | Staphylococcus aureus (virulent) | Streptococcus viridans (less virulent) |
| Valve affected | Often previously normal valve | Usually previously damaged/abnormal valve |
| Onset | Rapid, days | Slow, weeks–months |
| Vegetations | Large, destructive | Smaller |
| Course | Severe, rapidly fatal if untreated | Indolent |

### A7. PULMONARY THROMBOEMBOLISM**Pathogenesis:**
- Most emboli arise from deep vein thrombosis (DVT) of the lower limbs (femoral, iliac veins)
- Virchow's triad predisposes: venous stasis, hypercoagulability, endothelial injury
- Thrombus dislodges → travels through venous system → right heart → pulmonary arteries

**Morphology/consequences:**
- Small emboli — often asymptomatic or clinically silent
- Large emboli — can cause sudden death via saddle embolus occluding the pulmonary artery bifurcation
- Medium emboli — pulmonary infarction (wedge-shaped, haemorrhagic) if collateral circulation is inadequate
- Chronic recurrent emboli — pulmonary hypertension, cor pulmonale

## SECTION B: RESPIRATORY PATHOLOGY---
### B1. EMPHYSEMA vs CHRONIC BRONCHITIS (always compared — the classic COPD pair)| Feature | Emphysema ("Pink puffer") | Chronic bronchitis ("Blue bloater") |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Permanent airspace enlargement distal to terminal bronchiole, with wall destruction | Chronic productive cough for ≥3 months in 2 consecutive years |
| Main mechanism | Protease-antiprotease imbalance (↓alpha-1 antitrypsin), destroys elastin | Chronic irritation (smoking) → mucous gland hyperplasia |
| Appearance | Thin, breathless, pink complexion | Overweight, cyanotic, oedematous |
| Hypoxia/CO2 retention | Mild, compensates by hyperventilating | Marked hypoxia and CO2 retention |
| Cor pulmonale | Late | Early and more severe |
**Pathogenesis of emphysema:**
- Cigarette smoke → recruits neutrophils/macrophages → release proteases (elastase)
- Normally alpha-1 antitrypsin inhibits elastase — smoking also inactivates alpha-1 antitrypsin
- Net effect — unopposed elastase activity destroys alveolar walls → permanent airspace enlargement
- Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (genetic) — causes early-onset panacinar emphysema, especially in lower lobes

### B2. ASTHMA — microscopic bronchiole changes (5 features)- Bronchial smooth muscle hypertrophy

- Mucous gland hyperplasia with mucus plugging
- Basement membrane thickening
- Eosinophilic infiltration of the airway wall
- Curschmann spirals (mucus casts) and Charcot-Leyden crystals (eosinophil breakdown products) in sputum

*(Pathogenesis, Type I hypersensitivity mechanism — see Wednesday's Immunopathology section)*

### B3. DIFFUSE ALVEOLAR DAMAGE / ARDS (5 histomorphological features)- Hyaline membrane formation lining alveolar walls (protein-rich exudate)

- Alveolar septal oedema and congestion
- Type II pneumocyte hyperplasia (regenerative response)
- Interstitial and intra-alveolar inflammatory infiltrate
- Progression to interstitial fibrosis in the organising phase

**Causes of ARDS:** sepsis, aspiration, severe trauma, pneumonia, pancreatitis

### B4. PNEUMOCONIOSES — classification- **Coal worker's pneumoconiosis** — carbon dust, upper lobes, "black lung"

- **Silicosis** — silica dust (mining, quarrying), upper lobes, egg-shell calcification of hilar nodes, increased TB risk
- **Asbestosis** — asbestos fibres, lower lobes, ferruginous bodies, risk of mesothelioma and bronchogenic carcinoma
- **Berylliosis** — beryllium exposure, non-caseating granulomas (mimics sarcoidosis)

### B5. NASAL POLYPS (5 histomorphological features)- Oedematous stroma

- Eosinophilic inflammatory infiltrate
- Thickened basement membrane
- Covered by respiratory (ciliated columnar) epithelium
- Absence of malignant features (benign lesion) — associated with allergic rhinitis, chronic sinusitis, asthma, aspirin sensitivity

### B6. LUNG CARCINOMA**Classification of primary malignant lung neoplasms:**
| Type | Location | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Squamous cell carcinoma | Central | Strongly smoking-associated, keratin pearls, may cavitate |
| Adenocarcinoma | Peripheral | Most common overall (including in non-smokers), glandular pattern |
| Small cell carcinoma | Central | Neuroendocrine, worst prognosis, strongly paraneoplastic |
| Large cell carcinoma | Peripheral | Undifferentiated, poor prognosis |
**5 paraneoplastic syndromes of primary lung carcinoma:**
- SIADH (small cell) — hyponatraemia
- Ectopic ACTH secretion (small cell) — Cushing's syndrome
- Hypercalcaemia via PTHrP (squamous cell)
- Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (small cell) — autoantibodies against presynaptic calcium channels
- Clubbing / hypertrophic pulmonary osteoarthropathy (adenocarcinoma)

### B7. TUBERCULOSIS**Pathogenesis:**
- Inhalation of Mycobacterium tuberculosis droplets
- Alveolar macrophages phagocytose but fail to kill the organism initially
- Cell-mediated (Type IV) hypersensitivity develops over 2–4 weeks
- Granuloma formation walls off the organism — **Ghon focus** (primary lesion, usually subpleural, mid/lower zone)
- Ghon focus + hilar lymph node involvement = **Ghon complex**
- Outcome — usually contained (latent TB) or, if immunocompromised, progresses to active/miliary TB

**Morphology:**
- Caseating granulomas — central caseous necrosis surrounded by epithelioid macrophages, Langhans giant cells, lymphocytes
- Secondary/reactivation TB — typically apical, due to higher oxygen tension favouring the organism

**Clinical presentation:** chronic cough >2 weeks, haemoptysis, night sweats, weight loss, low-grade fever
**Diagnosis:** Ziehl-Neelsen stain (gold standard for acid-fast bacilli), sputum culture, GeneXpert

## SECTION C: GI / HEPATOBILIARY PATHOLOGY---
### C1. ALCOHOLIC LIVER DISEASE — morphologic changes (progressive spectrum)- **Hepatic steatosis (fatty liver)** — reversible, macrovesicular fat accumulation in hepatocytes

- **Alcoholic hepatitis** — hepatocyte swelling/ballooning, **Mallory-Denk bodies** (eosinophilic cytoplasmic inclusions), neutrophilic infiltrate, perivenular fibrosis
- **Cirrhosis** — irreversible, diffuse fibrosis with regenerative nodules, micronodular pattern typically

### C2. PANCREATITIS — aetiology- Gallstones (commonest cause) — obstruct the ampulla of Vater

- Alcohol (second commonest)
- Hypertriglyceridaemia
- Hypercalcaemia
- Drugs (thiazides, azathioprine)
- Trauma/post-ERCP
- Idiopathic

**Acute pancreatitis complication to remember:** hypocalcaemia = poor prognostic sign (fat saponification consumes calcium — links back to Chemical Pathology)

### C3. BARRETT'S OESOPHAGUS — pathogenesis- Chronic gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GERD) exposes lower oesophageal squamous epithelium to acid

- Chronic injury triggers **metaplasia** — squamous epithelium replaced by columnar epithelium with goblet cells (intestinal-type metaplasia)
- Considered a protective adaptation to acid, but is premalignant
- Progresses through dysplasia (low-grade → high-grade) → risk of oesophageal adenocarcinoma

### C4. GALLSTONES — types compared| Type | Composition | Risk factors |
|---|---|---|
| Cholesterol stones (most common in developed countries) | Cholesterol | "5 F's": Female, Fat, Forty, Fertile, Family history |
| Pigment stones (black) | Unconjugated bilirubin | Haemolytic conditions (sickle cell, spherocytosis) |
| Pigment stones (brown) | Calcium bilirubinate | Biliary infection/infestation (e.g. Clonorchis) |
| Mixed stones | Cholesterol + pigment | Most common overall |

### C5. COLORECTAL CARCINOMA**Pathogenesis — adenoma-carcinoma sequence:**
- Normal epithelium → APC gene mutation → adenoma formation
- KRAS mutation → adenoma growth/progression
- p53 mutation → progression to invasive carcinoma
- This stepwise accumulation of mutations is the classic model (Vogelstein sequence)

**Morphology:** most arise in the rectosigmoid region; annular "napkin-ring" lesions in the left colon (obstructive), polypoid fungating masses in the right colon (bleeding, anaemia)
**Clinical presentation:**
- Left-sided — change in bowel habit, obstruction, blood-streaked stool
- Right-sided — iron deficiency anaemia, occult bleeding, vague abdominal pain (presents later as the lumen is wider)

**Risk factors:** familial adenomatous polyposis (APC gene), hereditary non-polyposis colorectal cancer (Lynch syndrome), inflammatory bowel disease, low-fibre/high-fat diet, age

### C6. H. PYLORI GASTRITIS — pathogenesis- H. pylori colonises the gastric antrum, produces urease (neutralises acid locally, allowing survival) and cytotoxins (CagA, VacA)

- Triggers chronic inflammation — initially neutrophilic (active), then chronic lymphocytic/plasma cell infiltrate
- Damages the mucosal barrier → predisposes to peptic ulcer disease
- Chronic infection → intestinal metaplasia → risk of gastric adenocarcinoma and MALT lymphoma

### C7. INFLAMMATORY BOWEL DISEASE — Crohn's vs Ulcerative Colitis (frequently compared)| Feature | Crohn's disease | Ulcerative colitis |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Anywhere mouth to anus (commonly terminal ileum) | Colon only, starts at rectum |
| Distribution | Skip lesions | Continuous |
| Depth | Transmural | Mucosal only |
| Gross appearance | Cobblestone mucosa, strictures | Continuous ulceration, pseudopolyps |
| Granulomas | Present (non-caseating) | Absent |
| Complications | Fistulas, strictures, malabsorption | Toxic megacolon, ↑colorectal cancer risk (higher than Crohn's) |

### C8. BILIRUBIN METABOLISM DISORDERS (recap from Chemical Pathology — relevant here too)- Crigler-Najjar type II — reduced UGT activity

- Dubin-Johnson syndrome — conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia, black liver pigmentation on gross examination
