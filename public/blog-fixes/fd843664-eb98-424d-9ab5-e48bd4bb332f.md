# Valvular Heart Disease (Rheumatic and Non-Rheumatic)

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Cardiology**

> **In one line:** valvular heart disease is stenosis (obstruction), regurgitation (leak) or both of a heart valve; in Kenya it is **predominantly rheumatic**, damaging the **mitral valve most often, then the aortic**, and each lesion produces a predictable pressure or volume overload with characteristic murmurs.

## What you must know

- **Rheumatic heart disease (RHD)** is the commonest cause locally and the main cardiac disease of teenagers and young adults; it reflects poverty, overcrowding and untreated streptococcal throat infections.
- RHD affects **mitral > aortic**; the tricuspid is usually functionally involved; the **pulmonary valve is almost never** affected. Multiple valves are often involved.
- **Mitral stenosis**: loud S1, opening snap, mid-diastolic rumbling murmur with presystolic accentuation; complications are AF, thromboembolism, pulmonary hypertension.
- **Aortic stenosis**: triad of **exertional dyspnoea, angina, syncope**; slow-rising small-volume pulse and ejection systolic murmur radiating to the carotids.
- **Aortic regurgitation**: collapsing pulse, wide pulse pressure, early diastolic murmur at the left sternal edge.
- Medical therapy is limited; **definitive treatment is mechanical (valvotomy/balloon, repair or replacement)**, so prevention (rheumatic fever prophylaxis) is key.

## 1. Introduction and aetiology

- Predominantly rheumatic in this environment; management is unavailable in many places, expensive, complex and risky, hence the importance of **primary and secondary prevention**.
- Other causes: congenital, degenerative (calcific), ischaemic, inflammatory, infective endocarditis, connective tissue (Marfan's, SLE), syphilis.

### Rheumatic heart disease

- The consequence of **rheumatic endocarditis** and the **only long-term sequel of rheumatic fever**.
- **Fibrosis and distortion** of the valve apparatus produce stenosis, regurgitation or both.
- Left-sided valves are most often affected: mitral > aortic; tricuspid often functional; pulmonary almost never. Involvement is often multiple.
- Secondary prevention: long-term **benzathine penicillin** prophylaxis after rheumatic fever.

## 2. Mitral stenosis (MS)

### 2.1 Anatomy and aetiology

- Mitral valve: leaflets, commissures, chordae tendineae. **Normal valve area 4 to 6 cm^2; 1 to 2 cm^2 is significant stenosis; below 1 cm^2 is critical.**
- Aetiology: almost invariably **rheumatic**; also congenital, calcification, inflammatory.
- Pathology: **commissural fusion**, fibrosis and distortion of chordae and leaflets (fish-mouth or buttonhole orifice).

### 2.2 Pathophysiology

- Impaired valve opening, so poor left atrial emptying:
  - Raised LA pressure, then **pulmonary venous congestion**, then **pulmonary hypertension**, then RV hypertrophy and failure.
  - Enlarged LA, **LA thrombus**, **atrial fibrillation**.
- Inadequate LV filling gives a low cardiac output.

### 2.3 Symptoms

- Exertional dyspnoea and fatigue, palpitations (even at rest), cough, **haemoptysis**, orthopnoea and PND, abdominal discomfort and swelling (right heart failure).

### 2.4 Signs

| System | Findings |
|---|---|
| General | **Mitral facies** (malar flush) |
| Pulse | Low volume, rapid, **irregularly irregular** if AF |
| Palpation | Apex **not displaced**, **tapping** (palpable S1); **palpable P2**; **left parasternal heave** (RVH) |
| Auscultation | **Loud S1 and P2**; **opening snap**; **mid-diastolic rumbling murmur with presystolic accentuation** at the apex (loudest in left lateral position, in expiration) |
| Others | Features of tricuspid regurgitation; **Graham Steell murmur** (early diastolic murmur of pulmonary regurgitation); signs of heart failure |

> **Exam tip:** the closer the opening snap is to S2, the tighter the stenosis (high LA pressure opens the valve early). Presystolic accentuation is lost in AF.

### 2.5 Complications

Heart failure, **atrial fibrillation**, **thromboembolism** (stroke), infective endocarditis, pulmonary hypertension.

### 2.6 Investigations

- **CXR:** LA enlargement (double right heart border, widened carina, filling of the pulmonary bay), pulmonary congestion, **normal cardiothoracic ratio**, mitral valve calcification.
- **ECG:** left atrial enlargement (P mitrale), RVH, right axis deviation, AF.
- **Echo:** valve morphology; **Doppler** for valve area, gradient and pulmonary pressures; LA enlargement and RVH.

### 2.7 Management

- Medical (limited): diuretics; rate control with **beta-blockers or digoxin** (long diastole matters); anticoagulation for AF. Usual heart-failure therapy has little role.
- Definitive: **balloon valvotomy (percutaneous)**, closed or open surgical valvotomy, valve repair or replacement.

## 3. Mitral regurgitation (MR)

- **Aetiology:** locally predominantly rheumatic; also degenerative (mitral valve prolapse), ischaemic (papillary muscle dysfunction or rupture), ventricular dilatation (annular dilatation), inflammatory, infective endocarditis.
- **Pathology:** fibrosis and distortion (RHD); rupture of chordae or papillary muscle (ischaemia, endocarditis); annular dilatation; degenerative leaflet disease (MVP).
- **Pathophysiology:** the stroke volume rises to cover the regurgitant volume, giving **volume overload, eccentric hypertrophy (dilatation) of the LV** and an enlarged LA.
- **Symptoms:** insidious, may be absent even when severe; onset depends on complications (pulmonary hypertension, AF) or precipitants; congestive heart failure.
- **Signs:** large-volume pulse, **displaced heaving apex**, **soft S1**, S3, **apical pansystolic murmur radiating to the axilla**.
- **Complications:** heart failure, infective endocarditis, AF.
- **Investigations:** CXR (cardiomegaly, pulmonary congestion); ECG (LAE, LVH, AF); echo (morphology, quantification, LV size and function).
- **Management:** treat heart failure and complications; definitive **surgery (repair preferred, or replacement)**.

## 4. Aortic stenosis (AS)

- **Aetiology by age:** children, congenital; young adults, RHD (usually with mitral disease); older adults, degenerative calcific.
- **Pathophysiology:** obstruction to LV emptying, so **pressure overload**, marked **concentric LVH**, increased oxygen demand, and raised LV diastolic pressure from stiffness.
- **Symptoms:** long latent period; once symptomatic progression is rapid. Classical triad on exertion: **dyspnoea, angina, syncope**. Average survival after onset: angina about 5 years, syncope about 3 years, heart failure about 2 years.
- **Signs:** small-volume **slow-rising (plateau) pulse**; heaving, non-displaced apex; ejection click; **ejection systolic murmur radiating to the carotids**; S4; soft or absent A2 in severe disease; narrow pulse pressure.
- **Complications:** LV failure (diastolic and systolic), arrhythmias (AF, ventricular arrhythmias, **sudden death**), infective endocarditis.
- **Investigations:** CXR may be normal; normal heart size; valve calcification; **post-stenotic dilatation** of the aorta. ECG: marked LVH with ST depression and T inversion (strain). Echo with Doppler: valve area and gradient, LVH, LV function.
- **Management:**
  - Medical: very limited; cautious diuresis; heart failure therapy only in systolic dysfunction; treat complications; avoid vasodilators.
  - Definitive: **aortic valve replacement (AVR)**; balloon dilatation (children); **TAVI** (transcatheter aortic valve implantation) for high-risk patients.

> **Exam tip:** do AVR as soon as the patient has symptoms; asymptomatic severe AS is followed closely.

## 5. Aortic regurgitation (AR)

- **Functional anatomy:** cusp disease is the mechanism in rheumatic AR; root dilatation causes degenerative and inflammatory AR.
- **Aetiology:** congenital (bicuspid valve); acquired: rheumatic, **syphilis**, **aortic dissection**, inflammatory disorders (ankylosing spondylitis, Marfan's), degenerative, endocarditis.
- **Pathophysiology:** hyperdynamic circulation; regurgitation causes volume overload and **eccentric LVH (dilatation)**. Much more severe than MR, so very large hearts (**cor bovinum**).
- **Symptoms:** long latency; pounding in the chest and head, palpitations; heart failure late.
- **Signs:**

| Sign | Description |
|---|---|
| Pulse | Large-volume **collapsing (water-hammer)** pulse; wide pulse pressure |
| **Corrigan's** | Visible carotid pulsation |
| **Traube's** | Pistol-shot sounds over the femorals |
| **Duroziez's** | Systolic and diastolic femoral murmur on compression |
| **Quincke's** | Capillary pulsation in the nail bed |
| **de Musset's** | Head nodding with each beat |
| Apex | Displaced and hyperdynamic |
| Heart sounds | Soft S1, soft A2 |
| Murmur | **Early diastolic murmur at the left sternal edge (3rd/4th space)**, best with the patient sitting forward in expiration; aortic ejection systolic flow murmur; **Austin Flint** (apical mid-diastolic) murmur |

- **Investigations:** ECG: LVH with ST and T-wave changes. CXR: massive cardiomegaly. Echo: morphology, quantification, LV size and function.
- **Management:** treat heart failure; definitive **aortic valve replacement**.

## 6. Tricuspid valve

### 6.1 Tricuspid stenosis

- Predominantly rheumatic and almost always accompanies MS (the MS may mask it).
- Pathophysiology: RA-RV gradient causes raised RA pressure and systemic venous congestion; impaired RV filling gives low output.
- Symptoms: fatigue, abdominal swelling and discomfort, leg swelling, neck fluttering. **No chest symptoms even with MS.**
- Signs: **giant "a" waves** on JVP, low-volume pulse, **no pulmonary hypertension or RVH, clear lungs**; left sternal edge mid-diastolic murmur, louder in inspiration.
- Investigations: CXR: marked cardiomegaly from RA enlargement with clear lungs. ECG: RA hypertrophy (tall P waves), possibly biatrial, **no RVH**. Echo confirms gradient and MS.
- Management: sodium restriction and diuretics; valvotomy (open or closed) or valve replacement.

### 6.2 Tricuspid regurgitation

- Aetiology: often **functional** from RV annular dilatation (pulmonary hypertension with RVH, other causes of RV dilatation); also rheumatic, congenital, inflammatory, **carcinoid syndrome**, endocarditis in IV drug users, Ebstein's anomaly.
- Pathophysiology: RV volume overload; primary pathology is often pulmonary hypertension.
- Clinical: usually well tolerated without pulmonary hypertension. Right heart failure: fatigue, cachexia, leg and abdominal swelling, anorexia. Signs: **elevated JVP with large V (cv) waves**, left parasternal heave, loud P2, **pansystolic murmur at the left lower sternal edge (louder on inspiration, Carvallo's sign)**, **pulsatile hepatomegaly**, ascites.
- Investigations: ECG (RVH, primary lesion), echo (morphology, severity, pulmonary pressures), CXR (RVH cardiomegaly).
- Management: treat primary condition, diuretics; surgery: **annuloplasty** (tightening the valve) or valve replacement.

## 7. Pulmonary valve

- **Stenosis:** almost always congenital.
- **Regurgitation:** secondary to pulmonary hypertension (Graham Steell murmur); presentation and management are those of the primary disease.

## Key points to revise

1. RHD is the commonest valve disease locally: mitral > aortic; tricuspid functional; pulmonary rarely.
2. MS: loud S1, opening snap, mid-diastolic murmur; AF and thromboembolism; treat with rate control, anticoagulation and balloon valvotomy.
3. MR: apical pansystolic murmur to the axilla, displaced apex, volume overload.
4. AS: dyspnoea, angina, syncope; slow-rising pulse; ejection systolic murmur to the carotids; treat with AVR or TAVI.
5. AR: collapsing pulse and early diastolic murmur; Austin Flint; huge LV (cor bovinum); AVR.
6. Tricuspid stenosis: giant a waves, clear lungs, with MS; tricuspid regurgitation: large V waves, pulsatile liver.
7. Medical therapy is limited and mostly for complications; definitive therapy is mechanical; prevent with benzathine penicillin.
