## Detailed Notes
## Key Points - Antifungals target ergosterol or fungal cell walls, with polyenes, azoles, echinocandins, and allylamines representing major classes. - Antivirals are often virostatic and target specific viral enzymes or processes, with examples including anti-herpes drugs, anti-influenza agents, and anti-hepatitis treatments. - Antihelminthics target neuromuscular function or metabolic pathways of parasitic worms, with drugs like albendazole, mebendazole, and ivermectin being crucial. - Antiprotozoals cover a range of parasitic protozoa, with specific drugs for malaria, amoeba, leishmania, trypanosoma, and toxoplasma. - Antiretroviral therapy (ART) for HIV aims to suppress viral load to undetectable levels, preserve CD4 counts, prevent transmission, and reduce morbidity/mortality. - ART is recommended for all HIV-positive individuals regardless of CD4 count, with preferred regimens typically consisting of two NRTIs plus an INSTI. - Opportunistic infections in HIV are managed with prophylaxis based on CD4 count thresholds, with common examples including PCP, toxoplasmosis, and MAC. - Managing HIV co-infections requires careful consideration of drug interactions, especially with rifampicin and TB treatment, and the risk of Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS). - Antiretroviral drugs have significant drug interactions, particularly with CYP450 enzyme inducers/inhibitors, and require careful monitoring. - Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP) and Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) are vital tools for HIV prevention.

## Detailed Notes ### WEEK 8 — Antifungals, Antivirals, Antihelminthics & Antiprotozoals

#### ANTIFUNGALS

##### Key Concept Fungi are **eukaryotes** — harder to kill without harming host. Target: **ergosterol** (fungal cell membrane equivalent of cholesterol).

##### 1. Polyenes — *Amphotericin B, Nystatin* **MOA:** Binds ergosterol → pokes holes in fungal membrane → leakage → cell death

| Drug | Use | Key SE | |---|---|---| | Amphotericin B | Serious systemic fungal infections (Cryptococcus, Aspergillus, Candida) | **Nephrotoxicity** (big one!), fever/rigors, hypoK, anaemia | | Nystatin | Oral/vaginal candidiasis (topical only) | GI upset — too toxic for systemic use |

> **Medic tip:** Pre-hydrate before Ampho B IV. Monitor K⁺, creatinine daily. Liposomal form = less nephrotoxic.

##### 2. Azoles — *Fluconazole, Itraconazole, Voriconazole, Clotrimazole* **MOA:** Inhibit **14α-demethylase** (CYP450) → blocks ergosterol synthesis → membrane instability

| Drug | Use | Key SE | |---|---|---| | Fluconazole | Candida, Cryptococcal meningitis (maintenance) | Hepatotoxicity, **lots of drug interactions** | | Voriconazole | Aspergillosis (1st line) | Visual disturbances, photosensitivity | | Itraconazole | Histoplasma, dermatophytes | Negative inotrope — avoid in heart failure | | Clotrimazole | Topical skin/vaginal | Local irritation only |

>  **Azoles inhibit CYP3A4** — watch with warfarin, statins, immunosuppressants

##### 3. Echinocandins — *Caspofungin, Micafungin* **MOA:** Inhibit **β-1,3-glucan synthase** → disrupts fungal cell wall (no human equivalent!)

- Use: Invasive Candida, Aspergillus (when azoles fail) - SE: Well tolerated — mild LFT elevation, histamine reaction - Route: **IV only**

##### 4. Allylamines — *Terbinafine* **MOA:** Inhibit **squalene epoxidase** → blocks ergosterol synthesis

- Use: Dermatophytes (tinea, onychomycosis) - SE: GI upset, hepatotoxicity (rare), taste disturbance

#### ANTIVIRALS

##### Key Concept Viruses use **host machinery** — targets must be virus-specific. Most antivirals are **virostatic** (suppress replication, don't cure).

##### 1. Anti-Herpes Drugs — *Aciclovir, Valaciclovir, Ganciclovir*

**MOA:** Nucleoside analogues → activated by **viral thymidine kinase** → inhibit viral DNA polymerase → chain termination

| Drug | Use | Key SE | |---|---|---| | Aciclovir | HSV, VZV (shingles, chickenpox, encephalitis) | Nephrotoxicity (crystalluria — hydrate well!), neuro SE at high doses | | Valaciclovir | Prodrug of aciclovir — better oral bioavailability | Same as aciclovir | | Ganciclovir | **CMV** (retinitis, colitis in immunocompromised) | **Bone marrow suppression** (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia) |

> **Medic tip:** HSV encephalitis = **IV Aciclovir** immediately (don't wait for PCR). Ganciclovir — check FBC weekly.

##### 2. Anti-Influenza — *Oseltamivir (Tamiflu), Zanamivir* **MOA:** Neuraminidase inhibitors → block viral release from host cells

- Use: Influenza A & B — **must start within 48hrs of symptoms** - SE: Nausea, vomiting (oseltamivir); bronchospasm (zanamivir — avoid in asthma) - Who gets it: Elderly, immunocompromised, pregnant, severe disease

##### 3. Anti-Hepatitis

| Drug | Target | Use | Key SE | |---|---|---|---| | Tenofovir / Entecavir | DNA polymerase | Hepatitis B (chronic) | Nephrotoxicity (tenofovir), lactic acidosis | | Sofosbuvir + Daclatasvir | NS5B/NS5A | Hepatitis C (DAAs — cure >95%) | Well tolerated; fatigue, headache | | Pegylated IFN-α + Ribavirin | Immune modulation + viral RNA | HBV/HCV (older regimen) | Depression, flu-like, haemolytic anaemia (ribavirin), cytopenias |

> **Medic tip:** DAAs (Direct Acting Antivirals) have essentially replaced interferon-based HCV treatment. Check drug interactions — many via CYP enzymes.

#### ANTIHELMINTHICS

##### Key Concept Worms (helminths) are **multicellular eukaryotes** — drugs target neuromuscular function or metabolic pathways specific to parasites.

| Drug | MOA | Use | Key SE | |---|---|---|---| | **Albendazole** | Inhibits tubulin polymerisation → disrupts microtubules → worm paralysis/death | Roundworm, hookworm, tapeworm, **neurocysticercosis**, hydatid disease | GI upset, hepatotoxicity, **teratogenic** | | **Mebendazole** | Same as albendazole | Threadworm (1st line), roundworm, whipworm | Minimal systemic SE (poorly absorbed) | | **Ivermectin** | Hyperpolarises GABA-gated Cl⁻ channels → paralysis of worm | **Strongyloides**, onchocerciasis, scabies, head lice | Mazzotti reaction (if onchocerciasis — fever, rash from dying worms) | | **Praziquantel** | Increases Ca²⁺ permeability → spastic paralysis → worm detaches | **Schistosomiasis**, tapeworms, flukes | GI upset, headache, dizziness | | **Diethylcarbamazine (DEC)** | Immobilises microfilariae | Lymphatic filariasis, loiasis | Mazzotti-like reaction | | **Niclosamide** | Inhibits oxidative phosphorylation in worm | Tapeworms | Minimal (not absorbed) |

> **Medic tip:** Neurocysticercosis — use **Albendazole + steroids** (to manage inflammatory response as cysts die). Never treat loiasis with ivermectin if high microfilarial load → encephalopathy.

#### ANTIPROTOZOALS

##### 1. Antimalarials

| Drug | MOA | Use | Key Notes | |---|---|---|---| | **Chloroquine** | Inhibits haem polymerisation → toxic haem accumulates | P. vivax, P. ovale, P. malariae (sensitive) | Resistance common in P. falciparum; retinopathy with long-term use | | **Artemisinin (Artesunate/ACTs)** | Free radical damage to parasite | **Severe P. falciparum** (IV artesunate = 1st line) | ACTs = artemisinin combination therapy (prevent resistance) | | **Quinine** | Similar to chloroquine + other effects | Severe malaria (if artesunate unavailable) | Cinchonism (tinnitus, vertigo), hypoglycaemia, QT prolongation | | **Primaquine** | Kills liver hypnozoites + gametocytes | Radical cure of P. vivax / P. ovale | **Check G6PD** before use → haemolytic anaemia in deficiency | | **Atovaquone-Proguanil (Malarone)** | Mitochondrial electron transport inhibition | Prophylaxis + treatment | Well tolerated; expensive | | **Doxycycline** | Protein synthesis inhibition | Malaria prophylaxis | Photosensitivity, oesophageal ulceration |

> **Medic tip (critical):** Severe malaria = **IV Artesunate**. Always check G6PD before primaquine. Pregnant women — avoid primaquine, doxycycline.

##### 2. Anti-Amoeba / Protozoal GI

| Drug | Use | Key SE | |---|---|---| | **Metronidazole** | *Entamoeba histolytica*, *Giardia*, *Trichomonas*, anaerobes | Metallic taste, nausea, **peripheral neuropathy** (prolonged), **disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol** | | **Diloxanide furoate** | Luminal amoebicide (after metro for E. histolytica) | Mild GI | | **Tinidazole** | Giardia, amoeba (better tolerated than metronidazole) | Similar to metronidazole but less SE |

##### 3. Anti-Leishmania

| Drug | Use | Key SE | |---|---|---| | **Sodium stibogluconate** | Visceral/cutaneous leishmaniasis | Cardiotoxicity (QT), pancreatitis, nephrotoxicity | | **Amphotericin B (liposomal)** | Visceral leishmaniasis (1st line in many settings) | Nephrotoxicity (less with liposomal) | | **Miltefosine** | Oral option for leishmaniasis | GI SE, teratogenic |

##### 4. Anti-Trypanosoma

| Drug | Use | Key SE | |---|---|---| | **Suramin** | African trypanosomiasis (early — bloodstream) | Nephrotoxicity | | **Melarsoprol** | African trypanosomiasis (late — CNS) | **Reactive encephalopathy** (5–10% fatal!) | | **Benznidazole / Nifurtimox** | Chagas disease (*T. cruzi*) | Peripheral neuropathy, rash |

##### 5. Anti-Toxoplasma

- **Pyrimethamine + Sulfadiazine + Folinic acid** → 1st line *Toxoplasma gondii* (especially CNS toxoplasmosis in HIV) - Add folinic acid to prevent bone marrow suppression from pyrimethamine - Prophylaxis in HIV: **Co-trimoxazole** (when CD4 <200)

### WEEK 9 — HIV Management (Special Focus) Part 1

#### The Virus — Quick Clinical Recap - **HIV** = RNA retrovirus → infects **CD4+ T cells** (also macrophages, dendritic cells) - Uses **reverse transcriptase** to convert RNA → DNA → integrates into host genome (provirus — permanent) - **CD4 count** = marker of immune function | **Viral load** = marker of disease activity/treatment response - AIDS = CD4 <200 cells/μL OR AIDS-defining illness regardless of CD4

#### Goals of ART (Antiretroviral Therapy) > **As a medic, always remember:** > 1. Suppress viral load to **undetectable** (<50 copies/mL) > 2. Preserve/restore CD4 count > 3. Prevent transmission (**U=U** — Undetectable = Untransmittable) > 4. Prevent OIs (opportunistic infections) > 5. Reduce HIV-related mortality & morbidity

#### ART Drug Classes — Overview

##### 1. NRTIs — Nucleoside/Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors **MOA:** Incorporated into viral DNA → **chain termination** (no 3'-OH group for chain elongation)

| Drug | Key Notes | |---|---| | **Tenofovir (TDF/TAF)** | Backbone of most regimens; TDF = nephrotoxic, TAF = safer for kidneys | | **Emtricitabine (FTC) ** | Paired with tenofovir (TDF+FTC = Truvada) | | **Lamivudine (3T

C) ** | Also active vs HBV; widely used, well tolerated | | **Abacavir (AB

C)** | **HLA-B\*5701 test MANDATORY** before use → hypersensitivity reaction (can be fatal on rechallenge) | | **Zidovudine (AZT)** | Older; causes **bone marrow suppression**, lipoatrophy — less used now |

>  **Class SE — Mitochondrial toxicity** (lactic acidosis, hepatic steatosis, lipodystrophy) — more with older NRTIs (AZT, d4T)

##### 2. NNRTIs — Non-Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors **MOA:** Bind directly to reverse transcriptase at **allosteric site** (not active site) → conformational change → inactivation

| Drug | Key Notes | |---|---| | **Efavirenz (EFV)** | Once daily; **CNS SE** (vivid dreams, dizziness, depression) — take at night; teratogenic (avoid 1st trimester) | | **Nevirapine (NVP)** | Risk of **severe hepatotoxicity** and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome — esp. in women with CD4 >250 | | **Rilpivirine** | Better tolerated than EFV; must take with food; fails at high viral load >100,000 | | **Doravirine** | Newer, well tolerated, fewer lipid effects |

>  **Low genetic barrier to resistance** — single mutation (K103N) → class-wide resistance. Never use as monotherapy.

##### 3. PIs — Protease Inhibitors **MOA:** Inhibit **HIV protease** → blocks cleavage of polyprotein → immature, non-infectious viral particles

| Drug | Key Notes | |---|---| | **Lopinavir/ritonavir (LPV/r)** | Ritonavir = **pharmacokinetic booster** (inhibits CYP3A4 → ↑ lopinavir levels) | | **Atazanavir (ATV/r)** | Hyperbilirubinaemia (benign — jaundice without liver damage), renal stones | | **Darunavir (DRV/r)** | High genetic barrier to resistance; preferred PI | | **Ritonavir** | Used only as booster now — too toxic at full doses |

>  **Class SE:** Lipodystrophy, dyslipidaemia, insulin resistance, GI SE, **multiple drug interactions** (strong CYP3A4 inhibitors)

##### 4. INSTIs — Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors **MOA:** Inhibit **HIV integrase** → prevent insertion of viral DNA into host chromosome

| Drug | Key Notes | |---|---| | **Dolutegravir (DTG)** | **Preferred 1st line** globally (WHO); high barrier to resistance, once daily, well tolerated | | **Bictegravir (BIC) ** | Used in Biktarvy (BIC/TAF/FT

C); excellent tolerability | | **Raltegravir (RAL)** | Twice daily; lower resistance barrier than DTG | | **Elvitegravir** | Requires boosting (cobicistat) |

>  DTG SE: Weight gain, neuropsychiatric effects (insomnia, depression). Neural tube defects concern (periconception) — now risk considered low but counsel women.

##### 5. Fusion/Entry Inhibitors **MOA:** Block HIV entry into CD4 cells

| Drug | MOA | Use | |---|---|---| | **Enfuvirtide (T-20)** | Binds gp41 → blocks fusion | Salvage therapy; SC injection twice daily | | **Maraviroc** | CCR5 antagonist → blocks co-receptor | **Tropism test required** (only works for CCR5-tropic virus) | | **Ibalizumab** | Binds CD4 receptor | MDR-HIV |

#### Standard First-Line ART Regimen (WHO / UK Guidelines) > **2 NRTIs + 1 INSTI** is now the preferred backbone

**Preferred:** - **TDF + FTC (or 3TC) + DTG** — most settings - **TAF + FTC + BIC** (Biktarvy) — excellent tolerability

**Alternatives:** - TDF + 3TC + EFV (where DTG unavailable) - ABC + 3TC + DTG (if renal issues — check HLA-B*5701 first)

#### When to Start ART > **Start ART in ALL HIV+ patients regardless of CD4 count** (current WHO/BHIVA guidance)

**Start urgently (within days) if:** - CD4 <200 (high OI risk) - HIV-associated TB (with some caveats — see Week 10) - HIV-associated cryptococcal meningitis (delay ART 4–6 weeks to avoid IRIS!) - Pregnancy - HIV nephropathy

#### Monitoring on ART | Parameter | Frequency | |---|---| | Viral load | Baseline, 1 month, 3 months, then every 6 months | | CD4 count | Baseline, then every 6–12 months (less critical once VL undetectable) | | FBC, LFTs, U&E | Baseline, 1 month, then 3–6 monthly | | Fasting lipids/glucose | Baseline, then annually | | HLA-B*5701 | Before starting abacavir | | Tropism test | Before maraviroc |

#### OI Prophylaxis by CD4 Count | CD4 | Prophylaxis | |---|---| | <200 | **Co-trimoxazole** — PCP (*Pneumocystis jirovecii*) + Toxoplasma | | <100 | Co-trimoxazole covers above; consider **fluconazole** if cryptococcus endemic | | <50 | **Azithromycin** — MAC (Mycobacterium avium complex) prophylaxis |

### WEEK 10 — HIV Management (Special Focus) Part 2

#### Treatment Failure

##### Definition - **Virological failure:** VL >200 copies/mL on 2 consecutive measurements after ≥6 months on ART - **Immunological failure:** CD4 falls / fails to rise despite VL suppression - **Clinical failure:** New/recurrent AIDS-defining illness after 6 months on ART

##### Causes — Think Before Switching 1. **Non-adherence** (most common!) — always address first 2. Drug interactions reducing drug levels 3. Resistance (primary or acquired) 4. Pharmacokinetic issues (malabsorption)

> **Medic approach:** Check adherence → check drug levels/interactions → **resistance testing (genotype)** → switch based on resistance profile

#### HIV Drug Resistance

- **Genotypic resistance testing** = standard — identifies mutations in RT, protease, integrase genes - Key mutations to know clinically: - **K103N** → NNRTI class resistance - **M184V** → 3TC/FTC resistance (but increases susceptibility to TDF — can exploit this) - **K65R** → TDF resistance - **Q148H/R/K + G140S** → Raltegravir/Elvitegravir resistance (not DTG usually)

> **DTG has high barrier** — multiple mutations needed before resistance. This is why it's preferred.

#### HIV & TB Co-infection > Extremely common — TB is the leading cause of death in PLHIV globally

##### Management Principles: 1. **Start TB treatment first** 2. **Start ART within 2–8 weeks** of TB treatment (unless TB meningitis — delay to 8 weeks) 3. **Rifampicin interaction** = major problem: - Rifampicin is a **strong CYP3A4 inducer** → dramatically reduces PI and NNRTI levels - **Solution:** Use **Efavirenz** (dose adjust) or **DTG 50mg BD** (instead of once daily) when on rifampicin

> **IRIS (Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome):** Occurs when ART restores immunity → paradoxical worsening of TB. Treat with **NSAIDs/steroids** — don't stop ART or TB treatment.

#### HIV & Cryptococcal Meningitis - Most common: *Cryptococcus neoformans* — CD4 <100 - **Diagnosis:** India ink, cryptococcal antigen (CrAg) — serum or CSF - **Treatment:** - Induction: **Amphotericin B + Flucytosine** × 2 weeks - Consolidation: **Fluconazole 400mg** × 8 weeks - Maintenance: **Fluconazole 200mg** lifelong (until CD4 >200 on ART) - **Delay ART 4–6 weeks** after starting antifungals → starting ART too early = severe IRIS → death

>  Monitor **opening pressure** at LP — raised ICP = poor prognosis. Therapeutic LPs to relieve pressure.

#### HIV & PCP (*Pneumocystis jirovecii* Pneumonia) - CD4 <200; classic presentation: **subacute SOB, dry cough, fever, bilateral interstitial infiltrates on CXR** - O2 sats drop on exertion - **Diagnosis:** BAL (silver stain / immunofluorescence), elevated LDH, β-glucan - **Treatment:** - **Co-trimoxazole (high dose)** × 21 days (1st line) - Add **prednisolone** if PaO₂ <9.3 kPa (reduces inflammation/mortality) - Alternatives: Pentamidine IV, Atovaquone, Clindamycin + primaquine

#### HIV & Toxoplasmosis - CD4 <100; reactivation of latent *T. gondii* - **Presentation:** Focal neurological deficits, seizures, headache, ring-enhancing lesions on CT/MRI - **Distinguish from CNS lymphoma:** Toxo responds to treatment within 2 weeks — use empirical treatment as diagnostic test - **Treatment:** **Pyrimethamine + Sulfadiazine + Folinic acid** × 6 weeks acute, then maintenance until CD4 >200

#### HIV & CMV - CD4 <50 - **CMV retinitis** — most common: floaters, visual loss, "pizza pie" fundus - **CMV colitis/oesophagitis, encephalitis** in severely immunocompromised - **Treatment:** **Ganciclovir IV** (induction) → Valganciclovir oral (maintenance) - SE: Bone marrow suppression — monitor FBC

#### HIV in Special Populations

##### Pregnancy - **All pregnant HIV+ women → start ART immediately** - Preferred: **TDF + FTC + DTG** (or EFV if early pregnancy concern) - **Avoid:** Efavirenz in 1st trimester (historical teratogenicity concern — now DTG preferred), Didanosine+Stavudine (lactic acidosis) - Delivery: If VL <50 → **vaginal delivery safe**; If VL >400 → **elective C-section** - **No breastfeeding** in resource-rich settings (formula available) - Neonatal prophylaxis: **Zidovudine** × 4 weeks to neonate

##### PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis) - **TDF + FTC (Truvada)** once daily — high-risk HIV-negative individuals - Efficacy >99% if taken correctly - Check HIV status before starting + every 3 months - Also check renal function (TDF)

##### PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis) - **Start within 72 hours** (ideally <2hrs), continue **28 days** - Regimen: **TDF + FTC + Raltegravir** (or DTG) - Occupational (needlestick) and non-occupational (sexual exposure)

#### Quick Drug Interaction Summary (Clinically Critical) | Interacting Drug | Effect | Action | |---|---|---| | Rifampicin | ↓ PI and NNRTI levels (CYP inducer) | Use EFV or DTG BD | | Antacids/PPIs | ↓ Rilpivirine, Atazanavir absorption | Avoid or separate by hours | | Statins | ↑ Statin levels with PIs (rhabdomyolysis risk) | Use rosuvastatin (low dose) or pravastatin | | Metformin | ↑ Levels with DTG (same transporter) | Reduce metformin dose; monitor | | Warfarin | Unpredictable (PIs/NNRTIs alter CYP) | Monitor INR closely | | Antiepileptics (carbamazepine, phenytoin) | ↓ ART levels | Avoid or switch AEDs |

#### Key Numbers to Know (Exam + Clinical) | CD4 Count | Significance | |---|---| | <500 | Start ART | | <200 | AIDS diagnosis; start PCP prophylaxis | | <100 | Cryptococcal meningitis risk; Toxoplasma risk | | <50 | CMV, MAC risk | | **Undetectable VL** | Treatment success; U=U |

> 🩺 **Bottom line for a medic:** > - ART = start in everyone, preferably **TDF/TAF + 3TC/FTC + DTG** > - Viral load tells you if it's **working**; CD4 tells you **what infections to watch for** > - Know your OI prophylaxis thresholds > - Know the **Rifampicin-ART interaction** cold > - Cryptococcal meningitis — **delay ART**, not the other way round > - PrEP works; PEP has a 72hr window
