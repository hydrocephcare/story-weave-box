# Cough: Approach to the Patient

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Respiratory Medicine**

> **In one line:** cough is a protective reflex, a forced expulsive manoeuvre against a closed glottis triggered by airway irritation through the vagus nerve. It is classified by duration (acute under 3 weeks, subacute 3 to 8 weeks, chronic over 8 weeks), and the cause is found by a good history, examination, spirometry and, in chronic cough, always a chest X-ray.

## What you must know

- Cough is the **commonest respiratory symptom**; the afferent limb is mainly the **vagus nerve**, the centre is the **medullary nucleus tractus solitarius**, and the reflex has four phases (inspiratory, compressive, expiratory, relaxation).
- **Acute cough (up to 3 weeks):** commonest cause is **viral upper respiratory tract infection**; always look for red flags (pneumonia, PE, heart failure, foreign body).
- **Subacute (3 to 8 weeks):** post-infectious cough, bacterial sinusitis, asthma, or the start of chronic cough.
- **Chronic (8 weeks or more):** mnemonic **GASPS AND COUGH**. Always get a **chest X-ray**.
- Commonest causes of chronic cough in adults: **postnasal drip (upper airway cough syndrome), asthma, GERD, smoking/chronic bronchitis**, and **ACE inhibitors**.
- **ACE-inhibitor cough:** 5 to 20%, onset 1 week to 6 months; switch to an **ARB**.
- A change in the character of a chronic cough suggests a new serious problem (for example lung cancer).

## 1. Definition and importance

- A **forced expulsive manoeuvre, usually against a closed glottis, with a characteristic sound**.
- Caused by stimulation of sensory nerves in the mucosa of the pharynx, larynx, trachea and bronchi.
- A vital **protective mechanism**: protects the airway from pathogens, particles, food and foreign bodies, and clears accumulated secretions and mucus. (It also attracts attention and signals displeasure.)
- Intrathoracic pressure can reach about **300 mmHg** (which can reduce venous return and cerebral perfusion, causing syncope) and expiratory velocity can reach about **500 mph**, which is why infectious coughs spread disease widely.
- **Normal cough is lost** in respiratory muscle paralysis or **vocal cord palsy** (often from lung cancer infiltrating the **left recurrent laryngeal nerve**, giving a "bovine" cough).

**Consequences of an impaired cough reflex:** aspiration of oropharyngeal or gastric contents, acute airway obstruction, pneumonia, lung abscess, respiratory failure/ARDS, bronchiectasis (repeated aspiration), pulmonary fibrosis.

**Complications of cough itself:** headache, dizziness, musculoskeletal pain, syncope, urinary incontinence (not uncommon), rib fracture, pneumothorax, vomiting.

## 2. Mechanism of cough

**Afferent pathway**

- Sensory nerves in the airway mucosa are stimulated by **mechanical stimuli** (trauma, tumour, foreign body), **chemical stimuli** (strong smells, noxious gases, dust), or both (for example regurgitation).
- The **vagus** is the major afferent; others are the glossopharyngeal, phrenic and trigeminal nerves.
- Stimuli arise from the ear, pharynx, larynx, lungs, tracheobronchial tree, heart, pericardium and oesophagus. Impulses travel to the **cough centre (nucleus tractus solitarius) in the medulla**.

**Efferent pathway** (to respiratory muscles, laryngeal muscles and bronchial smooth muscle) has four phases:

1. **Inspiratory:** deep inspiratory gasp.
2. **Compressive:** glottis closes and respiratory muscles contract (Valsalva), building high intrathoracic pressure. Patients with paralysed vocal cords cannot do this well.
3. **Expiratory:** the glottis opens and air is expelled in a blast; needs working expiratory muscles.
4. **Relaxation:** prolonged post-tussive inspiration.

## 3. Classification and causes

### Acute cough (3 weeks or less)

**Causes**

- **Upper respiratory tract infection** (viral syndromes, viral or bacterial sinusitis with postnasal drip; also triggers exacerbations of asthma and COPD): **commonest**.
- **Pneumonia** (acute infection, fever).
- **Left ventricular failure.**
- **Foreign body aspiration.**
- **Pulmonary embolism.**
- Throat clearing from laryngitis or pharyngitis.

**Epidemiology of URTI:** 2 to 5 episodes a year in adults and 7 to 10 in children; 40 to 50% have cough, about 20% consult a doctor; self-medication is common; resolves within 2 weeks.

**Approach**

- Identify high-risk patients: acute cough can be the first sign of serious disease (lung cancer, **TB**, foreign body, allergy, interstitial lung disease); chronic cough is always preceded by acute cough. The main concern in Kenya is **TB** (so the patient does not infect others).
- Look for red flags (haemoptysis, breathlessness, chest pain, fever with focal signs, weight loss, night sweats, hoarseness, immunosuppression, HIV).

**Treatment of simple acute cough:** benign course; reassure. Voluntary suppression, linctuses and drinks only soothe the throat; the aim is to treat the underlying cause. If that fails, antitussives:

| Drug | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Dextromethorphan** | Opioid derivative, non-drowsy (for example Benylin); very effective at high dose (60 mg); beware combination products containing paracetamol |
| Menthol / steam inhalation | Acts on cough-reflex receptors; short-lived |
| Sedating antihistamines | Cause drowsiness; suppress the medullary cough centre; used at night |
| **Codeine or pholcodine** | Not better than dextromethorphan, more side effects, not recommended for simple cough; prescription only, mainly for patients with cancer or severe cough |

### Subacute cough (3 to 8 weeks)

- **Post-infectious cough:** begins with an acute respiratory infection without pneumonia (normal chest examination and CXR); resolves without treatment. Viruses are cytolytic and damage the airway epithelium, which takes time to recover. Caused by postnasal drip or tracheobronchitis.
- Also: bacterial sinusitis, asthma, and the start of chronic cough. Do not miss lung cancer or other serious disease.
- **Actions:** examine the chest; chest X-ray if there are signs or the patient smokes; measure airflow obstruction (one-off peak flow, serial peak flow, or spirometry).

### Chronic cough (8 weeks or more)

- Always begins as acute cough. **FEV1/FVC under 70%** indicates airflow obstruction (for example COPD); a high or preserved ratio with low volumes suggests **restriction** (for example interstitial lung disease or pulmonary fibrosis).
- **Triggers:** temperature change, scents, sprays, aerosols, exercise indicate raised cough-reflex sensitivity, which is not only seen in asthma but also in GERD, pertussis and ACE-inhibitor use.
- **Associations:** smoking (dose-related), pollutants (PM10), occupational and environmental irritants (for example cat dander), asthma, reflux, obesity, IBS in females.

**Mnemonic: GASPS AND COUGH**

| Letter | Cause |
|---|---|
| **G** | Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease |
| **A** | Asthma |
| **S** | Smoking / chronic bronchitis |
| **P** | Post-infection |
| **S** | Sinusitis / postnasal drip |
| **A** | ACE inhibitor |
| **N** | Neoplasm / lower airway lesion |
| **D** | Diverticulum (oesophageal) |
| **C** | Congestive heart failure |
| **O** | Outer ear |
| **U** | Upper airway obstruction |
| **G** | GI-airway fistula |
| **H** | Hypersensitivity / allergy |

> **Remember:** also consider **cough-variant asthma** (cough as the only asthma symptom), pertussis, TB, bronchiectasis and interstitial lung disease.

**Investigation of chronic cough**

- Purpose: exclude structural disease and identify the cause.
- Method: history and examination (including **occupation and exposures**) and **spirometry**. **Always get a chest X-ray in chronic cough.**

| Normal chest X-ray | Abnormal chest X-ray |
|---|---|
| GERD, postnasal drip, smoker's cough/chronic bronchitis, asthma, COPD and bronchiectasis (these can also be abnormal), radiolucent foreign body | Left ventricular failure, lung cancer, infection/TB, pulmonary fibrosis, pleural effusion |

**Key causes in more detail**

- **ACE inhibitors (non-structural):** incidence 5 to 20%; onset from one week to six months (the patient may not remember when it started). Mechanism: ACE normally breaks down **bradykinin and substance P**; inhibition lets them accumulate in the airway and stimulate cough. **Treatment: switch to an ARB** (for cardiac or renal indications); for hypertension alone, use any other antihypertensive.
- **Postnasal drip (upper airway cough syndrome):** nasal congestion or discharge, throat clearing, a sensation of drip, cobblestoned pharynx.
- **Lung disease (including tobacco):** favoured by breathlessness, wheeze, sputum, haemoptysis and abnormal chest signs (crackles).
- **GERD:** alone or combined accounts for **10 to 40%** of chronic cough. Mechanisms: micro-aspiration into the larynx/trachea, and acid in the distal oesophagus stimulating the vagus and cough reflex. Many have **no heartburn**. Needs ambulatory oesophageal pH monitoring or a prolonged trial of anti-reflux therapy.

## 4. Taking a history in a patient with cough

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Onset | Recent or long-standing |
| Duration | Chronicity: pertussis, TB, foreign body, asthma, drugs, bronchiectasis, ILD |
| Character | **Brassy** (loud, metallic, barking, subglottic oedema, croup, tracheal pressure); a change in a chronic cough suggests new pathology |
| Timing | **Nocturnal / early morning (about midnight to 3 a.m.) suggests asthma**; reflux cough appears later when the patient wakes or lies down |
| Precipitants | Emotion, weather (wind, rain, cold), dust, allergens, exercise, drugs |
| Relieving factors | Avoiding triggers |
| Sputum | Colour, volume, consistency, pattern (see below) |
| Haemoptysis | Colour, volume, consistency, pattern |
| Associated features | Breathlessness, chest pain, wheeze, hoarseness, postnasal drip, fever, weight loss |

**Sputum clues**

- Large-volume purulent (green or yellow) sputum with chronic cough: **bronchiectasis** or lobar pneumonia.
- **Foul-smelling, dark** sputum: **lung abscess** with anaerobes.
- **Pink frothy** secretion: **pulmonary oedema**.
- Rusty sputum: pneumococcal pneumonia. Red currant jelly: *Klebsiella*.

**Mnemonic for a productive cough (BPRODUCTIVE):** **B**lood in sputum? **P**roductive "phlegm"? **R**ecent travel? **O**dour? **D**uration? **U**RI symptoms (runny nose, conjunctivitis, sore throat, congestion)? **C**olour of sputum? **T**B exposure (last PPD, night sweats, weight loss)? **I**nfection? **V**olume (teaspoons)? **E**nergy (fatigue)?

**Practical points:** offer a cup of water or a tissue to a coughing patient; ask to see the tissue for blood; ask about past attempts to quit smoking and educate.

## 5. Practice questions (answers)

1. Commonest cause of acute cough: **URTIs (b)**.
2. 30-year-old with 5 days of cough, breathlessness, chest pain and crackles: first step **chest X-ray (a)**.
3. Dry cough after starting an ACE inhibitor: **switch to an ARB (b)**.
4. Chronic cough with a normal X-ray: **asthma (a)**.
5. Sudden non-productive cough and breathlessness with a clear chest: **pulmonary embolism (a)**.
6. Cough worse when lying flat: **gastro-oesophageal reflux (c)**.
7. Commonest cause of chronic slightly productive cough in adults: **chronic bronchial inflammation (b)**.

> **Exam tip:** chronic cough with a normal CXR: think GERD, postnasal drip, asthma, ACE inhibitor. Chronic cough with abnormal CXR: think TB, cancer, LVF, fibrosis, effusion.

## Key points to revise

1. Cough is a protective reflex: vagal afferents, medullary cough centre, four phases (inspiration, compression, expulsion, relaxation).
2. Acute cough is under 3 weeks (usually viral URTI); subacute 3 to 8 weeks (often post-infectious); chronic 8 weeks or more.
3. Red flags and TB must be considered in any persistent cough.
4. Chronic cough: GASPS AND COUGH; commonest are postnasal drip, asthma, GERD and smoking.
5. ACE-inhibitor cough (bradykinin accumulation) occurs in 5 to 20%; change to an ARB.
6. Always get a chest X-ray and spirometry in chronic cough.
7. Sputum character and timing of cough give diagnostic clues (nocturnal means asthma; foul sputum means abscess; pink frothy means pulmonary oedema).
8. Treat the cause first; antitussives (dextromethorphan) are second-line, codeine is rarely recommended.
