# Congenital Anomalies of the Urinary Tract in Children

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Paediatric urology and nephrology**

> **In one line:** the kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra develop together with the genital system, so errors of formation, ascent, rotation, drainage or canalisation produce a spectrum of anomalies, most now detected on **antenatal ultrasound**. The aims are to find **obstruction and reflux early**, preserve renal function and prevent infection.

## What you must know

- **Unilateral renal agenesis** is usually asymptomatic (the other kidney hypertrophies), but needs lifelong monitoring of **blood pressure and urine protein**; **bilateral agenesis** causes oligohydramnios, **Potter sequence** and pulmonary hypoplasia.
- **Horseshoe kidney** is the commonest fusion anomaly (1 in 400); **simple (pelvic) ectopia** is the commonest ectopia.
- **PUJ obstruction** is the commonest cause of antenatal hydronephrosis (41%) and the commonest congenital obstruction in a single system; ureter is **not** dilated.
- **Ectopic ureter** and **ureterocele** usually arise from the **upper pole** of a duplex system (**Weigert-Meyer rule**); girls leak continuously, boys get epididymitis.
- **VUR** is graded I to V on **MCUG**; **reflux plus infection** is the dangerous combination.
- **Posterior urethral valves (PUV)** are an emergency in male infants: poor stream, bladder outlet obstruction, renal impairment.
- **Hypospadias**: do **not circumcise**; repair at 6 to 9 months.
- Urachal remnants should be excised if they do not resolve (risk of **adenocarcinoma**).

## 1. Overview and approach

Congenital urinary disorders divide into:

| Upper tract | Lower tract |
|---|---|
| Kidney (agenesis, supernumerary, ectopia, fusion, malrotation, MCDK) | Bladder (urachal anomalies, diverticulum, duplication, exstrophy) |
| Ureter (obstruction, ectopic ureter, ureterocele, VUR, duplication) | Urethra (PUV, hypospadias, epispadias) |

The urinary and genital systems develop at the same time from related embryonic structures. **An anomaly in one system should prompt a search for an anomaly in the other** (for example a boy with renal agenesis may have absent vas or seminal vesicle on that side).

Most anomalies are found on routine antenatal ultrasound (about 22 and 32 weeks). Missed cases present later with UTI, abdominal or loin pain, haematuria, a mass or stones.

## 2. Anomalies of the kidney

### 2.1 Renal agenesis

Absence of the kidney. Associated with syndromes such as DiGeorge, Fraser, Kallmann, trisomy C and D and cat-eye syndrome.

**Unilateral renal agenesis (URA)**

- About **1 in 1100** births; male to female **1.8 : 1**; the **left** is absent more often.
- The ipsilateral ureter is completely absent in 50%.
- Other organ systems (cardiovascular, GI, musculoskeletal) are often involved.
- The ipsilateral adrenal is **not necessarily absent**, because it has a different embryology.
- **Genital link:** Müllerian duct anomalies in **25 to 50% of females** with URA; Wolffian duct anomalies in **10 to 15% of males**. About one quarter to one third of women with Müllerian anomalies have URA.
- Usually **asymptomatic and found incidentally** on ultrasound. The remaining kidney undergoes compensatory hypertrophy with **hyperfiltration**, so life is normal except that **severe contact sports** are discouraged.
- Clinical significance lies in: (1) problems of the contralateral kidney (obstruction, renal insufficiency, UTI); (2) associated genital anomalies; (3) trauma or other injury to the solitary kidney.
- **Follow-up:** annual **blood pressure** (hyperfiltration causes hypertension) and **urine dipstick** for proteinuria.

**Confirming absence:** first exclude **renal ectopia**. CT often fails to show a kidney, so use **DMSA** or MRI. Renal length measurement helps.

| Isotope renal scan | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Static (DMSA)** | Anatomy, shape, function; confirms absent or ectopic kidney |
| **Dynamic (MAG3/DTPA renogram)** | Drainage, i.e. obstruction |

> **Check:** DMSA is also used for pyelonephritis scarring; its sensitivity for acute pyelonephritis may be as low as 46%.

**Bilateral renal agenesis (BRA)**

- Likely inherited in an autosomal dominant manner in some families.
- After about 16 weeks **amniotic fluid is mainly fetal urine**. No kidneys means no urine, hence **oligohydramnios**. The uterus compresses the fetus and the lungs fail to develop (**pulmonary hypoplasia**).
- **Potter sequence:** sloping forehead, "parrot beak" nose, flattened face and low-set ears, shortened fingers, hypoplasia of internal organs (lungs, gut).
- 40% are stillborn; most die within 24 to 48 hours from **respiratory failure**, not from the absent kidneys. With modern intensive care a few survive and need dialysis and transplantation.
- Ureters nearly always absent; bladder absent or hypoplastic; adrenals usually normal; Müllerian anomalies common.

### 2.2 Supernumerary kidney

A true extra kidney with its **own collecting system, blood supply and encapsulated parenchyma**, either separate or loosely attached to the normal kidney on the same side. Ureteric relationships vary.

### 2.3 Anomalies of ascent, form and fusion

During ascent from the pelvis to the upper lumbar region the kidney **rotates medially by 90 degrees** (hilum moves from facing forwards to facing medially, calyces point laterally). Segmental vessels are added above and lost below. The kidneys meet the adrenal glands, which develop in situ.

| Anomaly | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Simple renal ectopia** | Kidney lies in the wrong place (pelvic, lower lumbar, sacral); **pelvic is commonest**; left more than right; pelvic ectopia about 1 in 2100 to 3000 autopsies. About **50% have hydronephrosis** (PUJ or VUJ obstruction, reflux grade III or more, malrotation). VUR in 30%; genital anomalies about 15%. Mostly asymptomatic, found by chance or with vague abdominal pain |
| **Cephalad / thoracic ectopia** | Kidney sits too high, even in the chest |
| **Crossed renal ectopia** | Kidney lies on the side opposite to where its ureter enters the bladder (ureter crosses the midline; CT shows this). **90% are fused** with the other kidney, usually the ectopic upper pole joining the lower pole of the normal one. Ureters are usually orthotopic |
| **Horseshoe kidney** | Fusion of the **lower poles in 90%** (upper in 10%). About **1 in 400**. The **isthmus** is bulky parenchyma. Calyces are normal in number but the pelvis stays vertical/anterior (malrotated). The isthmus is caught under the **inferior mesenteric artery**, so the kidney cannot ascend fully. **Variable multiple arterial supply** (do not ligate a single artery at surgery). **PUJ obstruction in about one third**; 60% asymptomatic. Associated with other congenital anomalies, notably **Turner syndrome** |
| **Malrotation** | Failure of the 90-degree rotation. Frequently seen in **Turner syndrome**. Usually asymptomatic |

> **Remember:** treat the complication (hydronephrosis, UTI, reflux, stones), **not the ectopic or malrotated kidney itself**.

**Multicystic dysplastic kidney (MCDK):** the kidney is replaced by cysts with **no renal pelvis and no communication between cysts and calyces**. Compare this with hydronephrosis from PUJ obstruction, where a dilated pelvis communicates with dilated calyces (the "Mickey Mouse" or communicating pattern). Most MCDKs involute; the contralateral kidney should be checked.

## 3. Anomalies of the ureter

**Presentation:** incidental in neonates and children (antenatal scan), or symptomatic with UTI, pain, mass, haematuria or stones. Causes may be intrinsic (segment not formed) or extrinsic (crossing vessels).

### 3.1 Obstruction (PUJO and VUJO)

- **Ultrasound** is the first test. **PUJO:** dilated renal pelvis communicating with dilated calyces; **ureter not dilated** (everything distal to the block is normal). **VUJO:** dilated pelvis **and** dilated ureter.
- About 80% of antenatally detected hydronephrosis resolves spontaneously.
- **Confirm with a renogram:** static (DMSA) for anatomy and function, dynamic for drainage. On a diuretic renogram a curve that **falls** after diuretic means no obstruction; isotope **retained** in the kidney means obstruction.
- **Treat if:** loin pain or UTI, increasing hydronephrosis on ultrasound, reduced function, or obstruction on the renogram.
- **Surgery:** **dismembered pyeloplasty** for PUJO (open or laparoscopic); **ureteric reimplantation** for VUJO (disconnect the ureter and reinsert it into the bladder).

### 3.2 Ectopic ureter

A ureter (single or duplex) that does **not** enter the bladder at the trigone. Orthotopic means inside the bladder; ectopic means outside.

| | Male | Female |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Always opens **above the external sphincter**: bladder neck, posterior urethra, vas deferens, seminal vesicle, ejaculatory duct | Anywhere from bladder neck to perineum: urethra, **vagina**, uterus, even rectum (distal to the sphincter) |
| Presentation | Recurrent infections, **epididymitis** | **Continuous urinary incontinence** (continuous wetting) with normal voiding |

In a **duplex system** the ectopic ureter is **always from the upper pole**, because its bud arises later and higher from the mesonephric duct (Weigert-Meyer rule, section 3.5).

### 3.3 Ureterocele

A cystic dilatation of the lower end of the ureter, within the bladder or straddling the bladder neck and urethra.

- **Intravesical (orthotopic):** simple, single system, "adult type".
- **Extravesical (ectopic):** associated with a **duplex system**, "infant type". About **80% occur with duplication**.
- Presentation: antenatal ultrasound, urinary retention, infection, stones. Girls and left side more often.
- A cystic mass in the bladder means: look at the upper tract for duplication.
- **Treatment:** cystoscopic **puncture** (decompression) in the newborn, because the neonatal bladder capacity (about 50 mL) is too small for reimplantation. Definitive treatment is decided later (heminephrectomy or reimplantation).

### 3.4 Vesicoureteric reflux (VUR)

**Antireflux mechanism ("flap valve"):** oblique course through the bladder wall, good muscular attachments for fixation, posterior (detrusor) support to allow occlusion, and adequate submucosal tunnel length.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Presentation | Asymptomatic, antenatal dilatation, **febrile UTI** |
| Diagnosis | **MCUG (VCUG)**: contrast in the bladder is seen to pass up into the ureter or kidney |
| Why it matters | Recurrent infection causes **pyelonephritis, scarring, loss of renal function**; reflux with infection is the most dangerous |

**MCUG grading**

| Grade | Finding |
|---|---|
| I | Contrast in the ureter only |
| II | Reaches the kidney, no dilatation |
| III | Mild dilatation of pelvis and ureter, calyces preserved |
| IV | Moderate dilatation with loss of calyceal sharpness |
| V | Severe dilatation, tortuous ureter |

**Management**

1. **Prophylactic antibiotic** once daily at about **one third of the treatment dose**, choosing an agent concentrated in urine.
2. **Endoscopic injection** (bulking agent) is the first step before open surgery, since surgery carries morbidity.
3. **Ureteric reimplantation** if: **breakthrough infection** on prophylaxis, **high-grade reflux (IV and V)**, or upper tract damage (for example DMSA function below 40%).

### 3.5 Duplex system and the Weigert-Meyer rule

A complete duplex has two moieties, each with its own pelvis and ureter; commoner in **females**. Most are uncomplicated and need no treatment unless the upper or lower moiety is affected.

| | Upper pole moiety (UPM) | Lower pole moiety (LPM) |
|---|---|---|
| Ureter insertion | **More distal and medial** (longer course) | **More proximal and lateral** (shorter) |
| Problems | **Ectopic ureter, ureterocele**, obstruction, hydroureter | **VUR** (commonest abnormality in duplex), PUJO |
| Imaging | Ultrasound: hydronephrotic UPM with tortuous hydroureter; VCUG for recurrent UTI | VCUG: **"drooping lily"** appearance of the collecting system |
| Treatment | **UPM heminephrectomy** removing poorly functioning moiety and as much ureter as possible | Prophylaxis; reimplantation or heminephrectomy of LPM |

Both present with recurrent UTI, hydronephrosis and flank pain. UPM ectopic ureter also causes incontinence in girls and epididymitis in boys.

> **Exam tip:** Weigert-Meyer: **U**pper pole = **U**reterocele/ectopic (inserts lower down); **L**ower pole = **L**eaking (reflux).

**PUJO** is the commonest congenital obstruction in a single system, often from crossing vessels of the renal pedicle. The ureter is not dilated unless there is high-grade VUR. A **diuretic renal scan** assesses obstruction; significant obstruction with reduced function needs **pyeloplasty** (surgical reconstruction of the renal pelvis, open or laparoscopic).

### 3.6 Antenatal hydronephrosis (ANH): causes

| Cause | Approximate share |
|---|---|
| PUJ obstruction | 41% |
| VUJ obstruction | 23% |
| Duplication anomalies | 13% |
| Posterior urethral valves | 10% |
| VUR | 7% |
| Others | 6% |
| Also: MCDK | |

Severity is graded by the Society for Fetal Urology (SFU) system.

## 4. Anomalies of the bladder

### 4.1 Urachal anomalies

The urachus is the embryonic channel between the bladder and umbilicus; it normally closes.

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Patent urachus** | Open throughout; urine drains from the umbilicus |
| **Urachal cyst** | Closed at both ends, cyst in between |
| **Umbilical-urachal sinus** | Open at the umbilicus only |
| **Vesicourachal diverticulum** | Open at the bladder only |

Usually found after birth because of **umbilical drainage**. Imaging: ultrasound, CT, VCUG.

**Treatment:** asymptomatic, observe (may resolve); infected, drain and give antibiotics then excise; persistent remnants, excise because of the risk of later **adenocarcinoma**.

### 4.2 Bladder diverticulum

- A pouch of bladder mucosa; can be seen on prenatal ultrasound. **VCUG is the gold standard** and shows associated VUR.
- **Primary:** congenitally weak bladder wall, herniation at the ureteral hiatus (para-ureteric).
- **Secondary:** acquired, due to infravesical obstruction.
- Symptomatic or very large diverticula, especially with VUR, are excised.

### 4.3 Bladder duplication

Often with duplication of external genitalia and lower GI tract (two bladders, each with its own ureter, one or two urethras). Aims: preserve the kidneys, prevent infection, then achieve continence and reconstruct genitalia. Treatment is individualised because of rarity and variety.

### 4.4 Exstrophy

Incidence 1 in 10,000 to 50,000. Normally the bladder is covered by skin, subcutaneous tissue, abdominal wall layers and peritoneum; in exstrophy these are **absent**, so the **posterior bladder wall is visible on the abdomen**.

| | Classic bladder exstrophy | Cloacal exstrophy |
|---|---|---|
| Feature | Open bladder; **anus is present** | Urinary and GI tracts open together; **no anus** |

(For exams, knowing the diagnosis is enough.)

## 5. Anomalies of the urethra

### Posterior urethral valves (PUV)

- Incomplete canalisation of the urethra leaves a **membrane (valve)** in the posterior urethra that obstructs outflow. **Males only. Emergency.**
- Incidence 1 in 8000 to 25,000 live births; 10% of antenatally diagnosed urinary obstruction; **commonest cause of urinary retention in male infants**.
- The bladder and kidneys develop under high pressure; **50% have renal impairment**.

| Presentation | Associated findings |
|---|---|
| Antenatal hydronephrosis, **oligohydramnios** (no urine output, so low amniotic fluid), urine retention, UTI, **poor stream**, incontinence | **VUR in 40%**; **CKD/ESRD in 40%**; bilateral renal dilatation; thick "valve bladder"; renal impairment |

> **Remember:** oligohydramnios means urine is not being made or not leaving; polyhydramnios means the fetus cannot swallow (for example oesophageal obstruction), so fluid is not absorbed.

**Investigation:** ultrasound (bilateral hydroureteronephrosis, dilated posterior urethra, thick trabeculated "**Christmas tree**" bladder); **MCUG** (filling defect, dilated posterior urethra).

**Management**

1. Initial: catheter/feeding tube drainage of the bladder, start prophylactic antibiotics, monitor renal function and electrolytes.
2. **Endoscopic valve ablation** soon after birth.
3. **Cutaneous vesicostomy** if preterm, low birth weight, azotemia or severe infection (the original text says "vasectomy", which is an error).

## 6. Congenital genital disorders

> **Exam tip:** be on the safe side and **delay circumcision** in any boy with an abnormal foreskin or meatus.

| | Hypospadias | Epispadias |
|---|---|---|
| Defect | External urethral meatus on the **ventral** surface | Meatus on the **dorsal** surface |
| Direction | Opens towards the scrotum | Opens towards the abdomen |
| Types | Distal (mid-shaft to glans); proximal (proximal shaft to perineum) | Commonest is **penopubic**, opening near the bladder neck, with **urinary incontinence** |
| Severity | Common | **More serious** |
| Key rule | **No circumcision**: the foreskin is needed for reconstruction | |
| Repair | Age **6 to 9 months** | |

## 7. Other syndromes

**Prune-belly (Eagle-Barrett, triad) syndrome:** 1 in 29,000 to 40,000 live births. Triad: **deficient abdominal wall musculature**, **bilateral intra-abdominal (undescended) testes**, and **anomalous urinary tract** (bilateral hydroureteronephrosis).

**Neurospinal dysraphism:** the commonest cause of **neurogenic bladder** in children (abnormal spinal canal and cord development). Cutaneous markers in about 90%: lipomeningocele, hair patch, dermal vascular malformation, sacral dimple, abnormal gluteal cleft. The bladder nerves are usually affected.

## Key points to revise

1. Unilateral renal agenesis: compensate by hypertrophy; check BP and urine protein yearly; look for genital anomalies.
2. Bilateral renal agenesis gives oligohydramnios, Potter sequence and death from pulmonary hypoplasia.
3. Horseshoe kidney: lower-pole fusion, isthmus held by the IMA, PUJO in one third.
4. PUJO is the commonest cause of antenatal hydronephrosis; ureter not dilated; diagnose with ultrasound and renogram, treat with pyeloplasty.
5. Weigert-Meyer: upper pole gives ectopic ureter/ureterocele; lower pole gives reflux.
6. VUR: graded I to V on MCUG; prophylaxis, then endoscopic, then reimplantation for breakthrough infection, grade IV to V or scarring.
7. PUV: male newborn emergency, Christmas tree bladder, treat with catheter then endoscopic ablation or vesicostomy.
8. Hypospadias: ventral meatus, no circumcision, repair at 6 to 9 months.
