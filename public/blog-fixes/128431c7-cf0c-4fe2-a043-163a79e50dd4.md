# Acute and Chronic Leukaemia

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Haematology**

> **In one line:** leukaemias are clonal malignancies of blood-forming tissue in which regulation of cell division is lost and dysfunctional cells accumulate. They are divided into **acute** (immature blasts, over 20% in blood or marrow, sudden onset, marrow failure) and **chronic** (mature cells, insidious onset, organomegaly), and into **myeloid** and **lymphoid** types: AML, ALL, CML and CLL.

## What you must know

- Four main types: **ALL, AML** (acute) and **CLL, CML** (chronic).
- **ALL** is the commonest leukaemia in children; **AML** is the commonest acute leukaemia in adults (about 85%); CLL and CML are diseases of adults.
- Acute leukaemia is fatal if untreated and diagnosed by **over 20% blasts** in peripheral blood or marrow.
- **Auer rods** = AML. **Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22), BCR-ABL** = CML. CLL: mature B cells (CD20 positive).
- Presentation of acute leukaemia is explained by **marrow failure** (anaemia, bleeding, infection) plus **infiltration** (organomegaly, bone pain, CNS and testis in ALL).
- **Best initial test:** peripheral blood smear. **Most accurate for subtype:** flow cytometry. **Marrow biopsy is always needed.**
- Prognosis in acute leukaemia depends chiefly on **age and cytogenetics**.
- ALL needs **CNS prophylaxis** and **maintenance therapy**; AML does not.
- Hyperleucocytosis (AML, WBC over 70 000) needs **leukapheresis** for leukostasis.

## 1. Definition and general features

- A group of malignant disorders of blood and blood-forming tissues (bone marrow, lymphatic system, spleen).
- Loss of regulation of cell division leads to accumulation of dysfunctional cells.
- Occurs at all ages; **ALL is commonest in children**; males more than females (1 to 2 : 1).
- Often thought of as a childhood disease, but **adults are affected 10 times more often than children**. Children almost always have acute forms (chronic leukaemia in a child is extremely unlikely).
- Acute leukaemia presents as an emergency; chronic leukaemia is often found incidentally in clinic.

## 2. Aetiology and risk factors

The cause is unknown; it may involve oncogene mutation, tumour suppressor alteration, host and environmental factors.

### Host factors

| Factor | Details |
|---|---|
| Congenital chromosomal abnormalities | **Bloom syndrome, Fanconi anaemia, Down syndrome** (30-fold higher incidence of acute leukaemia), **Klinefelter syndrome** |
| Immunodeficiency | Higher risk of lymphoid leukaemia and lymphoma (ataxia-telangiectasia, X-linked agammaglobulinaemia), related to T- and B-cell gene rearrangement |
| Chronic marrow dysfunction | Myelodysplastic and myeloproliferative syndromes carry a risk of **acute transformation** |

### Environmental factors

| Factor | Details |
|---|---|
| **Ionising radiation** | Hiroshima and Nagasaki survivors developed AML, ALL and CML. Be careful with radiation exposure in children (dental and other X-rays) |
| **Chemicals and drugs** | **Benzene**, chloramphenicol, phenylbutazone, **alkylating agents** (AML after melphalan). RAS mutations in AML linked to occupational chemical exposure |
| **Viruses** | **HTLV-I** causes adult T-cell leukaemia-lymphoma; HTLV-II has been isolated from atypical hairy cell leukaemia; **EBV** is linked to Burkitt lymphoma |

## 3. Comparison of acute and chronic leukaemia

| Feature | Acute | Chronic |
|---|---|---|
| Types | ALL, AML | CLL, CML |
| Age | All ages | Adults (usually older) |
| Onset | Sudden | Insidious |
| Cells | **Immature blasts** (large; over 20% in blood or marrow); clonal proliferation of immature haematopoietic cells | Mature WBC forms |
| Anaemia | Mild to severe | Mild |
| Thrombocytopenia | Mild to severe | Mild |
| WBC count | Variable | Increased |
| Organomegaly | Mild | Prominent |

> **Remember:** **myelogenous** leukaemia means proliferation of myeloid tissue (marrow, spleen) with granulocytes, myelocytes and myeloblasts in the circulation. These cells are normally confined to the marrow, so seeing them in **circulating blood** is abnormal. **Always examine the peripheral blood smear first.**

## 4. Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML)

- Uncontrolled proliferation of **myeloblasts** with hyperplasia of marrow and spleen.
- About **25% of all leukaemias** and **85% of adult acute leukaemias**; the predominant leukaemia in the neonatal period (as stated in the lecture).
- **Abrupt, dramatic onset** (serious infection, abnormal bleeding), over weeks to months.

### Clinical features

- **Pancytopenia** (marrow is crowded out by abnormal cells and normal elements are not made): weakness, fatigue, breathlessness, infections, gum bleeding, ecchymoses, epistaxis, menorrhagia.
- Infrequent bone pain (sternum, long bones).
- Fever is nearly always from infection (a small minority is from the leukaemia itself).
- Skin: pallor, petechiae, ecchymoses, infiltrates (**leukaemia cutis**, **granulocytic sarcoma / chloroma**).
- Eye: retinal haemorrhages or exudates, pale conjunctivae.
- Oropharynx: **gingival hypertrophy** (especially acute monoblastic leukaemia), candidiasis, herpetic lesions.
- Joints: arthralgia, bone tenderness.
- **Leukostasis**: WBC over 70 000 can give cerebral, retinal or pulmonary haemorrhage because myeloid cells occlude vessels (patients may present blind). Treat with **leukapheresis**.

### History to take

Prior myelodysplastic syndrome, myeloproliferative disorder, Fanconi anaemia; prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy; cardiac disease; previous transfusions or pregnancies; drug allergies; HSV history.

### WHO classification of AML

1. AML with recurrent genetic abnormalities
2. AML with multilineage dysplasia
3. Therapy-related AML and MDS
4. AML not otherwise categorised

**Auer rods** (and Sudan black B positivity, hypogranular neutrophils) support AML.

## 5. Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL)

- **Commonest leukaemia in children**; about 15% of adult acute leukaemias.
- Proliferation of immature lymphocytes in the marrow.

### Aetiology

- Genetic syndromes: **Down syndrome** (ALL and AML), Bloom syndrome, neurofibromatosis, Shwachman syndrome, ataxia-telangiectasia, Klinefelter syndrome.
- *In utero* exposure: ionising radiation; topoisomerase II inhibitors linked to MLL rearrangement (quinolones, flavonoids, catechins, podophyllin, benzene metabolites, oestrogens, dipyrone, the mosquitocide Baygon).

### Clinical features

- Marrow failure: anaemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia; infection and fever.
- Lymphadenopathy, hepatomegaly, splenomegaly.
- Bone pain and arthralgia (especially in children).
- **Extramedullary spread:** CNS at diagnosis (about 5% of children, 15% of adults), skin, **testes (10 to 15% of boys)**.
- Mediastinal mass (lymphoblastic lymphoma) or tissue mass (about 50% of T-cell ALL).
- Onset can be abrupt (fever, bleeding) or insidious (weakness, fatigue).
- A teenager with fever, malaise, weight loss and a lymphocyte count of 100 000 should have a **peripheral smear**: lymphoblasts (they do not usually occlude vessels).

### Laboratory

- High uric acid and LDH; **tumour lysis**: hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia. Hypercalcaemia can occur from infiltration or a PTH-like substance.

> **Exam tip:** **CNS prophylaxis** (intrathecal therapy) is needed in ALL but not in AML; this is a major difference in treatment. Lymphoblasts reach "sanctuary" sites such as the brain and testes, whereas myeloid cells usually do not, so AML is treated with systemic chemotherapy.

## 6. Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML)

- Excessive growth of **mature neoplastic granulocytes** in the marrow, which spill into the blood in massive numbers and infiltrate the **liver and spleen**.
- Chronic stable phase, then an **acute aggressive (blastic) phase** with very poor prognosis.
- **Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22)** produces the **BCR-ABL fusion gene** (a chimeric mRNA); it is the genetic marker and is diagnostic (if absent, the diagnosis of CML is doubtful).
- The one leukaemia that can be detected early in its course.
- Typical features: massive splenomegaly, high WBC with all stages of granulocytes, weight loss, sweats.

> **Check:** the lecture does not give CML treatment; standard first-line treatment is a **BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase inhibitor (imatinib)**.

## 7. Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL)

- Production and accumulation of **functionally inactive but long-lived (failed apoptosis), mature-appearing B lymphocytes** (CD20 positive; CD5 and CD23 also characteristic).
- Widespread **lymph node enlargement**; increased infections.
- Complications of early CLL are rare: **watch and wait, no treatment** until complications arise.
- Later: pain or paralysis from lymph nodes pressing on structures.
- Chronic disease may transform into an acute form (poor prognosis).

### Hairy cell leukaemia

- Clonal proliferation of abnormal B cells accumulating in marrow and spleen; about 2% of adult leukaemias, usually males over 40.
- B lymphocytes infiltrate marrow and liver; cells have a **hairy appearance**.
- Massive splenomegaly causes pancytopenia, infection and vasculitis.
- Treatment: alpha-interferon, **pentostatin, cladribine**.

## 8. Unclassified leukaemia

Subtype cannot be identified; cells may be lymphoid, myeloid or mixed; response to treatment is often poor.

## 9. Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Key point |
|---|---|
| **Aplastic anaemia** | Marrow fails to produce all cell lines (hypocellular marrow, pancytopenia, no organomegaly) |
| **Myelodysplastic syndrome** | Marrow makes cells but they are abnormal in shape and function |
| Multiple myeloma | |
| Lymphoma | |
| Severe megaloblastic anaemia | |
| **Leukaemoid reaction** (e.g. sepsis) | Very high WBC but no excess blasts |

> **Remember:** **blasts over 20%** in blood or marrow excludes these and confirms acute leukaemia. You cannot diagnose leukaemia without a peripheral blood evaluation.

> **Check:** the lecture implies red cells are "maintained" in aplastic anaemia; in fact red cells fall too (anaemia, with reticulocytopenia).

## 10. Clinical manifestations: summary

| Due to marrow failure | Due to infiltration |
|---|---|
| Overcrowding by abnormal cells and inadequate production of normal elements: anaemia, thrombocytopenia, reduced number and function of WBCs | Splenomegaly, hepatomegaly, lymphadenopathy, bone pain, meningeal irritation (ALL), oral lesions, **chloromas** (solid collections of leukaemic cells outside the marrow) |

## 11. Diagnosis

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Peripheral blood CBC and smear** | **Best initial test**; first step in any haematological disease; look for blasts |
| **Bone marrow aspirate / biopsy** | Increased cellularity, reduced erythropoiesis and megakaryocytes, replacement by **blasts over 20%** |
| **Flow cytometry** | **Most accurate** for cell subtype (CD markers, e.g. CD33 and CD34 for myeloid) |
| Morphology, histochemistry, immunology, cytogenetics | Classify and stage. **Auer rods** = AML |

## 12. Treatment

### Aim

Achieve **remission**: no blasts detectable in blood or marrow and normal marrow regenerates. In many ALL patients remission lasts indefinitely (cure). (No need to learn chemotherapy drug details.)

### Phases

| Phase | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Induction** (all patients) | Chemotherapy to destroy leukaemic cells and induce remission (about 28 days in hospital; patient may become critically ill; give psychological support) |
| **Intensification** | Higher doses of the same or other drugs |
| **Consolidation** | After remission, eliminate undetectable residual cells |
| **Maintenance** | Lower-dose therapy; **ALL only, not AML** |

- **Combination chemotherapy** is the mainstay: reduces drug resistance, spreads toxicity across drugs, and interrupts the cell cycle at several points.

### Bone marrow and stem cell transplantation

- Aim: eradicate leukaemic cells with high-dose chemotherapy with or without total body irradiation, destroying the patient's own marrow, then replace it with **HLA-matched** stem cells from a **sibling**, volunteer, identical twin or the patient's own earlier-collected cells. Mortality is significant.
- **Best indicator of prognosis is cytogenetics.** **Good cytogenetics** = low relapse risk = more chemotherapy. **Bad cytogenetics** = high relapse risk = go **straight to transplant** after remission.

### Worked case

A 17-year-old with WBC 50 000, Hb 10, platelets 15 000 and 30% circulating blasts:

1. **Peripheral blood:** morphology (abnormal blasts) and flow cytometry (blasts CD33 and CD34 positive, i.e. myeloid).
2. **Bone marrow biopsy always** (even if blasts are in blood): morphology (myeloblasts), flow cytometry (e.g. 50% blasts express myeloid antigens), and molecular tests (e.g. FLT3-ITD positive).
3. **Treatment:** induction (28 days), **assess response at day 28** (repeat marrow compared with baseline; **blasts under 5% = complete remission**), then consolidation (chemotherapy or stem cell transplant), then maintenance in ALL only.
4. Chemotherapy is not given continuously through the 28 days; after the active days, wait for count recovery (platelets half-life about 10 days, red cells 120, white cells 1 to 3).
5. **Discuss infertility** (fertility preservation) before treatment, particularly with aggressive ALL regimens and transplant.

## 13. Self-test cases

| Vignette | Answer |
|---|---|
| 47-year-old with headaches, fatigue, infections, petechiae, MCV 105, pancytopenia, hypocellular marrow, no organomegaly | **Aplastic anaemia** |
| 65-year-old, fatigue, fever, gum bleeding, Auer rods, hypogranular neutrophils, Sudan black B positive | **AML** |
| 5-year-old, petechiae, hepatosplenomegaly, suspected acute leukaemia: most appropriate initial investigation | **Direct microscopy of bone marrow cells** (per the lecture key) |

## Key points to revise

1. Acute leukaemia: over 20% blasts, sudden onset, marrow failure; chronic: mature cells, insidious, organomegaly.
2. ALL commonest in children; AML commonest acute leukaemia in adults.
3. AML: Auer rods, gum hypertrophy, leukostasis (leukapheresis).
4. ALL: CNS and testicular spread, needs CNS prophylaxis and maintenance therapy, tumour lysis.
5. CML: Philadelphia t(9;22) BCR-ABL, massive splenomegaly, blast crisis.
6. CLL: mature B cells (CD20 positive), watch and wait if early.
7. Diagnosis: peripheral smear first, then marrow biopsy and flow cytometry.
8. Induction, consolidation, maintenance (ALL only); cytogenetics decide chemotherapy versus transplant.
