# Preconception Care: Preparing for a Healthy Pregnancy

**Year 4 · Obstetrics and Gynaecology · Antenatal and preventive care**

> **In one line:** preconception care is the set of medical, behavioural and social interventions given **before** conception to improve maternal health and pregnancy outcome. Organogenesis is largely complete by 8-10 weeks, often before the woman knows she is pregnant, so the first antenatal visit is too late for many interventions.

## What you must know

- About half of pregnancies are **unplanned**, so preconception care applies to **every woman of reproductive age**.
- **Folic acid 400 µg daily**, started at least 1 month (ideally 3) before conception and continued to 12 weeks, reduces neural tube defects by about **70%**.
- **High-dose folic acid 4-5 mg daily** for a previous affected pregnancy, antiepileptic drugs, diabetes and obesity.
- Optimise chronic disease first: diabetes (HbA1c below 6.5%, ideally under 6.1%), hypertension, epilepsy, HIV (suppressed viral load).
- **Stop or replace teratogens**: warfarin, ACE inhibitors, statins, retinoids, methotrexate, misoprostol, tetracyclines, valproate.
- WHO inter-pregnancy interval: at least **24 months after a live birth**, 6 months after a miscarriage.

## 1. Rationale

- The embryo is most vulnerable in the first 8-10 weeks, so risk factors must be dealt with before pregnancy.
- Poor control of maternal disease (diabetes, epilepsy, hypertension), nutritional deficiency, infections and teratogenic drugs are the main modifiable causes of congenital malformation, miscarriage, preterm birth and stillbirth.
- Preconception care also lowers maternal morbidity (anaemia, pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes) and is a Safe Motherhood intervention.

## 2. Components

### 2.1 Folic acid

| Group | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| All women planning pregnancy | **400 µg daily** | From at least 1 month (ideally 3) before conception to 12 weeks |
| Previous neural tube defect, antiepileptic drugs, diabetes, obesity | **4-5 mg daily** | Same period |

Folic acid reduces neural tube defects (spina bifida, anencephaly) by about 70%. Kenya also fortifies maize and wheat flour.

### 2.2 Chronic disease optimisation

| Condition | Preconception action |
|---|---|
| **Diabetes** | Aim for **HbA1c below 6.5%** (preferably under 6.1%); stop teratogenic oral agents; switch to insulin or metformin as advised. Poor control raises malformation (cardiac, neural tube, caudal regression) and miscarriage risk. |
| **Hypertension** | Change ACE inhibitors, ARBs and diuretics to **methyldopa, labetalol or nifedipine** |
| **Epilepsy** | **Monotherapy** at the lowest effective dose; **avoid valproate** |
| **HIV** | Start or continue antiretrovirals with a **suppressed viral load** (prevents mother-to-child transmission) |
| Thyroid disease, asthma, renal and cardiac disease | Specialist review and drug adjustment |

### 2.3 Medication review

Stop or replace **teratogens**: **warfarin**, **ACE inhibitors**, **statins**, **isotretinoin and acitretin**, **methotrexate**, **misoprostol**, **tetracyclines** and **valproate**. Review every herbal and over-the-counter product too.

> **Exam tip:** the classic switch questions are warfarin to LMWH (heparin), ACE inhibitor to methyldopa/labetalol/nifedipine, and valproate to a safer antiepileptic.

### 2.4 Immunisation and infections

- Check **rubella** immunity. If non-immune, vaccinate (live vaccine) and **avoid pregnancy for one month**.
- Update **tetanus toxoid** and **hepatitis B**; check HPV status and **cervical screening**.
- Screen for and treat **HIV, syphilis** and other STIs.
- Malaria prevention counselling in endemic areas.

### 2.5 Lifestyle

- Stop **smoking and alcohol**; avoid recreational drugs.
- Healthy weight (**BMI 18.5-24.9**); obesity raises the risk of diabetes, pre-eclampsia and caesarean birth.
- Balanced nutrition, correct anaemia, avoid **raw meat** (toxoplasma, listeria) and limit caffeine.

### 2.6 Genetic and family history

Offer counselling to couples with a family history of inherited disease, **consanguinity**, advanced maternal age or a previous affected child. Screen for **sickle cell disease** and thalassaemia where prevalent.

### 2.7 Social and mental health

Screen for **intimate partner violence** and **depression**, and address financial and occupational risks (for example exposure to chemicals or radiation at work).

## 3. Planning pregnancy intervals

The WHO recommends waiting at least **24 months after a live birth** and **6 months after a miscarriage** before the next pregnancy, to reduce preterm birth, low birth weight and maternal anaemia. Family planning counselling is therefore part of preconception care.

> **Remember:** a short interval (under 6 months) is linked to preterm birth, low birth weight, uterine rupture after a caesarean scar and maternal anaemia.

## 4. A practical preconception visit

1. History: obstetric, medical, drug, family, social.
2. Examination: BMI, blood pressure, general and breast examination.
3. Tests: Hb, blood group, HIV, syphilis, HbA1c where indicated, rubella and hepatitis B status.
4. Start folic acid; adjust drugs; vaccinate.
5. Counsel on lifestyle and timing of conception.

## Key points to revise

1. Preconception care covers every woman of reproductive age because many pregnancies are unplanned.
2. Folic acid 400 µg daily before conception to 12 weeks; 4-5 mg for high-risk women.
3. Control diabetes (HbA1c below 6.5%), hypertension, epilepsy and HIV before conception.
4. Replace teratogens (warfarin, ACE inhibitors, statins, retinoids, methotrexate, misoprostol, tetracyclines, valproate).
5. Check rubella immunity; avoid pregnancy for 1 month after vaccination.
6. Stop smoking and alcohol; aim for BMI 18.5-24.9.
7. Wait at least 24 months after a live birth and 6 months after a miscarriage.
