## Detailed Notes

##  WEEK 6 — CELL WALL INHIBITORS

## Beta-Lactams — Core Recap - **MOA**: Mimic D-Ala-D-Ala → bind PBPs (transpeptidases) irreversibly → no peptidoglycan crosslinking → osmotic lysis → **bactericidal, time-dependent** - **Beta-lactam ring** is essential for activity — beta-lactamases cleave this ring = resistance

### Penicillins | Drug | Spectrum | Key Clinical Use | |---|---|---| | Benzylpenicillin (Pen G) | Narrow Gram+ | Strep, meningitis, syphilis, endocarditis | | Phenoxymethylpenicillin (Pen V) | Narrow Gram+ | Oral; strep throat; asplenia prophylaxis | | Flucloxacillin | **MSSA only** | Staph skin, bone, endocarditis | | Amoxicillin | Gram+, some Gram- | RTI, UTI, *H. pylori*, *Listeria* | | Co-amoxiclav | Broad + anaerobes | Bites, abscesses, mixed infections | | Piperacillin-tazobactam | Broad + **Pseudomonas** | Severe hospital infections, febrile neutropenia |

- **Flucloxacillin** = penicillinase-resistant → used specifically for *S. aureus* (MSSA) - **Flucloxacillin side effect**: cholestatic hepatitis (especially prolonged use, elderly)

### Cephalosporins — Generation Rules | Generation | Example | Key Coverage | |---|---|---| | 1st | Cefalexin, Cefazolin | Gram+ (surgical prophylaxis) | | 2nd | Cefuroxime | Gram+ + better Gram- | | 3rd | Ceftriaxone, Ceftazidime | Gram- (meningitis); Ceftazidime = Pseudomonas | | 4th | Cefepime | Pseudomonas + Gram+ | | 5th | Ceftaroline | **MRSA** activity |

- **Higher generation = better Gram-negative, less Gram-positive** (except 5th gen) - **Ceftriaxone**: once daily, biliary excretion, meningitis, gonorrhoea, CAP - **Ceftazidime-avibactam**: covers carbapenemase-producers (KPC, OXA-48)

### Carbapenems | Drug | Key Feature | |---|---| | Meropenem | Broadest; CNS safe; Pseudomonas | | Imipenem-cilastatin | Cilastatin prevents renal inactivation; seizure risk | | Ertapenem | Once daily; **NO Pseudomonas/Acinetobacter** | | Doripenem | Similar to meropenem |

- Resistant to most beta-lactamases except **carbapenemases (KPC, NDM, OXA-48)** - **Ertapenem trap**: looks like a carbapenem but misses Pseudomonas — don't use empirically for suspected MDR

### Monobactams - **Aztreonam**: Gram-negative only, no anaerobes, no Gram+ - Safe in penicillin allergy (minimal cross-reactivity) - Used in CF, febrile neutropenia (penicillin-allergic)

### Beta-Lactamase Inhibitors | Combination | Inhibitor | Extra Coverage Gained | |---|---|---| | Co-amoxiclav | Clavulanate | Beta-lactamase producing organisms | | Tazocin | Tazobactam | As above + Pseudomonas | | Ceftazidime-avibactam | Avibactam | KPC, OXA-48 carbapenemases | | Meropenem-vaborbactam | Vaborbactam | KPC carbapenemases |

## Glycopeptides

### Vancomycin - **MOA**: Binds **D-Ala-D-Ala** terminus → physically blocks transglycosylation + transpeptidation - **Gram-positive only** (too large for Gram-negative outer membrane) - **Bactericidal** against staph; **bacteriostatic** against enterococci - Routes: IV systemic; **oral = C. diff only** (not absorbed) - **TDM**: AUC/MIC 400–600 or trough 10–20 mg/L - **Red Man Syndrome**: histamine release → flushing, rash, hypotension → **NOT allergy** → slow infusion, antihistamine - **Toxicity**: nephrotoxicity (↑ risk with aminoglycosides), ototoxicity

### Teicoplanin - Same MOA as vancomycin - Longer half-life → once daily (after loading) - Less nephrotoxic; IM or IV

### VRE Resistance Mechanisms | Gene | Resistance | |---|---| | **VanA** | Vancomycin + teicoplanin resistant (D-Ala-D-**Lac**) | | **VanB** | Vancomycin only resistant; teicoplanin susceptible | | **VanC** | Low-level, intrinsic in *E. gallinarum* |

## Other Cell Wall Agents

### Fosfomycin - Inhibits **MurA** (first step in peptidoglycan synthesis) - Oral or IV - Uncomplicated UTI (single oral dose), MDR Gram-negatives (IV — combination) - Well tolerated; mainly GI side effects

### Daptomycin (Cell Membrane) - **MOA**: Ca²⁺-dependent insertion → membrane depolarisation → bactericidal - Gram-positive only - **NOT for pneumonia** (inactivated by surfactant)  - Side effect: **myopathy** → monitor CK, hold statins

##  WEEK 6 — PROTEIN SYNTHESIS INHIBITORS

## Aminoglycosides - **MOA**: Bind **30S** irreversibly → misreading of mRNA → aberrant proteins → bactericidal - Require oxygen for cell entry → **INACTIVE against anaerobes** - **Concentration-dependent** + long PAE → once daily dosing - **Hartford nomogram** for dosing in renal impairment

| Drug | Key Use | |---|---| | Gentamicin | Gram-negative sepsis, endocarditis synergy | | Amikacin | MDR Gram-negatives (resistant to other aminoglycosides) | | Tobramycin | *Pseudomonas* in CF (inhaled) | | Streptomycin | TB, brucellosis |

### Toxicity — CRITICAL - **Nephrotoxicity**: proximal tubule damage; reversible; ↑ risk with dehydration, NSAIDs, vancomycin - **Ototoxicity**: - **Cochlear** (hearing loss — **irreversible**, high-frequency first) - **Vestibular** (vertigo, ataxia, nystagmus) - **Neuromuscular blockade**: rare; avoid in myasthenia gravis - Monitor: pre-dose trough (must be undetectable with OD), peak Cmax/MIC ≥ 10

## Tetracyclines - **MOA**: Bind **30S** reversibly → block aminoacyl-tRNA entry → **bacteriostatic** - Chelate divalent cations (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe²⁺) → reduced absorption with dairy, antacids, iron

| Drug | Key Feature | |---|---| | Doxycycline | Most used; once daily; excellent oral bioavailability | | Minocycline | Good tissue penetration; acne; some MRSA | | Tigecycline | IV only; broad including MRSA, ESBL, anaerobes; **NOT Pseudomonas** |

**Uses**: atypicals (*Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Rickettsia, Coxiella*), Lyme disease, acne, malaria prophylaxis, MRSA soft tissue, STIs, brucellosis, cholera

**Contraindications**: pregnancy, children <8 years (teeth discolouration, bone growth inhibition), renal failure (except doxycycline)

**Side effects**: photosensitivity, oesophageal ulceration (take upright with water), GI upset, pseudotumour cerebri

## Macrolides - **MOA**: Bind **23S rRNA of 50S** → inhibit translocation → **bacteriostatic**; excellent intracellular penetration

| Drug | Key Feature | |---|---| | Azithromycin | t½ 68h → 3–5 day course; *Chlamydia* 1g stat; atypicals | | Clarithromycin | *H. pylori* triple therapy; MAC in HIV | | Erythromycin | Oldest; pro-motility (motilin agonist); pertussis; most GI side effects |

**Uses**: CAP (atypicals), *Chlamydia*, pertussis, *H. pylori*, MAC prophylaxis in HIV, gastroparesis (erythromycin)

**Side effects**: GI upset, **QT prolongation** (avoid with other QT drugs), hepatotoxicity, CYP3A4 inhibition → ↑ statin/warfarin/cyclosporin levels

**Resistance**: **MLSb** — methylation of 23S rRNA (ermB gene); efflux (mefA)

## Chloramphenicol - **MOA**: Binds **50S** → inhibits peptidyl transferase → **bacteriostatic**; broad spectrum - Good CNS penetration - **Uses**: meningitis (when beta-lactams can't be used), typhoid, eye drops (conjunctivitis) - **Toxicity**: - **Dose-dependent**: reversible bone marrow suppression - **Idiosyncratic**: aplastic anaemia (rare, irreversible, fatal)  - **Grey baby syndrome**: neonates lack glucuronyl transferase → toxic accumulation → cardiovascular collapse - Reserved due to toxicity; monitor FBC

## Clindamycin - **MOA**: Binds **50S** (same site as macrolides) → bacteriostatic - Gram-positive + anaerobes; excellent **bone penetration** - **Uses**: bone/joint infections, orofacial, MRSA soft tissue, necrotising fasciitis (toxin suppression), aspiration pneumonia - **Key side effect**: **C. difficile colitis** (high risk — highest of all antibiotics) - MLSb cross-resistance with macrolides

## Linezolid - **MOA**: Binds **23S rRNA (50S)** — unique site → inhibits **initiation complex** formation → bacteriostatic - No cross-resistance with other 50S inhibitors - **Uses**: MRSA, VRE; oral or IV (100% bioavailability) - **Side effects**: myelosuppression (FBC weekly), **serotonin syndrome** (SSRIs/MAOIs/tramadol), lactic acidosis, peripheral + optic neuropathy, tyramine interaction (MAO inhibitor activity)

## Streptogramins (Quinupristin-Dalfopristin) - Two components bind **50S** sequentially → synergistic bactericidal effect - **VRE** (E. faecium only — not E. faecalis), MRSA - IV only; significant drug interactions (CYP3A4 inhibitor) - Side effects: myalgia, arthralgia, infusion site reactions

##  WEEK 7 — QUINOLONES & FOLIC ACID ANTAGONISTS

## Fluoroquinolones — Full Must-Know

### MOA - Inhibit **DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II)** — primary target in **Gram-negatives** - Inhibit **topoisomerase IV** — primary target in **Gram-positives** - Stabilise DNA-enzyme complex → strand breaks → **bactericidal, concentration-dependent**

### Drugs | Drug | Spectrum | Key Use | Special | |---|---|---|---| | Ciprofloxacin | Gram- dominant | UTI, GI, Pseudomonas, anthrax | Best Pseudomonas quinolone | | Levofloxacin | Gram+ better | CAP, UTI, TB (2nd line) | Respiratory quinolone | | Moxifloxacin | Best Gram+, anaerobes | CAP, TB | No urinary excretion — NOT for UTI | | Norfloxacin | UTI only | Urinary concentration only | Not for systemic use |

### Pharmacokinetics - **~100% oral bioavailability** → easy IV to oral switch - Excellent tissue penetration: bone, prostate, intracellular, lung - Mainly renal excretion (except moxifloxacin — hepatic) - Reduced absorption with antacids, iron, calcium, zinc

### Side Effects — KNOW ALL - **Tendinopathy/tendon rupture** (Achilles most common) — ↑ risk: elderly, corticosteroids, renal failure - **QT prolongation** → torsades de pointes - **CNS**: seizures (↓ GABA), confusion, dizziness — caution in epilepsy - **Peripheral neuropathy** (FDA black box warning) - **Cartilage damage** → avoid in children + pregnancy - Photosensitivity - Dysglycaemia (hypo/hyperglycaemia in diabetics) - *C. difficile* risk

### Resistance - **GyrA/GyrB chromosomal mutations** — high-level, most common - **PMQR** (plasmid-mediated): qnr genes protect DNA gyrase from quinolone binding - **Efflux pumps** (qepA, oqxAB) - Resistance develops rapidly with subtherapeutic dosing

## Folic Acid Antagonists

### Pathway — MUST KNOW

- Bacteria must **synthesise** folate; humans **absorb** it from diet → selective toxicity - Combined = **sequential blockade** → synergistic, bactericidal

### Trimethoprim - Inhibits **bacterial DHFR** (50,000× more affinity for bacterial DHFR than human) - **Bacteriostatic** alone; bactericidal in combination - Uses: uncomplicated UTI, UTI prophylaxis - Side effects: hyperkalaemia (blocks renal K⁺ excretion — amiloride-like), raises creatinine (tubular secretion block — **not true renal failure**), folate deficiency, rash

### Sulfonamides - PABA analogues → competitively inhibit **dihydropteroate synthase** - Rarely used alone; part of co-trimoxazole - Side effects: rash, **Stevens-Johnson syndrome**, haemolytic anaemia (G6PD deficiency), kernicterus in neonates

### Co-trimoxazole (TMP-SMX) - Trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole (1:5 ratio) - **Bactericidal** due to sequential synergism

**Uses**: - **PJP** (*Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia) — treatment (high dose) + prophylaxis in HIV/immunosuppressed (CD4 <200) - *Toxoplasma gondii* (with pyrimethamine) - *Nocardia asteroides* - *Stenotrophomonas maltophilia* (MDR — often only option) - *Listeria* (penicillin-allergic) - MRSA soft tissue (some strains) - Traveller's diarrhoea

**Side effects**: - **Myelosuppression** (bone marrow suppression — give folinic acid NOT folic acid to counter) - **Hyperkalaemia** - **Rash / SJS** (sulfonamide) - Hepatotoxicity - Raises creatinine (not true AKI) - **Avoid in pregnancy** (1st trimester = neural tube defects; term = neonatal haemolysis/kernicterus) - **G6PD deficiency** = haemolytic anaemia

## 🫁 WEEK 7 — ANTIMYCOBACTERIALS

## Mycobacteria — Why Hard to Treat - **Slow-growing** → most antibiotics work on dividing cells; need prolonged therapy - **Waxy cell wall** (mycolic acids) → impermeable to most drugs; poor penetration - **Intracellular** (survive in macrophages) → need drugs with intracellular penetration - **Naturally resistant** to many antibiotics - **High mutation rate** relative to drug exposure → monotherapy = rapid resistance

## TB Treatment — RIPE Regimen (Must Know)

### Standard Regimen - **2 months intensive phase**: **R**ifampicin + **I**soniazid + **P**yrazinamide + **E**thambutol - **4 months continuation phase**: Rifampicin + Isoniazid - Total = **6 months**

### Why Combination? - Each drug targets a different subpopulation of bacilli - Prevents emergence of resistance - Never use monotherapy

## RIPE Drugs — Individual Detail

### Rifampicin (R) - **MOA**: Inhibits **bacterial RNA polymerase (β subunit)** → inhibits transcription → bactericidal - Kills **rapidly dividing** bacteria (most active drug in regimen) - **Potent CYP450 inducer** → major interactions: warfarin, OCP, antiretrovirals, azoles, steroids, methadone - **Red/orange** discolouration of urine, tears, sweat, saliva — warn patient, stains contact lenses - **Side effects**: hepatotoxicity, flu-like syndrome (intermittent therapy), thrombocytopenia - **Never monotherapy** — rapid resistance via single-step rpoB mutations - Also used: meningococcal prophylaxis, *Staph* biofilm infections, leprosy

### Isoniazid (INH) (I) - **MOA**: Prodrug → activated by **KatG catalase-peroxidase** → inhibits **InhA** (enoyl-ACP reductase) → blocks **mycolic acid synthesis** → bactericidal - Active against **rapidly dividing** and **dormant** bacilli - **Side effects**: - **Peripheral neuropathy** (most important) — due to pyridoxine (Vit B6) deficiency → **give pyridoxine prophylactically** - **Hepatotoxicity** (monitor LFTs) — most common cause of drug-induced liver injury in TB - **Drug-induced lupus** - **CNS effects**: psychosis, seizures (especially in pyridoxine deficiency) - CYP inhibitor → ↑ phenytoin levels - **Resistance**: KatG mutations (can't activate prodrug), InhA mutations

### Pyrazinamide (Z) - **MOA**: Prodrug → converted to **pyrazinoic acid** (active) in acidic environment → disrupts mycobacterial membrane energy - **Only works in acidic pH** → targets bacilli inside macrophage phagolysosomes (acidic environment)  - **Sterilising effect** — kills dormant/semi-dormant intracellular bacilli → allows **shortening regimen to 6 months** - **Side effects**: - **Hepatotoxicity** (most hepatotoxic of RIPE drugs) — monitor LFTs - **Hyperuricaemia** → gout (inhibits renal uric acid excretion) - Arthralgia, myalgia - Photosensitivity

### Ethambutol (E) - **MOA**: Inhibits **arabinosyl transferase** → blocks **arabinogalactan** synthesis (mycobacterial cell wall component) → bacteriostatic - Used to **protect against resistance** during initial phase while sensitivities awaited - **Key side effect**: **Optic neuritis** → red-green colour blindness, reduced visual acuity → **test colour vision + visual acuity before and during treatment**  - Dose-dependent; usually reversible if stopped early - Renally cleared → reduce dose in renal impairment

## Drug-Resistant TB

| Type | Definition | Treatment | |---|---|---| | **MDR-TB** | Resistant to rifampicin + isoniazid | Fluoroquinolones, linezolid, bedaquiline, clofazimine | | **XDR-TB** | MDR + resistant to fluoroquinolones + at least one injectable | Very limited options; bedaquiline, delamanid | | **RR-TB** | Rifampicin resistant only | Treat as MDR |

### Second-Line TB Drugs - **Bedaquiline**: inhibits mycobacterial ATP synthase; QT prolongation - **Delamanid**: inhibits mycolic acid synthesis; QT prolongation - **Linezolid**: used in MDR/XDR-TB; myelosuppression risk - **Fluoroquinolones** (moxifloxacin, levofloxacin): core of MDR-TB regimen - **Amikacin/Streptomycin**: injectable; ototoxicity/nephrotoxicity

## Latent TB Treatment - **Isoniazid** 6 months (+ pyridoxine) - OR **Rifampicin** 4 months - OR **Rifampicin + Isoniazid** 3 months - Aim: prevent reactivation in LTBI (latent TB infection)

## Leprosy (*Mycobacterium leprae*) - **Paucibacillary** (tuberculoid): Rifampicin + Dapsone × 6 months - **Multibacillary** (lepromatous): Rifampicin + Dapsone + Clofazimine × 12 months - **Dapsone**: sulfonamide-like, folate inhibitor; side effects = haemolytic anaemia (G6PD), methaemoglobinaemia - **Clofazimine**: anti-inflammatory + bactericidal; skin discolouration (red-brown), GI side effects

## Other Mycobacteria (NTM — Non-Tuberculous Mycobacteria) - ***M. avium complex* (MAC)**: in HIV (CD4 <50); treat with **azithromycin + ethambutol ± rifabutin**; prophylaxis = azithromycin (CD4 <50) - ***M. kansasii***: rifampicin-sensitive; treat like TB - ***M. abscessus***: highly drug resistant; amikacin + macrolide + imipenem
