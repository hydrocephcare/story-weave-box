## MYCOLOGY FUNDAMENTALS

**General characteristics of fungi**
- Eukaryotes
- Heterotrophic organisms
- Cell walls made of chitin
- Can be unicellular or multicellular
- Exist as yeasts, molds, or mushrooms
- Reproduce by both sexual and asexual spores
- NOT photosynthetic — this is the false statement MCQ trick *

**Hyphae and mycelium**
- Hyphae are filamentous structures making up the mycelium
- A fungal mycelium may contain: septate hyphae, coenocytic cytoplasm, chitinous cell walls, mitochondria — all of the above *
- Separating wall in hyphae is called a septum, not hyphae — MCQ trick
- Cell wall contains chitin — a polysaccharide *

**Cell membrane**
- Major component of fungal cell membrane: ergosterol *
- NOT cholesterol, NOT peptidoglycan — these are trick options
- Ergosterol is the target of most antifungal drugs

**pH for fungal growth**
- Best growth range: pH 4-6 *

**Staining**
- Fungal cell wall with periodic acid Schiff stain: appears red *

## FUNGAL CLASSIFICATION

**Classes based on reproduction** *

- Zygomycota: sexual reproduction by zygospores
- Ascomycota: sexual reproduction by ascospores
- Basidiomycota: sexual reproduction by basidiospores
- Chytridiomycota: sexual reproduction by zoospores
- Mitosporic fungi / Deuteromycetes / Fungi Imperfecti: NO known sexual stage *

**Deuteromycetes — Fungi Imperfecti** *
- Sexual reproduction method not clearly known *
- They are imperfect fungi
- Also called Deuteromycetes
- Answer is B and C together *

**Dimorphic fungi** *
- Exist as mold at environmental temperature and yeast at body temperature
- Most dimorphic pathogens are filamentous at 23°C and yeast at 37°C *
- Examples: Histoplasma, Blastomyces, Coccidioides, Paracoccidioides, Sporothrix

## FUNGAL REPRODUCTION

**Asexual spore formation** *
- Arthrospores: single celled spores from fragments of hyphae *
- Conidia: asexual spores
- Sporangiospores: asexual spores
- Yeast budding: asexual
- Basidiospores are NOT asexual — they are sexual, found in club-shaped structure called basidium *

**Sexual spores**
- Ascospores: produced in a sac
- Basidiospores: single celled, found in club-shaped structure *
- Zygospores: Zygomycota

**Asexual methods in fungi**
- Fragmentation, budding, fission
- NOT spermatization — that is sexual *

## ANTIFUNGAL DRUGS *

Essay and MCQ — modes of action heavily tested.

**Polyenes**
- Nystatin and Amphotericin B
- Target: ergosterol in cell membrane, disrupts membrane integrity *
- Nystatin belongs to polyene class *
- Amphotericin B: broad spectrum, used for blastomycosis, histoplasmosis, aspergillosis, valley fever
- NOT used for ergotism *

**Azoles**
- Fluconazole, Miconazole, Ketoconazole, Itraconazole, Voriconazole, Posaconazole
- Target: ergosterol biosynthesis — inhibit proper formation of plasma membrane *
- Miconazole inhibits plasma membrane formation *
- Fluconazole: drug of choice for most candida infections
- Posaconazole: alternative for fluconazole-resistant Candida glabrata *
- Itraconazole: drug of choice for sporotrichosis *

**Echinocandins**
- Micafungin, Caspofungin
- Target: glucan synthesis in cell wall — NOT ergosterol *
- Antifungal drug class that targets glucans *

**Allylamines**
- Terbinafine
- Target: ergosterol biosynthesis

**Griseofulvin**
- Target: DNA — inhibits microtubule formation, disrupts mitosis *

**Flucytosine**
- Target: DNA synthesis
- NOT a microtubule — trick MCQ option

**5-Flucytosine and Fluconazole similarity**
- Both are antifungals used for systemic infections *

**Drug that does NOT target ergosterol biosynthesis**
- Micafungin — targets cell wall glucans *

## SUPERFICIAL MYCOSES

These affect only the outermost layers of skin and hair. No inflammation.

**Tinea nigra**
- Caused by Hortaea werneckii
- Pigmented macules on palm and soles
- Non-inflammatory, non-scaly
- NOT difficult to isolate by microscopy — trick false statement *
- Usually related to poor hygiene

**White piedra**
- Caused by Trichosporon species
- Superficial infection of hair
- Species causing scalp hair white piedra: Trichosporon ovoides * — repeated MCQ
- Other species: T. inkin, T. asahii, T. mucoides

**Black piedra**
- Caused by Piedraia hortae
- Hard black nodules on hair

**Tinea versicolor / Pityriasis versicolor**
- Caused by Malassezia furfur
- Lesion characteristics: hypopigmented and hyperpigmented irregular macules on the skin *
- NOT round and smooth — that is the trick option
- Mild itching, scaling, non-contagious
- Appears on back, chest, upper arms, underarms
- Least frequently associated with immunocompromised persons *

**Superficial mycoses list** *
- Tinea nigra, Tinea versicolor, White piedra, Black piedra
- Tinea imbricate is NOT superficial — it is cutaneous *

## CUTANEOUS MYCOSES — DERMATOPHYTOSES *

Essay question in multiple papers — high priority.

**General characteristics**
- Affect hair, skin, and nails — keratinized tissues
- Caused by dermatophytes: Trichophyton, Microsporum, Epidermophyton
- Commonly called ringworm or tinea

**Three genera of dermatophytes** *
- Trichophyton: infects hair, skin, nails — has microconidia
- Microsporum: infects hair and skin — has macroconidia
- Epidermophyton: infects skin and nails — does NOT have microconidia *

**Zoophilic vs anthropophilic** *
- Acute infections: associated with zoophilic dermatophytes such as Microsporum canis *
- Chronic infections: associated with anthropophilic dermatophytes
- Zoophilic dermatophytes: Microsporum canis, Trichophyton equinum, Trichophyton simii, Microsporum nanum — all of these *

**Types of tinea** *
- Tinea pedis: athlete's foot — between toe webs and soles
- Tinea corporis: ringworm of body
- Tinea capitis: scalp ringworm
- Tinea unguium: nail infection — onychomycosis
- Tinea cruris: jock itch — groin
- Tinea versicolor: caused by Malassezia, not a dermatophyte

**Tinea pedis — athlete's foot** *
- Itchy, white flaky, stingy patches between toe webs and soles
- Characterized by inflammation and skin fissures
- Etiological agent: Trichophyton rubrum *
- Common cause of athlete's foot: wearing tight shoes, sweating, keeping feet wet — all of the above *

**Tinea capitis — scalp ringworm**
- In 2-year-old child with rash from pet cat: Microsporum canis *
- Microsporum canis is zoophilic — transmitted from animals

**Laboratory diagnosis of dermatophytes**
- Most reliable method for Trichophyton rubrum: skin scrapings with KOH microscopy *
- Circular dry scaly pruritic lesion with branching septate nonpigmented hyphae in KOH preparation: Dermatophytosis *
- Epidermophyton and Microsporum: both cause cutaneous mycoses, both have macroconidia *

**Nail infections — onychomycosis**
- Caused by: Epidermophyton floccosum, Trichophyton rubrum, Trichophyton mentagrophytes
- NOT caused by: Microsporum canis — trick answer *

## SUBCUTANEOUS MYCOSES

These involve the deeper dermis and subcutaneous tissue, usually after traumatic inoculation.

**Sporotrichosis** *
- Caused by Sporothrix schenckii — dimorphic fungus *
- Classic scenario: girl pricks finger on rose bush, develops pustule progressing to ulcer, nodules along lymphatic drainage *
- Ecology well known — lives in soil and decaying vegetation
- Most cases involve lymphangitic spread
- Drug of choice: itraconazole *
- NOT most patients immunocompromised

**Chromoblastomycosis / Chromomycosis** *
- West African farmer with persistent scaly papule progressing to cauliflower-like lesions
- In tissue: organisms convert to spherical cells reproducing by fission with transverse septations *
- Etiologic agents are environmental, NOT endogenous mammalian flora
- Caused by multiple species, NOT single species
- Most infections are subcutaneous, NOT systemic
- Production of sclerotic bodies: Phialophora verrucosa *

**Phaeohyphomycosis**
- Brain abscess with darkly pigmented septate hyphae: caused by Cladophialophora species *
- May show subcutaneous or systemic disease including sinusitis *
- Responds to itraconazole

**Mycetoma** *
- Essay question — appears in multiple papers
- Two types: *
- Eumycetoma: caused by true fungi e.g. Madurella mycetomatis
- Actinomycetoma: caused by bacteria e.g. Nocardia species *
- Responsible for mycosis of foot in Indian subcontinent: Madurella mycetomatis *

**Eumycetoma vs Actinomycetoma** *
- Eumycetoma: not widespread, limited spread
- Actinomycetoma: wide spread
- Both cause chronic granulomatous infection of subcutaneous tissue
- Both produce grains/granules in discharge

## SYSTEMIC MYCOSES *

Essay questions — very high priority. Histoplasmosis and Cryptococcosis are the most tested.

**General characteristics of systemic mycoses** *
- Caused by dimorphic fungi
- Pathogenesis mostly by inhalation of spores
- Affect both healthy and immunocompromised
- Aspergillosis and mucormycosis are also systemic
- Lungs are NOT the site of infection for ALL fungi — this is the false/EXCEPT statement *

## HISTOPLASMOSIS *

Essay in 2021/2022 and 2018/2019 — highest priority essay topic.

**Causative agent**
- Histoplasma capsulatum — dimorphic fungus *
- Also called Darling's disease *
- Laboratory: mold with tuberculate macroconidia and oval microconidia *

**Epidemiology**
- Geographic distribution: Mississippi and Ohio River valleys
- Farmer in Mississippi with chronic cough, opaque mass on chest X-ray, macrophages with multiple yeast forms: Histoplasmosis *
- Natural habitat: soil, especially enriched by bird and bat droppings
- NOT person-to-person transmission *

**Pathogenesis** *
- Inhalation of spores from soil
- Primary infection in lungs
- Macrophages engulf organisms but organisms survive intracellularly
- Disseminates in immunocompromised

**Clinical manifestations** *
- Asymptomatic in most immunocompetent
- Acute pulmonary: flu-like, fever, cough, chest pain
- Chronic pulmonary: resembles TB
- Disseminated: in immunocompromised, HIV patients

**Laboratory diagnosis** *
- Culture: slow growing, takes weeks
- Microscopy: yeast cells inside macrophages
- Serology: complement fixation
- Antigen detection in urine and serum

**Treatment** *
- Mild: itraconazole
- Severe/disseminated: amphotericin B then itraconazole

## CRYPTOCOCCOSIS *

Essay in 2017/2018 and 2018/2019 — very high priority.

**Causative agent**
- Cryptococcus neoformans — encapsulated yeast *
- Basidiomycete — etiology is a basidiomycete *
- Natural habitat: soil especially associated with pigeon droppings/feces *
- Associated with pigeon droppings — office pigeon roost scenario *

**Virulence factors**
- Polysaccharide capsule — major virulence factor *
- Capsule mediates attachment and immune evasion
- Pathogenesis NOT related to exotoxin A — trick MCQ *
- Also produces melanin

**Pathogenesis** *
- Inhalation of spores from pigeon droppings
- Primary infection in lungs
- Disseminates to CNS — meningitis in immunocompromised
- Budding yeasts found in lesions

**Clinical manifestations** *
- Pneumonia
- Meningitis — most serious, in HIV/AIDS patients
- Headaches, dizziness, confusion

**Laboratory diagnosis** *
- India ink stain: encapsulated yeast *
- Latex agglutination test: detects polysaccharide capsular antigen *
- Microscopy, culture: creamy mucoid colonies
- CSF analysis: reduced sugar, elevated protein, mononuclear leukocytes
- Biochemical tests: urease test, carbohydrate assimilation

**Treatment**
- Amphotericin B plus flucytosine for meningitis *
- Fluconazole for maintenance

**Predisposing factors**
- AIDS, CD4 count below 100
- Post-transplantation
- Use of corticosteroids
- Impaired cell-mediated immunity *

## COCCIDIOIDOMYCOSIS *

**Causative agent**
- Coccidioides immitis — dimorphic fungus *
- Also called Valley fever or desert rheumatism *
- Exists as mold in soil and spherule in tissues — hence dimorphic *

**Classic scenario** *
- Thousands in western USA developed flu or pneumonia in 1992, causative agent was fungal spores from soil and air — Coccidioides immitis *
- Formation of arthrospores and spherules: characteristic of Coccidioidomycosis *

**Epidemiology**
- Endemic to southwestern USA, Mexico
- Inhalation of arthrospores from soil

**Treatment**
- Fluconazole or amphotericin B for severe cases

## PARACOCCIDIOIDOMYCOSIS

**Causative agent**
- Paracoccidioides brasiliensis — dimorphic fungus *
- Also called South American or Brazilian Blastomycosis *
- Natural habitat: soil *

**Key facts** *
- Most patients acquired infection in South America
- Infection initiated in lungs by inhalation
- Many patients develop cutaneous and mucocutaneous lesions
- Vast majority of active disease patients are mature males *
- Physician in Latin American village finds only mature males affected: diagnosis is Paracoccidioidomycosis *
- NOT inherently resistant to amphotericin B — this is the false statement *

**Diagnosis**
- KOH wet mount microscopy *

## BLASTOMYCOSIS

**Causative agent**
- Blastomyces dermatitidis — dimorphic fungus

**Key facts**
- In tissue: large thick-walled single budding yeast cells with broad connections between parent and bud *
- Infection starts in lungs, disseminates to skin, bone, genitourinary tract
- NOT endemic to South America — that is Paracoccidioides
- NOT equally in men and women — males more affected
- NOT all cases require amphotericin B

## ASPERGILLOSIS

**Causative agent**
- Aspergillus fumigatus — most common cause of invasive aspergillosis *
- Recognized in tissue by presence of septate hyphae *

**Key facts** *
- Opportunistic infection — most common in immunocompromised
- 70-year-old leukemia patient post bone marrow transplant on cyclosporine and prednisone with pneumonia, KOH shows septate hyphae with parallel walls and low-angle branching: Aspergillus fumigatus *
- Clinical manifestations: local infections of ear, cornea, nails, sinuses *
- Patients with allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis have eosinophilia — NOT rarely *
- Aflatoxin produced by Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus parasiticus — carcinogen *

**Aflatoxin** *
- Produced by Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus parasiticus
- Most common mycotoxin infecting nuts and grains
- Potent carcinogen *
- Serious problem because they are potent carcinogens *

## CANDIDIASIS *

Essay question — clinical manifestation and treatment.

**Causative agent**
- Candida albicans — most common
- Opportunistic pathogen — part of normal flora *
- NOT transmitted by respiratory aerosol — trick MCQ *
- Budding yeast forming pseudohyphae when it invades tissue *
- Germ tube test: rapid presumptive identification of Candida albicans *

**Candidiasis forms** *
- Thrush: oral candidiasis
- Vuvovaginitis: causes more than 200 million cases yearly, itching and burning internal pain *
- Balanitis: candidiasis of penis *
- Onychomycosis: nail infection
- Systemic: in immunocompromised

**Epidemiology** *
- Patients with AIDS frequently develop mucocutaneous candidiasis such as thrush *
- Pregnancy increases risk of candidal vaginitis — NOT lowers *
- Patients with impaired neutrophils ARE at risk for systemic candidiasis
- Patients with diabetes are NOT resistant — they are MORE susceptible

**Treatment** *
- Fluconazole: first line
- Posaconazole: for fluconazole-resistant Candida glabrata *
- NOT griseofulvin, NOT amphotericin B orally for systemic candidiasis in this context

**Fungi reproducing by budding** *
- Candidiasis, cryptococcosis, and sporotrichosis — all show budding yeast cells in infected tissue *

## MUCORMYCOSIS

- Caused by Mucor species and Rhizopus
- Opportunistic — immunocompromised patients
- Rapidly fatal if untreated
- Aspergillosis and mucormycosis are systemic mycoses *

## PNEUMOCYSTIS JIROVECII

- Previously called Pneumocystis carinii
- Present in lungs of animals and humans
- Causes pneumonia in immunocompromised patients *
- Budding yeast cells and abundant pseudohyphae are typical — NOT of Pneumocystis, this is false *
- NOT thermodymorphic *
