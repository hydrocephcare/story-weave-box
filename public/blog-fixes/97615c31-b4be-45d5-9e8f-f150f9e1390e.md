# Fibromyalgia

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Rheumatology**

> **In one line:** fibromyalgia is a chronic widespread pain syndrome with fatigue, unrefreshing sleep and cognitive symptoms, caused by central sensitisation (amplified processing of pain in the CNS) rather than tissue inflammation. Routine tests are normal, diagnosis is clinical (ACR criteria), and treatment is education, graded aerobic exercise, sleep hygiene and, if needed, amitriptyline, duloxetine or pregabalin.

## What you must know

- Chronic **widespread musculoskeletal pain** plus **fatigue, waking unrefreshed and cognitive disturbance ("fibro fog")**.
- **Unknown cause**; central sensitisation, disturbed non-restorative sleep and neuroendocrine changes. **No tissue inflammation.**
- Strong **female predominance (about 10:1** in some series); prevalence 2 to 3% in the UK and USA, rising with age.
- **1990 ACR criteria:** widespread pain for 3 months plus **at least 11 of 18 tender points** (pressure 4 kg/cm2).
- **2010 ACR criteria:** Widespread Pain Index (WPI, 0 to 19) and Symptom Severity (SS, 0 to 12) scores; symptoms for at least 3 months; no other disorder explaining the pain.
- **Routine blood tests and imaging are normal**; tests are done only to exclude other conditions.
- Treatment: **education, aerobic exercise (30 minutes, 3 times a week), sleep hygiene**, then amitriptyline, SNRIs (duloxetine, milnacipran) or gabapentinoids (pregabalin, gabapentin).

## 1. Definition

- Fibromyalgia (FM) is a condition of **generalised pain and consequent disability**, characterised by **chronic widespread musculoskeletal pain**.
- Fatigue, cognitive disturbance, psychiatric symptoms and multiple somatic symptoms often accompany it.
- The aetiology is unknown and the pathophysiology uncertain. There is **no evidence of tissue inflammation** despite soft tissue pain.
- It is often associated with medically unexplained symptoms in other systems.

## 2. Epidemiology and risk factors

- Prevalence about **2 to 3%** in the UK and USA (some US data give 6.4%: 7.7% in women, 4.9% in men; Europe and South America 3.3 to 8.3%).
- Occurs at any age, including adolescence (prevalence in adolescents is similar to adults in many studies) but increases with age, peaking at about **7% in women over 70**.
- **Female predominance** of about 10:1 in the lecture figures. Between ages 20 and 55, fibromyalgia is the cause of generalised musculoskeletal pain in most women.
- More than **40% of patients in a tertiary pain clinic** meet criteria.
- Occurs across races and cultures. May **co-exist with inflammatory rheumatic disease** (axial spondyloarthritis, SLE); risk is higher with an existing rheumatic disease.

**Risk factors**

- Unresolved psychosocial distress: previous abuse, marital disharmony, alcoholism or illness in the family, previous injury or assault.
- Poor sleep health.
- Low income.

## 3. Pathophysiology

Two consistently reported abnormalities that may be linked are **disturbed, non-restorative sleep** and **pain sensitisation**, probably from abnormal central pain processing.

- **Central sensitisation:** blunted inhibitory pain pathways and altered neurotransmitters cause aberrant processing of sensory signals in the CNS. The pain threshold is lowered and normal sensations are amplified, so there is constant pain. Patients are hypersensitive to pain and constantly vigilant for it, which links with psychological problems.
- **Neuroendocrine changes:** altered serotonin, **substance P**, growth hormone and cortisol suggest autonomic and neuroendocrine regulation is disturbed. Possible thyroid and pituitary growth hormone imbalances have been suggested.
- **Stress response:** the stress adaptation response is disturbed. Frequent comorbid mood disorders support a role for stress. Triggers include physical, emotional or environmental stressors such as road traffic accidents, repetitive injuries and some diseases.
- **Sleep:** disruption of **delta-wave sleep (non-REM stage IV)** is linked with daytime fatigue and FM.
- **Proposed chain (Duna and Wilke, 1993):** disordered sleep leads to reduced serotonin production, reduced pain-modulating effects of endorphins and increased substance P, plus sympathetic nervous system changes causing muscle ischaemia and greater pain sensitivity.

## 4. Clinical features

**Core triad**

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Widespread musculoskeletal pain** | The chief complaint; bilateral, upper and lower body; may begin localised, commonly neck and shoulders |
| **Fatigue** | Especially on waking and in the mid-afternoon |
| **Cognitive disturbance ("fibro fog")** | Difficulty with attention and with tasks needing rapid shifts in thought (multitasking); memory problems, poor concentration |

**Other symptoms:** headache, dysmenorrhoea, extreme sensitivity to cold, restless legs, odd patterns of numbness and tingling, paraesthesia, exercise intolerance, stiffness, palpitations, impaired concentration and memory, diminished attention span.

> **Remember:** tender points are painful on palpation but there is no joint swelling, no synovitis, no muscle wasting and no weakness. Their presence points away from an inflammatory disease.

## 5. Diagnosis

### 1990 ACR classification criteria

Used in trials, but not useful for diagnosis in clinical practice. Both of the following must be met:

1. **Widespread pain** on both sides of the body and above and below the waist.
2. **At least 11 of 18 tender points** (9 bilateral sites).

| Tender point sites (bilateral) |
|---|
| Suboccipital muscle insertion |
| Anterior C5 to C7 intertransverse spaces |
| Mid upper border of trapezius |
| Origin of supraspinatus |
| Second costochondral junctions |
| 2 cm distal to the lateral epicondyles |
| Upper outer quadrants of the buttocks |
| Greater trochanteric prominence |
| Medial fat pad of the knees |

Palpation pressure should be **4 kg/cm2** (enough to whiten the examiner's nail bed).

**Limitations of the 1990 criteria:** examiners did not know how to examine tender points, did it wrongly or refused; important symptoms (fatigue, sleep, cognition) were left out; the threshold was so high it left little room for variation; patients whose symptoms improved failed the criteria. A 2011 modification and a 2016 modification followed.

### 2010 ACR preliminary diagnostic criteria

A patient meets criteria if all three are present:

1. **WPI at least 7 and SS score at least 5**, **or** WPI 3 to 6 and SS score at least 9.
2. Symptoms at a similar level for at least **3 months**.
3. No other disorder that would otherwise explain the pain.

| Scale | How it is scored |
|---|---|
| **Widespread Pain Index (WPI)** | Number of areas (out of 19) where the patient had pain in the previous week; score 0 to 19 |
| **Symptom Severity (SS) scale** | Severity over the past week of **fatigue, waking unrefreshed, cognitive symptoms** (each 0 to 3), total 0 to 12 (the full SS scale also scores the extent of other somatic symptoms) |

> **Check:** one slide states "SS of at least 7 and WPI of at least 15". This is inconsistent with the criteria on the previous slide. The correct 2010 criteria are **WPI at least 7 with SS at least 5, or WPI 3 to 6 with SS at least 9**.

### Investigations

- **No abnormalities on routine blood tests or imaging.** Tests are done to **screen for other conditions** that could explain some or all of the symptoms (for example FBC, ESR/CRP, thyroid function, CK, calcium, vitamin D, autoantibodies as indicated).

## 6. Differential diagnosis

FM can mimic many conditions because its symptoms are non-specific.

| Condition | Clues that separate it from FM |
|---|---|
| **Polymyalgia rheumatica** | Age over 50, shoulder and hip girdle stiffness, **raised ESR/CRP**, rapid steroid response |
| **Spondyloarthritis** | Inflammatory back pain, morning stiffness, raised inflammatory markers, HLA-B27, sacroiliitis |
| **Inflammatory myopathy** | True proximal muscle **weakness**, raised CK |
| **Systemic inflammatory arthropathies** (RA, SLE) | Synovitis, positive autoantibodies, raised inflammatory markers |
| **Hypothyroidism** | Raised TSH, low free T4 |

Also consider depression, chronic fatigue syndrome and vitamin D deficiency.

## 7. Management

Approach: **educate**, **exercise**, then **drug therapy** for those not responding to non-drug measures.

### Patient education

Patients should understand the illness before any drug is prescribed.

- **Reassure** that fibromyalgia is a real disease.
- Role of stress and mood: teach **relaxation techniques** and offer stress reduction programmes.
- **Good sleep hygiene**; recognise and treat sleep disorders that contribute (for example sleep apnoea, restless legs).
- Cognitive behavioural therapy is also helpful.

### Exercise

- **Cardiovascular fitness training** reduces pain and improves sleep.
- At least **30 minutes of aerobic exercise 3 times a week** at a heart rate near the target range. Start low and increase slowly.

### Drugs

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Tricyclic antidepressants** | **Amitriptyline**; **cyclobenzaprine** (a tricyclic-related muscle relaxant) |
| **SNRIs** | **Duloxetine, milnacipran** |
| **SSRIs** | Some benefit |
| **Anticonvulsants (gabapentinoids)** | **Gabapentin, pregabalin** |

- The three drugs **FDA-approved** for fibromyalgia are **pregabalin, duloxetine and milnacipran**.
- Simple analgesics may be tried; **strong opioids are not recommended** and NSAIDs/steroids are of little value because there is no inflammation.

## 8. Prognosis and complications

- Long-term studies (mainly from tertiary centres) show most patients continue to have **chronic pain and fatigue**; patients treated in primary care in the community have a **much better prognosis**.
- **Poor prognostic factors:** female sex, low socioeconomic status, unemployment, obesity, depression, history of abuse.
- **Complications:** persistent "fibro fog" with memory and concentration problems; patients are more likely to be hospitalised for any reason than the general population. Mood disorders and disability are common.

## Key points to revise

1. Fibromyalgia is chronic widespread pain with fatigue, non-restorative sleep and cognitive symptoms, without inflammation.
2. The mechanism is central sensitisation, with altered serotonin, substance P, cortisol and growth hormone, and disturbed delta-wave sleep.
3. Female predominance; peak prevalence in older women; risk factors include psychosocial stress, abuse and poor sleep.
4. 1990 ACR: widespread pain plus at least 11 of 18 tender points at 4 kg/cm2.
5. 2010 ACR: WPI at least 7 and SS at least 5, or WPI 3 to 6 and SS at least 9, for at least 3 months with no other explanation.
6. Routine labs and imaging are normal; exclude polymyalgia rheumatica, spondyloarthritis, myositis, inflammatory arthritis and hypothyroidism.
7. Management: educate, aerobic exercise (30 minutes, 3 times a week), sleep hygiene, then amitriptyline, duloxetine, milnacipran, pregabalin or gabapentin.
8. Outlook: usually chronic, but better in primary care populations; depression, obesity, low income and abuse worsen outcome.
