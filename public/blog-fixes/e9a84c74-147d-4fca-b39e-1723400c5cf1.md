# Pneumothorax

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Respiratory Medicine**

> **In one line:** pneumothorax is air in the pleural space. The negative pleural pressure is lost, so the lung recoils and collapses, causing hypoxia. If a ball-valve leak lets air in but not out (tension), pressure builds, compresses the heart and great veins, and causes obstructive shock. Small primary episodes can be observed or aspirated, secondary or large ones need a drain, and tension pneumothorax is decompressed at once without waiting for an X-ray.

## What you must know

- **Definition:** air or gas in the pleural space, outside the lung.
- **Primary spontaneous (PSP):** tall, thin young male smokers; rupture of apical subpleural blebs. **Secondary (SSP):** underlying lung disease, **COPD** most common, also TB, asthma, HIV with *Pneumocystis*.
- Signs: reduced expansion, **hyper-resonance**, absent fremitus, reduced or absent breath sounds.
- **Tension pneumothorax** = hypotension, hypoxia, distended neck veins; **clinical diagnosis, decompress before X-ray**.
- BTS size cut-off is a **2 cm rim** at the hilum. PSP: **aspirate first**; SSP over 2 cm: **chest drain**.
- Drain in the **safe triangle**, above the upper border of the rib; never clamp a bubbling drain.
- Recurrence: about one third after the first episode, half after the second; surgery (VATS pleurodesis) prevents it.

## 1. Introduction and definition

- **Laennec** described the clinical picture in **1819**; **Kjaergard (1932)** described primary spontaneous pneumothorax in healthy people.
- Incidence of primary pneumothorax: **18 to 28 per 100,000 per year in men** and **1.2 to 6 per 100,000 per year in women**.
- **Definition:** presence of air or gas in the pleural space, that is, air outside the lung within the pleural cavity.

## 2. Mechanism and pathophysiology

- Normal pleural pressure is **negative throughout the respiratory cycle** (about -5 cmH₂O), set by two opposing forces: the **outward pull of the chest wall** and the **elastic recoil of the lung**.
- When a communication forms between an alveolus (or the atmosphere) and the pleural space, **air flows in until the pressure difference disappears or the leak seals**.
- **Loss of negative pressure:** the lung recoils, volume falls, there is low V/Q and an anatomical shunt, giving **hypoxia**, reduced vital capacity and reduced PaO₂.
- **Positive pressure (tension):** compresses the great veins and heart, so venous return and cardiac output fall, causing **hypotension** (obstructive shock).

## 3. Classification

| Group | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Spontaneous** | Primary (no lung disease) | Young, healthy people |
| | Secondary (underlying lung disease) | Older, worse tolerated |
| **Acquired** | Traumatic (blunt or penetrating) | Rib fractures, stab or gunshot wounds |
| | Iatrogenic | Procedures, positive-pressure ventilation |

### Primary spontaneous pneumothorax

Rupture of **apical subpleural blebs or bullae** in young healthy people. Associations:

- **Smoking** (up to 20-fold risk)
- **Tall, thin males**
- Distal airway inflammation and structural bronchial abnormalities
- Genetic contribution (for example Birt-Hogg-Dube syndrome)

### Secondary spontaneous pneumothorax

| Common causes | Rarer causes |
|---|---|
| **COPD** (most common) | Interstitial lung disease, sarcoidosis |
| **Tuberculosis** | Eosinophilic granuloma (histiocytosis X) |
| **Asthma** | Lymphangioleiomyomatosis |
| Suppurative pneumonia | AIDS (*Pneumocystis* pneumonia) |
| Cystic fibrosis | Lung carcinoma, chemotherapy |
| | Scleroderma, **Marfan syndrome**, rheumatoid disease |
| | Pulmonary infarct, granulomatosis with polyangiitis |

> **Exam tip:** SSP is more dangerous than PSP. Poor lung reserve means even a small pneumothorax can cause respiratory failure. In Kenya, remember **TB** and HIV-related lung disease.

### Traumatic pneumothorax

- **Accidental:** blunt trauma with rib fractures; stab or gunshot wounds.
- **Iatrogenic:** **positive-pressure ventilation** (alveolar rupture, interstitial emphysema, often bilateral); biopsy, thoracocentesis, central venous lines, tracheostomy.

## 4. Clinical types

| | Closed | Open | Tension |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pleural tear | **Sealed** | **Open** | **Ball-valve** |
| Pleural pressure | **Below atmospheric** | **Equal to atmospheric** | **Above atmospheric** |

## 5. Clinical features

- **Constant:** sudden **dyspnoea** and **pleuritic chest pain**.
- **Uncommon:** cough, haemoptysis, orthopnoea, cyanosis, tachycardia, anxiety, vague malaise.

### Examination

Depends on size; vital signs are often normal in small ones.

- Reduced chest movement on the affected side
- Trachea shifted **away** if large
- **Tactile fremitus absent**
- **Hyper-resonant** percussion
- Breath sounds **reduced or absent**
- Liver edge displaced downwards in a right-sided pneumothorax
- Subcutaneous emphysema (crackling under the fingers)

### Tension pneumothorax

- Classically **hypotension plus hypoxia**; life-threatening.
- Rapidly progressive breathlessness; circulatory collapse (tachycardia, hypotension, sweating).
- **Distended neck veins**; tracheal deviation is a **late** sign.
- X-ray: mediastinal shift, flattened hemidiaphragm, lung collapse.
- More common with **positive-pressure ventilation** and **trauma**.
- **In ICU:** suspect in any ventilated or resuscitated patient who suddenly deteriorates with dyspnoea, cyanosis, marked tachycardia, hypotension and **airway pressure alarms**.

> **Exam tip:** tension pneumothorax is a **clinical diagnosis. Decompress before any X-ray.**

## 6. Investigations

### Erect PA chest X-ray

- **Visceral pleural line** with **no lung markings** beyond it.
- BTS size cut-off: **2 cm rim** at the level of the hilum.
- **Supine film:** **deep sulcus sign**, hyperlucent hemithorax.

### CT chest

- Useful when the lungs are obscured by surgical emphysema
- Distinguishes pneumothorax from a large **bulla**
- Shows occult pneumothorax; surgical planning

### Bedside ultrasound

- Loss of **lung sliding**, absent B-lines, "barcode" (stratosphere) sign on M-mode. The **lung point** is diagnostic.

## 7. Management

**Goals:** re-expand the lung, remove the cause, prevent recurrence.

### BTS approach to spontaneous pneumothorax

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Bilateral or unstable | **Chest drain** |
| **Primary**, not breathless, rim under 2 cm | Discharge; review in 2 to 4 weeks |
| **Primary**, breathless or rim over 2 cm | **Aspirate (16 to 18G cannula)**; if successful discharge and review, if it fails chest drain |
| **Secondary**, rim over 2 cm or breathless | **Chest drain** |
| **Secondary**, rim 1 to 2 cm | Aspirate; if it fails, drain; admit for 24 hours |
| **Secondary**, rim under 1 cm | Admit, high-flow oxygen, observe |

### Oxygen therapy

- High-concentration oxygen **lowers pleural capillary nitrogen**, increasing the gradient for absorption.
- Natural resorption is about **1.25 to 1.8% of hemithorax volume per 24 hours**; oxygen gives about a **4-fold increase**.
- Caution in CO₂-retaining COPD.

### Simple aspiration

- **First line for all primary pneumothorax needing intervention.**
- In secondary pneumothorax, only for **small (under 2 cm), minimally breathless patients under 50**; admit and observe for **at least 24 hours**.
- A repeat aspiration is reasonable in PSP if **under 2.5 L** was removed the first time.

### Needle decompression (tension)

- **14 to 16G cannula** in the **2nd intercostal space, mid-clavicular line**, or the **4th to 5th space just anterior to the mid-axillary line**.
- Always follow with a **chest drain**.

### Intercostal tube drainage

**Indications:** unstable pneumothorax, severe dyspnoea, large collapse, open or tension pneumothorax, recurrence, failed aspiration, bilateral, ventilated patients, haemopneumothorax.

**Safe triangle:**

| Border | Landmark |
|---|---|
| Anterior | Lateral border of **pectoralis major** |
| Posterior | Lateral border of **latissimus dorsi** (mid-axillary line) |
| Inferior | **Nipple level (5th intercostal space)** |
| Apex | Below the axilla |

- Insert **above the upper border of the rib** to avoid the neurovascular bundle.
- **Underwater seal:** bottle primed with about 200 mL saline; the fluid level should **swing with respiration**.
- **No bubbling** may mean the lung has re-expanded, the tube is blocked, or the tube holes lie in the chest wall.
- Remove about **24 hours after re-expansion**. **Never clamp a bubbling drain.**

### Surgical treatment

**Indications:** no response to medical treatment, **persistent air leak (over 3 to 5 days)**, haemopneumothorax, **bilateral** or **recurrent** pneumothorax, tension that fails to drain, thickened pleura preventing re-expansion, multiple blebs, high-risk jobs (pilots, divers).

**Pleurectomy and pleurodesis** by **VATS** or thoracotomy aim to:

1. Seal lung leaks
2. Remove blebs and bullae (**bullectomy**)
3. Fuse visceral to parietal pleura so the lung cannot fully collapse

**Methods:** **pleurectomy** (stripping parietal pleura), **pleural abrasion** (scourer), **chemical pleurodesis** (usually **talc**).

## 8. Complications

- Pyopneumothorax, hydropneumothorax, **haemopneumothorax**
- **Mediastinal and subcutaneous emphysema**
- Re-expansion pulmonary oedema
- Bronchopleural fistula, respiratory failure, cardiac arrest

## 9. Recurrence and advice

- After a first episode about **one third** recur; after a second about **one half** have a third; after three, recurrence is likely to continue.
- Advice: **stop smoking**; **no flying until X-ray resolution**; **diving is contraindicated** unless surgical pleurectomy has been done.

## Key points to revise

1. Pneumothorax is air in the pleural space; negative pleural pressure is lost and the lung collapses.
2. PSP: tall, thin young male smoker with apical blebs. SSP: COPD most common (also TB, asthma, HIV).
3. Signs: reduced expansion, hyper-resonance, absent fremitus and breath sounds.
4. Tension: hypotension, hypoxia and distended neck veins. Decompress first with a large cannula, then drain.
5. The BTS cut-off is 2 cm; PSP gets aspiration first, SSP over 2 cm gets a drain.
6. Safe triangle: pectoralis major, latissimus dorsi, 5th intercostal space; go above the rib.
7. Surgery (VATS pleurodesis, bullectomy) for persistent leak over 3 to 5 days, recurrence, bilateral disease and high-risk jobs.
8. Advise on smoking, flying and diving.
