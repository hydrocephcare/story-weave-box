# Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Rheumatology**

> **In one line:** SLE is a multisystem autoimmune disease of unknown cause, mainly in young women, in which **autoantibodies against nuclear antigens form immune complexes** that deposit in tissues (skin, joints, kidney, brain, serosa) and activate complement. The course is one of **remissions and relapses**, and almost any organ can be involved.

## What you must know

- Female : male is about **9 : 1**, peaking in the reproductive years; sex hormones, genes, UV light, drugs and infection all contribute.
- **ANA** is the screening test (sensitive, not specific); **anti-dsDNA** and **anti-Sm** are the lupus-specific antibodies.
- Commonest presenting complaints: **fatigue, fever, weight loss**, with skin and joint disease in most patients.
- **Pericarditis** is the commonest cardiac lesion, **pleurisy** the commonest pulmonary lesion, and **lupus nephritis** is the commonest serious organ complication.
- Falling **C3/C4 with rising anti-dsDNA** signals active disease (complement consumption).
- Diagnosis uses classification criteria: **ACR (4 of 11)** or **SLICC 2012** (4 criteria with at least 1 clinical and 1 immunological, or biopsy-proven lupus nephritis with ANA or anti-dsDNA).
- Core treatment: **hydroxychloroquine for everyone**, steroids for flares, immunosuppressants (cyclophosphamide, azathioprine, mycophenolate) for organ-threatening disease.
- With good care, 10-year survival is **over 90%** (slides also quote above 85%); infection is the leading cause of death.

## 1. Definition and epidemiology

- **Systemic lupus erythematosus** is a syndrome of unknown aetiology, most commonly affecting young women. Virtually any organ may be involved.
- Female : male = **9 : 1**; the difference is greatest in the reproductive years.
- The typical course is a series of **remissions and exacerbations**.
- In Africa and in people of African ancestry, disease tends to be more severe, with more nephritis; in Kenya, think of SLE in any young woman with fever, arthritis, rash and cytopenia, and remember HIV and TB are the main mimics.

## 2. Aetiology and pathogenesis

### 2.1 Genetic factors

| Feature | Figure |
|---|---|
| Patients with a first or second degree relative with lupus | 5 to 13% |
| Concordance in **monozygotic** twins | 24 to 58% |
| Concordance in dizygotic twins / siblings | 2 to 5% |
| Risk to a child of a lupus parent | 3 to 4% at worst |

- Most predisposing genes are **normal polymorphisms**. An unlucky combination each permits a small immune over-response or high presentation of target antigens; together they allow SLE to develop after an environmental trigger.
- Associations: **C2, C4 and C1q deficiencies** (early complement components; impaired immune-complex and apoptotic cell clearance), **HLA-DR2 and DR3**, the 1q41-42 region, Fc-gamma RIIA, IL-10 and Bcl polymorphisms.

### 2.2 Environmental factors

- **UV light (especially UVB)** flares SLE in most patients. Onset after a sunburn is not unusual. UV alters DNA chemistry and the location and availability of **Ro and RNP antigens** on skin cells.
- **Drugs**: hydralazine, procainamide, beta-blockers, isoniazid, penicillamine can induce lupus (see section 11).
- **Infection**: possible trigger or flare factor through **molecular mimicry**, epitope spreading and non-specific T or B cell activation. EBV, CMV and other viruses are of interest.
- **Allergy**: no direct evidence that it flares lupus.

### 2.3 Sex hormones

- In mice, castration of females or androgens/anti-oestrogens protect, whereas castration of males or oestrogens worsen disease.
- Lupus patients metabolise testosterone faster than normal, and oestrogenic metabolites of oestradiol persist longer in women.
- Neuroendocrine changes: **hyperprolactinaemia** and hypothalamic or pituitary abnormalities.

### 2.4 Abnormal immune system

- Sustained presence of autoantigens: **increased apoptosis and impaired clearance of apoptotic material**.
- Hyperactive B and T lymphocytes with increased surface activation molecules; overproduction of **IL-6 and IL-10**; defective regulation.
- Autoantibodies to DNA, RNA and many nuclear antigens. **Circulating immune complexes** deposit in kidney, skin, brain, lung and other tissues and cause damage mainly through **complement fixation and activation** (a type III hypersensitivity reaction; type II for cytopenias).

> **Remember:** the pathogenic sequence is genetic susceptibility + trigger (UV, infection, hormones, drug) + defective clearance of apoptotic debris, leading to autoantibodies, immune complexes, complement activation and tissue injury.

## 3. Clinical features

The clinical spectrum is very broad, which is why SLE is easy to miss.

### 3.1 General

- Fatigue, fever and weight loss are the commonest initial complaints.
- **Fever** from active disease occurs in 50 to 86% with no characteristic pattern. It is important to distinguish lupus fever from fever caused by complicating infection.
- **Fatigue** is common during activity and is often the only symptom left after a flare is treated; anaemia and low-grade inflammation add to it.
- **Raynaud's phenomenon** (white, then blue, then red colour change of the digits in cold or emotion) is common but non-specific.

### 3.2 Skin and mucosa (up to 85%)

| Lesion | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Malar (butterfly) rash** | Fixed erythema over cheeks and nasal bridge, **sparing the nasolabial folds**; flat or raised; worse in sunlight |
| Maculopapular eruption | Photosensitive |
| **Discoid lupus** | Red raised patches with keratotic scaling and follicular plugging; heals with scarring |
| Relapsing non-suppurative nodular panniculitis | Lupus profundus |
| Vasculitic lesions, purpura, livedo reticularis | Immune-complex vasculitis |
| **Alopecia** | Diffuse or patchy |
| **Oral or nasal ulcers** | Usually **painless**; document them |

### 3.3 Musculoskeletal

- **Arthritis** is usually symmetrical, **non-deforming and non-erosive**; swelling and tenderness must be present. Hands, wrists and knees are most often involved (Jaccoud arthropathy is the rare deforming exception).
- Myositis or muscle involvement in 30 to 50%.
- **Avascular necrosis** of bone (femoral head) may result from the disease itself or from **prednisolone** therapy.

### 3.4 Renal

- Haematuria, **proteinuria (more than 0.5 g/day or 3+)**, and cellular casts. May progress to nephrotic syndrome, hypertension and renal failure. See section 6 for the biopsy classification.

### 3.5 Nervous system

- Brain, nerve and psychiatric syndromes affect up to **two-thirds**: seizures, cranial or peripheral nerve palsy, severe depression, psychosis, stroke, headache.
- Spinal cord involvement is rare and mainly due to thrombosis of a vessel supplying the cord (transverse myelitis).
- Always exclude steroid psychosis, infection and uraemia.

### 3.6 Haematological

| Cell line | Feature |
|---|---|
| Red cells | Normochromic normocytic anaemia of chronic inflammation (also drug-related blood loss); **autoimmune haemolytic anaemia** (Coombs positive); rarely a red-cell production inhibitor (pure red cell aplasia) |
| Platelets | **Thrombocytopenia (below 100 x 10^9/L)** from anti-platelet or antiphospholipid antibodies |
| White cells | **Leucopenia (below 4.0 x 10^9/L)** from autoantibodies, reduced marrow output, splenic pooling and complement activation; immunosuppressive drugs add to it |

### 3.7 Lung

- **Pleurisy** is the commonest; effusions are small to moderate, unilateral or bilateral, usually **exudative**. Large effusions are uncommon.
- Other causes of effusion in SLE: nephrotic syndrome, infection, cardiac failure.
- Others: lupus pneumonitis, interstitial lung disease, pulmonary hypertension, shrinking lung, pulmonary embolism (APS).

### 3.8 Heart

- **Pericarditis** is the commonest cardiac manifestation.
- Myocarditis (resembles viral myocarditis).
- **Libman-Sacks endocarditis**: flat, reddish-tan sterile vegetations on the mitral valve and chordae (on both surfaces of the leaflets); may cause regurgitation.
- Hypertension, heart failure, accelerated atherosclerosis.

### 3.9 Gastrointestinal and hepatic

- Oesophagitis, dysphagia, nausea and vomiting (often drug-related).
- Chronic intestinal pseudo-obstruction, mesenteric vasculitis, protein-losing enteropathy, pancreatitis, lupus hepatitis.

### 3.10 Eye

- The retina is the usual site (cotton-wool spots). Patients on **chloroquine or hydroxychloroquine** need ophthalmology screening for retinal toxicity.

### 3.11 Overlap features

- **Secondary Sjogren syndrome**: dry eyes and dry mouth from lymphocytic infiltration of exocrine glands.
- **Secondary antiphospholipid syndrome**: recurrent arterial or venous thrombosis, fetal loss, thrombocytopenia, high-titre antiphospholipid antibodies (DVT presents with a red, warm, swollen, tender leg).
- The spleen shows a characteristic **periarteriolar ("onion-skin") fibrosis**.

## 4. Investigations

### 4.1 General tests

- FBC (anaemia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia), **ESR raised** while **CRP is often normal** (a raised CRP suggests infection or serositis), urinalysis, urine protein/creatinine ratio, urea and creatinine, liver function tests.
- **Complement (C3, C4)** low in active disease; immune complexes; anti-dsDNA titre for monitoring.
- Direct Coombs test; coagulation screen (prolonged APTT that does not correct suggests lupus anticoagulant).

### 4.2 Autoantibodies

| Group | Antibodies |
|---|---|
| To nuclear components | **ANA**, **anti-dsDNA**, ENA: **anti-Sm**, anti-RNP, anti-Jo1 |
| To cytoplasmic antigens | **Anti-Ro (SSA), anti-La (SSB)** |
| Cell-specific | Lymphocytotoxic, anti-neuronal, anti-erythrocyte, anti-platelet |
| To serum components | **Antiphospholipid antibodies** (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-beta-2 glycoprotein I), anticoagulants, rheumatoid factor |

- The **LE cell** test is obsolete, replaced by ANA and anti-dsDNA.
- **ANA** is the screening test: very sensitive (over 95%) but not specific. A negative ANA makes SLE very unlikely.
- **Anti-dsDNA and anti-Sm** are lupus-specific. Anti-dsDNA correlates with disease activity and nephritis.

### 4.3 ANA patterns (HEp-2 immunofluorescence)

| Pattern | Typical antibody |
|---|---|
| Homogeneous (diffuse) | Most common pattern in autoimmune disease overall; anti-histone, anti-dsDNA |
| **Rim (peripheral)** | **dsDNA** |
| Nucleolar | Systemic sclerosis |
| Speckled | Sm, SSA, SSB, RNP, Scl-70 |

- **Crithidia luciliae test**: the kinetoplast fluoresces with anti-native DNA antibody, which is very specific for SLE.
- **Immunoblotting** detects anti-Sm, RNP, SSA, SSB, Jo1, Scl-70 and ribosomal P.

### 4.4 Biopsy

- **Lupus band test**: immunofluorescence for IgG shows a band of immune complexes at the dermo-epidermal junction. In SLE it is positive in **involved and uninvolved skin**; in discoid lupus (DLE) only in involved skin.
- **Kidney biopsy** classifies nephritis and guides therapy (see below).

## 5. Lupus nephritis classification

The WHO classification is based on light microscopy, immunofluorescence and electron microscopy (the ISN/RPS 2003 classification is now preferred).

| Class | Pattern | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| I | Normal | Minimal mesangial |
| II (A/B) | Mesangial deposits (IIA) or mesangial hypercellularity (IIB) | Mild; few urine abnormalities |
| III | **Focal** segmental proliferative GN | Needs immunosuppression |
| IV | **Diffuse** proliferative GN | Commonest and worst; subendothelial deposits, "wire loops" |
| V | **Membranous** GN | Nephrotic syndrome, thrombosis risk |

**Activity and chronicity indices**

- Active lesions: cellular proliferation, necrosis, karyorrhexis, cellular crescents, wire loops, hyaline thrombi, leucocyte and interstitial infiltration.
- Chronic lesions: glomerular sclerosis, fibrous crescents, interstitial fibrosis, tubular atrophy.
- Each is scored 0 to 3 (necrosis, karyorrhexis and cellular crescents double-weighted): **maximum activity 24, maximum chronicity 12**.

> **Exam tip:** active disease is treatable; chronicity (scarring) is not. A high chronicity index predicts renal failure and little benefit from more immunosuppression.

## 6. Differential diagnosis

- Too broad because of the many manifestations.
- **Rheumatic**: rheumatoid arthritis, Sjogren syndrome, systemic sclerosis, dermatomyositis.
- **Non-rheumatic**: **HIV**, infective endocarditis, viral infections, haematological malignancy, vasculitis, ITP, other causes of nephritis, TB.
- **Overlap syndrome (mixed connective tissue disease)**: features of SLE, scleroderma and myositis with high anti-RNP.

## 7. Diagnosis

Diagnosis combines clinical and laboratory evidence.

### 7.1 ACR criteria (1982, revised 1997)

**4 of the 11** give sensitivity 85% and specificity 95%.

1. **Malar rash**
2. **Discoid rash**
3. **Photosensitivity**
4. **Oral ulcers**
5. **Arthritis** (2 or more peripheral joints with tenderness, swelling or effusion)
6. **Serositis** (pleuritis or pericarditis)
7. **Renal disorder** (proteinuria over 0.5 g/24 h or 3+, or cellular casts)
8. **Neurological** (seizures or psychosis, with other causes such as drugs excluded)
9. **Haematological** (haemolytic anaemia, leucopenia or thrombocytopenia)
10. **Immunological** (LE cells, raised anti-native DNA, anti-Sm, or false-positive syphilis serology)
11. **Positive ANA**

### 7.2 SLICC 2012

- SLE is classified if there is **biopsy-proven lupus nephritis with ANA or anti-dsDNA**, or **4 criteria including at least 1 clinical and 1 immunological**.

### 7.3 Worked cases from the lecture

| Case | Findings | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Malar rash, discoid rash, photosensitivity, oral ulcers | **Not SLE** | 4 criteria but **no immunological criterion** |
| 2 | Proteinuria 0.6 g/24 h with casts, pancytopenia (leucopenia, haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia), raised anti-dsDNA, positive ANA | **Not SLE** by this teaching point | 4 criteria but no clinical criterion in the SLICC sense of the case as framed |
| 3 | Malar rash, discoid rash, arthritis (knee and ankle), positive ANA | **SLE** | 4 criteria including clinical and immunological |
| 4 | Renal biopsy showing lupus nephritis, anti-Smith positive | **SLE** | Biopsy-proven nephritis with an immunological criterion meets SLICC |

> **Check:** under the strict SLICC rule, renal, haematological and immunological findings in case 2 are in fact clinical plus immunological criteria and would normally qualify for SLE. The slide's verdict follows the lecturer's framing; in exams, apply the SLICC rule (at least 1 clinical and 1 immunological) carefully to the criteria listed.

## 8. Lupus-related syndromes

### 8.1 Drug-induced lupus

- Classically **hydralazine, isoniazid, procainamide** (also penicillamine, beta-blockers).
- Male : female **equal**; nephritis and CNS disease rare.
- **Normal complement, no anti-dsDNA**; anti-histone antibodies positive.
- Symptoms resolve on stopping the drug.

### 8.2 Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS)

- Hypercoagulability with recurrent **venous or arterial thrombosis**; thrombocytopenia common; **first-trimester miscarriage** and other pregnancy loss.
- **Primary** when it occurs without SLE features; **secondary** when SLE features are present.
- Long-term **warfarin** is recommended after serious thrombosis because recurrence is common.
- In pregnancy: warfarin is teratogenic, so use **subcutaneous heparin plus aspirin**.

### 8.3 Raynaud's

- Not part of the diagnostic criteria; do not request ANA for isolated Raynaud's with no other evidence of autoimmune disease.

## 9. SLE and pregnancy

- SLE commonly affects women in the childbearing years. Flares in pregnancy are uncommon and usually easy to treat (arthritis, rash, fatigue).
- It raises the risk of **spontaneous abortion, intrauterine death, pre-eclampsia, growth restriction and preterm birth**. Lupus nephritis can worsen.
- Best outcomes when SLE is **quiescent for at least 6 months** before conception and renal function is normal or near normal.
- Pregnancy is no longer "contraindicated". Avoid fetotoxic drugs only (cyclophosphamide, methotrexate, mycophenolate, warfarin); continue hydroxychloroquine.
- Risks relate to renal failure, antiphospholipid antibodies and **anti-Ro/La** (neonatal lupus, congenital heart block).
- Follow up with an obstetrician experienced in high-risk care.

## 10. Management

### 10.1 Principles

- Lifelong regular follow-up. Advise **avoiding UV light** (sunscreen, protective clothing), infections, extreme stress and fatigue; stop smoking.
- Monitor: FBC, ESR, C3, immune complexes, liver function and anti-dsDNA, plus urine and blood pressure.
- **Grade disease activity** first: non-life-threatening (arthralgia, rash, Raynaud's, alopecia) versus severe (renal, cerebral, cardiac).

### 10.2 Drug groups

| Group | Examples | Use and points |
|---|---|---|
| **NSAIDs** | Ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac | Mild pain and arthritis; inhibit prostaglandin synthesis; use cautiously in renal disease |
| **Antimalarials** | **Hydroxychloroquine** 400 mg PO once or twice daily, maintenance 200 to 400 mg daily | Skin and joint disease, reduces flares; inhibits eosinophil chemotaxis and neutrophil locomotion, impairs complement-dependent antigen-antibody reactions; check FBC periodically and screen the retina |
| **Corticosteroids** | Prednisolone | See table below |
| **Immunosuppressants** | Cyclophosphamide, methotrexate, azathioprine, mycophenolate | Steroid-sparing; nephritis, CNS and severe disease |
| **Immunomodulators** | Belimumab, rituximab | Refractory disease |

### 10.3 Mild disease

- NSAID and/or antimalarial. Prednisolone remains the first choice to control activity; low dose **10 mg/day or less**.

### 10.4 Corticosteroid dosing by feature

| Feature | Initial prednisolone |
|---|---|
| Arthritis poorly responsive to NSAIDs, pleuritis, pericarditis | 20 to 30 mg/day, reduce by about 5 mg/week as symptoms abate |
| Haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia | 1 mg/kg/day for about 1 month; reduce by 10 mg/day if blood tests improve |
| Nephritis | 1 mg/kg/day for about 1 month |
| Neuropsychiatric lupus | Controversial: 1 to 2 mg/kg/day, or **methylprednisolone 0.5 to 1 g/day** (pulse) |

### 10.5 Special situations and other therapy

- **Antiphospholipid syndrome**: warfarin long term; heparin and aspirin in pregnancy.
- **Plasma exchange**, **IV immunoglobulin**, **stem cell transplantation**, immune therapy (anti-IL-10, anti-CD20, tolerance therapy).
- **Novel therapy**: immunoablative chemotherapy with or without autologous stem-cell transplant; B-cell toleragen; complement inhibitors (anti-C5, soluble CR1); adhesion molecule inhibitors (anti-ICAM-1, anti-CD11b/CD18); co-stimulation inhibitors (anti-CTLA-4, anti-CD40 ligand).

### 10.6 Biologics

| Drug | Mechanism | Use and dose |
|---|---|---|
| **Belimumab** | Inhibits **BLyS** (B-lymphocyte stimulator), needed for B-cell survival and maturation to plasma cells | Active, autoantibody-positive SLE failing standard therapy; **10 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks for 3 doses, then every 4 weeks**. Not studied in severe active lupus nephritis or CNS lupus, or with other biologics or IV cyclophosphamide |
| **Rituximab** | Anti-CD20 B-cell depletion | Mixed results; useful rescue in refractory disease. 1000 mg IV, repeat after 2 weeks (one course); repeat every 24 weeks (not sooner than 16 weeks); with methotrexate; premedicate with glucocorticoid 30 minutes before; maximum 1000 mg per dose |

> **Remember:** steroid-related complications (avascular necrosis, osteoporosis, infection, diabetes) cause much of the long-term harm, so use the lowest effective dose and add steroid-sparing agents early.

## 11. Prognosis and prevention

- Unpredictable course; 10-year survival over 85 to 90%.
- Most deaths are from **infection**, related to immunosuppression; then renal and cardiovascular disease.
- Preventive care: smoking cessation, yearly influenza vaccine, pneumococcal vaccine every 5 years, cancer screening, bone protection, BP and lipid control.

## Key points to revise

1. SLE is a multisystem autoimmune disease, 9 : 1 female, with remissions and relapses.
2. Triggers: UV light, infection, hormones, drugs (hydralazine, procainamide, isoniazid, penicillamine).
3. ANA screens; anti-dsDNA and anti-Sm are specific; low C3/C4 plus high anti-dsDNA means active disease.
4. Commonest organ lesions: pericarditis (heart), pleurisy (lung), nephritis (kidney); arthritis is non-deforming.
5. Diagnose with ACR (4 of 11) or SLICC (4 including 1 clinical and 1 immunological, or biopsy-proven nephritis plus ANA/dsDNA).
6. Lupus nephritis classes I to V; class IV diffuse is worst; activity index maximum 24, chronicity maximum 12.
7. Treat with hydroxychloroquine for all, steroids by organ, cyclophosphamide/azathioprine/mycophenolate for severe disease, belimumab/rituximab for refractory.
8. APS needs warfarin (heparin plus aspirin in pregnancy); plan pregnancy when disease has been quiet for 6 months.
