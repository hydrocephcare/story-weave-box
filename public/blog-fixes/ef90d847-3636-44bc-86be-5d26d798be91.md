## Summary
This document provides a comprehensive overview of various cardiac conditions, including cardiomyopathies, pericardial diseases, congenital heart defects, and cardiac tumors. It delves into their definitions, causes, pathophysiology, gross and microscopic pathology, clinical features, and key diagnostic points, often using mnemonics and comparison tables for clarity.

## Key Points
- Cardiomyopathies are categorized into Dilated (DCM), Hypertrophic (HCM), and Restrictive (RCM), each with distinct causes, pathophysiology, and clinical presentations.
- Pericardial diseases include pericarditis, pericardial effusion, cardiac tamponade, and constrictive pericarditis, differentiated by their acute vs. chronic nature and specific diagnostic findings.
- Congenital heart defects are broadly classified into acyanotic (left-to-right shunts) and cyanotic (right-to-left shunts), with specific conditions like VSD, ASD, PDA, TOF, TGA, and coarctation of the aorta detailed.
- Cardiac tumors, though rare, are discussed, with myxoma being the most common primary tumor in adults and rhabdomyoma in children, each presenting with unique clinical scenarios.

## PART 1: CARDIOMYOPATHIES

## Definition
Disease of the heart muscle itself — NOT caused by coronary artery disease, hypertension, or valvular disease

## There Are 3 Types — Remember **"DHR"**
- **D**ilated Cardiomyopathy
- **H**ypertrophic Cardiomyopathy
- **R**estrictive Cardiomyopathy

## 1A. DILATED CARDIOMYOPATHY (DCM)

## Definition
All 4 chambers dilate + systolic dysfunction → heart cannot CONTRACT properly

## Causes — Remember **"ABCDE"**
- **A**lcohol (most common toxic cause)
- **B**eri-beri (Thiamine/B1 deficiency)
- **C**oxsackievirus B (viral myocarditis → DCM)
- **D**rugs (Doxorubicin/adriamycin — chemotherapy)
- **E**clampsia/pregnancy (Peripartum cardiomyopathy)

| **Hemochromatosis** | Iron deposition in myocardium |
| **Sarcoidosis** | Granulomas in myocardium |
| **Thyroid disease** | Hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism |
| **Cocaine** | Toxic cardiomyopathy |

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Age** | Any age, peak 20-60 |
| **Symptoms** | Progressive heart failure — dyspnea, fatigue, orthopnea |
| **Signs** | S3 gallop, displaced apex beat, JVP raised |

- Peripartum cardiomyopathy → last month of pregnancy or 5 months postpartum
- **Worst prognosis** of all cardiomyopathies

## 1B. HYPERTROPHIC CARDIOMYOPATHY (HCM)

## Definition
Massive hypertrophy of the LEFT VENTRICLE (especially septum) WITHOUT dilation → diastolic dysfunction

## Key Feature
**Asymmetric Septal Hypertrophy (ASH)** — interventricular septum thicker than LV free wall

## Cause
**Autosomal dominant** genetic mutation — mutations in sarcomere proteins
Most common: **Beta-myosin heavy chain** or **myosin binding protein C** mutations

## Clinical Features
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Age** | Young adults, athletes |
| **Symptoms** | Dyspnea, angina, syncope on exertion |
| **Murmur** | Harsh systolic murmur — INCREASES with Valsalva/standing, DECREASES with squatting |
| **Pulse** | Jerky, bifid pulse |
| **Sudden death** | Exercise-triggered VF |

## The Murmur Trick — High Yield

## Key MCQ Facts
- **Most common cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes**
- **Autosomal dominant** — always ask about family history
- **Diastolic dysfunction** — problem with RELAXATION not contraction
- **EF is NORMAL or HIGH** (squeezes well, can't relax)
- **Small LV cavity** (opposite of DCM)
- **Myofiber disarray** = pathognomonic on microscopy
- Treatment: **Beta-blockers** (slow heart, improve filling)
- Avoid: nitrates, digoxin, diuretics (reduce preload → worsen obstruction)

## 1C. RESTRICTIVE CARDIOMYOPATHY (RCM)

## Definition
Heart muscle becomes STIFF and cannot relax → diastolic dysfunction → impaired filling
Walls are NOT thickened, NOT dilated — but rigid and non-compliant

## Causes — Remember **"HASSI"**
- **H**emochromatosis (iron deposition)
- **A**myloidosis (most common cause overall)
- **S**arcoidosis (granulomas)
- **S**cleroderma (fibrosis)
- **I**diopathic / Endomyocardial fibrosis (Loeffler)

### Most Important Causes
| Cause | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Amyloidosis** | Most common — amyloid deposits stiffen heart |
| **Hemochromatosis** | Iron overload — deposits in myocardium |
| **Sarcoidosis** | Non-caseating granulomas |
| **Loeffler endocarditis** | Hypereosinophilia → endomyocardial fibrosis |
| **Radiation** | Post-radiation fibrosis |
| **Glycogen storage diseases** | Pompe disease |

## Pathophysiology

## Key Features
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Systolic function** | NORMAL (EF preserved) |
| **Diastolic function** | SEVERELY impaired |
| **Ventricular size** | Normal (not dilated) |
| **Wall thickness** | Normal or slightly increased |
| **Atria** | Massively dilated (blood backs up) |
| **Symptoms** | Right heart failure predominates |

## Amyloidosis — Special Focus

## Key MCQ Facts
- **Least common** cardiomyopathy
- **Diastolic dysfunction** with NORMAL systolic function
- **Amyloidosis** = most common cause
- **Congo red stain** → apple-green birefringence = amyloid
- **Massive atrial dilation** — diagnostic clue
- Hardest to treat — no specific therapy for most causes

## CARDIOMYOPATHY COMPARISON TABLE

| Feature | DCM | HCM | RCM |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chamber size** | All dilated | LV small, thick | Normal |
| **Wall thickness** | Thin | Massively thick | Normal/slightly thick |
| **Dysfunction** | Systolic | Diastolic | Diastolic |
| **EF** | Reduced (<40%) | Normal/High | Normal |
| **Main cause** | Idiopathic/alcohol | Genetic (AD) | Amyloidosis |
| **Key microscopy** | Fibrosis, hypertrophy | **Myofiber disarray** | Amyloid deposits |
| **Sudden death** | Can occur | **Most common cause in athletes** | Rare |
| **Treatment** | Diuretics, ACEi, ICD | Beta-blockers | Treat underlying cause |

## 2B. PERICARDIAL EFFUSION

## Definition
Abnormal accumulation of fluid in pericardial space (>50mL)

## Types of Fluid
| Type | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Transudate** | Heart failure, hypoalbuminemia |
| **Exudate** | Infection, malignancy, autoimmune |

## 2C. CARDIAC TAMPONADE

## Definition
Life-threatening compression of the heart by pericardial fluid → impaired cardiac filling → reduced cardiac output

## Causes
Trauma, aortic dissection, MI rupture, malignancy, TB, post-cardiac surgery

## Pathophysiology

## Beck's Triad — MUST KNOW
1. HYPOTENSION — reduced cardiac output
2. RAISED JVP (distended neck veins) — backed up venous blood
3. MUFFLED HEART SOUNDS — fluid dampens sounds

## Pulsus Paradoxus
Normal: BP drops slightly (<10mmHg) during inspiration
Tamponade: BP drops >10mmHg during inspiration

Why?
During inspiration → right ventricle expands
→ Pushes septum LEFT (constrained by tamponade)
→ LV fills less → BP drops significantly

> **Pulsus paradoxus >10mmHg = hallmark of cardiac tamponade**

## ECG Findings
- Low voltage complexes (fluid insulates)
- Electrical alternans — QRS complex alternates in height
  (Heart swinging back and forth in fluid)
- Sinus tachycardia

## Treatment
**PERICARDIOCENTESIS** — emergency needle drainage of pericardial fluid
Needle inserted at left xiphocostal angle

## 2D. CONSTRICTIVE PERICARDITIS

## Definition
Chronic fibrosis and calcification of pericardium → forms rigid shell around heart → impairs filling

## Causes
| Cause | Notes |
|---|---|
| **TB** | Most common worldwide |
| **Viral pericarditis** | After healing |
| **Radiation** | Post-radiotherapy |
| **Cardiac surgery** | Post-operative |
| **Purulent pericarditis** | After bacterial infection |

## Pathophysiology

## Key Features
- Kussmaul's sign: JVP RISES on inspiration (opposite of normal)
- Pericardial knock: early diastolic sound
- CXR: pericardial calcification
- Features of RIGHT heart failure: JVP raised, hepatomegaly, ascites, edema
- Quiet, non-displaced apex beat

## Tamponade vs Constrictive Pericarditis

| Feature | Tamponade | Constrictive Pericarditis |
|---|---|---|
| **Cause** | Fluid accumulation | Fibrosis/calcification |
| **Onset** | Acute | Chronic |
| **Pulsus paradoxus** | **YES — prominent** | Mild or absent |
| **Kussmaul's sign** | Absent | **YES** |
| **CXR** | Enlarged cardiac silhouette | Pericardial calcification |
| **Treatment** | Pericardiocentesis | Pericardiectomy (surgery) |

## PART 3: CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (CHD)

## Overview
Structural abnormalities of the heart present from birth

## Two Main Groups

### Acyanotic (Left-to-Right Shunts) — "Pink Babies"

### Cyanotic (Right-to-Left Shunts) — "Blue Babies"

## 3A. VENTRICULAR SEPTAL DEFECT (VSD)

## Definition
Hole in the interventricular septum — most commonly in membranous septum

## Key Facts
**Most common congenital heart defect overall**

## Pathophysiology

## Clinical Features
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Small VSD** | Loud harsh pansystolic murmur (Maladie de Roger) — may close spontaneously |
| **Large VSD** | Heart failure in infancy, failure to thrive |
| **CXR** | Cardiomegaly, increased pulmonary markings |
| **Complications** | Pulmonary hypertension, IE, Eisenmenger |

## 3B. ATRIAL SEPTAL DEFECT (ASD)

## Definition
Hole in the interatrial septum

## Types
| Type | Location | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ostium secundum** | Fossa ovalis region | Most common (70%) |
| **Ostium primum** | Near AV valves | Associated with Down syndrome |
| **Sinus venosus** | Near SVC/IVC | Less common |

## Pathophysiology

## Key Features
- **Fixed wide split S2** — pathognomonic
- Soft systolic murmur (pulmonary flow murmur)
- Often asymptomatic until adulthood
- **Most common CHD presenting in adults**
- Associated with **Down syndrome** (ostium primum type)

## 3D. TETRALOGY OF FALLOT (TOF)

## Definition
Four congenital heart defects present together:
1. Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD)
2. Pulmonary Stenosis (RV outflow tract obstruction)
3. Overriding Aorta (aorta receives blood from both RV and LV)
4. Right Ventricular Hypertrophy (due to RV outflow obstruction)

## Pathophysiology

## Key Features
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Cyanosis** | Present from birth |
| **CXR** | **"Boot-shaped" heart** (due to RV hypertrophy and upturned apex) |
| **Murmur** | Harsh systolic murmur at LUSB (pulmonary stenosis) |
| **"Tet spells"** | Episodes of severe cyanosis, treated by squatting |

## Key MCQ Facts
- **Most common cyanotic CHD presenting after infancy** (after TGA at birth)
- **Squatting** is characteristic
- **VSD + Pulm Stenosis + Overriding Aorta + RVH** = Tetralogy of Fallot

## 3E. TRANSPOSITION OF THE GREAT ARTERIES (TGA)

## Definition
Aorta and pulmonary artery are transposed — aorta arises from RV, pulmonary artery arises from LV

## Pathophysiology

## Key Facts
- **Most common cyanotic CHD presenting at BIRTH** (before TOF)
- Associated with maternal **diabetes**
- **"Egg on a string"** CXR appearance — narrow mediastinum
- Treatment: **Prostaglandin E1** (keep PDA open) → **Arterial switch operation** (surgery)

## 3F. COARCTATION OF THE AORTA

## Definition
Narrowing of the aorta — most commonly just DISTAL to left subclavian artery (juxtaductal)

## Associations
- **Turner syndrome** (45, X) — most important association
- Bicuspid aortic valve (50% of cases)
- Berry aneurysms

## Pathophysiology

## Key Features
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **BP difference** | HIGH in arms, LOW in legs |
| **Pulses** | Weak/absent femoral pulses |
| **Collaterals** | Intercostal artery enlargement → **"Rib notching"** on CXR |
| **CXR** | **"Figure 3 sign"** — indentation of aorta |
| **Murmur** | Systolic murmur over back/scapula |

## Key MCQ Facts
- **Turner syndrome** → coarctation of aorta
- **Rib notching** on CXR = classic
- **Figure 3 sign** on CXR
- **Radio-femoral delay** — pulse in groin delayed vs wrist

## 3G. EISENMENGER SYNDROME

## Definition
Reversal of left-to-right shunt to right-to-left shunt due to pulmonary hypertension

## Pathophysiology

## Key MCQ Facts
- **Irreversible** — surgical repair contraindicated once Eisenmenger develops
- Complication of ANY large left-to-right shunt
- Patient was pink → now becomes CYANOTIC (late presentation)
- **Clubbing + polycythemia + cyanosis** in adult with known CHD = Eisenmenger

## CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE SUMMARY TABLE

| Condition | Shunt | Cyanosis | Key Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VSD** | L→R | No (unless Eisenmenger) | Most common CHD, pansystolic murmur |
| **ASD** | L→R | No (unless Eisenmenger) | Fixed split S2, common in adults |
| **PDA** | L→R | No (unless Eisenmenger) | Continuous machinery murmur, rubella |
| **TOF** | R→L | YES from birth | Boot-shaped heart, tet spells, squatting |
| **TGA** | None (parallel) | YES from birth | Egg on string CXR, maternal diabetes |
| **Coarctation** | None | No | Rib notching, Turner syndrome, BP difference |
| **Eisenmenger** | R→L (reversed) | YES (late) | Irreversible, cyanosis in adult with CHD |

## Overview
Cardiac tumors are RARE — metastatic tumors to heart are more common than primary

## 4A. CARDIAC MYXOMA

## Definition
Most common **PRIMARY** cardiac tumor in adults

## Location
**LEFT ATRIUM** in 75% of cases — attached to interatrial septum near fossa ovalis

## Gross Pathology

## Microscopic Pathology

## Pathophysiology

## Clinical Features
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Positional symptoms** | Syncope/dyspnea changes with position (classic) |
| **"Tumor plop"** | Early diastolic sound as tumor hits mitral valve |
| **Emboli** | Stroke, peripheral embolism |
| **Constitutional** | Fever, weight loss, elevated ESR, anemia |
| **Mimics** | Mitral stenosis, IE, autoimmune disease |

## Key MCQ Triggers
- Left atrium + pedunculated mass + positional syncope = **Myxoma**
- Fever + emboli + elevated ESR but cultures negative = **Myxoma** (not IE)
- "Tumor plop" on auscultation = **Myxoma**
- Treatment: **Surgical excision** — curative

## 4B. RHABDOMYOMA

## Definition
Most common **PRIMARY** cardiac tumor in **CHILDREN/INFANTS**

## Key Association
**Tuberous sclerosis** — hamartomatous condition
- Always think: child + cardiac tumor + tuberous sclerosis

## Location
Ventricular myocardium — often multiple

## Gross Pathology
Gray-white nodules in ventricular wall — often multiple

## Microscopic Pathology
**"Spider cells"** — large cells with central nucleus connected to cell membrane by cytoplasmic processes (glycogen-filled)

## Key Facts
- Most common cardiac tumor in CHILDREN
- Associated with **tuberous sclerosis**
- Often REGRESS spontaneously
- Can cause arrhythmias, outflow obstruction
- Treatment: often observation — may resolve on own

## Practice Questions
1. A 55-year-old man presents with fatigue, shortness of breath, and a history of heavy alcohol use. Echocardiogram shows dilation of all four cardiac chambers with a reduced ejection fraction. What is the most likely diagnosis? → Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM)
2. A young athlete collapses during a competition and is found to have a markedly thickened left ventricular wall on echocardiogram, particularly the interventricular septum. There is no chamber dilation and the ejection fraction is normal. What is the most likely diagnosis? → Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)
3. A patient presents with symptoms of right-sided heart failure, including leg edema and ascites. Echocardiogram reveals normal ventricular size and function but markedly thickened, non-compliant ventricular walls. What is the most likely diagnosis? → Restrictive Cardiomyopathy (RCM)
4. A patient presents with acute onset of sharp, pleuritic chest pain that is worse when lying down and relieved by sitting forward. A friction rub is heard on auscultation. ECG shows diffuse ST elevation in all leads. What is the likely diagnosis? → Pericarditis
5. During a physical examination, you note hypotension, distended neck veins, and muffled heart sounds in a patient with a rapid atrial fibrillation. What is the immediate life-threatening condition to consider? → Cardiac Tamponade
6. A 3-month-old infant presents with poor feeding, tachypnea, and a loud, harsh pansystolic murmur heard best at the left lower sternal border. Echocardiogram confirms a defect in the interventricular septum. What is the most likely diagnosis? → Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD)
7. A 40-year-old woman is found to have a continuous "machinery-like" murmur on cardiac auscultation. She has a history of prematurity and rubella infection in childhood. What is the most likely diagnosis? → Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA)
8. A child presents with cyanosis present from birth, a harsh systolic murmur, and squatting behavior during episodes of increased cyanosis. Chest X-ray shows a "boot-shaped" heart. What is the most likely diagnosis? → Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF)
