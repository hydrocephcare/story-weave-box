## SECTION FOUR: MOLECULAR BASIS OF CANCER, CELL CYCLE, APOPTOSIS & TUMOUR GROWTH
*Covers — Cell cycle regulation, Apoptosis in cancer, Angiogenesis, Tumour microenvironment, Cancer stem cells, Epigenetics in cancer, Cancer genetics & hereditary cancer syndromes*

## ▸ 4.1 CELL CYCLE & ITS REGULATION IN CANCER
**⟡ Normal Cell Cycle Phases:**
- **G1** → cell growth; preparation for DNA synthesis
- **S** → DNA replication
- **G2** → preparation for division
- **M** → mitosis (cell division)
- **G0** → quiescent/resting state (most normal cells)
**⟡ Cell Cycle Checkpoints:**

- **G1/S checkpoint** → most critical; "restriction point" — checks DNA integrity before replication
- **G2/M checkpoint** → ensures DNA replication is complete before mitosis
- **Spindle assembly checkpoint** → ensures chromosomes properly attached to spindle before separation
**⟡ Key Regulators:**

- **Cyclins** → regulatory proteins; levels rise and fall during cycle
- **CDKs (Cyclin-Dependent Kinases)** → enzymes activated by cyclins; drive cell cycle progression
- **CDK inhibitors (CKIs)** → e.g. p16 (CDKN2A), p21, p27 → brake on cell cycle
- **RB protein (Retinoblastoma protein)** → master brake of G1/S checkpoint
Hypophosphorylated RB → binds and inactivates E2F transcription factors → cell cycle BLOCKED

- Phosphorylated RB → releases E2F → cell cycle PROCEEDS
- In cancer: RB is lost/mutated → uncontrolled E2F activity → unrestrained proliferation
**⟡ TP53 — Guardian of the Genome:**

- Activated by DNA damage, hypoxia, oncogene activation
- Actions of TP53:
✦ Activates **p21** → CDK inhibition → cell cycle arrest (allows DNA repair)

- ✦ Activates **BAX** → apoptosis if damage irreparable
- ✦ Activates **GADD45** → DNA repair
- Mutated in > 50% of all human cancers
- Li-Fraumeni syndrome → germline TP53 mutation → multiple early-onset cancers in family
## ▸ 4.2 APOPTOSIS & ITS EVASION IN CANCER
**Definition:** Programmed cell death — orderly, energy-dependent elimination of damaged or unwanted cells without inflammation**
**⟡ Two Pathways of Apoptosis:**
**Intrinsic (Mitochondrial) Pathway:**
- Triggered by: DNA damage, hypoxia, oncogene activation, growth factor withdrawal
- Pro-apoptotic proteins (BAX, BAK) → mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilisation → cytochrome c release
- Cytochrome c → forms **apoptosome** with APAF-1 → activates caspase-9 → executioner caspases (3, 6, 7)
- Anti-apoptotic proteins (BCL-2, BCL-XL) → block cytochrome c release
- In cancer: BCL-2 overexpression (e.g. follicular lymphoma t(14;18)) → cells resist apoptosis
**Extrinsic (Death Receptor) Pathway:**

- Triggered by: FasL binding Fas receptor; TNF binding TNFR
- Receptor activation → FADD adaptor → activates caspase-8 → executioner caspases
- Leads to same downstream caspase cascade
**⟡ How Cancer Evades Apoptosis:**

- ✦ BCL-2 overexpression
- ✦ Loss of TP53 → no apoptosis signal after DNA damage
- ✦ Upregulation of IAPs (Inhibitor of Apoptosis Proteins)
- ✦ Downregulation of Fas receptor → immune evasion
- ✦ Activation of PI3K/AKT pathway → pro-survival signalling
## ▸ 4.3 ANGIOGENESIS IN TUMOURS
**Why tumours need angiogenesis:**
- Tumours > 1–2 mm cannot survive on diffusion alone → need blood supply
- Without new vessels → tumour cells become hypoxic → central necrosis
**⟡ Mechanism:**

- Tumour hypoxia → stabilises **HIF-1α** (Hypoxia Inducible Factor)
- HIF-1α → upregulates **VEGF** (Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor) and other angiogenic factors
- VEGF → stimulates endothelial cell proliferation, migration → new vessel sprouting
- New vessels are leaky, disorganised, inefficient → paradoxically creates more hypoxia
**⟡ Angiogenic vs Anti-angiogenic balance:**

- Pro-angiogenic: VEGF, FGF, PDGF, angiopoietins
- Anti-angiogenic: thrombospondin-1, angiostatin, endostatin
- In cancer: balance tips toward pro-angiogenic → "angiogenic switch"
**⟡ Clinical relevance:**

- **Bevacizumab (Avastin)** → anti-VEGF monoclonal antibody → anti-cancer therapy
- Anti-angiogenic drugs used in colorectal, lung, renal cell carcinoma
## ▸ 4.4 TUMOUR MICROENVIRONMENT (TME)A tumour is not just cancer cells — it is an entire ecosystem
**⟡ Components of the TME:**
- **Cancer-Associated Fibroblasts (CAFs)** → secrete growth factors, remodel ECM, promote invasion
- **Tumour-Associated Macrophages (TAMs)** → M2-polarised; promote tumour growth, suppress immunity, promote angiogenesis
- **Regulatory T cells (Tregs)** → suppress anti-tumour immune response
- **Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells (MDSCs)** → block cytotoxic T cell function
- **Extracellular Matrix (ECM)** → remodelled by MMPs to facilitate invasion
- **Blood vessels** → tumour-induced, disorganised, leaky
**⟡ Significance:**

- TME actively promotes tumour growth, immune evasion, metastasis
- Target of modern immunotherapy (e.g. PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitors)
## ▸ 4.5 EPIGENETICS IN CANCER
**Definition:** Heritable changes in gene expression without alteration of DNA sequence**
**⟡ Epigenetic mechanisms:**
- **DNA methylation** → methylation of CpG islands in gene promoters → gene silencing
In cancer: hypermethylation of tumour suppressor gene promoters → silencing without mutation (e.g. RB, CDKN2A, MLH1)

- Global hypomethylation → genomic instability → oncogene activation
- **Histone modification** → acetylation (activates), methylation (activates or represses depending on site), phosphorylation
Deacetylation of histones → chromatin condensation → gene silencing

- **Non-coding RNAs:**
miRNAs → post-transcriptional gene regulation

- In cancer: some miRNAs act as oncogenes (oncomiRs), others as tumour suppressors
**⟡ Clinical relevance:**

- Epigenetic changes are reversible → therapeutic targets
- **HDAC inhibitors** (e.g. vorinostat) → reactivate silenced tumour suppressor genes
- **DNA methyltransferase inhibitors** (e.g. azacitidine) → demethylate silenced genes → used in MDS, AML
## ▸ 4.6 CANCER STEM CELLS (CSCs)- Subpopulation of tumour cells with stem cell-like properties
- Capable of self-renewal and differentiation into all cell types within a tumour
- Responsible for:
✦ Tumour initiation

- ✦ Tumour maintenance and growth
- ✦ Resistance to chemotherapy and radiotherapy
- ✦ Tumour recurrence after treatment
- ✦ Metastasis
**⟡ Key markers (examples):**

- Breast CSCs → CD44+/CD24− phenotype
- Colon CSCs → CD133+
- Leukaemia CSCs → CD34+/CD38−

## ▸ 4.8 TUMOUR GRADING vs STAGING
**⟡ Grading** → assesses degree of differentiation (how abnormal cells look microscopically)- Grade 1 → well differentiated → low grade → better prognosis**
- Grade 2 → moderately differentiated → intermediate
- Grade 3 → poorly differentiated → high grade → worse prognosis
- Grade 4 → undifferentiated / anaplastic → worst
**⟡ Staging** → assesses extent of spread (clinical/anatomical)

- Uses TNM system (covered in Section Two)
- More important than grade for determining treatment and prognosis
- Stage I → localised; Stage IV → distant metastasis
**Key difference to remember:**

- **Grade** = what it LOOKS like under the microscope
- **Stage** = how FAR it has SPREAD in the body
## ▸ 4.9 LABORATORY & CLINICAL EVALUATION OF TUMOURS
**⟡ Biopsy types:**
- **Excisional** → entire lesion removed → best for small tumours
- **Incisional** → portion of large tumour removed
- **Core needle** → core of tissue extracted → good for breast, prostate, liver
- **Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA)** → cells aspirated → cytology only, no architecture
- **Frozen section** → rapid intraoperative diagnosis
**⟡ Special techniques in tumour diagnosis:**

- **Immunohistochemistry (IHC)** → antibodies detect specific tumour antigens → confirms cell of origin e.g. PSA (prostate), TTF-1 (lung), CK7/CK20 (carcinoma typing)
- **Flow cytometry** → immunophenotyping of leukaemias and lymphomas
- **Molecular testing** → PCR, FISH, sequencing → detect specific mutations, translocations (e.g. BCR-ABL in CML, t(9;22))
- **Tumour markers** → serum proteins used for monitoring (not screening alone):
Marker Associated Tumour AFP (Alpha-fetoprotein) Hepatocellular carcinoma, germ cell tumours CEA (Carcinoembryonic antigen) Colorectal, gastric, pancreatic cancer PSA (Prostate Specific Antigen) Prostate cancer CA-125 Ovarian cancer CA 19-9 Pancreatic cancer β-hCG Choriocarcinoma, germ cell tumours LDH Lymphoma, testicular cancer Calcitonin Medullary thyroid cancer

## ▸ 4.10 CANCER TREATMENT PRINCIPLES
**⟡ Surgery:**
- Curative for localised tumours
- Debulking for large tumours before chemotherapy/radiotherapy
**⟡ Radiotherapy:**

- Ionising radiation → DNA double-strand breaks → cell death
- Tumour cells divide rapidly → more sensitive than most normal cells
- Fractionation → normal cells repair between doses; tumour cells less able to
**⟡ Chemotherapy:**

- Targets rapidly dividing cells
- Classes:
Alkylating agents (cyclophosphamide) → cross-link DNA

- Antimetabolites (methotrexate, 5-FU) → inhibit DNA synthesis
- Topoisomerase inhibitors (doxorubicin, etoposide) → prevent DNA unwinding
- Vinca alkaloids (vincristine, vinblastine) → inhibit mitotic spindle
- Taxanes (paclitaxel) → stabilise microtubules → block mitosis
**⟡ Targeted Therapy:**

- Drugs designed against specific molecular targets
- Examples:
**Imatinib (Gleevec)** → BCR-ABL inhibitor → CML

- **Trastuzumab (Herceptin)** → anti-HER2 → HER2+ breast cancer
- **Bevacizumab** → anti-VEGF → anti-angiogenic
- **Erlotinib/Gefitinib** → EGFR inhibitors → NSCLC
**⟡ Immunotherapy:**

- **Checkpoint inhibitors:** anti-PD-1 (pembrolizumab, nivolumab), anti-CTLA-4 (ipilimumab) → release immune brakes → T cells attack tumour
- **CAR-T cell therapy** → patient's T cells engineered to express chimeric antigen receptors → recognise and kill tumour cells → used in B-cell leukaemias and lymphomas
- **Cancer vaccines** → e.g. HPV vaccine (preventive); therapeutic vaccines in trials

## ▸ 5.1 SPECIFIC MAJOR CANCERS — HIGH YIELD SUMMARY### → Lung Cancer- Leading cause of cancer death worldwide- **Risk factors:** cigarette smoking (most important — 87% of cases), asbestos, radon, occupational carcinogens
- **Types:**
**Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC)** → most aggressive; neuroendocrine origin; strongly associated with smoking; paraneoplastic syndromes common (SIADH, Cushing's, Eaton-Lambert); treated with chemotherapy (NOT surgery)

- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):**
*Adenocarcinoma* → most common overall; peripheral location; associated with EGFR, ALK mutations; occurs in non-smokers too

- *Squamous cell carcinoma* → central location; cavitates; produces PTHrP → hypercalcaemia; strongly linked to smoking
- *Large cell carcinoma* → peripheral; undifferentiated; worst prognosis among NSCLC
### → Colorectal Cancer (CRC)- 3rd most common cancer worldwide
- **Risk factors:** high fat/low fibre diet, IBD (especially UC), familial polyposis, Lynch syndrome, age > 50
- **Pathogenesis — Adenoma-Carcinoma Sequence:**
Normal colon → APC mutation → adenoma → KRAS mutation → larger adenoma → SMAD4/TP53 loss → carcinoma

- **Features:**
Right-sided → occult blood loss, iron deficiency anaemia, palpable mass

- Left-sided → change in bowel habit, rectal bleeding, obstruction
- **Tumour marker:** CEA (monitoring, not diagnosis)
- **Screening:** colonoscopy from age 45–50; earlier if high risk
### → Breast Cancer- Most common cancer in women worldwide
- **Risk factors:** early menarche, late menopause, nulliparity, HRT, obesity, BRCA1/2 mutations, family history
- **Types:**
*Invasive ductal carcinoma* → most common (70–80%)

- *Invasive lobular carcinoma* → bilateral tendency; loss of E-cadherin
- *Inflammatory carcinoma* → worst prognosis; dermal lymphatic invasion; peau d'orange skin
- **Molecular subtypes:**
Luminal A → ER+/PR+, HER2− → best prognosis

- Luminal B → ER+/PR+, HER2+ → intermediate
- HER2-enriched → ER−/PR−, HER2+ → treated with trastuzumab
- Triple negative (TNBC) → ER−/PR−, HER2− → worst prognosis; no targeted therapy; common in BRCA1 carriers
- **Tumour markers:** CA 15-3, CEA (monitoring)
- **Screening:** mammography (40–74 years annually or biennially)
### → Cervical Cancer- Most common gynaecological cancer in developing countries
- **Cause:** HPV (types 16 and 18 responsible for ~70% of cases)
- **Pathogenesis:** HPV E6 protein → degrades TP53; HPV E7 protein → inactivates RB → uncontrolled proliferation
- **Precursor lesion:** Cervical Intraepithelial Neoplasia (CIN) → CIN I → CIN II → CIN III → invasive carcinoma
- **Screening:** Pap smear (cytology); HPV co-testing; colposcopy for abnormal results
- **Prevention:** HPV vaccine (Gardasil — covers HPV 6, 11, 16, 18; given before sexual debut)
- **Treatment:** early stages → surgery; advanced → chemoradiation
### → Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)- Most common primary liver cancer
- **Risk factors:** HBV and HCV infection (most important globally), aflatoxin B1, alcoholic cirrhosis, NAFLD, haemochromatosis
- **Mechanism:** chronic inflammation → cirrhosis → HCC; HBV can integrate directly into genome → oncogene activation
- **Features:** right upper quadrant pain, hepatomegaly, jaundice, ascites
- **Tumour marker:** AFP (Alpha-fetoprotein) — markedly elevated
- **Screening:** AFP + liver ultrasound every 6 months in high-risk patients (cirrhosis, chronic HBV)
### → Prostate Cancer- Most common cancer in men (in many countries)
- **Risk factors:** age (> 50), African descent, family history, high fat diet
- **Pathology:** almost always adenocarcinoma; arises in peripheral zone
- **Grading:** Gleason score (1–5 scale, two patterns summed) → total score determines prognosis
- **Features:** often asymptomatic early; late → urinary obstruction, bone pain (osteoblastic metastases)
- **Tumour marker:** PSA — used for screening and monitoring
- **Treatment:** localised → radical prostatectomy or radiotherapy; advanced → androgen deprivation therapy (ADT)

- ✦ Accurate diagnosis of genetic condition
- ✦ Education about the condition, inheritance pattern, and recurrence risk
- ✦ Discussion of available testing options
- ✦ Psychosocial support for individuals and families
- ✦ Guidance on reproductive options
- ✦ Referral to appropriate specialists and support groups
**⟡ Key Principles:**

- **Non-directiveness** → counsellor presents information objectively; patient makes own informed decision
- **Autonomy** → patient's right to decide whether to be tested or not
- **Confidentiality** → genetic information is sensitive; patient controls who knows
- **Informed consent** → must be obtained before any genetic testing
- **Beneficence & Non-maleficence** → act in patient's best interest; avoid harm
**⟡ Situations where genetic counselling is indicated:**

- ✦ Personal or family history of a genetic disorder
- ✦ Advanced maternal age
- ✦ Previous child with chromosomal abnormality or birth defects
- ✦ Consanguineous relationships
- ✦ Recurrent pregnancy loss
- ✦ Positive carrier screening result
- ✦ Abnormal prenatal screening result (e.g. abnormal ultrasound, elevated AFP)
- ✦ Diagnosis of hereditary cancer syndrome
**⟡ Reproductive Options discussed in counselling:**

- Natural conception with prenatal diagnosis (amniocentesis / CVS)
- Preimplantation Genetic Diagnosis (PGD) with IVF → test embryos before implantation
- Use of donor egg or sperm
- Adoption
- Choosing not to have children
## ▸ 5.3 CANCER SCREENING & PREVENTION
**⟡ Principles of a Good Screening Test:**
- Disease must be common and serious
- Effective treatment must be available
- Detectable preclinical phase must exist
- Test must be sensitive, specific, safe, acceptable and affordable
- Early detection must improve outcomes
**⟡ Major Cancer Screening Programmes:**

Cancer Screening Method Target Population Cervical Pap smear + HPV testing Women 21–65 years; every 3–5 years Breast Mammography Women 40–74 years; annually or biennially Colorectal Colonoscopy / faecal occult blood test (FOBT) Age 45–75; colonoscopy every 10 years Prostate PSA blood test Men > 50 years; shared decision making Lung Low-dose CT scan (LDCT) Heavy smokers 50–80 years Liver (HCC) AFP + liver ultrasound Every 6 months in cirrhosis / chronic HBV patients
**⟡ Primary Cancer Prevention:**
- ✦ Tobacco cessation → prevents lung, oral, oesophageal, bladder, cervical cancers
- ✦ HPV vaccination → prevents cervical, anogenital, oropharyngeal cancers
- ✦ HBV vaccination → prevents hepatocellular carcinoma
- ✦ Healthy diet (high fibre, low saturated fat, antioxidants) → reduces colorectal, gastric cancer risk
- ✦ Physical activity and weight control → reduces breast, endometrial, colorectal cancer risk
- ✦ Sun protection (sunscreen, protective clothing) → prevents skin cancers
- ✦ Occupational exposure reduction → asbestos, benzene, chromium, radiation regulations
- ✦ H. pylori eradication → reduces gastric cancer risk
- ✦ Alcohol reduction → reduces liver, breast, colorectal, oesophageal cancer risk
**⟡ Chemoprevention:**

- Use of drugs to reduce cancer risk in high-risk individuals
- Tamoxifen / Raloxifene → reduce breast cancer risk in high-risk women
- Aspirin → reduces colorectal cancer risk (long-term use)
- Finasteride → reduces prostate cancer risk
## ▸ 5.4 EMERGING THERAPIES & FUTURE DIRECTIONS IN ONCOLOGY
**⟡ Liquid Biopsy:**
- Detection of circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA), circulating tumour cells (CTCs), or exosomes in blood
- Advantages:
✦ Non-invasive → simple blood draw

- ✦ Can detect cancer early before symptoms
- ✦ Monitor treatment response in real-time
- ✦ Detect resistance mutations early
- ✦ Assess tumour heterogeneity
- Currently used for monitoring in lung, colorectal, breast cancer
**⟡ Precision / Personalised Medicine:**

- Treatment tailored to the specific molecular profile of each patient's tumour
- Whole genome / exome sequencing of tumours → identify actionable mutations → match to targeted therapy
- Examples:
EGFR mutation in NSCLC → erlotinib

- ALK rearrangement in NSCLC → crizotinib
- BRAF V600E in melanoma → vemurafenib
- HER2 amplification in breast → trastuzumab
- MSI-high tumours (any type) → pembrolizumab
**⟡ CRISPR-Cas9 Gene Editing:**

- Precise editing of DNA at specific locations using guide RNA
- Applications in cancer:
✦ Correct germline mutations in hereditary cancer syndromes

- ✦ Enhance CAR-T cell therapy
- ✦ Create cancer models for research
- ✦ Disable oncogenes or restore tumour suppressor function
- Still largely experimental; ethical concerns about germline editing remain
**⟡ CAR-T Cell Therapy (Chimeric Antigen Receptor T cells):**

- Patient's own T cells extracted → genetically engineered to express CARs targeting tumour antigens → infused back
- Approved for:
✦ B-cell ALL (CD19-targeted)

- ✦ Diffuse Large B-cell Lymphoma
- ✦ Multiple Myeloma (BCMA-targeted)
- Side effects: cytokine release syndrome (CRS), neurotoxicity
**⟡ Oncolytic Viruses:**

- Viruses engineered to selectively infect and kill cancer cells
- T-VEC (talimogene laherparepvec) → modified herpes simplex virus → approved for melanoma
- Stimulates anti-tumour immune response as tumour cells lyse
**⟡ Cancer Vaccines:**

- **Preventive:** HPV vaccine (Gardasil), HBV vaccine → prevent virus-induced cancers
- **Therapeutic (in trials):** personalised neoantigen vaccines → target mutation-derived antigens unique to each patient's tumour → prime immune system to attack tumour
- mRNA vaccine technology (from COVID-19 platforms) now being applied to cancer vaccines
**⟡ Future of Genomic Medicine:**

- ✦ Population-wide genomic screening → identify carriers of hereditary cancer genes early
- ✦ Multi-cancer early detection (MCED) blood tests → detect > 50 cancer types from single blood draw
- ✦ AI and machine learning in pathology → automated tumour grading, mutation detection, prognosis prediction
- ✦ Single-cell sequencing → understand tumour heterogeneity at unprecedented resolution
- ✦ Epigenome editing → targeting methylation patterns therapeutically
- ✦ Microbiome-cancer interactions → gut microbiome influences immunotherapy response → microbiome modulation as adjunct therapy
