# Systemic Sclerosis

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Rheumatology**

> **In one line:** systemic sclerosis (scleroderma) is an autoimmune disease in which small-vessel injury and ischaemia, immune activation and fibroblast over-activity lead to obliterative vasculopathy and fibrosis of the skin and internal organs (gut, lung, kidney, heart). Raynaud's phenomenon is the usual first sign; the lung is now the commonest cause of death, and scleroderma renal crisis is treated with ACE inhibitors.

## What you must know

- Systemic sclerosis (SSc) is the preferred term to "scleroderma" because fibrosis occurs in **many organs**, not just skin.
- Two skin patterns: **limited cutaneous** (distal to elbows and knees, CREST, anti-centromere) and **diffuse cutaneous** (proximal to elbows and knees, trunk involved, anti-Scl-70, more ILD and renal crisis).
- **Raynaud's phenomenon** (pallor, cyanosis, erythema) is almost universal; secondary Raynaud's starts after 30 years, is severe and may cause digital pits and gangrene.
- **Pulmonary disease** (interstitial lung disease and pulmonary hypertension) is the **leading cause of death**; **scleroderma renal crisis** is the other major killer.
- ANA positive in about 70%; **anti-Scl-70 (anti-topoisomerase I)** in diffuse disease; **anti-centromere** in CREST; **anti-RNA polymerase III** predicts renal crisis.
- **ACE inhibitors** are the treatment of renal crisis, started promptly. Avoid high-dose steroids (prednisolone above 15 mg/day) because they raise the risk of renal crisis.
- Female:male is 4:1 (8:1 in the childbearing years); peak age 35 to 64 years.

## 1. Definition and types

- A **multisystem autoimmune disease** affecting small arteries, microvessels and fibroblasts, causing **vascular obliteration, collagen accumulation and fibrosis** of skin and internal organs.
- **Localised scleroderma** (morphoea, linear): skin, and sometimes underlying muscle and bone only. No internal organ disease.
- **Systemic sclerosis:** involves internal organs (digestive tract, heart, lungs, kidneys).

| Feature | Limited cutaneous SSc (lcSSc) | Diffuse cutaneous SSc (dcSSc) |
|---|---|---|
| Skin thickening | **Distal** to elbows and knees; face and neck; trunk spared | **Proximal** to elbows and knees; involves the trunk |
| Onset | Raynaud's precedes skin change by years | Raynaud's and skin change appear close together |
| Organ involvement | Less; isolated pulmonary hypertension can occur late | More likely: **pulmonary fibrosis and renal crisis** |
| Perioral change | Skin thickening around the mouth (pursed lips) | Present |
| Antibody | **Anti-centromere** (about 60% of CREST) | **Anti-Scl-70** (about 30%), anti-RNA polymerase III |
| Syndrome | **CREST** | Rapid progression in first 2 to 3 years |

### CREST syndrome (limited disease)

- **C**alcinosis
- **R**aynaud's phenomenon
- **E**sophageal dysmotility
- **S**clerodactyly
- **T**elangiectasia

> **Exam tip:** limited SSc has the **better prognosis** but is the one with late **pulmonary arterial hypertension**. Diffuse SSc carries the early ILD and renal crisis.

## 2. Epidemiology

- Peak age 35 to 64 years; also seen in children and the very old.
- Onset is younger in women and in diffuse disease.
- Female:male 4:1; 8:1 in the childbearing years.

## 3. Aetiology

The cause is **unknown**; genetic factors play at least a limited role. Environmental exposures linked to scleroderma or scleroderma-like illness:

- **Silica** exposure (increased risk in men, for example miners).
- **Vinyl chloride** exposure.
- **Aniline-contaminated rapeseed oil** (the Spanish toxic oil syndrome).
- **Drugs:** bleomycin, L-tryptophan, pentazocine.
- **Silicone breast implants:** no definite risk identified.

## 4. Pathogenesis

Three processes drive the disease, and it is unclear which comes first:

1. **Severe fibroproliferative vascular lesions** of small arteries and arterioles.
2. **Excessive, progressive deposition of collagen** and other extracellular matrix in skin and organs.
3. **Altered humoral and cellular immunity** (autoantibodies, T-cell activation).

General sequence:

1. An endogenous or exogenous trigger stimulates **antigen-presenting cells**, which activate **CD4+ T cells**.
2. Both cell types release **cytokines**, which drive growth factors that stimulate **fibroblasts** to make collagen.
3. Vascular damage: **intimal thickening** and luminal narrowing, so **ischaemia**.
4. Ischaemia and endothelial injury lead to platelet aggregation. Platelets stick to the endothelium and release **PDGF**, which is chemotactic and mitogenic for fibroblasts, so fibrosis continues.

## 5. Clinical features

### 5.1 Raynaud's phenomenon

- **Episodic vasoconstriction** of fingers and toes (also nose tip and earlobes).
- Triggers: **cold**, change in temperature, stress, vibration; worse in winter.
- Typical attack: **pallor, then cyanosis, then erythema** (vasoconstriction, then ischaemia, then reperfusion).
- Commoner in women.

| | Primary | Secondary (to SSc) |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Exaggerated physiological response to cold | Complication of SSc or other connective tissue disease |
| Age at onset | Young | Above 30 years |
| Family history | Often positive | Not usual |
| Digital ulcers, necrosis, gangrene | Absent | May occur; digital ischaemia and infarction |
| ANA | Negative | Positive |
| Severity | Mild | More severe |

### 5.2 Skin

**Early:**
- Perivascular infiltrate, mainly T cells.
- Swelling (puffy fingers) that becomes skin thickening, starting **distally** in the hands and feet.

**Late:**
- Finger-like projections of collagen run from dermis to subcutis, anchoring the skin. It becomes **firm, thick and tight**; fingers show **sclerodactyly**.
- Thickening moves **proximally**. Fibroblast activity and collagen deposition increase.
- Hair and wrinkles over the thickened skin disappear; **transverse creases on the dorsum of fingers are lost**.
- **Fixed flexion deformity** of the fingers.

**Other skin and soft tissue signs:**
- Face: tight, mask-like ("**Mauskopf**" or mouse-head) appearance; **thin lips with radial furrowing** around the mouth; **beak-like nose**; reduced mouth opening.
- Skin bound to subcutaneous fat, with thinning and atrophy; slow-healing ulcers over the extensor surface of the **PIP joints**.
- **Digital pits** (healed ischaemic ulcers) at the finger pulps, PIPs and elbows; may become infected.
- **Acro-osteolysis:** resorption of the terminal phalanges.
- **Calcinosis:** calcium deposits in skin and soft tissue.
- **Telangiectasia.**
- Skin thickening may **regress on its own over years**, in the reverse order (trunk first, then proximal limbs, then distal).

### 5.3 Musculoskeletal

- Morning stiffness; **arthritis in more than 50%** (swelling, stiffness and pain in the hand joints).
- **Contractures** from skin thickening restrict hand movement.
- **Polymyositis** may occur as part of an overlap syndrome or mixed connective tissue disease (MCTD).
- Carpal tunnel syndrome may be present.

### 5.4 Pulmonary (leading cause of death)

Death from renal disease has fallen since ACE inhibitors, so lung disease now leads. Main symptom: **exertional dyspnoea**.

**Interstitial lung disease (ILD)**
- Commonest with **diffuse** disease and **anti-Scl-70**.
- Inflammatory phase: **ground-glass opacities** and linear infiltrates in the **lower two-thirds** of the lungs on CT.
- Late fibrotic phase: **honeycombing**.
- Pulmonary function: **restrictive** pattern, low FVC, low residual volume, **low DLCO**.
- **HRCT** is the key test. BAL and lung biopsy are often not required.

**Pulmonary hypertension (PAH)**
- Seen in **10 to 15%** of patients, mainly limited disease; presents with exertional dyspnoea.
- Definition (slides): mean pulmonary artery pressure **above 25 mmHg at rest** (or above 30 mmHg on exercise) on **right heart catheterisation**; estimated systolic PA pressure above 35 mmHg on echocardiography.
- Pathology: **intimal fibrosis and medial hypertrophy** of pulmonary arterioles and arteries.

> **Check:** the current ESC/ERS definition of pulmonary hypertension is mean PA pressure above 20 mmHg at rest, with pulmonary vascular resistance above 2 Wood units for pre-capillary disease. The exercise criterion is no longer used. Right heart catheterisation is still the confirming test.

### 5.5 Renal: scleroderma renal crisis

- A main cause of death, mainly in **diffuse** disease.
- **Abrupt malignant hypertension**, severe headache, blurred vision, chest pain.
- Urine: **proteinuria, microscopic haematuria**; blood film may show fragmented red cells (microangiopathic haemolysis).
- Rapidly progressive **oliguric renal failure** follows.
- Differential: **TTP** (commonly misdiagnosed).
- Creatinine above 3 mg/dL at presentation (about 265 µmol/L) predicts a poor outcome and permanent dialysis, with high mortality.
- **Prompt ACE inhibitor** to control BP before failure sets in improves prognosis.

**Risk factors for renal crisis:**
- Rapidly progressive skin thickening in the first 2 to 3 years.
- **Steroids** (prednisolone above 15 mg/day).
- **Anti-RNA polymerase III antibody.**
- Pericardial effusion.

### 5.6 Gastrointestinal

- Often asymptomatic, or weight loss.
- **Oesophageal dysmotility in up to 90%**: smooth muscle atrophy and fibrosis of the lower oesophagus, low lower oesophageal sphincter tone and distal dilatation. Pathology shows inflammation and collagen in the lamina propria and submucosa.
- Symptoms: **dysphagia, reflux (GERD)** with erosive oesophagitis, which in turn causes more fibrosis. Complication: **Barrett's oesophagus**.
- **Stomach:** early satiety, delayed emptying, occasional outlet obstruction. **Gastric antral vascular ectasia (GAVE, "watermelon stomach")**: striped ectatic vessels causing occult recurrent GI bleeding and iron deficiency.
- **Small bowel:** bacterial overgrowth causing **malabsorption** (fat, protein, vitamin B12 deficiency), bloating and pain. Recurrent abdominal pain must be differentiated from **pseudo-obstruction** (autonomic neuropathy, dilated bowel).
- Investigations: oesophageal manometry, barium swallow (oesophagram), CT.

### 5.7 Cardiac

- Myocardial, pericardial and conduction involvement. Also secondary to renal disease and pulmonary hypertension.
- Silent until **heart failure** (mostly right-sided) or arrhythmias.
- **Myocarditis** with inflammatory myositis.
- **Conduction defects** from fibrosis of the conduction system.
- **Pericardial effusion:** usually mild; tamponade is rare.

## 6. Investigations

The diagnosis is **primarily clinical**.

- **ANA** positive in about 70%.
- **Anti-topoisomerase I (anti-Scl-70):** about 30% of diffuse SSc.
- **Anti-centromere:** about 60% of CREST.
- **Anti-RNA polymerase III:** renal crisis risk.
- IgG often raised; CRP usually normal unless severe organ disease or infection.
- Organ screening: HRCT chest, PFTs with DLCO, echocardiogram, ECG, urinalysis and creatinine, blood pressure, swallow studies.

### Classification criteria (older American College of Rheumatology preliminary criteria, as in the lecture)

Diagnosis: **1 major plus 2 or more minor criteria**.

| Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| **Major** | **Proximal scleroderma:** skin changes proximal to the MCP/MTP joints; may affect the whole limb, face, neck and trunk |
| Minor | **Sclerodactyly:** thickening and tightening limited to the fingers |
| Minor | **Digital pitting scars** or loss of finger-pad substance (ischaemia) |
| Minor | **Bibasal pulmonary fibrosis** (reticular or linear densities at the bases on CXR, not due to primary lung disease) |

> **Check:** the current standard is the 2013 ACR/EULAR classification (a points-based score) which includes Raynaud's, telangiectasia, nail-fold capillary changes and autoantibodies.

## 7. Differential diagnosis (skin thickening)

| Condition | Distinguishing points |
|---|---|
| **Scleredema** | Proximal thickening (trunk, shoulders, back); no Raynaud's; antibodies negative; seen in diabetes |
| **Scleromyxoedema** | Thickening of head, neck, arms, trunk; **monoclonal gammopathy**; skin biopsy |
| **Diabetes and hypothyroidism (myxoedema)** | Skin induration; diabetic cheiroarthropathy gives sclerodactyly of dorsal hands |
| **POEMS syndrome** | Polyneuropathy, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, monoclonal gammopathy, skin changes |
| **Nephrogenic systemic fibrosis** | Chronic kidney disease plus **gadolinium** MRI contrast; hands and feet |
| **Eosinophilic fasciitis** | Hands and feet **spared**; peripheral eosinophilia; "peau d'orange" skin; skin biopsy |
| **Graft-versus-host disease** | Previous bone marrow transplant; no Raynaud's; skin biopsy |

## 8. Management

There is no cure. Treatment depends on **disease activity (aggressive versus stable), inflammation versus vasoconstriction, and the organ involved**.

### 8.1 Raynaud's and digital ulcers

- General: avoid cold; **mittens** (conventional or heated); stop smoking; avoid vasoconstrictors.
- **Calcium channel blockers** (nifedipine, amlodipine) first line.
- Angiotensin II receptor antagonists (for example valsartan) or nitroglycerin patches.
- **Sildenafil** for refractory disease (not with nitrates).
- **IV prostanoids** (iloprost, epoprostenol) for severe disease with impending digital ischaemia.
- Prompt antibiotics for infected ulcers.

### 8.2 Gastrointestinal

- Reflux: **PPI** plus prokinetics (metoclopramide).
- Delayed gastric emptying and dysmotility: supportive, prokinetics.
- Bacterial overgrowth: rotating antibiotics (standard practice).

### 8.3 Renal and hypertension

- **ACE inhibitor**, aggressively titrated. Avoid high-dose steroids.

### 8.4 Pulmonary

| Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **ILD with active inflammation** | **Mycophenolate**, azathioprine or IV cyclophosphamide; low-dose steroids (about 10 mg/day) only if RNA polymerase III negative; **avoid steroids if RNA polymerase III positive** |
| **Pulmonary hypertension** | Vasodilators: **bosentan** (endothelin-1 antagonist), sildenafil, epoprostenol, treprostinil, iloprost |
| End-stage | Lung or heart-lung transplantation |

### 8.5 Myositis and overlap

- Methotrexate or azathioprine with low-dose steroids. Keep prednisolone at **10 mg or less** to avoid renal crisis.

### 8.6 Cardiac

- **Pericarditis:** NSAIDs; drain effusion if tamponade.
- **Myocarditis** (raised CK-MB and troponin): exclude coronary disease; MRI and biopsy confirm; treat with low-dose prednisolone (10 mg/day) and cyclophosphamide; nifedipine may help.

### 8.7 Skin

- Stable disease: no treatment.
- Advancing diffuse skin disease: **methotrexate, mycophenolate**; tocilizumab (trial at the time of the lecture); **D-penicillamine 125 mg/day**; anti-fibrotic research (imatinib).

> **Remember:** steroids in SSc are limited to the lowest dose possible. Renal crisis is treated with ACE inhibitors, even if creatinine is rising.

## Key points to revise

1. SSc = vasculopathy + autoimmunity + fibrosis; **limited** (CREST, anti-centromere) versus **diffuse** (anti-Scl-70, ILD, renal crisis).
2. Raynaud's (pallor, cyanosis, erythema) precedes disease; secondary Raynaud's gives digital pits and ulcers.
3. Skin changes: sclerodactyly, mask face, beak nose, calcinosis, acro-osteolysis.
4. **Lung disease** (ILD with low DLCO and honeycombing; PAH) is the leading cause of death.
5. **Renal crisis:** malignant hypertension, haematuria, proteinuria; treat with ACE inhibitor; risk factors: high-dose steroids, anti-RNA pol III.
6. Gut: oesophageal dysmotility in up to 90%, GAVE, malabsorption, pseudo-obstruction.
7. Treatment is organ-directed: CCBs for Raynaud's, PPIs, ACE inhibitors, immunosuppression for ILD, bosentan/sildenafil for PAH.
