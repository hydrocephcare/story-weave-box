# Congenital Heart Disease: Physiology, Acyanotic and Cyanotic Lesions

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Paediatric Cardiology**

> **In one line:** congenital heart disease (CHD) affects about 1% of live births. Blood always takes the path of **lower resistance and lower pressure**, so a hole between the sides sends blood from left to right into the lungs (acyanotic, heart failure at 2 to 3 months), whereas obstruction of the pulmonary outflow with a hole sends blue blood to the body (cyanotic); **duct-dependent** lesions collapse when the ductus arteriosus closes and need prostaglandin E.

## What you must know

- CHD occurs in about **1% of live births**; the cause is **mostly unknown**, about 10% are chromosomal.
- **VSD is the commonest CHD**; **tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) is the commonest cyanotic CHD**; **D-TGA is the commonest cause of severe cyanosis at birth**.
- **Left-to-right shunts** (VSD, ASD, PDA, AVSD) cause pulmonary over-circulation and heart failure after the pulmonary vascular resistance falls (about **2 months**).
- Untreated big shunts end in **Eisenmenger syndrome** (reversed shunt, cyanosis, irreversible; only cure is heart-lung transplant). **Improvement of heart failure symptoms may be a bad sign.**
- **Duct-dependent** lesions (critical PS, pulmonary atresia, TGA, critical AS, critical coarctation, interrupted arch, HLHS) deteriorate when the duct closes; give **prostaglandin E**.
- **Coarctation:** weak femoral pulses, upper limb BP higher than lower; do **four-limb BP**.
- **Tet spell:** knee-to-chest, oxygen, morphine, IV fluid, phenylephrine, beta blocker.
- Down syndrome: **AVSD** (about 50% have CHD).

## 1. Epidemiology

CHD affects about **1% of live births**. Approximate share of all CHD:

| Lesion | Share |
|---|---|
| **Ventricular septal defect (VSD)** | **30%** (commonest) |
| Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) | 12% |
| Atrial septal defect (ASD) | 7% |
| Pulmonary valve stenosis (PS) | 7% |
| Coarctation of aorta (CoA) | 5% |
| Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) | 5% |
| Aortic valve stenosis (AS) | 5% |
| D-Transposition of great arteries (D-TGA) | 5% |
| Atrioventricular septal defect (AVSD) | 2% |

> **Check:** other slide sets in the same course give VSD 35%, TOF 6%, TGA 4%, AVSD 4% and CoA 5 to 7%. Percentages vary between series; learn the order (VSD is always first).

## 2. Aetiology

Mostly **unknown** (multifactorial). Chromosomal abnormalities account for about 10%:

| Syndrome | Typical heart lesion |
|---|---|
| **Trisomy 21 (Down)** | **AVSD** |
| Trisomy 18, trisomy 13 | Complex lesions (VSD, PDA, ASD) |
| **DiGeorge (22q11.2 deletion)** | Interrupted aortic arch, conotruncal anomalies (TOF, truncus) |
| **Turner** | **Coarctation**, bicuspid aortic valve |
| **Williams** | Supravalvular aortic stenosis, branch pulmonary artery stenosis |
| **Noonan** | Dysplastic pulmonary valve stenosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy |
| Alagille | TOF, peripheral pulmonary stenosis |

Other risk factors: maternal diabetes (TGA), maternal rubella, **NSAIDs in late pregnancy** (see below), family history.

## 3. Physiology you need

### 3.1 Flow, pressure and resistance

- Blood flows from **high pressure to low pressure** (systemic is high pressure; pulmonary is low pressure) and towards **low resistance** organs (lungs).
- Flow depends on the radius to the **power of 4**, so a small change in vessel diameter changes flow greatly.
- **Series circuit:** R total = R1 + R2 + R3. **Parallel circuit:** 1/R total = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3; adding parallel resistances makes the total **lower than the lowest single resistance**.
- Any obstruction raises pressure **before** the obstruction. Pump failure of the LV makes blood back up into the LA, then lungs, then the right heart.
- If an obstruction exists, blood flows **away from the obstruction** if there is an alternative path.
- The LV can move from low pressure (accommodating LA flow) to high pressure (overcoming aortic pressure).

### 3.2 Duct-dependent circulation

| Type | Examples | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Pulmonary duct-dependent** | Critical pulmonary stenosis, pulmonary atresia (including TOF with atresia), tricuspid atresia | Lungs get blood only through the PDA (aorta to PA) |
| **Systemic duct-dependent** | Critical aortic stenosis, critical coarctation, interrupted aortic arch, HLHS | Body gets blood only through the PDA (PA to aorta) |

- **Prostaglandin E (IV)** keeps the duct open and is life-saving.
- Duct-dependent lesions deteriorate at **1 to 2 days**, when the duct normally closes.
- **Counselling point:** mothers should avoid **ibuprofen/NSAIDs in late pregnancy**, because they can close the fetal ductus and cause severe right heart failure.

### 3.3 Cardiac cycle

1. First sound: closure of mitral and tricuspid valves.
2. Second sound: closure of aortic and pulmonary valves.
3. Third sound: rapid LV filling in early diastole.
4. Fourth sound: atrial contraction.

**S3 and S4** together form a **gallop rhythm**, a sign of heart failure. Cardiac output rises in exercise through higher contractility, higher preload and lower afterload.

### 3.4 Fetal versus postnatal circulation

| Feature | Fetus | After birth |
|---|---|---|
| Gas exchange | Placenta (low resistance) | Lungs |
| Pulmonary vascular resistance | **High** (about 10% of blood goes to the lungs, 90% to the body via the ductus) | Falls sharply as the baby breathes and cries |
| Systemic resistance | Low (placenta) | **Rises** when the cord is clamped |
| Right atrial pressure | Higher than left | Lower than left |
| Foramen ovale | Open, right to left (essential for life) | **Closes** because LA pressure exceeds RA pressure |
| Ductus arteriosus | Kept open by placental **prostaglandins** | Physiological constriction in about **24 hours**, anatomical closure in about **2 weeks** |
| Ductus venosus | Open | Closes |

**Changes at birth:** closure of the ductus arteriosus, closure of the ductus venosus, closure of the foramen ovale, rise in systemic vascular resistance, and fall in pulmonary vascular resistance.

> **Remember:** pulmonary vascular resistance takes about 2 months to fall, which is why shunt lesions do not cause heart failure at birth and murmurs may be absent in the first week.

## 4. Classification

| Cyanotic | Acyanotic |
|---|---|
| **Decreased pulmonary flow:** TOF, tricuspid atresia, other univentricular hearts with pulmonary stenosis | **Left-to-right shunts:** VSD, ASD, AVSD, PDA |
| **Increased pulmonary flow:** D-TGA, total anomalous pulmonary venous return (TAPVR) | **Obstructive lesions:** aortic stenosis, pulmonary valve stenosis, coarctation of the aorta |

Critical obstructive lesions present with cyanosis or shock. Cyanosis with a normal chest is most often heart disease; persistent cyanosis in an otherwise well infant is nearly always structural heart disease.

## 5. Acyanotic heart disease: left-to-right shunts

### 5.1 Pathophysiology

- Blood passes to the pulmonary circulation: **Qp rises** and **Qs falls**; the **Qp:Qs ratio** rises above the normal 1:1.
- Pulmonary over-circulation leads to **congestive heart failure**.
- Driving pressure: LV pressure exceeds RV pressure in **systole** but is almost equal in diastole; aortic pressure exceeds pulmonary in **both** phases.

| | PDA | ASD | VSD |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shunt level** | Arterial | Atrial | Ventricular |
| **Chambers dilated** | LA and LV | **RA and RV** | LA and LV |
| **Pulmonary arteries** | Enlarged | Enlarged | Enlarged |

### 5.2 Clinical features

| Feature | PDA | ASD | VSD |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Symptoms** | Small: none. Moderate/large: CHF | Usually asymptomatic; older children: exertional breathlessness or arrhythmia; CHF rare | Small: none. Moderate/large: CHF |
| **Signs** | Small: silent. Large: **continuous "machinery" murmur**, **wide pulse pressure** | **Fixed widely split second heart sound**; ejection systolic murmur (pulmonary flow) | **Holosystolic (pansystolic) murmur**; small muscular VSD: ejection systolic murmur |

**Congestive heart failure in infants**

| Symptoms | Signs |
|---|---|
| Sweating (diaphoresis), poor feeding, failure to thrive, shortness of breath, recurrent chest infections, exercise intolerance | Tachycardia, tachypnoea, cardiomegaly, **hepatomegaly**, active precordium |

**VSD in particular:** no symptoms in the neonatal period because pulmonary vascular resistance is high; CHF begins at about **2 months** (sweating, poor feeding, failure to thrive, breathlessness, recurrent chest infection, exercise intolerance).

> **Remember:** in left heart pump failure (e.g. mitral regurgitation) the lungs are congested from the **venous** side; in a VSD the lungs are flooded from the **arterial** side.

### 5.3 Investigations (all shunts)

| Test | Findings |
|---|---|
| **Chest X-ray** | Increased pulmonary vascular markings, cardiomegaly |
| **ECG** | Small lesion normal; moderate to large: chamber enlargement |
| **Echo** | Confirms the diagnosis |
| **Cardiac catheterisation** | Not needed for diagnosis; for detailed haemodynamics or when catheter intervention is planned |

### 5.4 Management

**Medical (anticongestive):** diuretics, often combined with **captopril**; afterload-reducing agents (e.g. nitroglycerin); nutritional support. **ASD** usually needs no medication.

**Closure**

| Lesion | Usual intervention |
|---|---|
| **VSD** | Usually **surgical** closure at **4 to 8 months**; some in older children by catheter device |
| **ASD** | Mostly **catheter closure** at about **3 to 6 years**; some types need surgery |
| **PDA** | Mostly catheter closure; **surgery** for premature infants and symptomatic babies |

### 5.5 Complications if untreated

- **VSD and PDA beyond infancy:** **Eisenmenger syndrome**. Pulmonary vascular disease becomes irreversible, the shunt reverses (right to left), and the patient becomes **cyanotic**. Symptoms of heart failure disappear. The only treatment is **heart-lung transplant**.
- **ASD** (in adult life): Eisenmenger syndrome, **atrial arrhythmias**, **paradoxical embolism** (rare).
- **Differentiate** between a VSD that has become smaller (child improves) and Eisenmenger (child improves but is **cyanotic**).

> **Exam tip:** heart failure symptoms are worst up to about 3 months of age, then may improve as pulmonary vascular resistance rises. Improvement in a child with an untreated shunt is **not** a good sign. Look for cyanosis, clubbing and a loud second heart sound.

### 5.6 Atrioventricular septal defect (AVSD)

- About 4% of all CHD (2% in the figures above); **about 50% associated with Down syndrome**.
- Types: **complete**, **partial** (ASD primum, with mitral cleft regurgitation) and **transitional**; balanced versus unbalanced (one ventricle larger).
- **Pathophysiology:** like VSD plus ASD (shunts at atrial and ventricular level) **plus AV valve regurgitation**. Large shunts raise Qp:Qs.
- **Clinical:** asymptomatic in the neonatal period (high PVR), maybe slightly low oxygen saturation; CHF at a few months (sweating, poor feeding, failure to thrive, breathlessness, recurrent chest infection, exercise intolerance). On examination: Down features, tachypnoea, tachycardia, active precordium, **pansystolic murmur**, hepatomegaly.
- **Investigations:** CXR increased pulmonary markings and cardiomegaly; **ECG left axis deviation with RVH is very suggestive**; echo confirms; cath only if detailed haemodynamics needed.
- **Treatment:** anticongestive therapy and nutrition; **surgical repair of complete AVSD usually before 6 months** (to prevent Eisenmenger syndrome); balanced AVSD gets biventricular repair, unbalanced gets single-ventricle repair. Partial AVSD also needs surgery.

## 6. Acyanotic heart disease: obstructive lesions

### 6.1 Coarctation of the aorta (CoA)

- **5 to 7%** of CHD. Associated with **Turner syndrome** in females; **arch interruption** with DiGeorge. May be **discrete or diffuse**, mild to severe.

| | **Critical CoA** | **Mild CoA** |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | The **duct closes** spontaneously, obstructing flow to the distal arch | **Collaterals** develop over time; flow is maintained between proximal and distal aorta |
| **Effects** | Lower body hypoperfusion, hypotension, acidosis, LV dysfunction, **cardiogenic shock**. "Duct-dependent CHD" | Presents later |
| **Presentation** | At **2 to 3 weeks**: heart failure, circulatory collapse, shock, death | Murmur, **chronic hypertension**, headache, fatigue, stroke (rupture of cerebral aneurysm) |

**Examination**

- **Differential cyanosis** (severe CoA in a newborn) and signs of cardiac shock with severe metabolic acidosis.
- **Reduced or absent femoral pulses**; in older children **radiofemoral delay** (in infants you see weak flow instead).
- **BP in the lower limbs lower than the upper limbs** (normally the legs are higher, so any reversal should make you think of CoA). Always measure **four-limb BP**.
- Murmur: ejection systolic at the **back**, and a continuous murmur from collaterals.

**Investigations:** CXR cardiomegaly, **prominent aortic knob**, **rib notching** (intercostal collaterals; rarely before 10 years). ECG: RV hypertrophy in neonates, LV hypertrophy in older children. Echo makes the diagnosis; CT/MRI delineates arch anatomy.

**Treatment**

1. **Critical CoA:** **prostaglandin E2** to keep the duct open, then **surgery** (preferred as primary treatment; catheter dilatation risks injury to a major collateral and fatal bleeding).
2. **Balloon angioplasty with or without stent:** **recurrent CoA**, and primary treatment for **discrete CoA in older children**. A stent may be needed for adult-type CoA.

### 6.2 Pulmonary and aortic valve stenosis

| | Pulmonary valve stenosis | Aortic valve stenosis |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | No intervention | No intervention |
| **Moderate to severe** | Usually **balloon valvuloplasty** | Balloon, but may need **valve replacement** later |
| **Signs** | **Ejection systolic murmur** | **Carotid thrill** always present, ejection systolic murmur and **apical ejection click** |
| **Critical** | Duct-dependent (pulmonary) | Duct-dependent (systemic) |

Aortic valve replacement is needed in neonates and children with significant AS.

## 7. Cyanotic heart disease

Types: TGA, tricuspid atresia, truncus arteriosus, TAPVR, TOF, pulmonary atresia, Ebstein anomaly, hypoplastic left heart syndrome. **TOF is the commonest cyanotic CHD; TGA is the commonest cause of severe cyanosis after birth.**

### 7.1 Tetralogy of Fallot

- About 5 to 6% of CHD. Associated with **DiGeorge** and **Alagille** syndromes.
- **Four components:** large **VSD**, **pulmonary (infundibular) stenosis**, **overriding aorta**, **RV hypertrophy**.
- **Severity depends on the pulmonary stenosis.** Because of the stenosis, blood goes across the VSD into the aorta, causing cyanosis. With a hypertrophied infundibulum the obstruction increases over time.
- The second heart sound is **single** (small pulmonary component).

**Clinical features**

- Most newborns: asymptomatic with an **ejection systolic murmur**; **mild cyanosis that progresses**; **tet spells** if intervention is delayed.
- Older children develop **clubbing**.
- **Severe PS or pulmonary atresia:** severe cyanosis when the PDA closes (**duct-dependent**); needs **prostaglandin E2**.

**Tet spell** (usually at **9 to 12 months**): episodes of acute, severe cyanosis; a medical emergency. If prolonged beyond about **15 minutes**, treatment must be given.

| Treatment | Effect |
|---|---|
| Calm the child (stay with mother) | Reduces anxiety and PVR |
| **Oxygen** | Reduces PVR |
| **Morphine** (sedation) | Reduces PVR |
| **Knee-to-chest position** | Increases SVR |
| **Phenylephrine** | Increases SVR |
| **IV fluid** | Increases cardiac filling |
| **Beta blocker** (propranolol) | Reduces heart rate, relaxes infundibulum |

May need emergency surgery.

**Investigations:** CXR **boot-shaped heart** with **oligaemic lungs**; ECG RVH; echo confirms; CT/MRI for pulmonary artery branches.

**Treatment:** surgical repair (close the VSD and relieve the PS); the timing depends on the degree of stenosis (some neonatally, others at about 6 to 12 months).

> **Remember:** it is unusual for a child to have both heart failure and cyanosis. Heart failure is from pulmonary congestion; cyanosis is from reduced pulmonary flow. **Truncus arteriosus** is the exception (cyanosis plus heart failure).

### 7.2 D-transposition of the great arteries (D-TGA)

- About 4 to 5% of CHD. **Commonest CHD presenting with severe cyanosis at birth.** More common in males and in **infants of diabetic mothers**.
- The aorta arises from the **right ventricle** and the pulmonary artery from the **left ventricle** (discordant ventriculo-arterial connection).
- **Pathophysiology:** circulations in **parallel** rather than in series, so deoxygenated blood recirculates to the body. Survival needs mixing at:
  - **Atrial level (ASD/PFO)**: the most efficient
  - **Great artery level (PDA)**
  - **Ventricular level (VSD)** if present

**Presentation:** severe cyanosis soon after birth, **no respiratory distress**, **single second heart sound**, typically **no murmur**, **reverse differential cyanosis** if pulmonary hypertension (arms more cyanosed than legs), **hyperoxia test fails**.

**Investigations:** CXR **"egg on a string"**; ECG typically normal; echo confirms; cardiac cath for septostomy (and coronary anatomy).

**Management:** **prostaglandin E2**, **balloon atrial septostomy** for mixing (temporary), then **arterial switch operation (ASO)** for simple D-TGA (in the first 2 weeks of life).

### 7.3 Total anomalous pulmonary venous return (TAPVR)

- **All four pulmonary veins** drain to the right atrium (or its tributaries). Types: **supracardiac 50%**, **cardiac 25%**, **infracardiac 20%**, **mixed 5%**. Also **obstructed** or **non-obstructed**.
- **Features:** cyanosis at birth from mixing; tachypnoea from lung congestion; hypotension if obstructed. Obstructed forms (supracardiac or infracardiac) are the sickest, more so than TGA (cyanosed and hypotensive).
- **CXR:** "figure of eight" or "snowman" (supracardiac); small heart with an RDS-like picture (infracardiac obstructed). ECG: RVH. Echo confirms; CT/MRI delineates venous anatomy.
- **Treatment:** **surgery with excellent outcome**. Stabilise first: mechanical ventilation, correct acidosis, inotropes, **prostaglandin E1** to keep the PDA open (and the ductus venosus in type III). **Nitric oxide** may help post-operatively for pulmonary hypertensive episodes.

### 7.4 Other cyanotic lesions

| Lesion | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Hypoplastic left heart syndrome (HLHS)** | The **sickest of all neonates with CHD**: weak or absent **all** peripheral pulses (in CoA, only femoral pulses are weak). Stage 1: **Norwood** (arch reconstruction with BT shunt) |
| **Tricuspid atresia** | Stage 1: **BT shunt** to augment pulmonary flow |
| **Double inlet left ventricle** | Stage 1: **pulmonary artery banding** to restrict flow |
| **Single ventricle** | Heart cannot be divided into four chambers; staged surgery to the Fontan |
| **Truncus arteriosus**, **Ebstein anomaly**, pulmonary atresia | Complex lesions |

Staged univentricular palliation: **BT shunt, then Glenn, then Fontan**.

## 8. Additional high-yield points

- **Foramen ovale** closes because left atrial pressure becomes higher than right.
- **Innocent murmur:** soft, blowing, **systolic only**, left sternal edge, asymptomatic, normal heart sounds, no thrill, no radiation. Flow murmurs are often heard in fever or **anaemia**.
- **Central cyanosis** is less obvious in anaemia.
- **Cyanosis with respiratory distress:** cyanotic heart disease, respiratory disease, persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn, infection (GBS) or inborn error of metabolism (acidosis and shock).
- **Fetal anomaly scan** at **18 to 20 weeks**. **Detailed fetal echo** is indicated for an abnormal anomaly scan or a fetus at risk (Down syndrome, previous affected child, parent with CHD).
- **VSD:** the **smaller** the defect, the **louder** the murmur. Prevent endocarditis in a small VSD by good **dental hygiene** and avoiding piercings and tattoos.
- **PDA** connects the **pulmonary artery to the descending aorta**; in **preterm** infants it is due to prematurity, not true CHD.
- **Secundum ASD** involves the foramen ovale; **partial AVSD** (primum) has a regurgitant valve and requires surgery.
- **Interrupted aortic arch** is associated with **DiGeorge** (thymic aplasia, palatal defects, immunodeficiency, hypocalcaemia, 22q11.2 deletion).
- **Reverse differential cyanosis** (arms more cyanosed than legs) occurs in TGA with pulmonary hypertension, when oxygenated blood from the PA enters the descending aorta via the PDA.
- Clinical presentations by physiology: **cyanosis** (duct-dependent needs prostaglandin), **heart failure** (left-to-right shunt as PVR falls), **shock** (duct closure in severe left heart obstruction).

## Key points to revise

1. CHD is about 1% of births; VSD commonest, TOF commonest cyanotic, TGA commonest cause of severe neonatal cyanosis.
2. Left-to-right shunts cause heart failure at about 2 months; the late complication is Eisenmenger syndrome.
3. VSD: pansystolic murmur; ASD: fixed split S2; PDA: continuous machinery murmur and wide pulse pressure.
4. Closure: VSD surgery at 4 to 8 months; ASD and PDA by catheter; AVSD surgery before 6 months.
5. Duct-dependent lesions present in the first days with cyanosis or shock; start prostaglandin E.
6. Coarctation: weak femoral pulses, upper limb BP greater than lower, rib notching; critical form needs prostaglandin then surgery.
7. TOF: VSD, PS, overriding aorta, RVH; boot-shaped heart; tet spells treated with knee-to-chest, oxygen, morphine, fluids, phenylephrine, beta blocker.
8. D-TGA: egg on a string, single S2, no murmur; prostaglandin, balloon septostomy, then arterial switch.
9. TAPVR: figure of eight or snowman; obstructed forms are the sickest; surgery.
