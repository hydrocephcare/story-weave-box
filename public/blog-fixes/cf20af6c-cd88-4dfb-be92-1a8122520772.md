**Q41. Discuss the causative agent, pathogenesis, clinical manifestation and treatment of Cryptococcosis (20 marks)**

**Causative Agent:**
- *Cryptococcus neoformans* — var *neoformans* and var *grubii*
- *Cryptococcus gattii* — affects immunocompetent individuals
- Basidiomycete yeast — **only pathogenic basidiomycete**
- Unique features:
  - Large polysaccharide **capsule** — most important virulence factor
  - Produces **melanin** — protects against oxidative killing
  - **Urease** production
  - Growth at 37°C — thermotolerance
- Environmental reservoir: **pigeon droppings**, decaying wood, soil
- *C. gattii* — associated with eucalyptus trees
- Infects primarily **immunocompromised** — HIV/AIDS, transplant, lymphoma patients

**Virulence Factors:**
- **Capsule:**
  - Polysaccharide (glucuronoxylomannan)
  - Inhibits phagocytosis
  - Interferes with complement activation
  - Causes immune dysfunction
  - Detected in CSF/serum — diagnostic
- **Melanin:**
  - Deposited in cell wall
  - Protects against ROS and nitrogen species
  - Protects against antifungal drugs
  - Produced from dopamine in CNS — explains neurotropism
- **Phospholipase:**
  - Degrades cell membranes → allows invasion
- **Protease:**
  - Degrades host proteins → facilitates spread
- **Urease:**
  - Converts urea to ammonia → alkaline environment → aids survival

**Pathogenesis:**
- Inhalation of desiccated yeast/basidiospores from environment
- Deposited in alveoli → pulmonary infection
- **Alveolar macrophages** attempt phagocytosis — capsule resists
- Organism survives within macrophages — **Trojan horse mechanism**
- Spreads via blood — haematogenous dissemination
- Crosses **blood-brain barrier:**
  - Via infected macrophages (Trojan horse)
  - Transcytosis through endothelial cells
  - Melanin production from dopamine in CNS favours neurotropism
- Reaches meninges and brain → **cryptococcal meningitis**
- Capsular polysaccharide accumulates in CSF → increased intracranial pressure
- Minimal inflammatory response due to capsule → "soap bubble" lesions in brain

**Clinical Manifestations:**

*Pulmonary Cryptococcosis:*
- Often asymptomatic in immunocompetent
- Cough, fever, chest pain, dyspnoea
- Pulmonary nodules on chest X-ray
- Can resolve spontaneously in immunocompetent

*Cryptococcal Meningitis (most important):*
- Gradual onset — weeks to months
- **Headache** — most common symptom — progressive, severe
- Fever — low grade
- Neck stiffness — meningeal irritation
- Photophobia — sensitivity to light
- Altered consciousness — confusion, drowsiness
- Visual disturbances — papilloedema due to raised ICP
- Cranial nerve palsies
- Seizures in severe cases
- **"Spacing out"** — classic description — 450 mononuclear cells per milliliter

*Cutaneous Cryptococcosis:*
- Papules, nodules, ulcers
- Resembles molluscum contagiosum in AIDS patients
- Indicates disseminated disease

*Ocular Cryptococcosis:*
- Visual loss
- Due to raised intracranial pressure or direct invasion

*Disseminated:*
- Bone lesions
- Prostate — common reservoir — explains relapse
- Liver, spleen involvement

**Diagnosis:**
- **India ink stain** of CSF — encapsulated yeast with clear halo around it
- **Latex agglutination** — detects cryptococcal capsular antigen in CSF/serum — very sensitive and specific
- **Culture** on Sabouraud agar — mucoid white colonies
- **Niger/Birdseed agar** — brown colonies due to melanin production
- **CSF analysis:**
  - Elevated protein
  - Reduced glucose
  - Mononuclear pleocytosis
  - Elevated opening pressure
- **Blood culture** — positive in disseminated disease
- **Histology** — mucicarmine stain highlights capsule red

**Treatment:**
- **Induction phase (2 weeks):**
  - Amphotericin B + Flucytosine
  - Most fungicidal combination
- **Consolidation phase (8 weeks):**
  - Fluconazole 400mg daily
- **Maintenance phase (lifelong in HIV):**
  - Fluconazole 200mg daily
- **Management of raised ICP:**
  - Serial lumbar punctures — drain CSF
  - Acetazolamide — reduces CSF production
- **IRIS (Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome):**
  - Occurs when ART started in HIV patients
  - Inflammatory response worsens as immunity recovers
  - Manage with corticosteroids

**Q42. Discuss the causative agent, pathogenesis, clinical manifestation, diagnosis and treatment of Chromoblastomycosis (20 marks)**

**Causative Agents:**
- Dematiaceous (pigmented/melanized) fungi — contain melanin in cell walls
- **Most common:** *Fonsecaea pedrosoi*
- Others: *Phialophora verrucosa*, *Cladophialophora carrionii*, *Rhinocladiella aquaspersa*
- Found in soil, decaying wood, plant material — tropical and subtropical regions
- West Africa, Central and South America, Madagascar

**Pathogenesis:**
- **Entry:** Traumatic implantation of fungi into skin — thorn prick, splinter, wood fragment
- Common in barefoot agricultural workers
- Fungi introduced into dermis/subcutaneous tissue
- **Acute response:** Neutrophils recruited → acute inflammation
- Fungi resist phagocytic killing — melanin protects against oxidative burst
- **Chronic response:**
  - Macrophages surround organisms → granuloma formation
  - Multinucleate giant cells attempt to engulf organisms
  - Lymphocytes recruited — cell-mediated immunity
- Fungi convert to **sclerotic bodies (muriform/copper penny bodies)** in tissue:
  - Dark brown, round cells — 5-12 μm
  - Transverse septations — divide in multiple planes
  - Reproduce by **fission** — NOT budding
  - Pathognomonic of chromoblastomycosis
- Immune response cannot eliminate organism → chronic progressive disease
- **Fibrosis** — dense collagen deposition around lesions
- **Lymphatic obstruction** → lymphoedema of affected limb
- **Transepidermal elimination** — body attempts to extrude organisms through skin
- Over months/years → characteristic cauliflower-like warty lesions

**Clinical Manifestations:**

*Early lesion:*
- Small papule or nodule at site of injury
- Usually on foot, leg or hand
- Slight itching — often ignored

*Progressive disease:*
- Lesion slowly enlarges over months/years
- Develops into **verrucous (warty) plaques**
- Classic **cauliflower-like appearance** — hallmark of disease
- Purplish/reddish-brown colour — due to melanin
- Multiple satellite lesions develop
- New crop of wart-like purplish scaly lesions

*Complications:*
- **Lymphoedema** — massive swelling of limb
- **Secondary bacterial infection** — common — foul odour
- **Squamous cell carcinoma** — rare malignant transformation in chronic cases
- **Elephantiasis** — massive lymphoedema resembling leprosy
- Rarely disseminates to brain — cerebral chromoblastomycosis

**Diagnosis:**

- **KOH preparation** of skin scrapings — sclerotic bodies visible as dark brown cells with septations
- **Biopsy and histology:**
  - H&E stain — brown sclerotic bodies in tissue
  - Granulomatous inflammation
  - Transepidermal elimination of organisms
- **Culture on SDA:**
  - Slow growing — weeks
  - Black/dark brown colonies
  - Identify by microscopic morphology of conidia
- **Wood's lamp** — no fluorescence (unlike dermatophytes)

**Treatment:**
- Difficult — chronic disease requires prolonged therapy
- **Itraconazole** — drug of choice — 200-400mg daily for months/years
- **Terbinafine** — alternative or combination with Itraconazole
- **5-Flucytosine** — added for resistant cases
- **Physical methods:**
  - Cryotherapy with liquid nitrogen — for small lesions
  - Surgical excision — small localized lesions only
  - Heat therapy — local application of heat
- **Combination therapy** best for extensive disease
- Treatment duration — minimum 12 months
- Recurrence common after stopping treatment
