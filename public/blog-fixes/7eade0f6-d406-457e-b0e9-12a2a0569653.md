## SECTION B – SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS (15 MARKS)
*1. Outline the virulence factors of Streptococcus pyogenes. (5 marks)*

*Answer:*

Streptococcus pyogenes possesses multiple virulence factors:

*Surface components:*

- M protein: Inhibits phagocytosis by preventing complement C3b deposition and binding factor H; also mediates adherence to epithelial cells
- Lipoteichoic acid and F protein: Facilitate adherence to host cells
- Capsule (hyaluronic acid): Antiphagocytic, mimics host tissue

*Enzymes:*

- Streptokinase: Dissolves fibrin clots, facilitating spread
- Streptodornases (DNases): Liquefy pus by degrading DNA
- Hyaluronidase: Breaks down connective tissue, promoting spread
- C5a peptidase: Degrades complement component C5a, impairing neutrophil chemotaxis

*Toxins:*

- Streptolysins O and S: Lyse red blood cells, neutrophils, and other cells
- Pyrogenic (erythrogenic) exotoxins: Act as superantigens causing scarlet fever and toxic shock
- Streptolysin O is oxygen-labile and antigenic; Streptolysin S is oxygen-stable and non-antigenic

These factors collectively enable the organism to adhere, evade immune responses, spread through tissues, and cause local and systemic disease.

*2. Differentiate a syphilitic chancre from a Haemophilus ducreyi chancroid. (5 marks)*

*Answer:*

*Syphilitic chancre (Treponema pallidum):*

- Painless ulcer
- Single lesion (usually)
- Clean, indurated base with raised, firm borders
- Non-purulent discharge
- Regional lymphadenopathy: bilateral, firm, non-tender, non-suppurative
- Appears 10-90 days (average 21 days) after exposure
- Heals spontaneously in 3-6 weeks
- Highly infectious

*Chancroid (Haemophilus ducreyi):*

- Painful ulcer
- Multiple lesions common
- Soft, non-indurated base with ragged, undermined edges
- Purulent exudate
- Regional lymphadenopathy: unilateral, tender, may suppurate and form buboes
- Appears 4-7 days after exposure
- Does not heal spontaneously without treatment
- Infectious but less so than chancre

*Key differentiating features:* Pain (chancroid is painful, chancre is not), ulcer consistency (soft vs. hard/indurated), and lymph node characteristics (suppurative vs. non-suppurative).

*3. Explain the mechanism of action of Clostridium tetani toxin. (5 marks)*

*Answer:*

*Tetanospasmin mechanism:*

Clostridium tetani produces tetanospasmin (tetanus toxin), a potent neurotoxin that causes the characteristic spastic paralysis of tetanus.

*Steps:*

- *Production and release:* Tetanospasmin is produced at the wound site during vegetative bacterial growth under anaerobic conditions
- *Binding and uptake:* The toxin binds to receptors (gangliosides and glycoproteins) at neuromuscular junctions and enters motor neurons via receptor-mediated endocytosis
- *Retrograde transport:* The toxin is transported retrograde (backwards) through axons to the spinal cord and brainstem
- *Trans-synaptic migration:* Tetanospasmin crosses synapses to reach inhibitory interneurons (Renshaw cells)
- *Cleavage of SNARE proteins:* The toxin's light chain (a zinc metalloprotease) cleaves synaptobrevin (VAMP), a SNARE protein essential for vesicle fusion
- *Block of inhibitory neurotransmitter release:* This prevents release of inhibitory neurotransmitters (GABA and glycine) from Renshaw cells onto motor neurons
- *Result:* Unopposed motor neuron stimulation leads to sustained muscle contraction (spastic paralysis), causing trismus (lockjaw), risus sardonicus, opisthotonus, and generalized muscle spasms

Unlike botulinum toxin which causes flaccid paralysis by blocking acetylcholine release, tetanus toxin causes spastic paralysis by blocking inhibitory signals.

### *Question 1*
An 8-year-old boy presents with bloody diarrhea and fever after a camping trip. Gram stain shows curved Gram-negative rods.

*a. Identify the most likely organism.*

*Answer:*The most likely organism is *Campylobacter jejuni*. The clinical presentation of bloody diarrhea with fever following camping (suggesting exposure to contaminated food or water), combined with the characteristic curved (comma-shaped or spiral) Gram-negative rods on microscopy, is highly suggestive of Campylobacter infection. C. jejuni is the most common bacterial cause of gastroenteritis worldwide.

*b. Describe possible routes of exposure.*

*Answer:*

- *Contaminated food:* Undercooked poultry (chicken is the primary reservoir), unpasteurized milk, contaminated water used for drinking or food preparation
- *Contaminated water:* Drinking untreated stream or lake water during camping; swimming in contaminated water
- *Animal contact:* Direct contact with infected animals (pets, farm animals, especially puppies with diarrhea)
- *Cross-contamination:* Poor food handling practices during food preparation
- *Person-to-person:* Fecal-oral transmission (rare but possible in young children)

*c. Explain how food or water becomes contaminated.*

*Answer:*

- *Poultry contamination:* Campylobacter colonizes the intestinal tract of chickens and other birds asymptomatically. During slaughter and processing, intestinal contents contaminate carcasses
- *Water contamination:* Animal and bird feces containing Campylobacter contaminate surface water sources (streams, lakes, wells). Without proper treatment or boiling, water remains infectious
- *Milk contamination:* Occurs through fecal contamination during milking or from infected udders
- *Cross-contamination in food preparation:* Raw poultry juices contaminate other foods, cutting boards, or utensils
- *Environmental persistence:* The organism can survive in moist, cool environments (refrigeration temperatures), allowing persistence in food and water

*d. State potential complications and explain their pathogenesis.*

*Answer:*

*Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS):*

- Most serious complication, occurring in approximately 1 in 1,000 infections
- Pathogenesis: Molecular mimicry between Campylobacter lipooligosaccharide (LOS) and gangliosides (GM1, GD1a) on peripheral nerve myelin
- Cross-reactive antibodies attack peripheral nerves, causing demyelination
- Results in ascending flaccid paralysis, potentially requiring mechanical ventilation

*Reactive arthritis:*

- Occurs 1-3 weeks post-infection
- Pathogenesis: Immune-mediated inflammation triggered by bacterial antigens deposited in joints
- Affects large joints (knees, ankles), may be part of Reiter's syndrome with urethritis and conjunctivitis

*Bacteremia:*

- Rare, more common in immunocompromised patients, infants, and elderly
- Can lead to extraintestinal infections (meningitis, endocarditis, septic arthritis)

*Hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS):*

- Rare complication
- Post-infectious thrombotic microangiopathy affecting kidneys

*e. Name an antibiotic that shortens severe disease.*

*Answer: **Azithromycin* (a macrolide antibiotic) is the drug of choice. Alternative agents include erythromycin and fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin), though resistance to fluoroquinolones is increasing. Antibiotics are most effective when given early in illness (within first 3-4 days) and are particularly indicated for severe disease, immunocompromised patients, or bloody diarrhea. For mild, self-limited cases, supportive care with hydration is often sufficient.

### *Question 2*
A 17-year-old male presents with urethral discharge. Gram stain shows Gram-negative diplococci.

*a. Identify the disease and causative organism.*

*Answer: **Disease:* Gonorrhea (gonococcal urethritis)*Causative organism:* Neisseria gonorrhoeae

The presence of Gram-negative diplococci (kidney bean-shaped, in pairs) within neutrophils on urethral discharge Gram stain is diagnostic of gonococcal infection. This is the most common cause of purulent urethral discharge in sexually active males.

*b. Explain why his sexual partners require examination.*

*Answer:*Sexual partners require examination for several critical reasons:

- *High transmission rate:* Gonorrhea has a transmission rate of 20-50% per sexual contact, meaning partners are likely infected
- *Asymptomatic infection in women:* Up to 50% of infected women are asymptomatic but can still transmit infection and develop complications
- *Prevention of reinfection:* Treating the patient alone without treating partners leads to "ping-pong" reinfection
- *Prevention of complications:* Untreated infection in partners can lead to pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), infertility, ectopic pregnancy in women, and epididymitis in men
- *Break transmission chain:* Contact tracing and treatment prevent further spread in the community
- *Public health requirement:* Most jurisdictions require partner notification and treatment for gonorrhea as it is a reportable disease

*c. State other organisms for which he is at increased risk and why.*

*Answer:*Patients with gonorrhea are at increased risk for:

*Chlamydia trachomatis:*

- Coinfection occurs in 30-50% of cases
- Both are sexually transmitted and share similar risk factors
- Often asymptomatic, can cause non-gonococcal urethritis
- CDC recommends routine testing and empiric treatment for both

*HIV:*

- Gonorrhea causes urethral inflammation and disruption of mucosal barriers
- Increased genital tract HIV shedding in coinfected patients
- Enhanced HIV transmission and acquisition due to inflammatory response
- Behavior indicating unprotected sex increases risk for both

*Syphilis (Treponema pallidum):*

- Shares transmission routes and risk factors
- Genital ulcers increase HIV transmission risk
- May have concurrent infection

*Other STIs:* Herpes simplex virus, hepatitis B, trichomonas

*Why:* All share the common risk factor of unprotected sexual contact, and the presence of one STI indicates high-risk sexual behavior and increased likelihood of exposure to others.

*d. Explain the significance of the absence of inguinal lymphadenopathy and testicular pain.*

*Answer:*

*Absence of inguinal lymphadenopathy suggests:*

- *Not chancroid* (Haemophilus ducreyi): Would cause painful inguinal lymphadenopathy with possible bubo formation
- *Not lymphogranuloma venereum* (LGV - Chlamydia trachomatis serovars L1-L3): Would cause prominent inguinal/femoral lymphadenopathy with groove sign
- *Consistent with uncomplicated gonorrhea:* N. gonorrhoeae typically does not cause significant lymphadenopathy in urethritis

*Absence of testicular pain suggests:*

- *No epididymitis:* Inflammation has not spread to epididymis, which would cause testicular pain, swelling, and tenderness
- *Uncomplicated infection:* Disease is limited to anterior urethra
- *Good prognosis:* Less likely to have ascending infection or complications
- *Early presentation:* Patient sought care before development of complications

However, the clinician should still palpate for subtle epididymal tenderness and counsel about warning signs requiring return visit.

*e. State the recommended antibiotics.*

*Answer:*Current CDC recommendations (2021) for uncomplicated gonococcal urethritis:

*Single-dose intramuscular ceftriaxone 500 mg* (250 mg if <150 kg body weight)

*PLUS (for presumptive chlamydial coinfection):*

*Doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 7 days*OR*Azithromycin 1 gram orally single dose* (though doxycycline is now preferred due to concerns about azithromycin-resistant Mycoplasma genitalium)

*Rationale:*

- Ceftriaxone addresses increasing fluoroquinolone resistance
- Dual therapy covers likely chlamydial coinfection (30-50% of cases)
- Single-dose IM therapy ensures compliance
- Alternative for ceftriaxone allergy: cefixime 800 mg PO single dose or gentamicin 240 mg IM plus azithromycin 2 g PO

*Additional management:* Partner notification and treatment, test of cure if symptoms persist, HIV and syphilis testing, counseling on safe sex practices.

### *Question 3*
A 33-year-old HIV-positive man has fever, weight loss, rust-colored sputum, and abnormal chest X-ray. A tuberculin test is performed.

*a. Why was a chest X-ray requested?*

*Answer:*A chest X-ray was requested to:

- *Evaluate for pulmonary tuberculosis:* The clinical presentation (fever, weight loss, productive cough with rust-colored sputum) is highly suggestive of active pulmonary TB
- *Assess extent of disease:* Determine if disease is localized or disseminated, identify cavitary lesions, infiltrates, or miliary pattern
- *Identify characteristic findings:* Look for upper lobe infiltrates, cavitation, hilar/mediastinal lymphadenopathy, pleural effusion typical of TB
- *Screen for complications:* Detect pneumothorax, empyema, or extensive parenchymal involvement
- *Differentiate from other conditions:* Rule out other causes of respiratory symptoms in HIV patients (bacterial pneumonia, Pneumocystis jirovecii, Kaposi's sarcoma, fungal infections)
- *Assess HIV-related patterns:* HIV patients may show atypical radiographic features (lower lobe disease, diffuse infiltrates, absence of cavitation)
- *Guide treatment decisions:* Severity of radiographic findings influences treatment approach and monitoring
- *Baseline for follow-up:* Provides baseline to monitor treatment response

*b. Why was a sputum sample collected?*

*Answer:*Sputum was collected to:

*Microbiological diagnosis:*

- *Direct microscopy:* Ziehl-Neelsen or auramine-rhodamine staining to visualize acid-fast bacilli (AFB), providing rapid preliminary diagnosis
- *Culture:* Gold standard for definitive diagnosis; identifies Mycobacterium tuberculosis and allows drug susceptibility testing
- *Molecular testing:* GeneXpert MTB/RIF provides rapid detection of TB and rifampin resistance within 2 hours
- *Species identification:* Distinguishes M. tuberculosis from non-tuberculous mycobacteria

*Assess infectiousness:*

- Sputum smear positivity indicates higher bacterial load and greater infectiousness, requiring isolation precautions

*Monitor treatment:*

- Serial sputum samples (at 2, 4, 6 months) assess treatment response and bacteriological cure
- Conversion from positive to negative indicates treatment success

*Drug resistance testing:*

- Culture allows phenotypic and genotypic drug susceptibility testing to detect MDR-TB or XDR-TB

*c. Describe the appearance of a positive tuberculin skin test.*

*Answer:*A positive tuberculin (Mantoux) skin test appears as:

*Physical characteristics:*

- *Induration (not erythema):* A raised, firm, palpable swelling at the injection site on the volar surface of the forearm
- *Size:* Measured as transverse diameter of induration (perpendicular to long axis of forearm) at 48-72 hours post-injection
- *Shape:* Usually circular or oval
- *Color:* Skin may appear pale or slightly erythematous, but only induration is measured
- *Texture:* Firm to palpation, distinct from surrounding soft tissue
- *Border:* Well-demarcated edge distinguishing induration from normal skin

*Measurement:*

- Use fingertips or ballpoint pen to palpate edges
- Measure widest transverse diameter in millimeters
- Record actual measurement; do not record as simply "positive" or "negative"

*d. Interpret the meaning of such a result.*

*Answer:*Interpretation depends on induration size and patient risk factors:

*For this HIV-positive patient:*

- *≥5 mm is positive:* HIV infection significantly lowers threshold for positivity
- Indicates *cell-mediated immune response* to tubercular antigens (PPD)
- Suggests *latent TB infection (LTBI)* or active TB disease

*Positive TST indicates:*

- *Previous exposure* to M. tuberculosis
- *Immune response* to mycobacterial antigens
- *Does NOT distinguish* between latent infection and active disease
- *Does NOT confirm diagnosis* of active TB (requires clinical, radiographic, and microbiological evidence)

*In HIV patients, special considerations:*

- *Reduced sensitivity:* Anergy (inability to mount immune response) common with low CD4 counts (<200 cells/μL)
- *False negatives common:* Severe immunosuppression may prevent induration despite TB infection
- *IGRA tests preferred:* Interferon-gamma release assays (QuantiFERON, T-SPOT) are more specific and not affected by BCG vaccination

*Clinical context:* Given this patient's symptoms, abnormal CXR, and HIV status, a positive TST (or any suspicion) warrants aggressive workup for active TB disease, not just LTBI.

*e. Identify the disease and its causative agent.*

*Answer: **Disease:* Pulmonary tuberculosis (active TB disease)*Causative agent:* Mycobacterium tuberculosis

M. tuberculosis is an acid-fast, aerobic bacillus that causes chronic granulomatous disease. It is an obligate aerobe, explaining its predilection for oxygen-rich apical lung regions. The rust-colored (blood-tinged) sputum, constitutional symptoms, and radiographic abnormalities are classic for pulmonary TB.
