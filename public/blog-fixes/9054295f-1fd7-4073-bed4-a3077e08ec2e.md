# Arrhythmias in Children

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Paediatric Cardiology**

> **In one line:** an arrhythmia is an abnormality of heart rate or rhythm, arising from the atria, junction, ventricles or conduction system. Most childhood tachycardias are supraventricular (SVT) and are treated with vagal manoeuvres and adenosine if stable, or synchronised cardioversion if unstable. Ventricular arrhythmias, long QT syndrome and heart block are the dangerous ones because they can cause sudden death.

## What you must know

- The heart is a **functional syncytium**: myocytes joined by intercalated discs with gap junctions allow rapid, synchronous depolarisation.
- Conduction path: **SA node, interatrial tract (Bachmann's bundle) and internodal tracts, AV node, bundle of His, right and left bundle branches, Purkinje fibres.**
- **SVT** is the commonest abnormal tachycardia and the commonest arrhythmia needing treatment in children; peaks in the **first 3 months**, again at **8 to 10 years** and in adolescence.
- SVT management: **vagal manoeuvres, then rapid IV adenosine** if stable; **synchronised DC cardioversion 1 J/kg** if unstable. **Verapamil is contraindicated under 1 year.**
- **WPW**: short PR, delta wave and wide QRS seen in sinus rhythm; risk of sudden death.
- **Long QT**: QTc above 0.47 s (or QT above 0.44 s); treat with **beta blockers**; LQT3 has the highest risk of sudden death.
- **Congenital complete heart block**: maternal anti-Ro/La antibodies (SLE, Sjogren); pacemaker if rate 50/min or less, heart failure or CHD.
- Always look for reversible causes: **the Hs and Ts**.

## 1. Classification and presentation

- **Tachyarrhythmias** are classified by origin: **atrial, junctional, ventricular**. **Bradyarrhythmias** include sinus node disease and **heart blocks**.

### Tachyarrhythmias: symptoms

- General: palpitations, light-headedness, syncope, fatigue, shortness of breath, chest pain.
- **Infants**: poor feeding, tachypnoea, irritability, sleepiness, pallor, vomiting.
- **Haemodynamic instability**: respiratory distress or failure, hypotension, poor end-organ perfusion, loss of consciousness, sudden collapse.

### Causes

- **Primary**: underlying conduction abnormality.
- **Secondary (reversible Hs and Ts):**

| Hs | Ts |
|---|---|
| Hypovolaemia | Toxins |
| Hypoxia | Tamponade |
| H+ ions (acidosis) | Tension pneumothorax |
| Hypoglycaemia | Thrombosis (coronary, pulmonary) |
| Hypothermia | Trauma |
| Hypo-/hyperkalaemia | |

## 2. Atrial rhythms

| Rhythm | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Sinus arrhythmia** | Normal variation with respiration: rate slows on expiration, rises on inspiration. Exaggerated by digoxin; abolished by exercise. Varying PP interval; P wave and PR unchanged |
| **Sinus tachycardia** | Sinus node faster than usual in response to need for more cardiac output: exercise, anxiety, fever, hypovolaemia or shock, anaemia, heart failure, catecholamines, thyrotoxicosis, myocardial disease. Normal narrow P waves and QRS; beat-to-beat variability |
| **Premature atrial contractions (PACs)** | Benign without heart disease; common in newborns. Early P wave, often with different shape. Depending on timing: normal, aberrant (wide) QRS, or non-conducted (pause) |
| **Wandering atrial pacemaker** | Pacemaker shifts from SA node to another atrial site; normal variant; irregular rhythm |

## 3. Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)

- Originates above the ventricles. Commonest abnormal tachycardia and commonest treated arrhythmia in children.
- Peak ages: **first 3 months of life**, **8 to 10 years**, adolescence.
- **Features:** paroxysmal, sudden onset and offset, at rest. Triggers: infection in infants; bronchodilators and decongestants in children. Rate above about 180/min (age-dependent; in infants usually above 220).
- Short episodes are usually harmless. Episodes of **6 to 24 hours** or rate **above 300/min** can cause **heart failure**.
- Older children report palpitations. Younger children have a higher baseline rate that rises further with crying.
- **ECG:** P waves hard to see, 1:1 with a normal (narrow) QRS; looks like sinus tachycardia. **Differentiating features: rate above 230/min, unvarying rate, abnormal P wave axis.**

### Types

1. Re-entrant tachycardia **with an accessory pathway** (AVRT).
2. Re-entrant tachycardia **without** an accessory pathway (AVNRT).
3. **Ectopic/automatic** tachycardias (atrial ectopic, junctional ectopic, atrial flutter and fibrillation as related atrial arrhythmias).

### AV reciprocating tachycardia (AVRT) and WPW

- **Commonest mechanism of SVT in infants.** Re-entry using an accessory pathway; conduction may be bidirectional or retrograde only.
- **Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW):** a muscular bridge connects atria and ventricles on the right or left of the AV ring; conduction bidirectional. During SVT, impulses go **antegrade through the AV node and retrograde via the accessory pathway**, so QRS is narrow and P waves sit on the upstroke of the T wave.
- When tachycardia stops, the typical features appear: **short PR, delta wave, wide QRS**. There is a **risk of sudden death**.

### AV nodal re-entrant tachycardia (AVNRT)

- Common SVT in adolescents; uses two pathways within the AV node; precipitated by exercise; may present with syncope; good control on drugs; **beta blockers** are the maintenance drug of choice.

### Atrial ectopic tachycardia

- Uncommon. Variable rate, usually above 200/min, from a single automatic focus. On starting drugs the rate slows and then speeds up again. ECG: ectopic P waves with abnormal axis.

### Multifocal atrial tachycardia

- More common in infants. **Three or more ectopic P wave shapes** and varying PR intervals; chaotic pattern. Usually resolves by **3 years**.

### Junctional ectopic tachycardia (JET)

- Abnormal automatic focus in tissue near the AV node; discharges faster than the SA node, giving **AV dissociation**.
- Occurs **early post-operatively** or is congenital.
- **IV amiodarone** is the drug of choice for post-operative JET. Congenital JET needs **catheter ablation**. Maintenance: amiodarone or sotalol.

### Atrial flutter

- Intra-atrial re-entry circuit (right atrium around the tricuspid annulus). Atrial rate **400 to 600/min in neonates; 250 to 300/min in children**; ventricles respond to every 2nd to 4th beat.
- Seen in neonates with normal hearts and in children with CHD (large stretched atria) or after surgery.
- ECG: rapid, regular **saw-tooth flutter waves**.
- Treatment: temporary slowing with vagal manoeuvres, adenosine or calcium channel blocker; **synchronised DC cardioversion is the treatment of choice**. Chronic flutter has **thromboembolism and stroke risk** and needs anticoagulation. Maintenance with class 1 and 3 agents.

### Atrial fibrillation

- Uncommon in children. Atrial rate 400 to 700/min; **irregularly irregular** rhythm (no two R-R intervals alike), no clear P waves.
- Causes: post-operative, CHD with enlarged atria, thyrotoxicosis, pulmonary embolism, pericarditis, cardiomyopathy.
- Stable: calcium channel blocker, IV procainamide or amiodarone. Unstable: DC cardioversion.

### Management of SVT

1. **Vagal manoeuvres**: ice bag to the face (infants), Valsalva, straining, breath-holding.
2. **Stable:** **rapid IV push of adenosine** (risk of precipitating atrial fibrillation). In older children, IV **verapamil** (**contraindicated under 1 year**).
3. **Unstable:** **synchronised DC cardioversion 1 J/kg.**
4. **Resistant:** IV procainamide, quinidine, flecainide, sotalol or amiodarone.
5. **Persistent:** **catheter ablation** (radiofrequency, cryo, or surgical), success **80 to 95%**.
6. **Maintenance** after sinus rhythm: **beta blockers** are the drug of choice in WPW and non-WPW SVT; **digoxin** can be used in infants without an accessory pathway.

> **Exam tip:** digoxin and verapamil can accelerate conduction down an accessory pathway; avoid them in WPW with pre-excitation. Adenosine is given as a very fast bolus followed by a saline flush because its half-life is seconds.

## 4. Ventricular arrhythmias

### Premature ventricular contractions (PVCs)

- Uncommon in children; unifocal or multifocal. Early wide QRS with T wave opposite to QRS; **bigeminy** (sinus beat then PVC repeating) is common.
- **Benign** if unifocal, abolished by exercise and the heart is normal: no treatment, avoid caffeine and stimulants.

### Ventricular tachycardia (VT)

- At least **3 PVCs at above 120/min**; paroxysmal or incessant. Wide QRS (above 0.08 s); P waves hidden or unrelated to QRS.
- Associated with myocarditis, **anomalous left coronary artery**, mitral valve prolapse, cardiac tumours, cardiomyopathy, post-operative state.
- Treat promptly to prevent degeneration into VF. **Stable:** IV amiodarone, lidocaine or procainamide plus correct the cause. **Unstable:** DC cardioversion.

### Ventricular fibrillation (VF)

- Seen in **long QT syndrome, Brugada syndrome**, cardiomyopathies and structural heart disease with ventricular dysfunction. **Sudden death** unless an effective beat is quickly restored.
- **Immediate DC defibrillation and CPR.** If ineffective: IV amiodarone, lidocaine and repeat defibrillation. Treat the cause afterwards.

## 5. Long QT syndromes

- Genetic abnormalities of **ventricular repolarisation**; long QT on ECG; malignant ventricular arrhythmias and **sudden death**. At least **50% are familial**; precipitated by exercise.
- Triggers: **LQT1** stress or exercise (e.g. swimming); **LQT2** intermediate (e.g. sudden noise); **LQT3** during **sleep**, with the **highest probability of sudden death**.
- **Diagnosis:** syncope, seizures or palpitations; **QTc above 0.47 s or QT above 0.44 s**; notched T waves; low heart rate for age; family history of LQTS or sudden death.
- **Treatment:** **beta blockers** (blunt the heart rate response to exercise). Pacemaker if beta blocker-induced profound bradycardia. **Implantable cardiac defibrillator (ICD)** for drug-resistant disease or LQT3.

> **Remember:** avoid QT-prolonging drugs (macrolides, some antimalarials such as halofantrine, antiemetics like ondansetron, antipsychotics) in these children, and correct hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia.

## 6. Bradyarrhythmias

- **Symptoms:** altered consciousness, fatigue, dizziness, syncope; instability gives hypotension, poor perfusion, respiratory distress, sudden collapse.
- **Causes:** primary (abnormal pacemaker or conduction system, congenital or post-surgical, cardiomyopathy, myocarditis); secondary reversible causes: hypoxia, hypotension, acidosis, heart block, hypothermia, hyperkalaemia, head trauma, drugs (cholinesterase inhibitors, calcium channel blockers, beta blockers, digoxin, alpha-2 agonists, opioids).

| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Sinus bradycardia** | Physiological (sleep, athletes) or pathological (electrolyte disturbance, infection, drugs, hypoglycaemia, hypothyroidism, raised ICP) |
| **Sinus arrest** | Failure of impulse formation in the SA node |
| **Sinoatrial block** | Block between SA node and atrium; rare in children; digoxin toxicity, extensive atrial surgery |
| **Sick sinus syndrome** | SA node and/or atrial pathway abnormality; after CHD surgery (**Fontan, Mustard, Senning**) or in normal hearts. Usually asymptomatic and untreated; marked slowing causes dizziness and syncope; pacemaker if symptoms recur |

### AV blocks

| Degree | ECG | Causes and significance |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | Prolonged PR interval | AV nodal disease, myocarditis, hyperkalaemia, drugs (calcium channel blockers, beta blockers, digoxin), **acute rheumatic fever**. Usually asymptomatic |
| **2nd, Mobitz I (Wenckebach)** | PR lengthens progressively until a P wave is not conducted, then cycle restarts with a short PR | Block within the AV node; drugs. May cause dizziness; transient and benign; rarely progresses to complete block |
| **2nd, Mobitz II** | Constant prolonged PR; a set proportion of P waves not conducted | Defect below the AV node/His bundle; acute coronary syndrome. Palpitations, presyncope, syncope; can progress to complete block; often needs pacemaker |
| **3rd (complete)** | AV dissociation; no atrial impulses conducted | Congenital, conduction disease (tumours, abscess, myocarditis) or injury (VSD surgery). Most symptomatic: fatigue, presyncope, syncope. Usually needs pacemaker |

### Congenital complete AV block

- **Autoimmune injury** to the fetal conduction system from maternal **anti-SSA/Ro and anti-SSB/La** antibodies (maternal **SLE or Sjogren syndrome**). **NKX2-5** mutation causes complete block with ASD. High fetal loss; may cause **hydrops fetalis**.
- Child: tiredness, frequent naps, irritability, features of heart failure; **prominent peripheral pulses** (increased stroke volume); murmur. ECG: P waves and QRS with no constant relationship.
- **Pacemaker** if heart rate 50/min or less, signs of heart failure, or CHD present.

## 7. Antiarrhythmic drugs: Vaughan Williams classification

| Class | Action | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1a** | Fast sodium channel blockers; prolong repolarisation | Quinidine, procainamide, disopyramide |
| **1b** | Fast sodium channel blockers; shorten repolarisation | Lidocaine, mexiletine, phenytoin |
| **1c** | Sodium channel blockers | Flecainide, propafenone |
| **2** | Beta blockers | Propranolol, atenolol |
| **3** | Potassium channel blockers; prolong repolarisation | Amiodarone (also sotalol) |
| **4** | Calcium channel blockers / miscellaneous | Verapamil; adenosine and digoxin are listed as miscellaneous |

> **Check:** the slide calls class 3 "potassium channel openers"; class 3 drugs actually **block** potassium channels, which prolongs repolarisation. Strictly, class 4 is calcium channel blockers (verapamil); adenosine and digoxin are unclassified.

## Key points to revise

1. Conduction: SA node, internodal tracts, AV node, His, bundle branches, Purkinje.
2. SVT is the commonest treated arrhythmia: rate above 230/min, fixed, abrupt onset; peaks under 3 months and at 8 to 10 years.
3. SVT: vagal manoeuvres, adenosine, cardioversion 1 J/kg if unstable; avoid verapamil under 1 year; beta blockers for maintenance; ablation 80 to 95% successful.
4. WPW: delta wave, short PR, wide QRS; AVRT is the commonest infant SVT; risk of sudden death.
5. Atrial flutter: saw-tooth waves; cardioversion; anticoagulate if chronic. Atrial fibrillation: irregularly irregular.
6. JET after cardiac surgery: IV amiodarone.
7. VT (3 or more PVCs above 120/min) and VF need cardioversion or defibrillation; consider long QT and Brugada.
8. Long QT: QTc above 0.47 s, beta blockers, ICD for LQT3 or refractory; congenital heart block: maternal anti-Ro/La, pacemaker if rate 50/min or less.
