# Breastfeeding and Weaning

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Infant Nutrition**

> **In one line:** breastfeeding is initiated by prolactin (milk synthesis) and sustained by oxytocin (milk ejection) in response to suckling, and regular milk removal keeps supply going. The WHO-UNICEF target is initiation within 1 hour, exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, then continued breastfeeding to 2 years or more with safe, age-appropriate complementary foods from 6 months.

## What you must know

- WHO-UNICEF Global Strategy (2003): **initiate within 1 hour of birth, exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months, continue for 2 years or more** with adequate, safe complementary feeding after 6 months.
- **Prolactin** makes milk (galactokinesis stage, via suckling suppressing dopamine); **oxytocin** ejects milk (let-down reflex via myoepithelial contraction).
- Milk removal controls supply: unemptied alveoli raise pressure and **feedback inhibitor of lactation (FIL)** reduces production.
- **Colostrum** is rich in IgA and protein, laxative and "the baby's first immunisation"; mature milk has all vitamins except **K**, and low iron.
- Breastfeeding lowers infant infection (pneumonia, meningitis, otitis media, gastroenteritis), allergy, obesity and diabetes risk, and reduces maternal PPH.
- Adequacy: weight gain **500 g/month (125 g/week)**, regain birth weight by **10 days**, **6 or more wet napkins in 24 hours** with pale urine.
- Complementary foods are given from **6 to 23 months** and should be timely, adequate, safe and properly fed.

## 1. Breast anatomy

- **Stroma:** supportive connective tissue, fat, blood and lymphatic vessels and nerves.
- **Parenchyma:** **10 to 15 ducts** run from the nipple and end in grape-like clusters of **alveoli** (the basic unit). Each duct serves a lobule through ductules; lobules are separated by fat and connective tissue.

## 2. Physiology of lactation

Four stages: **mammogenesis** (growth and development of the gland), **lactogenesis** (synthesis and secretion of milk), **galactokinesis** (milk ejection and removal; the slides call it milk production and secretion), and **galactopoiesis** (maintenance of lactation).

### Mammogenesis

| Stage | Events |
|---|---|
| Birth | Rudiment of 10 to 12 primitive ductal elements |
| Pre-puberty | Slow, steady duct growth, branching and canalisation |
| Puberty | **Oestrogen** drives ducts to grow into the fat pad; **progesterone** causes lobular-alveolar development |
| Pregnancy | 1st trimester: ductular sprouting; 2nd trimester: lobule formation exceeds ductular sprouting |

### Lactogenesis

**Stage 1 (early to mid pregnancy):**

- Lobules enlarge and multiply under **progesterone and oestrogen**.
- The maternal anterior pituitary secretes **prolactin**; the placenta secretes **human chorionic somatomammotropin (placental lactogen)**.
- High placental **progesterone and oestrogen inhibit** further differentiation and milk secretion. Even so, after about 16 weeks small amounts of milk containing lactose and casein may appear. Secretion is triggered by raised prolactin, cortisol and insulin.

**Stage 2 (late pregnancy to day 8 postpartum):**

- After delivery, the **sudden fall in placental progesterone and oestrogen** unmasks the lactogenic effect of prolactin.
- Fully differentiated acini form, **tight junctions between alveolar cells close**, and lumens distend with milk.
- Maternal insulin, growth hormone, cortisol and parathyroid hormone mobilise amino acids, fatty acids, glucose and calcium for milk synthesis.

### Galactokinesis (milk production and ejection)

| Hormone | Source and action |
|---|---|
| **Prolactin** | Made by lactotrophs in the anterior pituitary. **Suckling, via neural pathways, inhibits dopamine**, which raises prolactin. It acts on receptors in lactocytes lining the alveoli, stimulates ductal growth and epithelial cell proliferation, and induces **milk synthesis** |
| **Oxytocin** | Suckling stimulates the nipple-areolar complex; afferent signals reach the **paraventricular nucleus** of the hypothalamus, and oxytocin is released from the **posterior pituitary**. It causes the **myoepithelial cells** around the ducts to contract, ejecting milk (let-down) and emptying the breast quickly |

> **Remember:** prolactin = milk **production**; oxytocin = milk **ejection**. Dopamine inhibits prolactin, so dopamine antagonists can raise it.

### Regulation of milk production

- Lactation needs **regular milk removal and nipple stimulation**.
- Failure to empty the breast distends alveoli and raises intramammary pressure; this impedes capillary flow (less nutrient supply) and disrupts cell connections and attachment to the basement membrane, impairing synthesis and secretion.
- **Feedback inhibitor of lactation (FIL)** is made in the epithelial cells; its concentration rises with milk accumulation and **down-regulates** production.
- Milk volume is usually higher in the morning, and fat content rises through the day.

## 3. Advantages of breastfeeding

**For the infant**

- Always available, at the right temperature, sterile, with adequate nutrients.
- **Fewer infections:** pneumonia, meningitis, otitis media, gastroenteritis; lower risk of obesity and diabetes later.
- **Secretory IgA** and other immunoglobulins prevent organisms adhering to gut mucosa; **passive T-cell immunity**; **lactoferrin** inhibits *E. coli* growth.
- Fewer allergies and feeding difficulties; mother-infant bonding.

**For the mother**

- Reduced postpartum haemorrhage and faster uterine involution (oxytocin effect).
- Temporary reduction in fertility (lactational amenorrhoea).

## 4. Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiative: the ten steps

1. Written breastfeeding policy communicated routinely to all staff.
2. Train all staff in the skills to implement it.
3. Inform all pregnant women about the benefits and management of breastfeeding.
4. Help mothers start breastfeeding within **half an hour of birth**.
5. Show mothers how to breastfeed and maintain lactation even if separated from their infants.
6. Give newborns **no food or drink other than breast milk** unless medically indicated.
7. **Rooming-in**: mother and infant together 24 hours a day.
8. Encourage breastfeeding **on demand**.
9. **No artificial teats or pacifiers** (dummies) for breastfed infants.
10. Foster breastfeeding support groups and refer mothers to them on discharge.

> **Exam tip:** the slides state step 4 as "within half an hour"; the current WHO-UNICEF guidance is "within the first hour". Know both, and use "within 1 hour" in the strategy statement.

## 5. Reflexes and technique

- **Reflexes that aid lactation:** rooting, sucking, swallowing.

| Good positioning | Good attachment |
|---|---|
| Baby's head and body in line | More areola visible above the top lip than below |
| Held close to mother's body | Mouth wide open |
| Whole body supported | Lower lip turned outwards |
| Approaches breast nose to nipple | Chin touches the breast |

Poor attachment causes sore nipples, poor milk transfer and low supply.

## 6. Composition of milk

| Feature | Colostrum | Mature milk |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Deep yellow, serous fluid | Suspension of fat and protein in a carbohydrate-mineral solution |
| Reaction | Alkaline | |
| Content | Higher specific gravity, vitamin A, protein and NaCl; lower carbohydrate, fat and potassium | Protein: one-third casein, two-thirds lactalbumin (alpha-lactalbumin); rich in lactose, oligosaccharides, calcium, phosphate, potassium; **low iron**; all vitamins **except K** |
| Immune factors | Rich in IgA, IgM and IgG; large polynuclear leucocytes; lactalbumin and lactoglobulin; fat globules | Complement, macrophages, lactoferrin, lysozyme, growth and bioactive factors |
| Function | **Laxative** (clears meconium); "baby's first immunisation" | Nutrition and protection |

> **Remember:** vitamin K is low in breast milk, which is why all newborns receive vitamin K at birth.

## 7. Adequacy of milk and improving lactation

**Signs of adequate intake**

- Weight gain **500 g/month or 125 g/week**; birth weight regained by **10 days**.
- **Wetness test:** urinates **6 or more times in 24 hours**; urine is colourless or pale yellow with breast milk alone.

**Solutions for low supply:** frequent feeds (3-hourly), longer feeds, proper positioning and attachment, freedom from worry and stress.

**Tips to improve lactation**

- Good health, early and sufficient treatment of illness, balance of rest and exercise, freedom from worry.
- Care of breasts and nipples in pregnancy; frequent feeding after birth; avoid engorgement.
- Plenty of fluids and adequate nutrition.

## 8. Weaning and complementary feeding

- **Weaning** means accustoming the child to foods other than milk. **Complementary foods** are foods that complement breast milk. They are given from **6 to 23 months**, and breastfeeding may continue beyond 2 years.
- Complementary feeding should be **Timely** (at the recommended age), **Adequate** (enough macro- and micronutrients), **Safe** (hygienically prepared, fed and stored) and **Properly fed** (responsive to hunger signals, with frequency and method suitable for age).
- **AFASS** (for replacement feeding when breastfeeding is not possible, for example in HIV): **A**cceptable, **F**easible, **A**ffordable, **S**afe, **S**ustainable.

### What to give

- **Cereals:** white porridge or overcooked rice.
- **Vegetables:** mashed pumpkin, potato, carrot; greens such as spinach and peas.
- **Proteins:** start about a month after solids begin; red meat first, white meat and egg white towards the end of the first year.
- Family history of **atopy:** introduce fish, eggs and milk later, one at a time.
- Caution with avocado and pawpaw; introduce citrus fruits last.
- Oil and salt in moderation. **Never leave a feeding child alone** (choking).

### Quantity and frequency

| Age | Meals per day | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| 6 to 8 months | 2 to 3 plus frequent breastfeeds | 2 to 3 tablespoons per feed, rising gradually to half a 250 ml cup |
| 9 to 11 months | 3 to 4 plus breastfeeds | Half a 250 ml cup or bowl |
| 12 to 23 months | 3 to 4 plus breastfeeds | Three-quarters to one 250 ml cup or bowl |

- Non-breastfed child: add **1 to 2 cups of milk** and **1 to 2 extra meals** a day.
- For all, 1 to 2 snacks according to appetite.

## Key points to revise

1. Optimal feeding: start within 1 hour, exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, continue to 2 years or more, with complementary foods from 6 months.
2. Prolactin produces milk (suckling inhibits dopamine); oxytocin ejects it (paraventricular nucleus, posterior pituitary, myoepithelial contraction).
3. Falling placental oestrogen and progesterone after delivery trigger full lactation; milk stasis and FIL reduce supply.
4. Colostrum: IgA-rich, laxative, high protein and vitamin A, "first immunisation"; mature milk lacks vitamin K and is low in iron.
5. Breastfeeding protects against infection and allergy, bonds mother and baby, reduces PPH and delays fertility.
6. Ten steps of the Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiative: rooming-in, on-demand feeds, no teats or pacifiers, no other food or drink.
7. Adequacy: 125 g/week, birth weight regained by 10 days, 6 or more wet napkins daily.
8. Complementary food: timely, adequate, safe, properly fed; 2 to 3 meals at 6 to 8 months, 3 to 4 meals from 9 months.
