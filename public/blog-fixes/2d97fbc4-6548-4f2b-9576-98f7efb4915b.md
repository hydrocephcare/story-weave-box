# Interpretation of Abnormal Liver Function Tests

**Year 4 · Internal Medicine · Hepatology**

> **In one line:** "liver function tests" are really three groups of markers: **hepatocellular damage** (AST, ALT), **cholestasis** (ALP, GGT) and **synthetic function** (albumin, INR, bilirubin). The pattern, not any single number, points to the diagnosis; interpret it with the history and examination, and do not rely on algorithms when the clinical clues are clear.

## What you must know

- About **2.5% of the population** have raised LFTs, and **normal LFTs do not exclude liver disease** (for example, cirrhosis can have normal enzymes).
- **ALT** is more liver-specific than **AST**; AST is also found in heart, skeletal muscle, kidney, brain and red cells.
- **ALP** comes from liver and bone (also placenta, kidney, intestine, white cells). Confirm a hepatic source with **GGT**, 5-nucleotidase or ALP isoenzymes.
- **GGT** is very sensitive but non-specific; an isolated rise needs no further work-up.
- **Albumin, INR (PTI) and bilirubin** reflect **synthetic function**; **thrombocytopenia** is a sensitive sign of fibrosis.
- In cholestasis the first test is **liver ultrasound (or CT)** to look for **dilated bile ducts**.
- **Liver biopsy** is the most accurate way to grade liver disease because enzymes correlate poorly with histological activity.

## 1. Principles of investigation

- Interpret LFTs **in clinical context**.
- Take a careful history: **risk factors, drugs (including over-the-counter and herbal), alcohol, comorbidity, autoimmunity**.
- Examine for stigmata of liver disease.
- **Chase the likely diagnosis** rather than following an algorithm, unless there are no clues.
- If abnormalities are mild, there are no risk factors and nothing suggests serious liver disease, **repeat the LFTs after an interval**, with lifestyle modification.

## 2. The three groups of tests

| Group | Tests | Tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Hepatocellular damage** | AST, ALT | Injury to hepatocytes |
| **Cholestasis** | ALP, GGT, (conjugated bilirubin) | Bile flow obstruction or impaired secretion |
| **Synthetic function** | Albumin, INR (prothrombin time index), bilirubin | How well the liver is working |

## 3. Markers of hepatocellular damage

- **AST:** found in liver, heart, skeletal muscle, kidney, brain and red cells. In the liver, about 20% of activity is cytosolic and **80% mitochondrial**. Cleared by sinusoidal cells; **half-life about 17 hours**.
- **ALT:** **more specific to the liver**; very low concentrations in kidney and skeletal muscle. **Half-life about 47 hours**.
- Typical patterns (general knowledge):
  - **AST:ALT above 2:1** suggests alcoholic liver disease (alcohol depletes pyridoxal phosphate and causes mitochondrial injury).
  - ALT greater than AST is typical of viral hepatitis and fatty liver, until cirrhosis develops.
  - Very high levels (over 1000 IU/L) suggest viral or drug-induced hepatitis or ischaemic injury.
  - A raised AST with raised CK points to a muscle source.

## 4. GGT

- Gamma-glutamyl transferase is found in hepatocytes and biliary epithelium, pancreas, renal tubules and intestine.
- **Very sensitive but non-specific**; raised in **any** liver disease, hepatocellular or cholestatic. Usefulness is limited.
- Uses: **confirm a hepatic source for a raised ALP** (GGT raised along with ALP means liver, not bone), and as a marker of **alcohol** intake.
- An **isolated rise does not need further evaluation**: watch and repeat at 3 months, and investigate only if other LFTs become abnormal.
- Induced by alcohol, enzyme-inducing drugs (phenytoin, carbamazepine) and obesity.

## 5. Cholestasis

### ALP

- Sources: **liver and bone** (also placenta, kidneys, intestine, white cells).
- Hepatic ALP is on the surface of bile duct epithelium; accumulating **bile salts increase its release**. Enzyme induction takes time, so ALP may **not be the first enzyme to rise**; **half-life about 1 week**.
- ALP isoenzymes, **5-nucleotidase** or GGT help find the origin.
- **Isolated raised ALP** occurs in the **third trimester of pregnancy** and in **adolescents** (bone growth). Bone is excluded by a raised GGT, 5-NT or isoenzymes.
- A raised hepatic ALP may suggest biliary obstruction, chronic liver disease or a hepatic mass or tumour.

### Approach

1. **Liver ultrasound or CT** is the most important investigation: look for **dilated ducts**.
2. **Dilated ducts** (obstruction): carcinoma of the pancreas, common bile duct stones, cholangiocarcinoma, liver metastases. Next: MRCP or ERCP.
3. **Non-dilated ducts:**

| Cause | Clues |
|---|---|
| **Drugs** (cholestatic jaundice) | Antibiotics, NSAIDs, hormones, ACE inhibitors (also co-amoxiclav, flucloxacillin, anabolic steroids) |
| **Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)** | **Anti-mitochondrial antibody (M2 fraction)**, raised **IgM**; middle-aged women, itching |
| **Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC)** | Associated with **IBD (about 70%)**, **p-ANCA**; diagnose with **MRCP** and liver biopsy |
| Chronic liver disease | Stigmata, low albumin, prolonged INR |
| **Cholangiocarcinoma** | Beware fluctuating levels |
| Primary or metastatic cancer, lymphoma | Infiltration of liver |
| Infiltrative: sarcoid; inflammatory: polymyalgia rheumatica, IBD | Raised ALP without liver disease |

- **Liver biopsy** is often required when the cause remains unclear.

## 6. Bilirubin, albumin and INR

- These are useful indicators of **liver synthetic function**. In primary care, when they accompany other abnormalities, they should raise concern.
- **Thrombocytopenia** is a sensitive indicator of **liver fibrosis** (portal hypertension, hypersplenism).
- **Unconjugated bilirubin** is raised in **haemolysis** and **Gilbert's syndrome**, a benign inherited disease (isolated unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia, other LFTs normal, rises with fasting or illness).
- A low albumin and prolonged INR that does not correct with vitamin K suggest **significant hepatocellular failure**.

> **Remember:** INR is the best bedside marker of synthetic function in acute liver injury because factor VII has a very short half-life; albumin has a long half-life (about 3 weeks) so it falls late.

## 7. Acute hepatitis

- Viral causes: **Hepatitis A, B, C, E, CMV, EBV**.
- **ALT usually peaks before jaundice appears.**
- Jaundice occurs in about **70% of hepatitis A**, **35% of acute hepatitis B** and **25% of hepatitis C**.
- Check exposure history (travel, food, injections, sexual contact, transfusion).
- Tests: **Hepatitis A IgM, Hepatitis B core IgM and HBsAg, Hepatitis C IgG or HCV RNA**.

## 8. Approach to mildly abnormal ALT/AST (2 to 5 times normal, about 100 to 200)

1. History and examination.
2. **Stop hepatotoxic drugs.**
3. **Continue statins**, but monitor LFTs monthly.
4. Lifestyle modification: lose weight, reduce alcohol, control diabetes (non-alcoholic fatty liver disease is the commonest cause).
5. **Repeat LFTs at 1 month and 6 months.**

### If still abnormal at 6 months

Consider referral to secondary care and further tests:

| Test | Looks for |
|---|---|
| Hepatitis B and C serology | Chronic viral hepatitis |
| Iron studies: **transferrin saturation, ferritin** | Haemochromatosis |
| **Autoantibodies and immunoglobulins** | Autoimmune hepatitis (ANA, SMA, raised IgG), PBC |
| **Caeruloplasmin** | Wilson's disease (young patients) |
| **Alpha-1 antitrypsin** | Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency |
| **Coeliac serology** | Coeliac disease |
| TFTs, lipids, glucose | Thyroid disease, metabolic syndrome |
| **Liver biopsy** | Consider especially if ALT above 100 |

## 9. Value of liver biopsy

- Most accurate way to **grade the severity** of liver disease.
- **Aminotransferase levels correlate poorly** with histological activity.
- Narrows the diagnostic options even when not diagnostic.
- Contraindications: coagulopathy, ascites, uncooperative patient (use transjugular route when needed).

## Key points to revise

1. 2.5% of people have raised LFTs; normal LFTs do not exclude liver disease.
2. ALT is liver-specific; AST also comes from muscle, heart and others; AST:ALT over 2 suggests alcohol.
3. GGT is sensitive but non-specific; use it to confirm hepatic ALP and watch isolated rises.
4. Cholestasis: ultrasound first. Dilated ducts mean obstruction; non-dilated ducts mean drugs, PBC (AMA, IgM), PSC (p-ANCA, MRCP) and infiltration.
5. Albumin, INR and bilirubin show synthetic function; low platelets suggest fibrosis; unconjugated bilirubin rises in haemolysis and Gilbert's.
6. In acute viral hepatitis ALT peaks before jaundice; know the serology for A, B and C.
7. Mild ALT rise: stop hepatotoxins, lifestyle changes, repeat at 1 and 6 months, then work-up for viral, iron, autoimmune, Wilson, alpha-1 antitrypsin, coeliac.
8. Biopsy grades disease best because enzymes correlate poorly with histology.
