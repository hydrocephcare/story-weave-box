# Disorders of Development: Delay, Intellectual Disability, Cerebral Palsy, Autism and ADHD

**Year 4 · Paediatrics · Developmental and neurodevelopmental paediatrics**

> **In one line:** a developmental disorder is a failure to reach milestones at the expected age or a pattern of development that differs from the norm. Most causes arise before or around birth, the young brain responds best to early stimulation and therapy, so recognise red flags early, test hearing and vision first, and start intervention without waiting for a cause.

## What you must know

- **Global developmental delay (GDD):** more than 2 SD below the mean in **two or more domains**, in a child **under 5 years**.
- **Regression** (loss of acquired skills at any age) is always serious and needs urgent assessment.
- **Intellectual disability (ID):** IQ below 70 plus limited adaptive function, onset before 18 years.
- **Cerebral palsy (CP):** permanent, **non-progressive** disorder of movement and posture from an insult to the developing brain; **spastic** is commonest (75 to 80%).
- **Autism spectrum disorder (ASD):** deficits in social communication and interaction plus restricted, repetitive behaviours; diagnosed clinically; early behavioural intervention is the treatment that works.
- **ADHD:** 6 or more symptoms, onset before 12 years, in two or more settings.
- In Kenya, **birth asphyxia, neonatal jaundice, prematurity and CNS infections** cause much avoidable disability.

## 1. Terms you must use correctly

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Developmental delay** | Milestones reached later than expected in one or more domains |
| **Global developmental delay** | Significant delay (more than 2 SD below the mean) in **two or more domains**, in a child **under 5 years** |
| **Developmental regression** | **Loss** of skills already acquired; always serious |
| **Developmental disorder (deviance)** | Skills develop in an abnormal sequence or pattern (for example autism) |
| **Intellectual disability** | Deficits in intellectual function **and** adaptive behaviour, beginning in the developmental period; diagnosed when old enough for reliable IQ testing (usually after 5 years) |

## 2. Red flags: when to worry

- No **social smile** by 8 weeks; no **head control** by 4 months; not **sitting** by 9 months; not **walking** by 18 months (earlier in boys with possible muscle disease).
- No **babbling** by 9 to 12 months; no **words** by 16 months; no **two-word phrases** by 2 years.
- No pointing or gestures by 12 months; poor eye contact; no response to name.
- **Persistent primitive reflexes** or asymmetry; hand preference before 12 months.
- **Any loss of skills at any age.**
- Parental concern, which is a strong predictor of a real problem.

## 3. Causes of developmental disorders

| Period | Causes |
|---|---|
| **Prenatal** (largest group) | Genetic: chromosomal (**Down syndrome**), **fragile X**, single-gene disorders, neural tube and brain malformations. Congenital infections (TORCH, congenital HIV, Zika). **Congenital hypothyroidism**, **iodine deficiency**. Maternal: **fetal alcohol syndrome**, drugs, poor nutrition, diabetes |
| **Perinatal** | **Birth asphyxia (hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy)**, prematurity and intraventricular haemorrhage, **neonatal jaundice with kernicterus**, neonatal sepsis and meningitis, neonatal hypoglycaemia |
| **Postnatal** | **Meningitis and encephalitis**, **cerebral malaria**, head injury, prolonged seizures, severe malnutrition, **lead** poisoning, iron deficiency, neglect and lack of stimulation, HIV encephalopathy |

> **Remember:** prevention is the most effective intervention in Kenya: good antenatal and delivery care, newborn resuscitation, phototherapy for jaundice, and vaccination.

## 4. Approach to a child with developmental delay

### History
- Pregnancy, birth and neonatal events; feeding and growth.
- The **exact age of each milestone** and whether any skill has been lost.
- Seizures, sleep, behaviour, hearing and vision.
- Family history of delay, consanguinity, early deaths; social circumstances and stimulation.

### Examination
- **Growth and head circumference** (microcephaly or macrocephaly).
- **Dysmorphic features** (Down syndrome, fetal alcohol syndrome, fragile X), skin stigmata (café-au-lait spots, ash-leaf macules: neurocutaneous syndromes), hepatosplenomegaly (storage disorders), thyroid status.
- **Neurological examination:** tone, power, reflexes, primitive reflexes, posture, gait, cranial nerves, eye movements, fundi.
- **Vision and hearing:** formal testing for every child with delay.
- **Developmental assessment** across all domains.

### Investigations (stepwise)
1. **Hearing and vision tests** first.
2. **Thyroid function tests** (congenital hypothyroidism is treatable), full blood count and ferritin, lead level where exposure is possible, HIV test.
3. **Genetic tests:** karyotype or chromosomal microarray; **fragile X** testing in boys with unexplained ID.
4. **Neuroimaging (MRI)** and **EEG** if seizures, focal signs, microcephaly or regression.
5. **Metabolic screen** (ammonia, lactate, amino acids, organic acids) when there is regression, episodic illness, coarse features or organomegaly.

> **Remember:** a precise cause is found in only about half of children. The work-up must **not delay early intervention**.

## 5. Intellectual disability

**IQ below 70** with limited adaptive functioning, starting before 18 years.

| Severity | IQ | Typical outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | 50 to 69 | Learns academic skills slowly; independent living with support; about 85% of cases |
| **Moderate** | 35 to 49 | Basic self-care and simple work with supervision |
| **Severe** | 20 to 34 | Needs support with daily life; limited speech |
| **Profound** | Below 20 | Needs constant care |

- **Associations:** epilepsy, cerebral palsy, hearing and vision problems, behaviour problems, autism and ADHD.
- **Management** is multidisciplinary: early stimulation, special education, speech and occupational therapy, behaviour support, treatment of epilepsy and other health problems, family counselling and community support.

## 6. Cerebral palsy

A group of permanent but **non-progressive** disorders of **movement and posture** from an insult to the **developing (fetal or infant) brain**. The lesion does not progress, but the clinical picture changes with time.

![Cerebral palsy types: the brain regions affected in each type · Ceimile95, CC BY-SA 4.0, Wikimedia Commons](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0f/Cerebral_Palsy_Types.jpg)

### Causes and risk factors
- **Prenatal:** brain malformations, congenital infections, maternal illness, twins.
- **Perinatal:** **prematurity** (periventricular leukomalacia), **hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy**, severe neonatal jaundice (kernicterus gives dyskinetic CP), neonatal infection, hypoglycaemia.
- **Postnatal:** meningitis, cerebral malaria, head injury, stroke.

### Types

| Type | Features | Typical cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Spastic** (75 to 80%) | Increased tone, brisk reflexes, upgoing plantars, contractures. Subtypes: **diplegia** (legs more than arms), **hemiplegia** (one side), **quadriplegia** (all four limbs, worst) | Diplegia: prematurity. Quadriplegia: severe HIE |
| **Dyskinetic** (athetoid or dystonic) | Involuntary movements, fluctuating tone | Kernicterus, severe asphyxia |
| **Ataxic** | Poor balance and coordination, intention tremor | Cerebellar malformations, genetic |
| **Mixed** | Combination | |

- Severity is graded by the **Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS) levels I to V** (I: walks without limits; V: needs a wheelchair and full care).

### Presentation
Delayed motor milestones, abnormal tone (floppy early, stiff later), persistent primitive reflexes, early hand preference, feeding difficulty, toe-walking or scissoring of the legs.

### Associated problems
- Intellectual disability (about half); **epilepsy** (about one third).
- Visual and hearing impairment.
- **Feeding and swallowing problems with poor growth**, drooling, gastro-oesophageal reflux, constipation.
- **Hip subluxation, scoliosis and contractures**.
- Speech and behavioural problems.

### Management (a team effort)
- **Physiotherapy and occupational therapy**; positioning, splints and orthoses; walking aids and wheelchairs.
- **Spasticity:** stretching, baclofen, botulinum toxin injections, orthopaedic surgery for fixed deformity.
- **Seizures:** antiepileptic drugs.
- **Nutrition:** feeding strategies, thickened feeds and, if needed, gastrostomy.
- **Speech and communication therapy**, hearing and vision care, special education.
- **Family support**, caregiver training, social services; annual **hip surveillance** by X-ray.

> **Exam tip:** the brain lesion in CP is static. If a child is losing skills, it is not CP: look for a progressive neurological, metabolic or neurodegenerative cause.

## 7. Autism spectrum disorder

**Definition:** persistent deficits in **social communication and social interaction**, with **restricted, repetitive patterns** of behaviour, interests or activities, present from early childhood.

### Early signs
- Poor eye contact; **no response to name** by 12 months.
- No **babbling, pointing or gestures** by 12 months; **no words by 16 months**; **no spontaneous two-word phrases by 24 months**.
- **Loss of language or social skills** at any age.
- No **pretend play**; lining up toys; hand-flapping or other stereotypies; insistence on sameness; unusual sensory responses (sound, touch, texture).

### Diagnosis and management
- Clinical: developmental history, observation and standard tools (for example **M-CHAT-R/F** screening at 18 and 24 months).
- **Always test hearing.** Look for associated conditions: ID, epilepsy, ADHD, anxiety, sleep problems, and genetic causes such as fragile X and Rett syndrome.
- **No cure.** Early **intensive behavioural and developmental intervention**, **speech and language therapy**, **parent training**, structured special education, and treatment of co-existing problems.
- Drugs (risperidone, aripiprazole) only for severe irritability or aggression, not core symptoms.

## 8. Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)

A persistent pattern of **inattention** and/or **hyperactivity and impulsivity**, more frequent and severe than in children of the same age.

- **Six or more symptoms** in either group, starting **before 12 years**, in **two or more settings** (home, school), with clear impairment.
- School-age presentation: poor concentration, forgetfulness, disorganisation, fidgeting, excessive talking, interrupting, unsafe behaviour.
- Exclude first: **hearing and vision problems, chronic illness, seizures (absence), anxiety, depression, poor sleep, lead poisoning**, stressful home.
- **Management:** parent training and behaviour therapy, classroom support, structured routine. **Stimulants (methylphenidate)** or **atomoxetine** for moderate to severe symptoms, monitoring growth, appetite, sleep and blood pressure.

## 9. Other developmental problems

- **Specific learning disorders** (dyslexia): difficulty with reading, writing or arithmetic despite normal intelligence and teaching.
- **Speech and language disorders:** isolated delayed language needs a hearing test; may signal autism or ID.
- **Developmental coordination disorder** (clumsy child).
- **Hearing impairment:** a leading, treatable cause of language delay; screen newborns and any child with a language problem.
- **Duchenne muscular dystrophy:** boys with delayed walking, calf hypertrophy, **Gowers sign**, very high creatine kinase.
- **Genetic syndromes:**
  - **Down syndrome** (trisomy 21): hypotonia, upslanting palpebral fissures, single palmar crease, mild to moderate ID; heart defects, hypothyroidism, hearing loss, increased leukaemia risk.
  - **Fragile X:** long face, large ears, macro-orchidism, autism.
  - **Rett syndrome:** girls, normal at first, then regression with loss of hand use and hand-wringing.
- **Developmental regression** needs urgent assessment for neurodegenerative and metabolic disease, HIV encephalopathy, epileptic encephalopathy, and treatable causes such as hypothyroidism and subacute infection.

## Key points to revise

1. GDD means delay in two or more domains in a child under 5; regression is always a red flag.
2. Prenatal causes dominate, but asphyxia, jaundice and CNS infections are major preventable causes in Kenya.
3. Test hearing and vision first, then thyroid function; genetic and imaging tests are targeted.
4. Cerebral palsy is non-progressive and affects movement and posture; spastic is commonest; manage as a team and look for epilepsy, feeding problems and hip disease.
5. ID is IQ below 70 with impaired adaptive function; mild (50 to 69) is the commonest.
6. Autism is diagnosed clinically; early behavioural intervention is the effective treatment.
7. ADHD needs 6 or more symptoms in two or more settings before 12 years, after excluding other causes.
