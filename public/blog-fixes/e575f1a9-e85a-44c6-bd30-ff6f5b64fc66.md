## General Examination — Revision Notes
## The Setting
- Introduce yourself as a "student doctor," explain what you will do, and obtain consent before proceeding
- Warm, private, quiet room; chaperone required when a male examines a female (especially for breast, rectal, and vaginal examination)
- Examine from the right side of the bed; patient semi-recumbent at 45 degrees
- Ask about pain before touching; look at the patient's face throughout for signs of discomfort
- First assess: how ill is the patient? — well, mildly ill, or severely ill. In a severely ill patient, manage the acute emergency first

## 1. Posture and Gait
Start observing from the moment the patient enters the room.
| Gait / Posture | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Hemiplegic gait | Stroke |
| Parkinsonian gait (shuffling, stooped) | Parkinson's disease |
| Antalgic gait (limp) | Painful joint |
| High-stepping gait | Sensory ataxia |
| Veers toward one side | Cerebellar lesion — veers toward the side of the lesion |
| Difficulty rising from chair | Proximal muscle weakness |
| Propped up in bed | Left heart failure (orthopnoea) |
| Lies supine, still, legs drawn up | Peritonitis |
| Restless, rolls around | Renal colic |
| Neck bent backwards | Meningism |

## 2. Speech and Interaction
- Does the patient smile symmetrically? Asymmetry = facial weakness
- Eye contact, facial animation, emotional expression
- **Expressionless face** — Parkinson's disease or severe depression
- **Hoarse voice** — laryngeal disease, recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy, myxoedema
- **Pressured speech** — thyrotoxicosis or mania
- **Monotonous speech** — severe depression
- **Slurred speech** — cerebellar disease or previous stroke

## 3. Physique and Nutrition
- Note if patient is cachectic, slim, plump, or obese
- Indicators of poor nutrition: temporalis muscle wasting, cracked skin, hair loss, poor wound healing
- **Atrophic glossitis** — smooth tongue with loss of papillae; vitamin B12 deficiency
- **Angular stomatitis** — cracking at the angles of the mouth; vitamin B or iron deficiency
- **Pellagra** — photosensitive dermatitis; niacin (B3) deficiency

## 4. Temperature
- Diurnal variation: lowest in the morning, highest between 6–10 PM
- Ovulation causes a 0.5°C rise in women
- Normal ranges:
  - Oral: 35.8–37°C
  - Axillary: 35.3–36.5°C
  - Rectal: 36.3–37.5°C (highest — closest to core)

## 5. Hands
**Handshake**
Weak grip → neurological or musculoskeletal disorder
**Tremor**
| Type | Cause |
|---|---|
| Fine tremor | Thyrotoxicosis, recent beta-agonist use |
| Pill-rolling tremor | Parkinsonism |
| Jerky (asterixis/flap) | Hepatic or uraemic encephalopathy |
| Intention tremor | Cerebellar disease |
**Dupuytren's contracture** — thickening of tissue over the flexor tendon of the ring finger at the distal palmar crease → progresses to flexion contracture of MCP and PIP joints. Risk factors: family history, alcoholism, diabetes, liver disease.
**Clubbing** — nails convex both longitudinally and transversely; Lovibond's angle increased.
Causes: cyanotic heart disease, bronchiectasis, empyema, bronchial carcinoma, fibrosing alveolitis, IBD, infective endocarditis.
**Nail signs:**
| Sign | Appearance | Association |
|---|---|---|
| Osler's nodes | Transient, tender swellings | Infective endocarditis (septic emboli) |
| Splinter haemorrhages | Tiny subungual blood streaks | Trauma or vasculitis |
| Koilonychia | Spoon-shaped concave nails | Severe iron deficiency |
| Leuconychia | Opaque white nails | Chronic liver disease, hypoalbuminaemia |
| Beau's lines | Horizontal nail depressions | Systemic illness, chemotherapy, malnutrition |

## 6. Odour
- Alcohol
- Organophosphate (pesticide poisoning)
- Halitosis — suppurative lung disease, gingivitis

## 7. Face and Neck
- **3rd nerve palsy** — ptosis, eye deviated down and out, dilated pupil
- **7th nerve palsy** — unilateral facial weakness (upper vs lower motor neuron pattern)
- **Parotid swelling** — mumps, alcoholism, acute parotitis, tumour
- **Malar flush** — mitral stenosis
- **Malar rash (butterfly rash)** — SLE
- **JVP** — assess for elevated venous pressure
- **Neck swellings** — thyroid, thyroglossal cysts, enlarged lymph nodes

## 8. Lymph Nodes
- Use pulps of index and middle fingers to palpate
- Assess: size, shape, mobility, position, consistency, tenderness, coalescence
- Tender/painful nodes → infection
- Non-tender, firm nodes → malignancy (until proven otherwise)
Drainage regions:
- Upper limb and breast → axillary nodes
- Lower limb → inguinal nodes (superficial vertical and horizontal groups)
- Head and neck → cervical nodes

## 9. Axillae
- **Axillary adenopathy** — infection, lymphoma, breast cancer spread
- **Absence of axillary hair** — chronic liver disease
- **Acanthosis nigricans** — dark velvety skin in axillae; associated with insulin resistance and occasionally gastric cancer

## 10. Skin
- **Temperature** — hot in thyrotoxicosis, febrile illness; cold and clammy in shock
- **Hydration** — assess skin turgor (pinch skin)
- **Pallor** — check palms, conjunctiva, tongue
- **Jaundice** — check sclera, posterior tongue, skin
- **Cyanosis** — bluish discolouration; check hands (peripheral) and tongue (central)

## 11. Pulses
Palpate: radial, brachial, carotid, femoral, popliteal, posterior tibial, dorsalis pedis. Always compare both sides.

## 12. Blood Pressure
- Normal: <140/90 mmHg
- Hypotension: systolic <90 and/or diastolic <50
- **Orthostatic hypotension** — drop of >20/10 mmHg within 3 minutes of standing from supine. Causes: hypovolaemia, autonomic neuropathy

## 13. Legs and Feet
- Expose groin to toes
- **Peripheral vascular disease** — shiny skin, absent/reduced hair, pallor on elevation
- **Oedema** — check behind malleoli; in bedridden patients check the sacrum
- **Varicose veins** — examine with patient standing
- **Asymmetrical leg swelling** — suspect DVT

## 14. Breast Examination
- Always with a chaperone; patient disrobed to waist
- Inspect in three positions: seated arms at sides → seated arms raised → supine
- Inspect for: asymmetry, dimpling, contour changes, nipple inversion, skin colour
- Palpate using pads of middle three fingers in rotatory movements, pressing against chest wall
- Examine all four quadrants + axillary tail + areola

## Respiratory History and Physical Examination — Revision Notes
## Respiratory Symptoms
1. Breathlessness (dyspnoea)
2. Cough
3. Sputum production
4. Wheezing
5. Chest pain

## 1. Breathlessness (Dyspnoea)
Breathlessness inappropriate to the level of exertion, or occurring at rest.
**MRC Dyspnoea Scale:**
| Grade | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | Breathless only with strenuous exercise |
| 2 | Breathless when hurrying on level ground or walking up a slight hill |
| 3 | Walks slower than contemporaries on level ground; stops after ~1 mile |
| 4 | Stops for breath after ~100 metres or after a few minutes on level ground |
| 5 | Too breathless to leave the house; breathless when dressing/undressing |
Ask about: duration (acute/subacute/chronic), diurnal variation, triggers, and associated symptoms.

## 2. Cough
- **Acute:** <3 weeks | **Chronic:** >8 weeks
- Dry vs productive
- Haemoptysis (blood-stained sputum)
- Diurnal variation — nocturnal cough suggests asthma
- Triggers: dust, pollen, animal fur
- Post-tussive vomiting (cough so severe it induces vomiting)

## 3. Sputum
- Note colour (clear, white, yellow, green, blood-stained)
- Asthma — small amounts of thick, jelly-like sputum; may be cast-shaped (airway mould)
- **Bronchiectasis** — copious sputum >1 cup (>250 mL) in 24 hours

## 4. Haemoptysis
Coughing up blood. Distinguish from:
- Haematemesis (vomiting blood — upper GI source)
- Epistaxis (nosebleed — tracked down to throat)
- Melaena (black stool — not haemoptysis but associated upper GI bleed)

## 5. Wheezing
- Ask if patient hears noises from the chest
- **Nocturnal wheeze** — typical of asthma; may be noticed by a partner before the patient is aware

## 6. Chest Pain
| Type | Character | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Pleuritic | Sharp, stabbing; worse on deep breathing/coughing | Pleural inflammation from underlying lung infection |
| Constant, non-positional | Unrelated to breathing | Chest wall invasion by lung or pleural tumour |
| Pneumothorax | Worse on breathing, more aching than stabbing | Spontaneous pneumothorax |
| Pulmonary embolism | Pleuritic, dull, or cardiac-type | Depends on extent; massive PE can mimic MI |

## Social and Occupational History
**Smoking history** — age started, age stopped (if ex-smoker), cigarettes/packets per day. Discuss cessation after completing the full assessment, not at the start.
**Recreational drugs** — cocaine, marijuana, heroin, methamphetamines; note route (inhaled, IV, oral) and frequency.
**Occupational history** — take chronologically from leaving school to present. Occupational lung diseases include: occupational asthma, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, silicosis, asbestosis.
**Family history** — asthma, atopy (allergic rhinitis, eczema, allergic conjunctivitis).

## General Inspection — Respiratory Patient
Look at the patient and surroundings:
- Inhalers, nebulisers, sputum pot, peak flow meter, oxygen mask, CPAP machine at bedside
- Is oxygen being used? What rate and delivery device?
- Audible wheeze or stridor?
- Hoarse voice?
- Continually coughing? Dry or productive?
- Can the patient produce a normal explosive cough or is it weak?
- Physique and nutrition — cachexia suggests malignancy or chronic disease
- **Pursed lip breathing** — characteristic of COPD (prolongs expiration to prevent small airway collapse)

## Hands — Respiratory Signs
| Sign | Association |
|---|---|
| Clubbing | Bronchiectasis, lung carcinoma, fibrosing alveolitis, empyema |
| Pallor | Anaemia |
| Warm palms, bounding pulse | CO2 retention |
| Central/peripheral cyanosis | Hypoxia |
| Asterixis (flap) | CO2 retention (type 2 respiratory failure) |
| Tobacco staining | Smoking history |
| Thin skin, bruising | Prolonged steroid use |

## Venous Pulses / JVP
- **Cor pulmonale** — elevated JVP + ankle oedema (right heart failure secondary to lung disease)
- **Superior vena cava (SVC) obstruction** — facial puffiness, engorged neck veins, collateral veins on anterior chest wall

## Head and Face
- **Anaemia** — pallor of conjunctiva
- **Central cyanosis** — blue tongue/lips
- **Horner's syndrome** (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis) — Pancoast tumour of the lung apex
- **Oral candidiasis** — inhaled steroid use, immunosuppression, debilitation

## Observing the Chest
- Normal respiratory rate: 14–16 breaths/minute at rest
- Assess: rate, rhythm, expansion, symmetry, surgical scars
**Chest shapes:**
| Shape | Description | Association |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | Bilaterally symmetrical; AP diameter < transverse | — |
| Barrel chest | AP:transverse ratio = 1:1 | COPD (air trapping) |
| Flat chest | AP:transverse ratio = 1:2 | Bilateral lung fibrosis |
| Kyphosis | Forward bending of spine | — |
| Scoliosis | Lateral bending of spine | — |
| Pectus excavatum (funnel chest) | Hollowing over lower sternum | — |
| Pectus carinatum (pigeon chest) | Forward protrusion of sternum | — |

## Chest Palpation
- **Surgical emphysema** — air in subcutaneous tissue; feels like popcorn or bubble wrap; caused by trauma, pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum, chest instrumentation
- **Tenderness** — trauma, costochondritis, rib metastases, herpes zoster (before rash appears), intercostal muscle pain
- **Tracheal position** — use 2nd and 4th fingers on sternal notch edges; 3rd finger assesses whether trachea is central or deviated
Tracheal/mediastinal shift:
- Pushed away from lesion — massive pleural effusion or tension pneumothorax
- Pulled toward lesion — lung fibrosis or collapse
- **Chest expansion** — thumbs meet in midline without touching skin; deep breath separates thumbs; if one thumb stays closer to midline → reduced expansion on that side
**Surface anatomy landmarks:**
- Tracheal bifurcation (carina) — sternal angle anteriorly; T4/5 disc posteriorly
- Oblique/major fissure — line from T2 spinous process to 6th rib (nipple level)
- Horizontal/minor fissure (right side only) — horizontal line at 4th costal cartilage level meeting the oblique fissure

## Percussion
Technique: middle finger of left hand pressed firmly on chest wall (DIP joint slightly hyperextended); strike with tip of right middle finger.
| Note | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resonant | Normal |
| Dull | Consolidation, collapse, pleural thickening, pleural effusion |
| Stony dull | Pleural effusion |
| Hyperresonant | Pneumothorax |

## Auscultation
Use the diaphragm of the stethoscope. Ask patient to breathe in and out through the mouth. Compare left and right at each level.
**Breath sounds:**
- **Vesicular** — normal; no pause between inspiration and expiration
- **Bronchial** — harsh, high-frequency; distinct pause; expiratory phase longer and hissing. Heard over consolidated lung (e.g. pneumonia)
- **Reduced/absent** — pleural effusion, pneumothorax, severe obstruction
**Added sounds:**
| Sound | Character | Causes |
|---|---|---|
| Polyphonic wheeze | Musical, widespread, mainly expiratory | Asthma, COPD (diffuse airflow obstruction) |
| Monophonic wheeze | Fixed, single pitch | Localised bronchial narrowing — tumour, foreign body |
| Stridor | Loud, inspiratory and expiratory; heard without stethoscope | Large airway, tracheal, or laryngeal disease |
| Early inspiratory crackles | Short, explosive | COPD |
| Coarse localised crackles | Loud, bubbling | Bronchiectasis |
| Widespread crackles | Bilateral | Pulmonary oedema |
| Fine late inspiratory crackles | Fine, end of inspiration | Diffuse interstitial fibrosis |
| Pleural rub | Grating, creaking, sandpaper-like | Pleural inflammation — inflamed layers rubbing together |

## Vocal Resonance, Fremitus, and Egophony
**Vocal resonance** — auscultate chest while patient says "ninety-nine." Consolidated lung conducts sound better → louder and clearer sounds.
**Whispering pectoriloquy** — patient whispers "one-two-three"; normally faint. Clear and distinct whispers over consolidated lung.
**Tactile vocal fremitus** — place flat hand or ulnar border on chest; patient says "ninety-nine"; feel for vibrations.
**Egophony** — patient says "E" and it is heard as "A" through the stethoscope; sign of consolidation (pneumonia) or pleural effusion. In effusion, heard just above the fluid level.

## Interpreting the Signs Together
| Condition | Movement | Percussion | Breath sounds | VR / TVF | Trachea |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidation | Reduced (affected side) | Dull | Bronchial | Increased | Central |
| Pleural effusion | Reduced (affected side) | Stony dull | Reduced/absent | Reduced | Pushed away (if large) |
| Pneumothorax | Reduced (affected side) | Hyperresonant | Reduced/absent | Reduced | Pushed away (if tension) |
| Lung collapse/fibrosis | Reduced (affected side) | Dull | Reduced | Reduced | Pulled toward lesion |

## Full Chest Examination — Sequence
**With patient at 45°:**
1. Observe surroundings and patient generally
2. Examine hands — clubbing, cyanosis, flap, tobacco staining; take pulse
3. Count respiratory rate
4. Assess JVP
5. Check face — anaemia, cyanosis, Horner's, oral candidiasis
6. Inspect anterior chest — shape, scars, movement
7. Palpate — trachea, apex beat, chest expansion, axillary nodes
8. Percuss anterior chest and axillae
9. Sit patient forward — inspect posterior chest, cervical/supraclavicular nodes, percuss back
10. Auscultate posteriorly — breath sounds, vocal resonance, TVF; check for sacral oedema
11. Auscultate anteriorly — breath sounds, vocal resonance, TVF; check ankle oedema
12. Thank patient; ensure they are covered

## Investigations
Sputum examination, CXR, lung ultrasound, pleural biopsy, bronchoscopy and endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS), PET scan, lung biopsy, lung function tests (spirometry), six-minute walk test.
