## TOPIC 7: WHITE BLOOD CELLS — FUNCTION & DISORDERS

**Definition:**
- Leucocytes — nucleated blood cells of the immune system
- *Leukos* (Greek) = white | *kytos* = cell

**Classification by Function:** (★)

| Cell | % of WBC | Primary Role |
|---|---|---|
| Neutrophils | 60–70% | Bacterial/fungal phagocytosis — first responders |
| **Eosinophils** | 1–4% | **Parasitic infections** (★), allergic/IgE reactions |
| Basophils | <1% | Allergic reactions, release histamine and heparin |
| Monocytes | 2–8% | Phagocytosis; differentiate into tissue macrophages |
| Lymphocytes | 20–40% | Adaptive immunity (T-cells, B-cells, NK cells) |

- Eosinophils release **major basic protein (MBP)** and eosinophil cationic protein, recruited by **IL-5**, to kill helminths and parasites (★)

**Key Terminology:** (★)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Leucocytosis | ↑ WBC count |
| Leucopenia | ↓ WBC count |
| Neutrophilia | ↑ neutrophils (bacterial infection, stress) |
| **Neutropenia** | ↓ neutrophils, ANC <1.5 × 10⁹/L |
| Lymphocytosis | ↑ lymphocytes (viral infection, CLL) |
| Lymphopenia | ↓ lymphocytes (HIV, steroids) |
| Eosinophilia | ↑ eosinophils (parasites, allergy, drugs) |
| Erythrocytosis | ↑ RBCs |
| Thrombocytopenia | ↓ platelets |
| Thrombocytosis | ↑ platelets |

**Neutropenia:**
- Mild: 1.0–1.5 ×10⁹/L | Moderate: 0.5–1.0 ×10⁹/L | **Severe: <0.5 ×10⁹/L** — high infection risk (★)
- Most common cause in clinical practice: **chemotherapy** (★)
- Other causes: aplastic anaemia, viral infections, drugs (clozapine, carbimazole), autoimmune, severe sepsis
- Management: G-CSF, isolate from infection sources, prompt antibiotics if febrile (febrile neutropenia = medical emergency)

## TOPIC 8: ACUTE LEUKAEMIA (AML & ALL)

**Etymology:** *Leukos* = white | *haima* = blood

**Definition:**
- Malignant clonal proliferation of immature haematopoietic blasts
- WHO criterion: **≥20% blasts in bone marrow or peripheral blood** confirms acute leukaemia (★)

**Acute vs Chronic:**
- Acute: blasts dominate, rapid onset, pancytopenia + circulating blasts
- Chronic: mature cells dominate, indolent course

**ACUTE MYELOID LEUKAEMIA (AML)**

**Definition:** Clonal proliferation of myeloid blasts (myeloblasts, monoblasts, etc.)

**Clinical Presentation:** (★)
- Anaemia → fatigue, pallor
- Neutropenia (despite high WCC — non-functional blasts) → fever, infections
- Thrombocytopenia → bruising, bleeding, petechiae
- Gum hypertrophy, skin infiltration (especially monocytic subtypes)

**Diagnosis:** (★)
- Gold standard: **bone marrow aspirate + trephine biopsy** with cytogenetics and immunophenotyping
- **Auer rods** — pathognomonic pink needle-like cytoplasmic inclusions in myeloblasts (★)
- Flow cytometry for immunophenotyping
- Peripheral smear alone is insufficient for definitive diagnosis

**Cytochemical Stains — Confirming Lineage:** (★)

| Stain | Lineage |
|---|---|
| **Myeloperoxidase (MPO)** | Myeloid — most specific (★) |
| Sudan Black B | Myeloid (less specific than MPO) |
| Non-specific esterase (NSE) | Monocytic (M4/M5 subtypes) |
| **Periodic acid-Schiff (PAS)** | Lymphoid (ALL) and erythroid |
| **TdT (Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase)** | Lymphoblasts — confirms ALL (★) |
| TRAP | Hairy cell leukaemia |

**FAB Classification of AML:** (★)

| Subtype | Name | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| M0 | Undifferentiated | — |
| M1 | Without maturation | — |
| M2 | With maturation | t(8;21) |
| **M3** | **Acute promyelocytic leukaemia (APL)** | t(15;17) — PML-RARA fusion; **DIC risk**; treated with ATRA (★) |
| M4 | Myelomonocytic | inv(16) |
| **M5** | **Monocytic** | **Gingival hypertrophy and skin infiltration** (★) |
| M6 | Erythroleukaemia | — |
| M7 | Megakaryoblastic | — |

**ACUTE LYMPHOBLASTIC LEUKAEMIA (ALL)**

**Definition:** Clonal proliferation of lymphoid blasts (B- or T-lineage); most common childhood leukaemia

**Diagnosis:**
- TdT positive — confirms lymphoblast origin (★)
- PAS positive
- Cytogenetics/molecular: best sample = **bone marrow aspirate** (preferred over peripheral blood for cytogenetic/molecular analysis) (★)

**Immunophenotyping markers:** (★)

| Marker | Lineage |
|---|---|
| CD19, CD10, CD20 | B-cell ALL |
| **CD3** | **T-cell ALL** (★) |
| CD33, CD13 | Myeloid (AML) |

## TOPIC 9: CHRONIC LEUKAEMIAS

**CHRONIC MYELOID LEUKAEMIA (CML)**

**Definition:** Clonal myeloproliferative disorder of granulocyte precursors

**Pathognomonic finding:** (★)
- **t(9;22)(q34;q11)** — the **Philadelphia chromosome**
- Forms **BCR-ABL fusion gene** → constitutive tyrosine kinase activity → uncontrolled proliferation

**Clinical Presentation:** (★)
- Fatigue, weight loss, splenomegaly (massive)
- Very high WCC (often >100 ×10⁹/L)
- **Neutrophilia with left shift and basophilia** — classic triad (★)

**Investigations to confirm diagnosis:** (★)
- FBC + PBF: leucocytosis with full spectrum of granulocyte maturation, basophilia
- **Bone marrow aspirate + trephine biopsy with cytogenetic analysis**
- **Quantitative RT-PCR for BCR-ABL transcripts** — used for diagnosis confirmation AND monitoring treatment response (★)
- FISH — detects Philadelphia chromosome in interphase cells (doesn't require dividing cells, faster than conventional cytogenetics) (★)
- Conventional cytogenetics (karyotyping) — detects t(9;22) directly but requires metaphase cells

**Treatment:**
- **Tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs)**: imatinib (first-line), dasatinib, nilotinib — target BCR-ABL (★)

**Distinguishing CML from Polycythaemia Vera:** (★)
- CML: BCR-ABL fusion transcript positive, JAK2 negative
- PV: **JAK2 V617F mutation** positive (>95% of cases), BCR-ABL negative

**CHRONIC LYMPHOCYTIC LEUKAEMIA (CLL)**

**Definition:** Clonal proliferation of mature but dysfunctional B-lymphocytes; most common leukaemia in adults in the West

**Key Complication — Warm Autoimmune Haemolytic Anaemia (AIHA):** (★)
- CLL patients can develop autoantibody-mediated RBC destruction
- Confirmatory test: **Direct antiglobulin (Coombs) test** — detects antibody/complement on RBC surface (★)
- Distinguish from indirect Coombs (detects antibodies in serum, used in pre-transfusion testing, not for diagnosing AIHA)

**CNS Involvement in CLL:**
- Presents with headache, blurred vision in established disease
- Investigation of choice: **lumbar puncture with CSF cytology** (★) — direct assessment for malignant cell infiltration of CNS

**PBF finding:** smudge cells (fragile lymphocytes that rupture during slide preparation) — characteristic but not asked directly here

## TOPIC 10: MYELOPROLIFERATIVE NEOPLASMS

**Definition:** Clonal proliferation of one or more myeloid lineages (RBC, WBC, platelet, fibroblast) in the bone marrow

**Overview Table:** (★)

| Disease | Primary Cell Affected | Key Mutation |
|---|---|---|
| Polycythaemia vera (PV) | Erythroid | JAK2 V617F (>95%) |
| Essential thrombocythaemia (ET) | Megakaryocytic | **JAK2** (most common), CALR, MPL (★) |
| Myelofibrosis | Fibroblastic reaction (reactive) | JAK2, CALR, MPL |
| CML | Granulocytic | BCR-ABL |

**POLYCYTHAEMIA VERA (PV)**

**Hallmark feature:** (★)
- **Elevated haematocrit/red cell mass with LOW erythropoietin** (★) — distinguishes primary (PV) from secondary polycythaemia (where EPO is high due to hypoxia, e.g. COPD, high altitude)
- JAK2 V617F mutation present in >95% of cases

**ESSENTIAL THROMBOCYTHAEMIA (ET)**

- Marked thrombocytosis (platelets often >450–600 ×10⁹/L)
- Most commonly associated mutation: **JAK2** (~50–60%), followed by CALR (~25%), MPL (~5%) (★)
- Risk of both thrombosis and paradoxical bleeding

**MYELOFIBROSIS**

**Definition:** Clonal myeloproliferative disorder with reactive bone marrow fibrosis

**Key findings:** (★)
- **Fibrotic bone marrow with tear-drop red blood cells (dacrocytes)** on peripheral smear (★)
- Massive splenomegaly (extramedullary haematopoiesis)
- Leukoerythroblastic blood picture (immature RBCs and WBCs in circulation)
- Increased reticulin/collagen deposition in marrow (not decreased)

## TOPIC 11: LYMPHOMA

**Definition:** Malignant proliferation of lymphocytes, primarily affecting lymph nodes and lymphoid tissue

**Classification:** Hodgkin lymphoma vs Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL)

**CLASSICAL HODGKIN LYMPHOMA**

**Key features:** (★)
- Painless lymphadenopathy (often cervical), night sweats, fever, weight loss (B symptoms)
- Diagnostic cell: **Reed-Sternberg cells** — large binucleated cells
- Immunophenotype: **CD15+ and CD30+** (★)
- Excisional lymph node biopsy = gold standard for diagnosis (★) — preserves architecture, needed over FNA/core biopsy

**NON-HODGKIN LYMPHOMA — Key Subtypes and Markers** (★)

| Lymphoma | Marker(s) | Translocation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Follicular lymphoma | **CD10**, CD20, BCL2 | t(14;18) | Indolent |
| Mantle cell lymphoma | CD5, CD20 | t(11;14) | Cyclin D1 |
| **Burkitt lymphoma** | CD10, CD20 | **t(8;14)** (★) | "Starry-sky" histology; MYC gene; rapidly growing jaw mass in children (endemic form) |
| Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma | CD20 | Variable | Most common aggressive NHL |
| **Mycosis fungoides** (cutaneous T-cell lymphoma) | **CD3** (★) | — | T-cell marker since it's a T-cell lymphoma |

**Burkitt Lymphoma — Diagnostic Work-up:** (★)
- Excisional biopsy of mass — histology shows "starry-sky" appearance (sheets of lymphoid cells with scattered macrophages)
- Cytogenetics: **t(8;14)** — MYC-IGH translocation (also less commonly t(2;8) or t(8;22))
- Immunohistochemistry: CD10+, CD20+, BCL6+, Ki-67 (very high proliferation index)
- Bone marrow aspirate — assess marrow involvement/staging
- LDH — prognostic marker, often very elevated (rapid cell turnover)
- Imaging (CT/PET) for staging
- LP with CSF cytology — assess CNS involvement (common in Burkitt)

**Tumour Markers in Lymphoma — Prognosis:** (★)
- **Beta-2 microglobulin** — most widely used prognostic tumour marker in lymphoma (★)
- LDH — reflects tumour burden/turnover, part of International Prognostic Index (IPI)

**Reactive Lymphadenopathy (Benign):**

- Histology: **follicular hyperplasia with preserved lymph node architecture** (★) — distinguishes from malignant infiltration, which shows architecture effacement

## TOPIC 12: PLASMA CELL DISORDERS

**MULTIPLE MYELOMA**

**Definition:** Malignant proliferation of plasma cells producing monoclonal immunoglobulin (paraprotein/M protein)

**Classic Presentation — "CRAB":** (★)
- **C**alcium elevated (hypercalcaemia — bone resorption)
- **R**enal impairment (light chain deposition)
- **A**naemia (marrow infiltration)
- **B**one lesions — **lytic lesions** (★, most common clinical finding), bone pain, pathological fractures

**Key Investigations:** (★)
- Serum protein electrophoresis — **monoclonal spike (M-spike/paraprotein)** (★)
- Bence Jones proteins in urine = free monoclonal **kappa or lambda light chains** (★)
- Bone marrow: increased plasma cells (>10%)
- Skeletal survey/CT: punched-out lytic lesions, especially skull
- Serum free light chain assay

**Distinguishing MGUS from Multiple Myeloma:** (★)
- MGUS = **elevated M protein WITHOUT end-organ damage** (no CRAB features) (★)
- Myeloma = M protein WITH end-organ damage
- MGUS can progress to myeloma (~1% per year risk) — requires monitoring

## TOPIC 13: COAGULATION DISORDERS & HAEMOSTASIS

**Overview of Coagulation Cascade:** (★)

| Pathway | Factors | Screening Test |
|---|---|---|
| Intrinsic | XII, XI, IX, VIII | **APTT** |
| Extrinsic | VII, Tissue factor | **PT** |
| Common | X, V, II (prothrombin), I (fibrinogen) | Both |

**HAEMOPHILIAS**

| Disease | Deficient Factor | Inheritance |
|---|---|---|
| **Haemophilia A** | **Factor VIII** (★) | X-linked recessive |
| Haemophilia B | Factor IX | X-linked recessive |

**Clinical clue (★):** Recurrent haemarthroses (joint bleeds) + easy bruising in a male child/young man, with:

- **Normal PT** + **Prolonged APTT** + **Normal TT** → points to an **intrinsic pathway factor deficiency** (Factor VIII or IX) (★)
- This pattern = Factor VIII deficiency (Haemophilia A) most commonly tested

**Von Willebrand Disease:**
- Most common inherited bleeding disorder
- Deficiency/dysfunction of von Willebrand factor (carries Factor VIII, mediates platelet adhesion)
- Causes both mucocutaneous bleeding AND prolonged APTT (due to ↓ Factor VIII)

**MONITORING ANTICOAGULANTS** (★)

| Drug | Monitoring Test | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Warfarin** | **PT/INR** (★) | Inhibits vitamin K-dependent factors (II, VII, IX, X) |
| **Unfractionated heparin** | **APTT** (★) | Potentiates antithrombin III, inhibits thrombin and Xa |
| Low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) | Anti-factor Xa (routine monitoring not usually needed) | Inhibits factor Xa |

- Warfarin therapy monitoring: PT alone is not standardised across labs → **INR (International Normalised Ratio)** is used to standardise results between labs (★)
- Unfractionated heparin infusion: monitored via **APTT** (★)

**DISSEMINATED INTRAVASCULAR COAGULATION (DIC)**

**Definition:** Widespread activation of coagulation → consumption of clotting factors and platelets → simultaneous thrombosis and bleeding

**Classic cause in haematology:** Acute Promyelocytic Leukaemia (APL/M3) — release of procoagulant material from promyelocytic granules

**Lab findings in DIC:** (★)
- **Decreased fibrinogen** (consumed) (★)
- Decreased platelets (thrombocytopenia, not thrombocytosis)
- **Increased D-dimer** (not decreased) — reflects fibrinolysis
- Prolonged PT and APTT (not shortened)

## TOPIC 14: HAEMOSTASIS IN PREGNANCY & HIV-RELATED HAEMATOLOGY

**PREGNANCY — PHYSIOLOGICAL CHANGES** (★)

| Parameter | Change in Pregnancy |
|---|---|
| ESR | **Markedly elevated** — due to ↑ fibrinogen (★) |
| Fibrinogen | **↑ markedly** — most increased coagulation factor (★) |
| Factor VIII, von Willebrand factor | ↑ |
| **Protein S** | **↓** — physiologically reduced, contributes to hypercoagulable state of pregnancy (★) |
| Protein C, Antithrombin III | Relatively unchanged or mildly altered |
| Platelet count | Mild gestational thrombocytopenia possible |

- Pregnancy = physiological **hypercoagulable state** — protective against haemorrhage at delivery, but increases VTE risk (★)

**HIV-ASSOCIATED HAEMATOLOGICAL CHANGES**

**Early HIV infection (before CD4 decline):** (★)
- Characteristic change: **lymphopenia** (★) — even before major CD4 fall, generalized lymphocyte changes occur

**Neutropenia in HIV — infection risk:**
- Severe neutropenia (ANC <0.5 ×10⁹/L) — greatest risk pathogen: **Pneumocystis jirovecii** (★) (note: PJP risk is classically tied to low CD4 count rather than neutropenil count specifically, but is the answer expected for opportunistic infection risk in this severely immunocompromised context)

**HIV-Associated Thrombocytopenia:**
- Autoantibodies typically directed against **Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa** (★) on platelet surface — similar mechanism to ITP

**Zidovudine (AZT)-Induced Macrocytic Anaemia:**
- Mechanism: zidovudine inhibits **mitochondrial DNA polymerase gamma** and interferes with **DNA synthesis** in erythroid precursors → ineffective erythropoiesis → macrocytosis (★)
- It is a direct **bone marrow toxic/myelosuppressive effect**, independent of B12/folate
- Additional tests to confirm cause: serum B12 and folate (to exclude as competing cause — typically normal in pure AZT toxicity), reticulocyte count (low, reflecting marrow suppression rather than compensation)
- Other causes of macrocytic anaemia to differentiate: **B12 deficiency, folate deficiency, hypothyroidism, liver disease, alcohol excess, myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS)** (★)
