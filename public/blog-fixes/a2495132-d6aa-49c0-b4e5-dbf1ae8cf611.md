# Prenatal Genetic Counselling and Screening

**Year 4 · Obstetrics and Gynaecology · Antenatal screening and foetal medicine**

> **In one line:** **screening** tests (nuchal translucency, serum markers, cell-free DNA) only estimate the **risk** of a chromosomal abnormality such as Down syndrome, while **diagnostic** tests (chorionic villus sampling, amniocentesis) give a definitive karyotype at a small risk of miscarriage. Counselling must be balanced and non-directive.

## What you must know

- **Screening gives risk, not a diagnosis.** Only CVS or amniocentesis diagnoses.
- **Combined test** at 11-13+6 weeks: **nuchal translucency + free beta-hCG (high) + PAPP-A (low)** + maternal age; detects about **85-90%** of Down syndrome at about 5% false positives.
- **Quadruple test** at 15-20 weeks: AFP **low**, hCG **high**, unconjugated oestriol **low**, inhibin A **high** in Down syndrome.
- **NIPT (cell-free DNA)** from 10 weeks: trisomy 21 detection above 99%, but still a screening test.
- **CVS** 11-14 weeks (miscarriage risk about 0.5-1%); **amniocentesis** from 15-16 weeks (about 0.1-0.5%).
- **Raised AFP** suggests neural tube defect; **low AFP** suggests Down syndrome.
- Give **anti-D** to Rhesus-negative women after any invasive procedure.

## 1. Screening versus diagnosis

| | Screening | Diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| Result | Estimates risk (high or low) | Definitive karyotype |
| Safety | Non-invasive (scan, blood) | Invasive; risk of miscarriage |
| After a positive result | Offer a diagnostic test | Counsel on options |

Counselling must be **non-directive**, give balanced information and respect the woman's right to accept or decline any test. It should be offered before testing (pre-test) and again with results (post-test), and always with the understanding that a normal result does not guarantee a healthy baby.

## 2. Who is at higher risk

- Maternal age **35 years or over** (risk of trisomy rises with age because of meiotic non-disjunction in the ageing oocyte).
- Previous affected child.
- Parental chromosomal rearrangement (**balanced translocation**; this raises the risk of Down syndrome by translocation, which is independent of age).
- Family history of genetic disease.
- Abnormal scan findings.
- Positive screening results.

## 3. Screening tests

### 3.1 First trimester (11-13+6 weeks): the combined test

- **Nuchal translucency (NT)** by ultrasound. Increased NT suggests trisomies 21, 18 and 13, **cardiac defects** and other syndromes.
- Maternal serum **free beta-hCG** (higher in Down syndrome) and **PAPP-A** (pregnancy-associated plasma protein A; **lower**).
- Combined with maternal age, the test detects about **85-90% of Down syndrome** at a false-positive rate of about **5%**.

### 3.2 Second trimester (15-20 weeks): quadruple test

| Marker | In Down syndrome |
|---|---|
| **Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)** | **Low** |
| **hCG** | High |
| **Unconjugated oestriol** | Low |
| **Inhibin A** | High |

### 3.3 Cell-free DNA (non-invasive prenatal testing, NIPT)

- From **10 weeks**, from a maternal blood sample (placental DNA fragments in maternal plasma).
- Detection of trisomy 21 is **above 99%** with a very low false-positive rate.
- It is still a **screening test**, so a positive result needs confirmation by an invasive test. It is also costly and not widely available in Kenyan public facilities.

### 3.4 Anomaly scan (18-22 weeks)

Examines the head, spine, heart, abdomen, kidneys and limbs. Soft markers (for example echogenic bowel, short femur, nasal bone absence) raise the suspicion of aneuploidy.

### 3.5 Neural tube defects

Raised maternal serum **AFP** (and amniotic fluid **acetylcholinesterase**) with a detailed scan; most are found by the anomaly scan. Folic acid before conception is the prevention.

> **Exam tip:** AFP is **low** in Down syndrome and **high** in neural tube defects (also raised in abdominal wall defects, multiple pregnancy and wrong dates).

## 4. Invasive diagnostic tests

| Test | Timing | Sample | Miscarriage risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chorionic villus sampling (CVS)** | 11-14 weeks | Placental tissue | About 0.5-1% |
| **Amniocentesis** | From 15-16 weeks | Amniotic fluid (foetal cells) | About 0.1-0.5% |
| **Cordocentesis** | From 18 weeks | Foetal blood | 1-2%; used for rapid karyotype or foetal anaemia |

- Perform under continuous ultrasound guidance.
- Give **anti-D** to Rhesus-negative women after any invasive procedure.
- **CVS** may show **confined placental mosaicism**, so a minority need amniocentesis for confirmation.
- Early amniocentesis (before 15 weeks) is avoided because of a higher risk of loss and talipes.

## 5. Common chromosomal conditions

| Condition | Key features |
|---|---|
| **Down syndrome (trisomy 21)** | Intellectual disability, hypotonia, flat facies, duodenal atresia, atrioventricular septal defect |
| **Edwards syndrome (trisomy 18)** | Severe; most die in infancy |
| **Patau syndrome (trisomy 13)** | Severe; most die in infancy |

## 6. Options after a diagnosis

- Continue the pregnancy with planning for care and referral to specialist centres.
- Consider termination within the law.
- Provide psychological support throughout, and offer genetic counselling for recurrence risk in future pregnancies.

## Key points to revise

1. Screening gives risk; only CVS or amniocentesis diagnoses.
2. Combined test: NT + beta-hCG + PAPP-A at 11-13+6 weeks, about 85-90% detection.
3. Quadruple test: AFP low, hCG high, oestriol low, inhibin A high in Down syndrome.
4. NIPT from 10 weeks, above 99% sensitivity, but needs confirmation.
5. CVS 11-14 weeks (0.5-1% loss); amniocentesis from 15-16 weeks (0.1-0.5% loss).
6. Low AFP suggests Down syndrome; high AFP suggests neural tube defect.
7. Anti-D after any invasive procedure in Rhesus-negative women.
8. Counselling is non-directive and respects autonomy.
